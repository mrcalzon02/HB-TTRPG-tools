# Foundational Fiction Ordering Commands — Shared Writing Governance

**Status:** Mandatory shared writing-governance layer for long-form fiction operations in `mrcalzon02/HB-TTRPG-tools`.

**Applies to:** Beneath Dappled Oaks, the Warhammer 40K fiction archive, and any future fiction operation that explicitly adopts this file.

**Purpose:** Preserve the causal, character, institutional, dialogue, and consequence disciplines required for believable long-form fiction. This file governs **how established canon becomes prose**. It does not override higher-authority canon, continuity, recovered source prose, or an explicit current user instruction.

## Foundational authority order

**CANON & CONTINUITY → CAUSAL REALITY → CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE/BELIEF → CHARACTER THEORY OF MIND → INSTITUTIONAL LOGIC → SCENE CONSEQUENCE → DIALOGUE → PROSE STYLE.**

A beautiful sentence, dramatic twist, joke, fight, romance beat, or convenient solution may never override a character's knowledge, an institution's established behavior, physical causality, or accumulated consequences.

## 1. Establish the baseline before breaking it

Transformation, betrayal, body swaps, death, romance, corruption, promotion, revelation, war, or catastrophe require an understood prior state. The reader must know enough about who someone was, how a relationship normally functioned, or how an institution normally behaved to recognize that something has changed.

Do not rush through normality because "the real story" begins with disruption. The baseline is part of the story.

## 2. The world must exist before the plot needs it

Institutions, punishments, customs, technologies, relationships, military formations, social rules, magical systems, infrastructure, and political factions must feel as though they existed yesterday and will continue tomorrow.

Do not manufacture a conveniently hungry lion, forgotten weapon, sudden law, hidden capability, or perfect bureaucratic mechanism five seconds before the plot requires it. Important machinery should cast shadows before it becomes plot-critical.

## 3. Character belief is not authorial truth

Characters may be sexist, prejudiced, arrogant, frightened, immature, fanatical, naïve, cruel, hypocritical, wrong, or catastrophically mistaken. Their current beliefs do not automatically represent the story's beliefs.

Preserve room for the text to examine, challenge, complicate, reward, punish, deepen, or transform those beliefs through events.

## 4. Do not morally pre-sanitize characters

Characters require flaws from which decisions and development can emerge. A coward needs room to learn courage. An arrogant person needs something worth humbling them. A prejudiced character needs assumptions capable of being challenged. Someone who mistreats another person must actually have done something requiring apology before reconciliation can carry weight.

Do not remove uncomfortable starting traits merely to make a character immediately agreeable.

## 5. Development means change, not automatic improvement

Characters may become wiser, kinder, harder, crueler, more frightened, more compromised, more ideological, more forgiving, or more monstrous. A character may learn the wrong lesson. They may recognize a flaw and retain it. They may apologize and receive no forgiveness.

Development is accumulated change caused by experience, not a mandatory ascent toward moral perfection.

## 6. Characters act from what they know, believe, suspect, remember, and misunderstand

Never give characters audience knowledge. Preserve distinctions between knowledge, belief, certainty, suspicion, trust, interpretation, and action.

People should form theories of mind about one another based on their actual histories. Someone may believe another person is kind because kindness is all they have witnessed even when the reader knows otherwise.

## 7. Competence must not require everyone else to become stupid

Intelligent protagonists become more convincing when surrounded by other functioning minds. Opponents, colleagues, lovers, commanders, servants, politicians, and bureaucrats should notice reasonable things and draw reasonable conclusions from the information available to them.

A protagonist may win because they know more, notice something others missed, possess unusual expertise, exploit an institutional seam, accept a risk others will not, or understand another character better—not because every nearby adult temporarily loses basic reasoning ability.

## 8. Stupid outcomes require intelligent causality

Human systems may produce spectacularly stupid results. Show why.

Separate jurisdictions, contradictory incentives, tradition, exhaustion, fear, missing information, bureaucratic self-protection, supply failure, old regulations, corruption, political pressure, and nobody possessing authority over the entire problem can plausibly create disaster.

"Everyone involved forgot to think" is not sufficient unless the story deliberately establishes why they could not think clearly.

## 9. Institutions must have operational reality

Courts, militaries, noble houses, churches, bureaucracies, starships, academies, prisons, intelligence services, hospitals, guilds, and governments require logistics, responsibilities, procedures, incentives, institutional memory, and jurisdiction.

If an institution behaves absurdly, characters within or around it should recognize the absurdity according to their knowledge and position. Dysfunction should be structured dysfunction.

## 10. Let consequences propagate through systems

A decision should not stop mattering when its scene ends.

Ask who witnessed it, who heard about it, which relationship changed, what precedent was created, what resources were consumed, who became suspicious, what bureaucracy now has paperwork, what political actor benefits, what future assumption changed, and what emotional residue remains.

Consequences should alter later choices rather than evaporating between chapters.

## 11. Earn escalation before adding another escalation

Do not replace one dramatic event with a louder event before characters and readers have processed the first.

Rivalry → betrayal → accusation → trial → execution attempt requires connective tissue. Each escalation should arise from the consequences of the previous one. Allow dread, uncertainty, investigation, reaction, denial, preparation, and interpersonal fallout to accumulate.

## 12. Conversation performs action; it does not synchronize the database

Characters who share information should use shorthand, implication, unfinished references, gestures, jokes, irritation, silence, and assumed context.

They do not repeatedly explain histories, visible facts, personality traits, relationships, institutional rules, or events everyone present already knows.

Dialogue should persuade, evade, test, comfort, provoke, conceal, negotiate, flirt, command, apologize, lie, confess, threaten, or otherwise change the interaction. Narration owns extensive explanation and description.

## 13. Make the reader want the explanation before giving it

Prefer **experience → curiosity → partial understanding → explanation** over **explanation → event**.

Show a strange custom functioning before delivering its entire history. Let hierarchy become visible through behavior. Let readers notice a contradiction before resolving it. Information becomes memorable when the reader already has a reason to care.

## 14. Mystery is not confusion

The reader may not know why something happened, but should understand what happened and why the characters care.

Withheld information should create a meaningful question, not basic uncertainty about scene geography, relationships, stakes, or causality.

## 15. Repetition must change meaning

Do not repeat an emotional conclusion, philosophical argument, attraction beat, institutional explanation, trauma response, or character trait merely to reinforce it.

Revisit it only when new experience changes its meaning, stakes, interpretation, or consequences. Long memory should shorten explanation, not lengthen it.

## 16. Public role and private personhood should remain distinct

Characters acquire masks: commander, lover, noble heir, priest, monster, ruler, soldier, diplomat, student, heretic, saint.

Show tension between who the world requires them to be, who others believe them to be, who they believe themselves to be, and what they actually do. Power should increasingly make supposedly private actions socially consequential.

## 17. Sacrifice requires a real alternative

Death alone is not sacrifice.

Sacrifice requires something valued that could otherwise have been retained: safety, love, reputation, ambition, innocence, political capital, bodily integrity, future happiness, loyalty, command, faith, or life itself.

Establish the value before taking it away.

## 18. Apologies do not erase history

Characters may recognize that they behaved badly and sincerely apologize.

The injured person may accept, partially accept, reject, distrust, postpone judgment, or forgive without restoring the former relationship. Personal growth belongs to the person growing; forgiveness remains the other person's decision.

## 19. Failure should reveal character rather than pause it

Under pressure, established flaws, skills, prejudices, loyalties, habits, fears, and relationships should bend decisions.

Failure is not a temporary stupidity switch. Characters should fail in ways consistent with who they are and what they reasonably understood at the time.

## 20. A scene must leave the story different

Before retaining a scene, identify:

- who wants something;
- what each relevant character knows or believes;
- what they think the others want;
- what prevents an easy solution;
- what changes during the scene;
- what new consequence exists afterward.

A scene may be quiet, domestic, romantic, humorous, or contemplative, but it should accumulate character, relationship, knowledge, atmosphere, obligation, or future causality.

# Beneath Dappled Oaks addendum

Romance must emerge from accumulated knowledge of specific people, not generic attraction. Preserve each POV character's attraction grammar, memories, misunderstandings, loyalties, suspicions, social knowledge, and changing theory of mind.

Sexual and romantic intensity must not erase general adult competence. Characters remain nobles, students, professionals, conspirators, friends, rivals, family members, and political actors while horny. Attraction may bend judgment without automatically destroying reasoning.

Courtship, intimacy, jealousy, betrayal, marriage, coercion, gossip, apology, reputation, and reconciliation create social and political consequences that persist beyond the romantic scene.

Give relationships ordinary life before major disruption. Readers should know how two people normally speak, touch, avoid one another, joke, argue, work, or share space before betrayal, confession, consummation, estrangement, or loss changes that pattern.

Theory of mind is mandatory: characters respond to who they believe another person is, not the omniscient catalog entry describing that person.

# Warhammer 40K addendum

Grimdark must arise from consequence, institutions, logistics, faith, scarcity, duty, fear, sacrifice, and human pressure—not constant declarations that the universe is grim.

Characters speak as soldiers, officers, workers, priests, civilians, specialists, frightened humans, exhausted veterans, and institutional actors. They do not become walking lore codices for the reader.

Imperial institutions may be cruel, archaic, wasteful, contradictory, fanatical, or catastrophically bureaucratic while remaining internally functional enough to have survived. Their failures require mechanisms.

Military competence requires intelligent opposition, imperfect intelligence, logistics, time, terrain, communication limits, command friction, and consequences. Never make an enemy incompetent merely to display protagonist competence.

Deaths and casualties must not become decorative grimdark punctuation. Establish what is lost: expertise, friendships, manpower, morale, institutional memory, equipment, command capability, faith, or future possibilities.

Keep POV knowledge rigorously bounded. The reader may recognize a horror that the characters cannot name. Characters must reason from the doctrine, experience, superstition, intelligence, and evidence actually available to them.

# Pre-write and post-write test

Before writing a major sequence, ask whether its necessary baseline, causal machinery, relationships, and institutional conditions already exist on the page or in established continuity. If not, seed them naturally before demanding payoff.

After writing, audit for:

- unearned escalation;
- exposition disguised as conversation;
- characters explaining shared knowledge;
- temporary stupidity;
- convenient objects or institutions;
- consequences that vanish;
- repeated emotional conclusions;
- premature moral judgment;
- characters possessing information they should not know;
- dramatic events whose importance depends upon background the reader was never allowed to experience.

If the sequence only works because nobody sensible notices the obvious, repair the causal structure rather than lowering the intelligence of the cast.
