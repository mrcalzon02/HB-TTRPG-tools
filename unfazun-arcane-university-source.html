# Alkroves Ichesnas Arcane Academia Universitalis

## The Great Arcane University of Unfazun

The **Alkroves Ichesnas Arcane Academia Universitalis**, usually shortened to the **Great Arcane University**, was never merely a school. It was a fortified academic district, a treasury authority, a military institution, a religious center, and one of the principal anchors of Unfazun’s Outer Watch.

At its height, the university possessed seven great stories above the cavern floor and eight subterranean levels driven into the stone beneath it. Its towers rose beside the lower terminus of the Asphelt Lightwell, the immense enchanted staircase that once connected the mining realms above with the sacred kingdoms, royal tombs, and primordial forges of the Eighth and Ninth Layers.

Thousands studied here. Wizards debated beside runesmiths. Enchanters worked beside blacksmiths. Paladins attended lectures on planar law, while clerics studied the limits of divine authority. Sorcerers learned to restrain inherited power. Archaeologists catalogued relics recovered from the Great Forges. Treasury examiners trained the students and officials who would accompany the rare expeditions into the deeper ruins.

The university was also where dwarven necromancy ceased to be a secret tradition and became the center of a civil war.

When Moraden withdrew his blessing from necromantic clerics, the paladins of the Deep Ward marched against professors, ancestral custodians, mortuary priests, and noble houses that refused to surrender their honored dead. Silence spells were made permanent throughout the campus to prevent spellcasters from speaking their invocations. Lecture halls became killing grounds. Dormitories became fortresses. Libraries burned beneath sheets of magical frost. Entire towers were dropped into the levels beneath them.

The university now survives as an interlocked labyrinth of broken architecture.

A person may enter through a ground-floor gatehouse, descend a shattered stair, cross the remains of a third-story lecture hall, crawl through a fallen tower, and emerge in a cellar originally constructed six levels below the cavern floor. Floors have become walls. Walls have become bridges. Towers have sunk into libraries, while basements have been lifted or twisted by failing geomantic enchantments.

The ruin does not feel empty.

It feels as though the university is holding its breath.

# Conditions Within the University

## The Supernatural Cold

The temperature within the ruin is lethally low. Ordinary flame gutters blue and produces little warmth. Metal adheres to bare skin. Breath crystallizes upon beards, helmets, and clothing.

The university’s surviving thermal wards provide isolated islands of tolerable temperature. Some activate when approached by a dwarf. Others demand an academic seal, spoken formula, bloodline signature, or payment in coin.

Where the wards have failed completely, people may freeze without realizing that they are dying. The cold numbs pain, slows thought, and creates a profound desire to sit down and rest.

Those who fall asleep sometimes hear the distant sound of hammers.

## The Fields of Silence

Many areas are under permanent magical silence. These fields vary in strength.

Some suppress only deliberate spellcasting. Others swallow every sound. A few permit speech but remove specific words associated with magic, death, gods, or royal authority. Within the oldest fields, spoken words may be stolen and repeated elsewhere in the university.

The undead have adapted to these regions. Ghouls communicate by tapping stone. Vampire spawn use hand signs once employed by duelists. Ghosts write warnings in frost.

## The Broken University Bell

The university once used an enchanted bell network to signal class changes, emergencies, military mobilization, and ritual observances.

The bells still ring, although most no longer exist.

When a phantom bell sounds, doors may open, stairways may shift, silence fields may move, and the dead may briefly reenact the final day of the civil war.

The most dangerous chime is the sevenfold alarm known as the **Revocation Bell**. When it rings, every surviving university ward identifies necromancy as an active threat.

Unfortunately, many of the university’s inhabitants are necromantic beings.

## The Wandering Architecture

The ruin does not randomly change. It follows an ancient academic timetable.

At particular intervals, rotating bridges, moving stairways, rune-powered lifts, and corridor partitions attempt to direct nonexistent students toward classes that ended centuries ago.

The schedule is incomplete, but not entirely broken.

Anyone who recovers a surviving timetable can predict portions of the university’s movements.

# Powers Within the Ruin

## The Pale Collegium

A coterie of vampire spawn occupies the former faculty quarter. They imitate academic life, appointing themselves professors, deans, and examiners.

Their leader, **Rector Emeritus Volkran Dhal**, was once a respected professor of sympathetic enchantment. He insists that the university remains operational and that all visitors must enroll, submit a thesis, or leave.

The Pale Collegium considers the ghouls to be undisciplined students, the revenant paladins to be trespassers, and the deeper necromancers to be academic rivals.

## The Famished Houses

The university’s ghouls retain fragments of their former student identities. They divide themselves according to dormitory, discipline, and graduating class.

Some still wear frozen academic robes. Some collect books. Others scratch examination answers into floors using finger bones.

They are ravenous, but their hunger can occasionally be redirected by invoking old rivalries, promising access to examinations, or offering the remains of a professor.

## The Ashen Convocation

The remains of paladins and martial clerics patrol portions of the campus as frozen revenants.

They continue enforcing the final decree of Moraden: necromancy must be destroyed, its practitioners condemned, and all stolen dead released.

They do not automatically attack the living. However, they interpret healing magic, resurrection, summoned spirits, and many forms of divine intervention through the laws of the final civil war.

## The Unreturned Faculty

Some of the university’s necromancers remain in sealed laboratories beneath the campus. They survived by transferring portions of themselves into preserved bodies, lecture apparatus, soul vessels, and ancestral reliquaries.

They no longer agree on why they fought.

Some believe they were defending dwarven civilization. Some insist that death itself is an enemy. Some understand that their work became monstrous but cannot bring themselves to surrender the people they preserved.

## The Iron Archivist

An ancient administrative construct called **Kheled-Index-Seven** continues cataloguing the dead, the missing, the expelled, and the overdue.

It is one of the few entities capable of opening every academic door in the university.

It will provide access to authorized students, faculty, royal inspectors, and properly registered corpses.

# Room-by-Room Dungeon Key

The numbering below represents an exploration key, not the original floor plan. Collapses have folded distant areas together, and several rooms may physically occupy different elevations despite being directly connected.

---

# I. The Frostbound Approaches

## 1. The Processional of Empty Banners

A broad avenue leads through the frozen ruins toward the university gate. Stone poles line both sides, but the banners hanging from them have become rigid sheets of ice.

Each banner bears the name of a faculty, student house, military order, or royal sponsor. The names of departments associated with necromancy have been chiseled away.

Frozen bodies stand along the avenue as though waiting for a procession. When the distant university bell rings, the corpses turn their heads toward the gate.

One corpse clutches a copper admissions token.

**Connections:** The avenue leads to Area 2. A collapsed drainage channel reaches Area 31.

## 2. The Outer Watch Gatehouse

The gatehouse is embedded in the city’s defensive wall. Its upper stories have collapsed, creating a steep mound of arrow slits, broken offices, and frozen masonry.

A stone admissions examiner sits behind a desk carved into the gate. The construct asks each visitor to state their name, discipline, sponsor, and purpose.

Those who answer convincingly receive a temporary student designation. Those who threaten the examiner cause the gatehouse to activate its murder holes, silence wards, and defensive runes.

A hidden military stair descends behind the desk.

**Connections:** The main doors open into Area 3. The military stair reaches Area 81. Rubble passages connect to Area 5.

## 3. The Court of Silenced Welcome

This courtyard once welcomed caravans and new students. It is now enclosed by broken walls and the leaning remains of three towers.

No sound exists here. Footsteps, falling stones, weapons, and screams are completely swallowed.

Dozens of dead students lie beneath the frost. Most were killed while attempting to cast spells during the final assault.

A trail of written messages crosses the paving stones:

**DO NOT SPEAK WHEN YOU LEAVE. IT LISTENS FOR THE FIRST WORD.**

The warning refers to a frost-wraith nesting above the eastern arch.

**Connections:** Arches lead to Areas 4, 6, and 9. A collapsed arcade leads to Area 12.

## 4. The Avenue of Founders

Statues of the university’s founders stand in two rows. Their faces have been methodically removed, except for that of **Alkroves Ichesnas**, the institution’s first rector.

Each statue holds a symbolic object representing a discipline: hammer, staff, shield, book, lantern, key, chain, skull, crown, and flame.

The skull has been broken from its statue. Replacing it causes a hidden door to open beneath Ichesnas’s pedestal.

The concealed chamber contains the original university charter, which explicitly permits the study of ancestral necromancy under royal and divine supervision.

**Connections:** Returns to Area 3. The hidden stair descends to Area 49.

## 5. The Fallen Provost’s Tower

The tower has collapsed sideways through the gatehouse and into an administrative building. Its central spiral stair now forms a horizontal passage lined with doors in the floor and ceiling.

Several offices can be entered only by climbing downward through their former walls.

A dead provost remains strapped into a chair to prevent the sideways chamber from carrying his body away. His signet opens many administrative locks.

The tower groans whenever the university’s architecture moves.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 2, 7, 17, and 36.

## 6. The Hall of Admissions

Long counters divide the chamber into lanes. Thousands of stone tablets sit in wall recesses, each bearing the name of a student.

Some names glow faintly. These students remain somewhere within the university, living, undead, imprisoned, or preserved.

A spectral clerk repeatedly asks visitors whether they are applying as the living, the dead, the returned, or the legally ambiguous.

Registration can grant access to portions of the university, but the category selected influences how its wards treat the applicant.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 3, 7, 8, and 10.

## 7. The Registry of Names

The registry contains rotating stone cylinders engraved with student and faculty records. Turning the cylinders allows visitors to search by family, discipline, status, or cause of death.

Several cylinders spin by themselves, repeatedly displaying names that were erased after the civil war.

A concealed record reveals that hundreds of “voluntary ancestral defenders” were legally reclassified as university property after resurrection.

The provost’s signet from Area 5 permits alteration of one surviving record.

**Connections:** Connects to Areas 5, 6, 19, and 50.

## 8. The Hall of Cloaks and Seals

Academic robes hang from bronze hooks. Most crumble when touched, but several remain protected by enchantment.

Robes correspond to different faculties and grant limited recognition from doors, constructs, and academic spirits. Wearing the wrong robe in the wrong department may be considered impersonation.

A small changing chamber contains the body of a student who attempted to wear seven faculty robes simultaneously.

He still carries six forged identification seals.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 6, 9, and 25.

## 9. The Grand Lecture Rotunda

The rotunda has partially collapsed into the level beneath it. Stone benches descend toward a central lectern, but half the seating now hangs vertically along a broken wall.

A ghostly lecturer periodically appears and begins a lesson titled **The Moral Distinction Between Service and Enslavement After Death**.

The lecture changes depending on who is listening. Living creatures hear a defense of necromancy. Undead hear an accusation. Clerics hear Moraden’s condemnation.

Completing the lecture without interruption reveals a stair beneath the lectern.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 3, 8, 13, and 61.

## 10. The Hall of Bells

Hundreds of small bells once hung here, each connected to a classroom, residence, workshop, or defensive post.

Most have been torn down. Their severed ropes dangle from the ceiling like frozen vines.

Seven bells remain functional. Ringing them alters parts of the ruin:

- The Copper Bell opens student facilities.
- The Iron Bell activates martial defenses.
- The Silver Bell calls faculty spirits.
- The Black Bell awakens the dead.
- The White Bell suppresses necromancy.
- The Golden Bell activates treasury protocols.
- The Cracked Bell advances the university to the next portion of its ancient timetable.

The bells make no sound in this room. Their chimes are heard elsewhere.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 6, 11, 18, and 53.

## 11. The Northern Cloister

The cloister surrounds a garden of petrified fungi and frozen subterranean trees. The garden’s heat enchantment survives beneath the soil, creating narrow channels of steaming water.

Vampire spawn use the covered walkway to travel between their territories without crossing the open courts.

Names are carved into the pillars. Each marks a student killed during the first night of the civil war.

One pillar bears tomorrow’s date.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 10, 17, 22, and 49.

## 12. The Sunken Court

An entire courtyard has collapsed into the dining level below. Tables, statues, trees, and paving stones lie intermingled at different angles.

A frozen fountain hangs from the broken edge above, its water forming a solid descending spiral.

Ghouls live within cavities beneath the rubble. They avoid the center of the court, where a paladin remains frozen upright with both hands around the throat of a necromancer.

Disturbing either corpse awakens both.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 3, 23, 25, and 37.

# II. The Instructional Halls

## 13. The Chamber of Practical Thaumaturgy

This classroom was designed to survive magical accidents. Its walls are layered with copper, lead, silver, cold iron, volcanic glass, and rune-carved granite.

Twenty practice circles cover the floor. Several still contain failed spells.

One holds a ball of fire frozen solid. Another contains a student’s shadow. A third causes anything placed within it to become convinced that it is being watched.

The instructor’s console can deactivate one containment circle at a time.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 9, 14, 16, and 43.

## 14. The Hall of Runic Geometry

The floor is carved into an enormous geometric proof. Portions of the pattern continue across the walls and ceiling.

Gravity changes according to which rune lines a creature crosses. Careless movement may send explorers falling sideways or upward.

Students once learned to construct stable enchantments by walking the proof in the correct sequence.

Completing the sequence opens a sealed demonstration chamber containing an intact master runesmith’s kit.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 13, 15, 37, and 51.

## 15. The Counterspell Amphitheater

Two raised platforms face one another across a deep pit. Students once attempted to cast spells while their classmates interrupted them.

The permanent silence field here activates and deactivates in rotating wedges.

Spectral students continue the exercise. They hurl harmless phantom magic until someone casts a real spell, at which point the chamber attempts to counter it with overwhelming force.

At the bottom of the pit lies a heap of confiscated spellcasting focuses.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 14, 16, 20, and 47.

## 16. The Laboratory of Applied Silence

Black stone partitions divide the room into testing cells. Each cell suppresses a different element of communication: sound, gesture, writing, telepathy, prayer, memory, or intent.

The central control desk records how long subjects remain capable of spellcasting.

A dead researcher has scratched a final note into the desk:

**SILENCE DOES NOT STOP THE DEAD FROM LISTENING.**

One experimental field contains an invisible entity that has been trapped without sensation for centuries.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 13, 15, 18, and 64.

## 17. The Faculty Commons

Leather chairs surround a dead hearth. Cups, pipes, journals, and unfinished correspondence remain where the professors abandoned them.

The Pale Collegium uses this room as neutral territory. Vampire spawn arrive according to a schedule, pretending to discuss departmental policy.

They may invite visitors to participate in a faculty review. The discussion is civil unless anyone refers to the university as ruined, abandoned, or closed.

A warm bottle of preserved royal brandy remains in a locked cabinet.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 5, 11, 18, 52, and 60.

## 18. The Scriptorium of Repeated Errors

Rows of writing desks face a wall covered in moving text. Invisible quills copy lectures, legal rulings, and examination materials.

The quills have been copying the final emergency decree for centuries. The floor is buried beneath brittle sheets of parchment.

Hidden among them is an earlier version of the decree that ordered the arrest of necromancers rather than their immediate execution.

The difference suggests that someone altered the command after it was issued.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 10, 16, 17, and 50.

## 19. The Vault of Examinations

Iron drawers contain sealed examinations from every faculty.

Opening a drawer causes the room to administer the corresponding test. Correct answers unlock academic privileges. Failure may summon an examiner, impose a curse, or mark the student for remedial instruction.

The final necromancy examination asks only one question:

**When does duty end?**

No surviving answer has been accepted.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 7, 20, 34, and 57.

## 20. The Dueling Gallery

A long chamber is divided by waist-high stone barriers. Students once practiced wand duels, shield spells, runic counters, and controlled magical combat.

The gallery remains occupied by two skeletal duelists who have fought the same match for centuries.

Each believes the other began using forbidden magic first.

If separated, they can describe conflicting versions of the civil war’s opening moments.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 15, 19, 21, and 47.

## 21. The University Infirmary

Stone beds line a chamber heated by failing amber crystals. Frost covers half the room, while the other half remains feverishly hot.

Several patients are magically preserved between life and death. They cannot awaken while the university’s legal system still classifies them as casualties under treatment.

A healing construct moves between the beds, replacing organs, setting bones, and amputating necrotic tissue.

It interprets undeath as an advanced but treatable medical condition.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 20, 22, 29, and 65.

## 22. The Apothecary of Deep Remedies

Cabinets contain mineral medicines, fungal extracts, powdered gems, embalming salts, dragon-bone ash, and preserved subterranean organisms.

Most labels use obsolete academic notation.

A colony of intelligent medicinal molds has spread across the northern wall. The molds remember every patient they were used to treat.

They can identify several important undead inhabitants by their former names.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 11, 21, 23, and 39.

## 23. The Refectory of the Thousand Seats

Long stone tables fill a vaulted hall large enough to feed an army.

Frozen meals remain on some tables, preserved from the university’s final day. Other tables are piled with gnawed bones.

The Famished Houses meet here under temporary truces. They argue over seating privileges, faculty favoritism, and which dormitory technically won the final inter-house competition.

The old kitchen bells occasionally ring, causing every ghoul in the room to sit and wait for food.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 12, 22, 24, 25, and 27.

## 24. The Kitchens and Brewer’s Descent

Massive ovens stand cold beneath blackened chimneys. Copper brewing vats occupy the lower chamber.

The university brewed nourishing black ale designed to sustain students through long periods of study. Several sealed casks remain drinkable and provide temporary resistance to cold.

A brewer’s lift descends to storage tunnels, but its platform is missing. The shaft is inhabited by pale, eyeless things that climb only when they smell warm food.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 23, 31, and 78.

# III. The Student Houses

## 25. House Anvil

House Anvil housed blacksmiths, runesmiths, and martial enchanters.

Its common room has been fortified with overturned beds, broken doors, and anvils chained together as barricades. Ghoul students defend it as though the civil war continues outside.

Their prefect, **Dorga Emberthumb**, retains enough intelligence to negotiate. She demands proof that the visitors belong to a recognized faculty.

A hidden floor compartment contains student-made weapons confiscated before graduation.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 8, 12, 23, 26, and 37.

## 26. House Lantern

House Lantern once housed diviners, astrologers, and scholars of planar movement.

Its ceiling displays a false night sky. The stars have shifted into positions that do not correspond to the present world.

Every dormitory chamber shows a different possible future for its occupant. Most depict death somewhere within the university.

One room shows the university restored, filled with living students, with the explorers themselves displayed as statues.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 25, 27, 33, and 44.

## 27. House Deepstone

This residence belonged to archaeologists, geomancers, and students preparing for expeditions into the lower layers.

Maps cover every wall. Many depict tunnels that no longer exist, while others show passages that have never been discovered.

The resident ghouls compulsively excavate through floors and walls.

Their latest tunnel has reached a sealed chamber from before the university’s construction.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 23, 26, 28, 45, and the ancient chamber beneath Area 72.

## 28. House Crown

House Crown housed noble students, royal scholars, treasury heirs, and politically favored clerics.

Its rooms are larger and better preserved than the other dormitories. The doors recognize noble bloodlines and insult anyone without one.

A dining chamber contains portraits of students who later became kings, judges, generals, and necromancer-lords.

One portrait changes to resemble whoever currently possesses the strongest claim to Unfazun’s abandoned throne.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 27, 34, 54, and 73.

## 29. The Communal Bathing Vault

A series of stone pools once used geothermal water drawn from the deeper layers.

Most are frozen. One remains steaming, but the water reflects the bather as they would appear after death.

The walls are covered in student graffiti, romantic declarations, political slogans, examination complaints, and increasingly desperate messages written during the siege.

A submerged maintenance passage leads beneath the infirmary.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 21, 30, 31, and 33.

## 30. The Frozen Laundry

Enchanted drums, presses, and drying racks fill the room. Sheets and academic robes hang as hard as boards.

A laundry construct continues folding clothing around the frozen bodies inside it.

Numerous secret messages are stitched into robe hems. Students used the laundry system to pass information between dormitories during the civil war.

One message identifies a faculty member responsible for creating the permanent silence zones.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 29, 31, and 35.

## 31. The Servants’ Labyrinth

These narrow passages carried food, fuel, laundry, messages, and waste throughout the university.

Unlike the formal corridors, the servants’ tunnels connect almost every major wing.

They are cramped, unheated, and filled with collapsed ducts. Small undead creatures use them to avoid the university’s larger predators.

Marks scratched into the walls form an unofficial map created by generations of servants who understood the building better than its architects.

**Connections:** Reaches Areas 1, 24, 29, 30, 32, 40, 50, 58, 65, and 78.

## 32. The Custodians’ Rune Closet

Mops, brushes, chisels, buckets, warding salt, repair runes, and tiny maintenance constructs fill this remarkably intact chamber.

The custodians possessed master overrides because they needed access to every part of the university.

A ring of service keys hangs from the belt of a frozen dwarf beneath a fallen shelf.

One key is missing. It remains in the possession of the Iron Archivist.

**Connections:** Leads to Area 31 and a concealed service stair to Area 43.

## 33. The Chapel of Student Petitions

This small chapel was intended for students who wished to ask Moraden for endurance, inspiration, or forgiveness.

Thousands of written prayers fill cracks in the walls.

The most recent prayers do not ask for victory. They ask whether the god can still hear anyone inside the silence fields.

A headless cleric kneels before the altar. If the altar is repaired, the cleric’s spirit appears and warns that the university’s deepest necromantic vault was never successfully purged.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 26, 29, 34, and 70.

## 34. The Hall of Debts

Students receiving royal funding signed contracts here. Their future labor, discoveries, inventions, and military service were pledged against the cost of their education.

Stone tablets display debts that continued accumulating after death.

Several undead students are magically prevented from leaving the university because they have not completed their required service.

Destroying or amending the records may release them.

The central ledger claims that the university itself owes an impossible sum to the kings entombed below.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 19, 28, 33, 35, and 56.

## 35. The Society Behind the Wall

A false dormitory wall conceals a meeting room used by a student necromancy society.

The chamber contains candles, copied texts, ancestor masks, political pamphlets, and records of debates about the morality of returning the dead.

The students were not united. Some advocated consent, limited service, and release. Others argued that every dwarf owed eternal duty to the survival of the people.

A final vote was interrupted before the result could be recorded.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 30, 34, 36, and 62.

## 36. The Broken Spiral

A tower stair has collapsed through four different floors. Portions of its spiral remain suspended in open space.

Gravity changes unpredictably near the center.

The stair provides one of the few routes between the upper ruins and the deep university, but several sections must be climbed, crossed upside down, or jumped while the structure rotates.

Frozen bodies drift in the central void.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 5, 35, 48, 53, and 61.

# IV. The Great Workshops

## 37. The Runesmiths’ Hall

A hundred stone workbenches fill this broad chamber. Half-finished runes cover shields, weapons, tools, doors, cookware, prosthetics, and pieces of architecture.

The hall’s central hammer strikes by itself whenever someone speaks a deliberate lie.

A masterwork rune remains unfinished on the largest anvil. Completing it could restore power to one major university system.

A revenant instructor attacks anyone who handles tools without first demonstrating proper workshop safety.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 12, 14, 25, 38, and 40.

## 38. The Enchantment Crucible

A circular furnace occupies the center of the room, fed by channels that once carried heat from the Great Forges.

The fire is gone, but a sphere of concentrated enchantment still floats above the crucible.

Objects placed within it acquire temporary magical properties shaped by the strongest nearby emotion.

During the civil war, weapons enchanted here absorbed fear, hatred, grief, and religious certainty.

Several remain buried in slag beneath the furnace.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 37, 39, 43, and 46.

## 39. The Elemental Binding Laboratory

Bronze cages contain remnants of fire, stone, wind, lightning, and molten metal elementals.

The supernatural cold has weakened most of them. The fire elemental is now a fist-sized ember that begs to be carried somewhere warm.

Freeing it can restore several furnaces, but it remembers being enslaved and may retaliate against dwarven constructs.

A shattered cage once contained an elemental of pure cold. Its occupant now moves freely through the university.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 22, 38, 40, and 44.

## 40. The Practical Smithy

This industrial classroom contains rows of forges, quenching troughs, anvils, and mechanical hammers.

The forges are cold, but the ventilation system still draws air downward toward the deeper levels.

Student projects remain locked in iron cabinets. Most are crude. Several are unexpectedly ingenious.

A service passage contains the corpse of a professor who died protecting a box of deliberately ordinary iron nails.

The nails were intended to teach students that not every problem required magic.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 31, 37, 39, 41, and 78.

## 41. The Golem Foundry

Stone limbs hang from chains. Metal torsos stand in assembly frames. Rows of empty heads face the central work floor.

The university produced laborers, guards, mining constructs, and experimental teaching assistants here.

A partially completed golem wanders the chamber asking visitors to define its purpose. It becomes increasingly unstable when given contradictory instructions.

Its chest contains a sealed compartment meant to house a soul crystal.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 40, 42, 43, and 65.

## 42. The University Armory

Weapons designed for magical instruction line the walls: rune-hammers, counterspell shields, staves, training axes, ward-breaking picks, and ceremonial weapons.

Most dangerous items were removed during the civil war.

The remaining armory is guarded by animated suits of armor that demand a valid mobilization order.

A concealed rack contains weapons specifically designed to incapacitate spellcasters without killing them. None appear to have been used.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 41, 47, 69, and 81.

## 43. The Wardstone Laboratory

Blocks of enchanted stone sit within testing frames.

These wardstones once powered the university’s defenses, climate controls, silence fields, doors, and structural supports.

A central map displays the condition of surviving systems. Several can be redirected, but restoring one area requires depriving another.

The map also reveals a large unregistered power source beneath Area 72.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 13, 32, 38, 41, 44, and 48.

## 44. The Observatory of Deep Cold

A crystal dome looks out across the Great Ice Caverns.

The glass is cracked, allowing frost to grow inward in enormous branching patterns. These patterns resemble writing when viewed from above.

The observatory studied the supernatural cold and concluded that it was not merely an absence of heat. It behaved like an intelligent pressure seeking entry into warm spaces.

A frost elemental watches from outside the dome.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 26, 39, 43, and 45.

## 45. The Geomantic Survey Hall

A three-dimensional stone map of the Seventh, Eighth, and Ninth Layers fills the chamber.

Lights once marked mines, roads, temples, tombs, fortresses, and magma channels. Only a few still glow.

One light pulses beneath the ruined Great Forges.

Another moves slowly through the rock toward Unfazun.

The university’s treasure-expedition routes can be reconstructed here.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 27, 44, 46, 73, and 80.

## 46. The Transmutation Vault

The room contains sealed cabinets built from materials resistant to magical alteration.

The floor is divided into testing squares. Each has transformed differently: stone to salt, iron to glass, wood to bone, gold to ash, and flesh to black crystal.

A dwarf-shaped figure of solid gold stands in the central square.

The figure is alive, conscious, and furious that rescuers have taken so long.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 38, 45, 47, and 55.

## 47. The Destructive Testing Range

This immense corridor was used to test spells, weapons, armor, and defensive enchantments.

Targets stand at measured intervals. The far wall has been rebuilt hundreds of times.

An automated system evaluates anything carried into the room and attempts to test it to failure.

The range can be used against pursuing enemies, but it does not distinguish between equipment and people.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 15, 20, 42, 46, and 48.

## 48. The Disaster Regulation Chamber

Levers, valves, ward controls, and structural diagrams cover the walls.

This chamber coordinated responses to magical fires, escaped elementals, collapses, summoned creatures, and laboratory contamination.

During the civil war, someone used it to deliberately drop three towers into the necromancy wing.

The controls can shift rubble, vent cold, open emergency routes, or collapse additional sections.

One lever is labeled:

**TOTAL CAMPUS BURIAL—ROYAL AUTHORITY REQUIRED.**

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 36, 43, 47, 61, and 79.

# V. The Library Labyrinth

## 49. The Great Library of Ichesnas

The library occupies a vast series of balconies surrounding a central abyss.

Shelves extend across walls, bridges, suspended platforms, and rotating stone columns. Some books are chained. Others move between shelves under their own power.

The bottom of the library cannot be seen.

The Iron Archivist travels along rails embedded in the walls, demanding the return of books overdue by several centuries.

The library contains routes to nearly every major faculty, but access depends on classification.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 4, 11, 50, 51, 52, 53, and 57.

## 50. The Cataloguing Chamber

Stone cards fill thousands of drawers. Each describes a book, relic, corpse, thesis, weapon, student, professor, or historical event held by the university.

The catalogue treats people and objects with equal administrative detachment.

Searching for oneself produces a record describing how the university expects that person to die.

The Iron Archivist uses this chamber to update its records.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 7, 18, 31, 49, 51, and 58.

## 51. The Living Indexes

Rows of preserved heads sit in crystal containers. Each belonged to a librarian, scholar, or condemned criminal whose memory was deemed useful.

The heads awaken when asked a sufficiently precise question.

They despise vague inquiries and may deliberately mislead anyone who fails to define their terms.

One index remembers the location of the final necromantic conclave. Another knows who altered the paladins’ arrest order into an execution decree.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 14, 49, 50, 52, and 57.

## 52. The Forbidden Stacks

Black iron shelves contain material prohibited even before necromancy was outlawed.

Subjects include draconic soul-binding, wyrm-man anatomy, divine coercion, memory extraction, royal curses, forge-heart construction, and methods of compelling the honored dead.

The Pale Collegium guards these stacks but rarely reads them. The books remember their former readers and whisper accusations.

A chained volume contains evidence that the corruption of dwarven necromancy was deliberately encouraged by an outside power.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 17, 49, 51, 54, and 63.

## 53. The Faculty Archive

Private journals, correspondence, research notes, disciplinary records, and sealed evaluations fill this quieter wing.

The archive reveals that many professors recognized the moral collapse of necromantic practice long before the civil war.

Some attempted reform. Some resigned. Some were silenced. Others enriched themselves by selling ancestral labor to mines, noble houses, and military orders.

A hidden drawer contains letters from clerics describing the gradual loss of Moraden’s favor.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 10, 36, 49, 54, and 60.

## 54. The Draconic War Collection

Dragon scales, broken weapons, burned banners, melted coins, maps, skeletons, and battlefield accounts are displayed in glass cases.

The collection presents the resurrection of the dwarven dead as the act that saved the species.

A second, concealed exhibit documents the cost: souls torn from promised rest, heroes driven mad by repeated resurrection, and kings who begged to be released.

A dragon skull mounted above the central gallery still possesses a functioning memory.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 28, 52, 53, 55, and 56.

## 55. The Wyrm-Men Specimen Vault

Tall preservation cylinders contain twisted creatures produced during the ancient wars.

Some resemble dwarves with draconic features. Others resemble dragons reshaped for tunnels and mines.

Labels describe them as enemy infiltrators, failed weapons, prisoners, or recovered citizens.

Several cylinders are empty.

Claw marks lead into the ventilation ducts.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 46, 54, 56, and 66.

## 56. The Hall of Ancestral Contracts

Stone tablets record agreements between the living and the dead.

Early contracts are respectful and limited. A hero might consent to return once in defense of a specific hold.

Later contracts become broader. Service continues for generations. Consent is presumed through bloodline, citizenship, debt, criminal sentence, or royal necessity.

The final tablets declare every dead dwarf a permanent strategic reserve of the kingdom.

Destroying the contracts releases many undead but also dissolves ancient protections tied to them.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 34, 54, 55, 57, and 67.

## 57. The Crypt of Theorems

The greatest scholars of the university were buried beneath carved summaries of their discoveries.

Their spirits remain available for academic consultation.

The crypt has become a battlefield of arguments. Dead philosophers debate whether identity survives resurrection, whether obedience without consent is slavery, and whether a civilization facing extinction has the right to violate its own dead.

A sealed tomb belongs to Alkroves Ichesnas himself.

It is empty.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 19, 49, 51, 56, 58, and 63.

## 58. The Memory Vault

Crystal cylinders contain extracted memories donated, purchased, inherited, or confiscated from generations of students and faculty.

Touching a cylinder allows someone to experience its contents.

Memories of ordinary life are stored beside military secrets and forbidden rituals: first lectures, family meals, forge ceremonies, humiliating failures, marriages, executions, and the raising of dead kings.

One cylinder contains the memory of the moment Moraden withdrew his blessing.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 31, 50, 57, 59, and 68.

## 59. The Absolute Reading Room

This chamber is under perfect silence. Even thought seems muted.

Books placed upon the central desks write their contents directly into the reader’s mind.

Prolonged study causes memories to blur with the text. Readers may forget which experiences belong to them.

A scholar sits at the final desk, apparently alive. She has been reading continuously since the war and now contains portions of thousands of books.

She no longer remembers her own name.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 58, 60, and 64.

## 60. The Rector’s Archive

A circular office overlooks the library abyss.

Rector Volkran Dhal uses it as his private court. Portraits of former rectors turn to observe visitors, occasionally correcting Dhal’s claims.

The archive contains university seals, treasury authorizations, faculty appointments, expulsion orders, and the original command to seal the lower campus.

A concealed letter shows that Dhal invited the paladins into the university, believing a controlled confrontation would force the necromancers to accept reform.

He did not expect a massacre.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 17, 53, 59, 61, and 73.

# VI. The Necromantic Annex

## 61. The Black Stair

A stair of polished black stone descends through the collapsed heart of the campus.

Every seventh step bears the name of a necromancer executed during the civil war.

The stair is watched by spectral paladins. They allow passage only to those who swear that the dead possess the right to refuse service.

Breaking the oath within the annex causes the names beneath the traveler’s feet to awaken.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 9, 36, 48, 60, and 62.

## 62. The Anatomical Theater

Concentric seating surrounds a stone dissection table.

The university taught medicine and necromancy side by side here. Diagrams distinguish between healing a body, animating it, recalling its spirit, manufacturing an imitation, and compelling the original soul.

A corpse on the table repeatedly reassembles itself whenever disturbed.

Hidden student records from Area 35 reveal that volunteers were gradually replaced with prisoners and debtors.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 35, 61, 63, and 65.

## 63. The Laboratory of Soul Acoustics

Bronze horns, silver strings, crystal resonators, and tuning forks fill the room.

Researchers attempted to identify the individual “tone” of a soul so it could be summoned without error.

The permanent silence field here suppresses physical sound but amplifies spiritual noise.

Visitors hear the dead calling from every nearby chamber.

One instrument resonates with a soul that is not dwarven, mortal, or native to the world.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 52, 57, 62, 64, and 68.

## 64. The Hall of the Unspoken Lecture

Students sit motionless at stone desks. Their bodies are dead, but their minds remain bound to the lesson.

The professor communicates entirely through projected thoughts.

The lecture concerns methods of controlling spellcasters trapped in silence fields. It became the foundation of the tactics later used to massacre the necromantic faculty.

Anyone entering the room is mentally assigned a seat and cannot willingly leave until dismissed.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 16, 59, 63, 65, and 69.

## 65. The Embalming Forges

These low furnaces produced preservation salts, replacement bones, metal joints, rune plates, and armored shells for ancestral bodies.

The room resembles both a smithy and a mortuary.

Mechanical arms continue repairing damaged undead, including any that enter accidentally.

A conveyor leads toward the deeper reliquaries. It occasionally carries a newly repaired corpse into the room from somewhere below.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 21, 31, 41, 62, 64, and 66.

## 66. The Reliquary of Heroes

Thousands of stone alcoves contain the remains of warriors, explorers, kingsguards, saints, miners, engineers, and legendary craftspeople.

Some alcoves are empty. Others contain only identifying plaques because the bodies marched into the Draconic Wars and never returned.

The remaining heroes awaken when the university is threatened.

Most do not understand how much time has passed.

One ancient warrior asks whether the dragons have finally been defeated and whether he may now go home.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 55, 65, 67, and 71.

## 67. The Gate of Kings

A monumental door depicts the lineages of the dwarven kings.

Each royal figure is shown descending into death, entering the promised halls, and returning when the people face extinction.

The final image has been violently defaced. It once showed the kings returning to rest.

The door can be opened only by royal blood, a treasury seal, or the willing sacrifice of an ancestral contract from Area 56.

Beyond it lie the mechanisms once intended to summon the kings of old.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 56, 66, 68, and 72.

## 68. The Final Conclave

A circular council chamber contains the bodies of the university’s senior necromancers.

They sit around a table as though still debating. Each preserved corpse holds a different soul vessel.

When visitors enter, the conclave awakens and resumes its final vote.

The proposal before them is whether to raise the kings of old against the paladins.

The vote was never completed.

The necromancers may attempt to recruit the visitors as neutral deciding members.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 58, 63, 67, 69, and 72.

## 69. The Hall of Revoked Provenance

Broken holy symbols cover the floor.

Clerics lost Moraden’s favor here in the middle of prayers, healing rites, and resurrection ceremonies.

The chamber preserves the exact spiritual instant of revocation. Divine spellcasters entering it feel the attention of something vast and profoundly disappointed.

Frozen revenant paladins guard the far doors.

They demand that every soul vessel carried by the visitors be surrendered and destroyed.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 42, 64, 68, 70, and 81.

## 70. The Battlefield Chapel

The chapel’s pews have been overturned into barricades.

Necromancers defended the altar while paladins forced entry through three doors. Both sides remain as frozen corpses locked in combat.

At the center stands a statue of Moraden with its face split in two.

Prayer causes one half to speak for justice and the other for mercy.

Reconciling the two voices may release the dead combatants. Choosing one causes the opposing side to rise.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 33, 69, 71, and 82.

## 71. The Glacier Vault

A wall of supernatural ice fills the chamber.

Shapes are visible within it: bodies, weapons, banners, furniture, and something enormous with a crown.

The ice was created during the final attempt to prevent the dead kings from being raised.

Breaking it frees several honored heroes and a royal revenant, but also releases the ancient cold elemental that escaped from Area 39.

A narrow path through the glacier reaches the university’s lowest vault.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 66, 70, 72, and 80.

## 72. The Well of Returning Names

This cylindrical chamber descends farther than torchlight can reach.

Thousands of names are carved around the walls. The names belong to dwarves recalled from death through the university’s rituals.

A suspended mechanism of silver rings, black chains, rune stones, bells, and soul crystals hangs above the abyss.

The mechanism was built to locate the honored dead in Moraden’s promised halls and draw them back into prepared bodies.

It remains active.

Something beneath the university has been feeding names into it.

The unregistered power source detected in Area 43 comes from below this chamber, where an older structure predating Unfazun has been exposed by the excavations of House Deepstone.

The university did not invent its necromancy.

It discovered something already waiting in the deep.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 27 through the ancient excavation, 67, 68, 71, and the unknown structure below.

# VII. Treasury and Expedition Levels

## 73. The Caravan Muster Hall

This massive chamber served as the departure point for the university’s treasury expeditions.

Painted lines divide the floor into stations for students, guards, miners, clerics, physicians, surveyors, porters, and royal levy officials.

Ghostly expedition members periodically assemble, undergo inspection, and march toward a sealed gate.

A surviving expedition manifest lists a caravan that departed shortly before the civil war and never returned.

Its destination was a forge complex believed to still be operational.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 28, 45, 60, 74, and 77.

## 74. The Hall of Levies

Officials calculated the crown’s share of recovered wealth here.

Scales large enough to weigh carts stand beside smaller instruments capable of measuring dust, magical residue, or fragments of enchanted metal.

The room’s constructs assess everything entering the chamber and declare its taxable value.

Living creatures may be classified as labor assets.

A sealed accounting station contains records proving that the university’s expeditions recovered far more wealth than was publicly reported.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 73, 75, 76, and 77.

## 75. The Assay Vault

Recovered metals, stones, artifacts, and forge products were tested here.

Several samples remain sealed beneath crystal covers: impossibly pure gold, metal that remembers its original shape, black iron warm to the touch, and a silver alloy that vibrates in the presence of dragons.

One sample is labeled only:

**NOT ORE. DO NOT RETURN BELOW.**

The object resembles a lump of dark metal until it detects blood.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 74, 76, and 79.

## 76. The Royal Ledger Chamber

The walls consist of enormous stone accounting tablets.

They record wealth extracted from the Great Forges over thousands of years. Entire wars, roads, cities, armies, and dynasties appear as expenses in the margins.

The ledgers demonstrate that the university remained wealthy enough to fund itself for millennia.

They also show unexplained payments to an entity identified as **THE CREDITOR BENEATH THE HAMMERS**.

The most recent payment was scheduled for the year the city fell.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 74, 75, 77, and 80.

## 77. The Expedition Barracks

Rows of compact bunks surround equipment cages.

Unlike the student dormitories, the barracks are severe, orderly, and built for rapid departure.

The bodies of an entire expedition remain here, fully equipped and seated on their beds.

They were killed without wounds. Every corpse faces toward the lower gate.

Their commander’s journal ends with the words:

**The forge answered us. It knows the university is listening.**

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 73, 74, 76, 78, and 79.

## 78. The Supply Cisterns

Store chambers contain preserved grain, hardtack, lamp oil, rope, bridge components, mining tools, climbing equipment, medical supplies, spare weapons, and blocks of concentrated mushroom meal.

Much remains usable.

An underground water reservoir occupies the lowest portion of the chamber. Its surface is frozen, but something moves beneath the ice.

The supply lifts connect to the kitchens, smithies, servants’ passages, and expedition barracks.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 24, 31, 40, 77, and 79.

## 79. The Great Descent Lift

A platform large enough to carry wagons hangs above an immense shaft.

Its chains descend into darkness. The control station recognizes only treasury-authorized expeditions.

The lift can be restored using the Disaster Regulation Chamber and at least one functioning wardstone.

Halfway down, the shaft passes the crushed remains of several university towers driven deep into the earth during the civil war.

Something has built nests inside them.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 48, 75, 77, 78, and 80.

## 80. The Shattered Lightwell Landing

The remains of the Asphelt Lightwell open beyond a monumental arch.

The once-radiant stair is broken. Thousands of enchanted steps float at different heights above a seemingly bottomless cavern.

Some still shine. Others are dark, inverted, or moving slowly through the air.

The landing is littered with abandoned carts, dead guards, treasury seals, and expedition equipment.

Far below, fragments of the Lightwell continue toward the sacred Eighth and Ninth Layers.

A golden step occasionally rises from the darkness carrying objects left upon it centuries ago.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 45, 71, 76, 79, 81, and the deeper layers.

# VIII. The Outer Watch and Final Defenses

## 81. The Outer Watch Command Hall

This military chamber is built into the university’s section of Unfazun’s defensive wall.

A relief map displays streets, towers, gates, shelters, and defensive positions across the dead city.

Small stone figures still move across it.

Some represent undead factions within the university. Others show creatures roaming Unfazun. Several are approaching from beyond the known city.

The command throne is occupied by a revenant marshal who believes the civil war remains active.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 2, 42, 69, 80, and 82.

## 82. The Siege Passage

A fortified tunnel runs through the thickness of the Outer Watch wall.

Arrow slits face both outward toward the Ice Caverns and inward toward the university.

This reveals an uncomfortable truth: the defenders expected enemies from both directions.

The passage is filled with frozen ammunition, collapsed barricades, and paladin dead.

A breach in the outer wall allows entry from the cavern, but something has recently been dragging bodies inside.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 70, 81, and 83.

## 83. The Bridge of the Expelled

A narrow stone bridge crosses a chasm separating the university from an isolated fortification.

Statues along the bridge depict disgraced professors, expelled students, heretics, failed expedition leaders, and scholars condemned by the crown.

The statues whisper their defenses when approached.

Halfway across, a permanent silence field prevents the accused from speaking.

The far side contains a prison used for scholars considered too knowledgeable to release and too valuable to execute.

**Connections:** Leads to Areas 82 and 84.

## 84. The Gate of the Lower Road

This immense gate once opened onto the road descending toward the royal tombs and Great Forges.

It was sealed with layers of stone, iron, divine wards, royal commands, and university enchantments.

Something has been striking it from the other side.

The impacts occur at precise intervals corresponding to an ancient dwarven work shift.

Written across the inner surface in fresh frost are the words:

**THE WORK DAY IS NOT YET ENDED.**

Opening the gate provides the clearest surviving route into the Eighth Layer, the tomb kingdoms, and eventually the mountain-sized forges that created the ancient wealth of dwarven civilization.

It may also release whatever has continued working below since Unfazun died.

# The Hidden Structure Beneath the University

The deepest revelation of the Great Arcane University is that dwarven necromancy did not originate solely from clerical experimentation, ancestral devotion, or desperation during the Draconic Wars.

The first excavators beneath Unfazun discovered an older mechanism.

Its builders are unknown.

It rests beneath the Well of Returning Names and extends downward toward the Great Forges. It may be connected to the mysterious Creditor recorded in the Royal Ledgers, the intelligence within the supernatural cold, or the force that transformed the honorable return of willing heroes into an industry of eternal enslavement.

The structure recognizes names.

It understands obligation.

It treats memory as a form of ownership.

The ancient dwarves believed they had discovered a method by which their heroes could be called home for one final defense.

In truth, they may have discovered a system designed to ensure that nobody ever truly left.

# Using the University as a Campaign Dungeon

The Great Arcane University should not be cleared in a single expedition. It is large enough to support repeated incursions, faction negotiations, recoveries, shifting alliances, and discoveries that alter the meaning of earlier rooms.

The surface ruins introduce the silence fields, student dead, and Pale Collegium. The instructional halls establish the university as a functioning institution rather than a simple necromancer’s fortress. The dormitories reveal the human cost of the war. The workshops provide resources and control over the ruin’s systems. The library contains the historical truth. The necromantic annex forces the party to confront the moral question at the center of Unfazun. The treasury levels connect the university to the Great Forges and the deeper dwarven civilization.

Several major objectives can change the dungeon permanently:

Restoring a master rune in the Runesmiths’ Hall can reactivate heat, doors, structural supports, the Lightwell, or defensive wards.

Altering the Registry of Names can grant legal access, release indebted undead, declare someone dead, or recognize a new rector.

Changing the ancient timetable can redirect moving stairways and open portions of the buried university.

Destroying the ancestral contracts can release thousands of souls while collapsing protections built upon their service.

Completing the vote of the Final Conclave determines whether the remaining necromancers accept dissolution, attempt to summon the kings, or resume their war against Moraden’s paladins.

Opening the Gate of the Lower Road begins the descent into the royal tombs, the mountain-sized magmatic forges, and the deeper truth behind the fall of dwarven civilization.

The ruin’s central question should remain present in every wing:

**When a people owe everything to the sacrifices of their ancestors, where does remembrance end and ownership begin?**
