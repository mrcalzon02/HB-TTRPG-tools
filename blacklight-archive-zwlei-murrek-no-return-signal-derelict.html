Recovered operating context

Wet-deck service features now encountered as ruins.

Pressure membranes and circulation

On an intact Mur'rek, translucent pressure membranes carry coolant and data fibers through their rims, shallow circulation grooves move clean warm water, and contamination irises close when foreign chemistry exceeds tolerance. In the derelict, each membrane, groove, and iris must be inspected independently: it may be dry, stagnant, fused, torn, sealed, or retaining local pressure.

Living repair and service access

The class uses living repair film, flexible trunk junctions, and peel-back wet-polymer membranes instead of conventional dry metal service panels. In the abandoned hull these materials remain recognizable but cannot be assumed responsive, sterile, structurally sound, or biologically dormant.

Guide and machine language

Dense Zwlei living-script markings identify flow state, authority, contamination, and maintenance lineage. Bioluminescent guide nodules once sequenced toward bridge and engineering spaces. Surviving markings remain valuable orientation evidence even where powered guides are dark.

Variant-derived: the hull's placement in the No Return Signal junkyard derelict field, its abandoned/ruined disposition, and the compartment-level derelict-condition annotations. The No Return Signal campaign record does not supply a proper name for the junkyard, so this archive intentionally does not invent one.

Recovered terminology remains functional translation; original Zwlei naming encodes relationship and state. This page is a text conversion and archive variant, not a newly generated vessel image or replacement class design.
