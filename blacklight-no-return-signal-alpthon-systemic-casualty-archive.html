CHARLES // WHY I CANNOT JUST TAKE OVER THE SHIP

player-facing systems explanation

Charles: “I know you are frustrated that I cannot simply assume direct control of every electronic system aboard. I would also prefer that. The problem is that this ship was decommissioned, not merely switched off. Its protected storage, authority credentials, configuration databases, network maps, system software and much of its operating state were deliberately wiped before disposal. What survived is mostly hardware, immutable boot logic, local safety firmware and isolated controller memory.”

Charles: “So, in practical terms, I am rebuilding the operating system from scratch while we are inside it. Every time we power up another subsystem I have to determine what it is, how it communicates, what its telemetry means, what other machinery it depends on, which commands are safe, and which security domain is allowed to talk to it. In many cases I am effectively writing the driver while the device is already running.”

Operational rule: Charles knows what currently reaches Charles. Damage and decommissioning left many ship systems isolated. A local controller may know a room is depressurized, contaminated or overheating while Charles has no surviving route, credentials, protocol definition or calibrated interpretation for that data.

WHY ANYTHING SURVIVED

The wipe succeeded at its intended boundary. It did not sanitize every semiconductor aboard. Ar'nock modular architecture places computation and service history inside many functional assemblies. The remaining story is scattered across the ship's organs.
