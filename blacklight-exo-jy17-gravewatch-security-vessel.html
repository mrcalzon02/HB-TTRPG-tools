JY-17 Security Patrol Dossier

Vessel: JY-17 Automated Security Patrol Vessel GRAVEWATCH-3. Originally a crewed yard-security cutter with autonomous watchkeeping, it has been left running long after the personnel assigned to JY-17 departed. It continues a slow programmed sweep through the Grave Orbit wreck field, challenges suspicious movement, records incidents, and reports to what its software still calls JY-17 Security Management.

The deception is mostly accidental: its warnings were written for a staffed facility. The patrol still announces that supervisors have been notified, boarding personnel are responding, and security management has granted authority. In reality, the management endpoint is JY17-SMB-1 Management Relay Beacon, a hardened communications beacon that automatically acknowledges properly formatted reports and continuously rebroadcasts status. There is no occupied management station behind it.

Why the close call works: GRAVEWATCH-3 is genuinely capable of seeing movement, interrogating transponders, maneuvering through the yard, marking contacts, launching the occasional surviving inspection drone, and using limited automated weapons. But its classifier expects a human operator to resolve ambiguity. If a suspicious contact abruptly stops moving or powers down, confidence often decays until the protocol records the event as withdrawn or inconclusive and the ship resumes its patrol. That creates exactly the wrong lesson for an interloper: they did not detect us; finish quickly and leave before the real people come back.

Deep-scan finding: there are almost certainly no living organisms aboard. Habitation spaces are cold, life support is in preservation mode, crew-service systems are disconnected, there is no respiration signature, and internal thermal activity is concentrated around reactor, power, sensor, and drive machinery. The ship is not dead. It is simply uncrewed.

What a Thorough Scan Reveals

Thermal: nearly the entire vessel is cold. Persistent heat comes from the compact reactor, power conversion, attitude-control hardware, sensor electronics, and occasional maintenance activity. Crew compartments remain near preservation temperature with no warm-body signatures.

Atmospheric: no shipwide breathable environment. Several machinery and archive volumes carry dry preservation atmosphere; crew areas are depressurized or maintained only enough to prevent material degradation.

Communications: incident reports go to JY17-SMB-1 Management Relay Beacon. Its replies are fixed-format acknowledgments such as “REPORT RECEIVED — CONTINUE ASSIGNED PROTOCOL.” Packet timing and structure are too uniform to be a staffed control room.

Behavioral: the patrol frequently threatens inspection teams, boarding officers, management review, and additional patrol assets that do not exist. It can still enforce rules itself, but much of the institutional threat is a recording from an organization that has vanished.
