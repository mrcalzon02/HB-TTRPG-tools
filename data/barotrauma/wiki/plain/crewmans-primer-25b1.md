THE CHILDREN OF THE HONKMOTHER

The Children of the Honkmother claim to hear a sacred call emanating from the Europan depths.

This call is variously described as:

- The laughter beneath the ice.

- The Great Honk.

- The Mother’s Call.

- The Joyful Pressure.

- The Sound Beyond Sonar.

- A revelation that, when described by less charitable medical officers, bears a striking resemblance to oxygen deprivation.

Members frequently identify themselves through clown masks, greasepaint, colorful clothing, oversized shoes, novelty horns, and an attitude toward restricted machinery best summarized as:

“It would be funny.”

The movement began as a mixture of shipboard humor, occupational folklore, countercultural performance, and resistance to the rigid discipline of early Coalition vessels.

In its mildest form, Honkmother tradition consists of jokes, absurd clothing, harmless pranks, mock ceremonies, and efforts to preserve morale aboard vessels where laughter is otherwise in dangerously short supply.

There is nothing inherently criminal about wearing clown clothing.

There is nothing automatically dangerous about carrying a horn.

There is nothing wrong with attempting to make exhausted crew members laugh during a difficult voyage.

The difficulty begins when amusement becomes doctrine.
