## XXI. JUSTICE ABOARD SHIP

There is no single universal justice system across Europa.

Coalition authorities, separatist settlements, corporations, unions, independent outposts, and criminal organizations all possess their own interpretations of law.

Many of these interpretations disagree violently.

Every captain should therefore establish onboard rules before someone tests them.

The crew should know:

- Who may order detention.

- Who may carry weapons.

- What constitutes sabotage.

- How theft is handled.

- How complaints are raised.

- How disputes are reviewed.

- When station authorities become involved.

- What emergency powers command may exercise.

- Who succeeds the captain.

Justice should not depend entirely on whether the captain has eaten recently.

Rules applied only to unpopular crew members are not discipline.

They are favoritism with paperwork.

Serious incidents should, where possible, involve several officers. Security may investigate. Medical may assess injuries or impairment. Department heads may provide testimony.

The captain makes the final shipboard decision.

They should avoid deciding the outcome first and then sending security to locate a reason.
