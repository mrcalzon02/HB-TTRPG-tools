## XXII. MUTINY

Mutiny is an organized attempt to unlawfully seize, remove, confine, or override command.

It is not:

- A disagreement.

- A safety report.

- A wage grievance.

- An engineer saying the reactor cannot produce the requested power.

- A medic refusing to distribute morphine as a recreational beverage.

- A mechanic asking the captain to stop driving into walls.

Mutiny may involve:

- Seizing the helm.

- Detaining the captain.

- Disarming loyal personnel.

- Taking control of weapons.

- Sabotaging command systems.

- Issuing contradictory orders.

- Preventing completion of the mission.

- Organizing to replace lawful command by force.

The captain’s response should reflect the danger.

Verbal disputes may still be negotiated.

Armed seizure of the helm may require security intervention.

Remember that most mutinies begin long before anyone reaches the command room.

They begin with:

- Unpaid wages.

- Reckless orders.

- Repeated humiliation.

- Ignored safety concerns.

- Arbitrary punishment.

- Unequal treatment.

- The conviction that lawful complaints will never be heard.

The most effective method of preventing mutiny is competent, fair command maintained before anyone begins quietly counting the weapons in the armory.
