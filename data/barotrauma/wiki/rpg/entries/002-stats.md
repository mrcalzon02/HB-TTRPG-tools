## STATS

 Strength, Agility, Intelligence, Charisma. “S.A.I.C.” 

Players start with a base of 3 points in each stat and can allocate an additional 10 points to any of the stats at the start of the game. It is important to consider the role and abilities of each class and allocate points accordingly. For example, a player who wants to focus on combat may want to allocate more points to strength, while a player who wants to focus on negotiations and diplomacy may want to allocate more points to charisma. The choice of stats is up to the player, but it is important to consider the role and abilities of each class in order to allocate points effectively. Players will also have to consider the drawbacks of their chosen background and how it will affect their abilities in the game. It is important to balance the benefits of a Fate with the drawbacks of a disadvantage in order to achieve success in the game.

Talents are special abilities that give the player an edge in certain tasks or situations. Each class has access to a specific set of Talents, and players can choose one Talent at the start of the game. Talents can provide bonuses to specific stats, allow the player to perform tasks more effectively, or grant special abilities. It is important to choose a Talent that complements the player's class and playstyle.

In Barotrauma, players must balance their stats, Background, and Talent in order to succeed in their missions and overcome the challenges they will face in the subsurface ocean of Europa.

A basic 2d6 task involves rolling two six-sided dice and adding the results together. The player's stat is then subtracted from the difficulty of the task. For example, if Sarah is attempting to repair a broken piece of equipment and the difficulty of the task is 6, she would roll the two dice and add the results together. Let's say she rolls a 3 and a 4, for a total of 7. She would then subtract her intelligence stat of 8 from the difficulty, resulting in a final roll of -1. This means that Sarah was able to successfully repair the equipment, as her roll was higher than the difficulty of the task.

If another player, with a lower intelligence stat, attempted the same task, they may not be as successful. For example, if they had an intelligence stat of 4 and rolled a total of 6 on the dice, their final roll would be 2, meaning they were not able to repair the equipment. The higher the player's stat, the more likely they are to succeed in tasks that require that particular skill.

Basic combat in Barotrauma is affected by the stats of the player and their opponent. When a player initiates combat, they will roll a 2d6 and add their strength stat to the result. The opponent will also roll a 2d6 and add their strength stat to the result. The player with the higher final roll will land a hit and deal damage to the other player.

If the player has a higher agility stat than their opponent, they may be able to dodge or evade attacks, giving them a chance to roll an additional die and take the highest result. This can give them an advantage in combat, as they can potentially avoid taking damage.

If the player has a lower strength stat than their opponent, they may struggle in physical combat and may need to rely on other tactics, such as using weapons or gadgets, or using their intelligence to come up with a strategy.

Players can also use their charisma stat to negotiate or intimidate their opponents in combat situations, potentially avoiding combat altogether. This can be especially useful if the player is outmatched or outnumbered.
