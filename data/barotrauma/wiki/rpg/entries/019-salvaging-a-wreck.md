## SALVAGING A WRECK

"Salvaging isn't about what you find, it's about what you can sell. And in this line of work, we'll take anything we can get our hands on."

Salvaging a wrecked pirate ship can be a complex process, as it requires careful planning and execution. First, the player must locate the wreckage and assess the damage. If the ship is severely damaged or heavily debris-filled, it may be difficult or impossible to salvage.

Locating the wreckage: To locate the wreckage, the player must roll a 2d6 and add their intelligence stat. The difficulty of this roll will depend on the distance and visibility of the wreckage. If the roll is successful, the player has located the wreckage and can proceed to the next step. If the roll is unsuccessful, the player must continue searching for the wreckage.

Assessing the damage: To assess the damage, the player must roll a 2d6 and add their intelligence stat. The difficulty of this roll will depend on the severity of the damage and the player's expertise in ship repair. If the roll is successful, the player has accurately assessed the damage and can proceed to the next step. If the roll is unsuccessful, the player may overestimate or underestimate the damage and may encounter difficulties during the salvage process.

Next, the player must determine what items on the ship are valuable and worth salvaging. This can include weapons, equipment, and other valuable items. The player must carefully search the ship and make a list of what they find.

Identifying valuable items: To identify valuable items, the player must roll a 2d6 and add their intelligence stat. The difficulty of this roll will depend on the rarity and complexity of the items. If the roll is successful, the player has accurately identified the valuable items and can proceed to the next step. If the roll is unsuccessful, the player may overlook valuable items or mistakenly identify non-valuable items as valuable.

Once the valuable items have been identified, the player must carefully remove them from the ship. This can be a dangerous process, as the ship may be unstable or have hidden hazards. The player should use caution and take all necessary precautions to ensure their safety.

Removing items from the ship: To remove items from the ship, the player must roll a 2d6 and add their strength stat. The difficulty of this roll will depend on the size and weight of the items and the stability of the ship. If the roll is successful, the player has successfully removed the items from the ship and can proceed to the next step. If the roll is unsuccessful, the player may encounter difficulties removing the items or may damage the items during the process.

Once the items have been removed from the ship, they must be transported back to the player's submarine. This can be a challenging process, as the items may be heavy or difficult to move. The player should use caution and take all necessary precautions to ensure their safety and the safety of the items.

Transporting items to the submarine: To transport the items to the submarine, the player must roll a 2d6 and add their strength stat. The difficulty of this roll will depend on the size and weight of the items and the distance to the submarine. If the roll is successful, the player has successfully transported the items to the submarine and can proceed to the next step. If the roll is unsuccessful, the player may encounter difficulties transporting the items or may damage the items during the process.

On average, a player can expect to salvage around 500-1000 credits worth of items from a wrecked pirate ship. However, this can vary depending on the size of the ship, the condition of the items, and other factors.
