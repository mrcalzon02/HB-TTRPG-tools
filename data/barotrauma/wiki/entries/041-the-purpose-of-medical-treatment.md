## THE PURPOSE OF MEDICAL TREATMENT

Medical treatment exists to restore a crew member to useful health, prevent an injury from worsening, and keep the casualty alive until more complete treatment is available.
These are not always the same objective.
A minor bruise may require no treatment beyond time and caution. Moderate bleeding may require bandaging before pain relief is considered. A crew member suffering oxygen deprivation requires breathable air before narcotics. A patient with catastrophic blood loss requires blood replacement more urgently than they require reassurance.
The medical officer must determine what is wrong before reaching for medication.
That evaluation does not need to become an hour-long consultation while the submarine fills with water. It should, however, involve more than asking, “Does it hurt?” and injecting the patient with the first substance found in the cabinet.
At minimum, determine:
* What injured the patient.
* Where the injury is located.
* Whether the patient is bleeding.
* Whether significant blood has already been lost.
* Whether the patient can breathe.
* Whether they are conscious and responsive.
* Whether they have been poisoned, infected, burned, irradiated, or exposed to pressure.
* Whether they have already taken medication.
* Whether another casualty requires more urgent attention.
The medic’s purpose is not to eliminate every discomfort immediately. It is to identify which conditions are dangerous, which are becoming dangerous, and which can safely be left to recover without consuming limited supplies.
