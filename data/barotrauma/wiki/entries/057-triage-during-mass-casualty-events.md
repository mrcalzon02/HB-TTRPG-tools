## TRIAGE DURING MASS-CASUALTY EVENTS

When several crew members are injured simultaneously, the medical officer must establish priorities.
The loudest patient is not necessarily the most critical.
Treat first those who will die without immediate assistance and who can still be saved through timely intervention. Then treat those whose conditions may deteriorate. Minor injuries can wait.
A practical order of attention is:
1. Patients who cannot breathe or are suffering severe oxygen deprivation.
2. Patients with uncontrolled bleeding or catastrophic blood loss.
3. Patients who are unconscious, rapidly deteriorating, or suffering life-threatening poisoning.
4. Patients with severe burns, major trauma, or dangerous infection.
5. Patients with moderate injuries that impair their ability to function.
6. Patients with minor injuries and manageable pain.
7. Crew members requesting narcotics because they bumped their knee while climbing a ladder.
Triage may require directing uninjured personnel to assist.
A mechanic can apply a bandage. A security officer can carry a casualty. An assistant can retrieve blood packs or oxygen. An engineer can help move patients out of a flooded compartment.
The medical officer should coordinate these efforts rather than attempting to perform every task alone.
During a major emergency, give direct instructions:
* “Apply pressure to that wound.”
* “Bring two blood packs.”
* “Move this patient into oxygen.”
* “Carry the unconscious diver to medical.”
* “Do not administer anything else.”
* “Security, restrain the infected patient.”
* “Captain, medical requires evacuation.”
Clear medical command saves lives.
Frantic rummaging through cabinets does not.
