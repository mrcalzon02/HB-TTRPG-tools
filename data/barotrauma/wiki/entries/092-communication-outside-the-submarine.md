## COMMUNICATION OUTSIDE THE SUBMARINE

The submarine must know where the away team is and what condition it is in.
Away personnel should report at meaningful stages of the operation:
* Departure from the airlock.
* Arrival at the objective.
* Discovery of a major hazard.
* Contact with hostile forces.
* Discovery of survivors.
* Completion of the objective.
* Beginning the return.
* Entry into the airlock.
* Final accounting of personnel.
Do not provide constant commentary about every stone, fish, door, and empty container.
Do not remain silent for the entire mission.
The purpose of communication is to keep the team coordinated and allow the submarine to respond if conditions change.
Useful reports are brief and specific.
“Mining team at the deposit. No contacts.”
“Wreck entered through upper breach.”
“Hostile life in the western tunnel.”
“One diver injured; returning.”
“Artifact recovered. It is emitting heat.”
“Team inside the airlock. All personnel accounted for.”
If communication fails, the away team should follow the contingency established before departure.
This may mean returning immediately, waiting at a known location, or using another signal.
Do not continue deeper into an unknown ruin after losing all contact unless the mission and emergency circumstances clearly justify it.
