## XIX. MID-MISSION PAY DISPUTES

Compensation disputes may arise during a voyage.
The mission may prove more dangerous than advertised. The route may become longer. Casualties may force surviving crew members to assume additional duties. An allegedly straightforward salvage contract may develop teeth, guns, infection, and an alien defense system.
The crew may request:
* Hazard pay.
* Increased mission shares.
* Compensation for additional duties.
* Payment for extended operations.
* Relief at the next station.
* Renegotiation before accepting another objective.
The captain should not immediately classify this as mutiny.
Listen.
Determine whether circumstances changed.
Consult department heads or representatives.
Consider the vessel’s finances.
Negotiate while the complaint remains a complaint.
Replacing the entire engineering department at the next station generally costs more than paying the existing one enough to stop discussing industrial action beside the reactor.
When immediate payment is impossible, possible arrangements include:
* Deferred wages.
* Increased salvage shares.
* Priority payment from the next contract.
* Additional leave.
* Recorded debt.
* Mission bonuses.
* Release from contract.
A debt acknowledged may be tolerated.
A debt denied becomes a tradition, usually involving sabotage.
