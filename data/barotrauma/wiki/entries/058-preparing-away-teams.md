## PREPARING AWAY TEAMS

The medical officer’s responsibility extends beyond the infirmary.
Away missions expose personnel to hostile creatures, gunfire, pressure, drowning, infection, venom, environmental hazards, and the perennial danger of someone forgetting to bring enough oxygen.
Before a salvage, mining, exploration, assault, or recovery operation, the medical officer should help prepare an appropriate field kit.
The kit should reflect the actual mission.
A short mining excursion near the submarine does not require the entire medical cabinet. An assault on a hostile installation should not depart with one bandage and a positive attitude.
An away-team medical kit may include:
* Basic wound treatment.
* Supplies for controlling serious bleeding.
* Blood replacement material when justified.
* Appropriate pain relief.
* Antidotes relevant to the expected threat.
* Treatment for burns or environmental exposure.
* Additional oxygen.
* Emergency stimulants or revival equipment.
* Tools required to transport or stabilize casualties.
Distribute supplies sensibly. Do not place the entire medical reserve on one diver who may be the first person incapacitated.
The medical officer should know who is carrying the field supplies and whether a trained medic is accompanying the mission. When possible, retain enough material aboard to treat casualties caused by a simultaneous attack on the submarine.
Europa has a habit of creating two emergencies whenever the crew prepares for one.
