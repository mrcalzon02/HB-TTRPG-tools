## XXXVI. THE SAVILLE OBSERVANCE

Each year, participating vessels observe the Saville sacrifice.
The exact date varies between station calendars, but the traditional observance includes:
* Reading the names of the detained crew.
* A moment of engine silence.
* One controlled sonar pulse.
* Inspection of crew contracts.
* Confirmation of beneficiary records.
* Contributions to the mutual-aid fund.
* Recognition of mariners lost during the previous year.
The sonar pulse represents the message carried along the route when the Saville dead were returned.
One pulse means:
We heard.
A second vessel may answer:
We remember.
The ceremony is brief.
Submariners generally prefer memorials that do not interfere excessively with departure schedules.
The meaning is not diminished by efficiency.
