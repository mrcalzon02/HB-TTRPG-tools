## EXTENDED FOOTNOTE: CONCERNING CULTS, CLOWNS, PARASITES, AND OTHER CONSEQUENCES OF PROLONGED HUMAN ISOLATION

The preceding remarks concerning clown attire should not be interpreted as a complete dismissal of the increasingly serious social phenomenon known as the Children of the Honkmother.
Nor should the good-natured treatment of ordinary shipboard foolishness be mistaken for permission to participate in either of the two major cultic movements currently spreading through Europan settlements:
* The Children of the Honkmother.
* The Church of the Husk.
The two organizations possess very different beliefs, methods, aesthetics, and preferred forms of catastrophic workplace disruption. They are grouped together here not because they cooperate—they usually do not—but because both represent disturbing responses to the psychological conditions imposed upon humanity beneath Europa’s ice.
Every person on Europa lives beneath kilometres of frozen water and crushing pressure.
Every station depends upon machinery that may fail.
Every submarine travels through an environment occupied by creatures that regard humanity as food, competition, contamination, or an unusually noisy snack enclosed in metal.
Everyone knows someone who failed to return.
Everyone has heard a distress signal that ended too suddenly.
Everyone understands that a damaged pump, delayed cargo shipment, depleted fuel reserve, failed harvest, missing medical crate, or cracked pressure seal can convert an ordinary week into a mass-casualty event.
Humanity has not adapted to these conditions with universal dignity.
Some people pray.
Some organize.
Some drink.
Some join the separatists.
Some paint their faces, put horns on their shoes, and declare that the ocean has begun speaking to them through a bicycle horn.
Others willingly infect themselves with a parasite responsible for turning living human beings into hollow, murderous corpses.
The human capacity for coping is broad.
It is not always useful.
