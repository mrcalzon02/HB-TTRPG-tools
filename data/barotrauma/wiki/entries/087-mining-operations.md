## MINING OPERATIONS

Mining appears simple.
Locate a deposit. Exit the submarine. Cut the material free. Carry it back.
The basic procedure is simple.
The environment is not.
Before mining begins, determine whether the submarine can hold position safely and whether its weapons have firing coverage that will not endanger the divers.
The mining team should approach the deposit together. Security should watch the surrounding water while the miners work.
Recover the material systematically.
Do not scatter ore across the cavern floor. Do not fill every personal inventory slot and then abandon mission tools because there is no room left. Use bags, crates, or other storage whenever available.
The team should communicate when:
* The deposit has been reached.
* Mining has begun.
* Hostile movement is observed.
* Oxygen is becoming low.
* Carrying capacity has been reached.
* The deposit is exhausted.
* The team is returning.
Mining teams should also understand the materials they recover.
Certain minerals may be valuable because they can be refined into reactor fuel, ammunition, medicines, explosives, or electrical components. Others may be worth more when sold directly.
The away team does not need to conduct a full market analysis at the cave wall.
It should avoid discarding potentially useful material because its value is not immediately obvious.
When carrying capacity becomes limited, consult the engineering department or captain regarding priorities.
