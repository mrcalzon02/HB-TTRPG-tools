## MAINTAINING A WATCH

Once outside the vessel, every crew member should remain aware of their surroundings.
Europa’s oceans contain natural predators, territorial creatures, parasites, hostile human forces, pirates, separatists, rogue clowns, automated defences, unstable machinery, and entities whose classification is still disputed by several scientific institutions.
Threats may approach from above, below, behind, or from inside the structure the team is exploring.
Do not allow every member of the team to stare at the same mineral formation.
Do not let the entire group crowd around one storage container while nobody watches the corridor.
Do not become so absorbed in operating a plasma cutter that you fail to notice the creature biting the person beside you.
Maintain periodic visual checks.
Listen to the radio.
Watch the sonar when available.
Be alert for:
* Movement in dark water.
* Sudden changes in local wildlife.
* Damaged or opened doors.
* Fresh blood.
* Unexplained equipment.
* Disturbed silt.
* Electrical activity.
* Mechanical sounds.
* Acidic clouds.
* Unidentified growth.
* Missing team members.
* Radio silence.
* Objects that have moved since the last observation.
A person who sees a threat should report it immediately and clearly.
“Movement behind us.”
“Crawler above the entrance.”
“Possible human contact in the wreck.”
“Green cloud forming near the cave wall.”
“Unknown machine active inside the ruin.”
Do not scream incoherently into the radio and assume the team will understand what has happened.
Location matters.
Direction matters.
The nature of the threat matters.
