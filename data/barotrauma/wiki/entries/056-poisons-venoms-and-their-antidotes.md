## POISONS, VENOMS, AND THEIR ANTIDOTES

The medical department may possess substances that have legitimate medical, research, security, or mission-specific uses but are also capable of causing paralysis, organ failure, unconsciousness, psychosis, or death.
These materials must be controlled.
Do not carry poisons casually in personal clothing. Do not leave them beside ordinary medication. Do not replace legitimate ampoules with dangerous compounds as a joke. Do not inject unconscious patients with experimental chemicals unless circumstances justify the risk and the procedure has been authorized.
Labels should remain visible. Dangerous substances should be stored separately from routine treatment whenever the vessel’s layout permits.
The medical officer should maintain sufficient antidotes to respond to the toxins most likely to be encountered. They should not consume the entire antidote supply treating imagined exposure or experimenting on volunteers.
A poison and its antidote are not a matched recreational set.
