## FOREWORD

No Vessel Sails Alone
Every submarine operating beneath the ice of Europa depends upon more than its captain, crew, reactor, and pressure hull.
It depends upon dockworkers who load its cargo.
It depends upon fabricators who produce its parts.
It depends upon miners who recover its fuel stock.
It depends upon station personnel who maintain docking arms, pumps, atmosphere, communications, and navigation beacons.
It depends upon medical workers who receive its wounded.
It depends upon merchants willing to sell it food, oxygen, ammunition, tools, medicine, batteries, welding fuel, and reactor rods.
It depends upon other submariners who answer distress calls in waters where no government vessel is likely to arrive in time.
This interdependence is the foundation of Standard Union 208.
The union’s central principle is simple:
No mariner beneath the ice survives entirely by their own hand.
The most heavily armed captain still needs fuel.
The wealthiest station still needs food.
The most feared cartel still needs spare parts.
The most isolated outpost still requires someone willing to pilot a cargo vessel through the dark.
Europa has many governments, several currencies, dozens of legal systems, and more self-appointed authorities than it has reliable toilets. Yet nearly all of them have learned to recognize Standard Union 208 labor protections.
Some recognize them because they believe in fair treatment.
Some recognize them because they respect tradition.
Some recognize them because their insurers require it.
Some recognize them because the last administrator who ignored the union spent three months attempting to operate an outpost without food deliveries, replacement oxygen membranes, medical stock, or anyone willing to repair the docking collar.
The motivation is less important than the result.
Do not trifle with Union 208.
The sea is cold, the routes are long, and stations that lose the cooperation of mariners tend to die surprisingly quickly.
