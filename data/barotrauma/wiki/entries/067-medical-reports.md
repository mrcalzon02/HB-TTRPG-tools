## MEDICAL REPORTS

The medical officer must inventory the infirmary before departure.
This includes more than glancing into one cabinet and concluding that several colorful ampoules remain.
Report shortages of:
* Morphine and other necessary analgesics.
* Bandages and wound-treatment supplies.
* Blood packs and replacement fluids.
* Antidotes.
* Antibiotics.
* Anti-radiation medicine.
* Emergency stimulants.
* Sedatives.
* Treatment for burns and environmental injuries.
* Supplies needed for away-team medical kits.
* Any specialist medicine required by the anticipated mission.
The captain cannot purchase what the medical officer does not report.
If the submarine leaves a well-stocked station and the medic later announces that no morphine remains aboard, the captain is entitled to ask several pointed questions.
The vessel was just in port.
The merchants had medical supplies.
The ship possessed funds.
The opportunity existed.
Why was nothing said?
A shortage concealed through inattention is functionally little different from a shortage created through waste. In both cases, a wounded crew member may suffer because the medical department failed to perform a basic responsibility.
Medical personnel should also explain unusual levels of consumption.
If the infirmary began the last voyage with a full narcotics cabinet and returned with nothing despite suffering no serious casualties, “Jerry from engineering encouraged everyone to see how much morphine it would take to kill them” will not improve the captain’s disposition.
Inventory is not merely counting.
It is accountability.
