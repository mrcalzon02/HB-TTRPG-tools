## SILENT RUNNING

When avoiding sound-sensitive predators or hostile detection systems, the captain may order the oxygen generator depowered.
This can significantly reduce the submarine’s acoustic footprint.
It can also place every living person aboard on a timer.
The moment oxygen production stops, the submarine begins consuming the breathable atmosphere already present inside its compartments. The precise safe duration depends upon:
* Crew size.
* Submarine volume.
* Existing oxygen level.
* Number of flooded or sealed compartments.
* Condition of ventilation.
* Fires.
* Hull breaches.
* Crew exertion.
* Use of diving masks or suits.
* Whether someone has decided silent running is the ideal time for a sprinting competition.
A large, lightly crewed vessel may remain habitable for a considerable period.
A small submarine packed with personnel may consume its breathable reserve much faster.
The captain must understand that disabling oxygen generation is not free stealth.
It is the exchange of one danger for another.
