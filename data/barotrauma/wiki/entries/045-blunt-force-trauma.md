## Blunt-Force Trauma

The submarine is filled with ladders, hatches, machinery, narrow access ways, steep companionways, and crew members who believe descending headfirst is faster.
As a result, the infirmary will receive a steady procession of people who have fallen down ladders, collided with doors, been struck by loose equipment, or injured themselves during abrupt changes in depth and motion.
Not every bruise requires narcotics.
Minor blunt-force injuries can frequently be observed and allowed to recover naturally. A crew member suffering a small amount of pain after falling down a ladder may be advised to rest, work more carefully, and—in the traditional medical terminology of the Europan fleet—sleep it off.
More serious trauma requires attention when it causes substantial pain, impaired movement, unconsciousness, internal injury, or a rapidly worsening condition.
The medical officer must distinguish discomfort from disability.
