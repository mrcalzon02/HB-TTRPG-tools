## X. STANDARD EMPLOYMENT CONTRACT LAW

Union 208 agreements form the basis of common submarine employment law across much of Europa.
Local jurisdictions may vary, but a valid maritime employment contract is generally expected to identify:
* The employer.
* The captain.
* The vessel.
* The crew member.
* The assigned profession.
* The expected duties.
* The base wage.
* Mission shares.
* Salvage rights.
* Hazard compensation.
* Contract duration.
* Expected route.
* Conditions for extension.
* Medical provisions.
* Death benefits.
* Disciplinary procedures.
* Grounds for termination.
* Conditions for resignation.
* The responsible jurisdiction.
* The process for dispute resolution.
A contract that omits all meaningful obligations while granting the employer unlimited authority is not considered “flexible.”
It is considered suspect.
The fact that a crew member signed a document does not automatically make every term enforceable.
Union arbiters routinely reject provisions that involve:
* Unpaid indefinite service.
* Debt inheritance.
* Forced medical experimentation.
* Unrestricted corporal punishment.
* Summary execution for minor violations.
* Confiscation of all personal salary.
* Forced abandonment.
* Waiver of rescue rights.
* Retroactive wage reduction.
* Unlimited extension without compensation.
* Ownership of a crew member’s body after death.
* Mandatory participation in clown-related research.
The last provision was added after an arbitration panel determined that existing language was somehow not specific enough.
