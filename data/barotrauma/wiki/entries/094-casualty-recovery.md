## CASUALTY RECOVERY

If a crew member is injured outside the submarine, the team must decide whether treatment can be safely provided at the location or whether the casualty must be evacuated.
Control immediate bleeding. Restore oxygen. Remove the casualty from continuing danger. Use field supplies to stabilize them.
Do not administer every drug in the medical kit merely because the team is frightened.
Report:
* Who is injured.
* The nature of the injury.
* Whether the casualty is conscious.
* Whether the area remains dangerous.
* Whether the team can return without assistance.
Examples:
* “Miner injured by crawler. Moderate bleeding controlled. Returning.”
* “Diver unconscious with oxygen loss. Medical needed at airlock.”
* “Security has a GSW. Hostiles still active.”
* “One casualty immobile. Request recovery team.”
The submarine should prepare medical assistance before the casualty arrives.
Do not drag an injured diver into the airlock and only then inform the medical officer that they have not been breathing for several minutes.
If a casualty cannot be recovered without causing additional deaths, the team leader may order withdrawal.
This is a grave decision.
It is sometimes necessary.
Europa readily turns one casualty into an entire missing team when rescuers abandon judgment.
