## WRECK AND SALVAGE OPERATIONS

Wrecks may contain cargo, tools, weapons, medicine, fuel, machinery, mission records, and survivors.
They may also contain flooding, electrical hazards, unstable reactors, armed occupants, parasites, structural collapse, hostile wildlife, and former crew members who are no longer entirely former.
Approach every wreck as an unknown environment.
Do not assume it is abandoned because no lights are visible.
Do not assume the occupants are dead because nobody answered the radio.
Do not assume the dead occupants will remain inactive.
Before entry:
* Inspect the exterior.
* Identify accessible hatches or breaches.
* Look for signs of movement.
* Check for weapon systems.
* Determine whether the wreck is stable.
* Establish a return route.
* Inform the submarine where the team is entering.
Inside the wreck, maintain formation and mark the route when necessary.
Avoid opening every door simultaneously. A sealed compartment may contain water pressure, fire, infection, hostile life, or a remarkably angry survivor.
Recover useful supplies, but remember the purpose of the mission.
An away team assigned to retrieve one cargo manifest should not keep the submarine waiting while it removes every chair, cup, and damaged coil from the wreck.
Prioritize:
1. Mission objectives.
2. Survivors.
3. Critical supplies.
4. Valuable or useful salvage.
5. Objects of uncertain value.
6. Decorative rubbish.
Do not allow greed to keep the team inside a deteriorating structure after conditions have become unsafe.
