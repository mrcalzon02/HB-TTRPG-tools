## XVI. THE RIGHT TO MEDICAL CARE

Injured or ill crew members are entitled to timely treatment consistent with available resources.
Medical personnel must prioritize according to clinical need, not rank, wealth, popularity, or ability to threaten the doctor.
The captain is entitled to command consideration where survival of the vessel genuinely depends upon their recovery.
This does not mean a captain with a bruised wrist takes priority over an engineer suffering catastrophic blood loss.
Union medical law also recognizes:
* Confidential handling of personal medical information.
* Protection from involuntary experimentation.
* The right to refuse nonemergency treatment.
* The duty to report contagious or vessel-threatening conditions.
* Access to pain treatment when medically justified.
* Protection against punitive withholding of medication.
* Compensation for occupational injury.
Medical treatment is not a privilege granted for good behavior.
Neither is the medical cabinet a public narcotics buffet.
The distinction is addressed elsewhere in this guide and should not require another incident involving Jerry from engineering.
