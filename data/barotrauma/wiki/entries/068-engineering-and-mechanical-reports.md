## ENGINEERING AND MECHANICAL REPORTS

Engineers and mechanics should inspect the submarine’s operational reserves before departure.
Critical concerns include:
* Reactor fuel rods.
* Welding fuel.
* Oxygen tanks.
* Diving-suit oxygen supplies.
* Portable batteries.
* Electrical components.
* Mechanical repair materials.
* Replacement tools.
* Fabrication resources.
* Hull-repair supplies.
* Fire extinguishers.
* Damaged pumps, engines, batteries, junction boxes, or docking systems.
* Materials required to manufacture ammunition or medicine underway.
Saying, “There is a fuel rod in the reactor,” is not a complete fuel report.
The relevant questions are:
* How much life remains in that rod?
* Are replacement rods aboard?
* How much power will the next route require?
* Will the submarine be operating high-load systems?
* Can additional fuel be fabricated?
* Is the next outpost close enough to depend upon immediate resupply?
* What happens if the vessel must reverse course or remain active longer than expected?
The same reasoning applies to welding fuel.
One partially filled welding tank may be sufficient while the submarine is docked and intact. It will not remain sufficient after multiple hull breaches, a prolonged creature attack, and an away team returning with three empty tanks.
Inspect reserves according to the likely demands of the next voyage, not according to the calm conditions present at the station.
