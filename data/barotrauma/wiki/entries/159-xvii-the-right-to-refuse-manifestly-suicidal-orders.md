## XVII. THE RIGHT TO REFUSE MANIFESTLY SUICIDAL ORDERS

A crew member may refuse an order when:
* The order presents an immediate and extreme danger.
* The danger is not reasonably necessary to preserve the vessel or crew.
* The crew member lacks required equipment or training.
* The order serves no legitimate operational purpose.
* Compliance would violate a recognized humanitarian protection.
* The order requires deliberate criminal harm unrelated to survival.
This right is narrow.
It does not permit refusal of every unpleasant, dangerous, or inconvenient task.
Submarine work is inherently dangerous.
A mechanic may still be ordered to repair a breach during an attack.
A security officer may still be ordered to confront hostile boarders.
A medic may still be required to enter a damaged compartment to reach casualties.
The relevant question is whether the danger is necessary, proportionate, and connected to preserving the vessel or completing agreed work.
“Go outside without a suit because I am curious what happens” is not a lawful order.
“Repair the external docking mechanism before we are crushed against the station” may be.
