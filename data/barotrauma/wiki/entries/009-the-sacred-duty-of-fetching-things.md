## THE SACRED DUTY OF FETCHING THINGS

Fetching supplies is sometimes dismissed as unimportant work.
It becomes considerably more important when the item being fetched is:
* The last welding tank.
* A replacement fuel rod.
* The blood pack required by a dying crew member.
* Ammunition for the only gun facing the threat.
* A diving suit for the person trapped inside a flooding compartment.
* A fire extinguisher.
* The captain’s emergency revolver.
* The engineer who knows why the reactor has begun making that noise.
A good assistant learns where supplies are stored.
A great assistant learns where supplies are actually stored after the crew has finished placing them anywhere except their designated storage.
The labels may say:
* Welding equipment.
* Medical supplies.
* Electrical components.
* Ammunition.
* Diving equipment.
The truth may be:
* Welding equipment in medical.
* Medical supplies in the captain’s cabin.
* Electrical components in a toolbox carried by an unconscious mechanic.
* Ammunition scattered across three compartments.
* Diving equipment worn by someone who went to the station bar.
Finding things is a legitimate maritime skill.
The assistant who knows where the replacement fuel rods are may briefly become the most important person on the submarine.
Enjoy the moment.
Then bring the rods to engineering.
