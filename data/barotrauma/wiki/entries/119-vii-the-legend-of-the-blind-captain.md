## VII. THE LEGEND OF THE BLIND CAPTAIN

Veteran captains occasionally swear upon their epaulets that they can navigate Europa without active sonar.
They claim to follow cavern roofs by engine echo, hull vibration, water movement, intuition, or an ancestral maritime sense inherited from relatives who once operated fishing boats on Earth.
Some of them can.
Some of them merely collide with confidence.
Running without active sonar may be appropriate when avoiding creatures sensitive to sound or attempting to conceal the vessel’s position. It should not become a theatrical demonstration of seniority.
Navigation information may come from:
* Active sonar.
* Passive sonar.
* Cameras.
* Periscopes.
* Viewports.
* Gunnery reports.
* Away-team observations.
* Known charts.
* Prior experience.
* The mechanic shouting that the wall is getting closer.
When operating without active sonar:
* Reduce speed.
* Assign visual observers.
* Use passive contacts.
* Follow known terrain.
* Request reports from gunnery.
* Use brief sonar pulses when necessary.
* Resist the urge to prove that rocks respect rank.
Navigating by instinct is impressive.
Navigating by instinct while ignoring a fully functional sonar set is how captains become cautionary anecdotes.
