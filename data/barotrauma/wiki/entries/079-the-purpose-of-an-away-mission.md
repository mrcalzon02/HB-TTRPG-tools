## THE PURPOSE OF AN AWAY MISSION

Every departure from the submarine should have a clear objective.
That objective should be stated before the airlock opens.
Examples include:
* Mine a specific mineral formation.
* Recover cargo from a wreck.
* Search an abandoned vessel for survivors or supplies.
* Retrieve a mission item.
* Collect biological material.
* Inspect an alien ruin.
* Repair an external component.
* Clear a hostile nest.
* Establish whether a route is safe.
* Board and secure an enemy vessel.
* Recover a missing crew member or previous away team.
“Going outside to look around” is not a sufficiently defined mission unless the captain has expressly authorized reconnaissance.
Before departure, every member of the team should know:
* Where they are going.
* What they are expected to accomplish.
* Who is leading the operation.
* What equipment they are carrying.
* Which hazards are expected.
* How long the submarine intends to remain in position.
* What signal means the mission has been aborted.
* How the team will return.
An away mission does not require a lengthy ceremony. It does require enough planning that the crew does not discover, halfway through a flooded cave, that nobody brought a plasma cutter, ore bag, medical kit, or spare oxygen.
