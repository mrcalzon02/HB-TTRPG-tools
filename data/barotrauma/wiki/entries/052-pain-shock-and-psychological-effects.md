## Pain, Shock, and Psychological Effects

Pain is medically relevant, but pain alone does not determine treatment priority.
A shouting patient with a minor wound may appear more urgent than a silent patient who is bleeding internally, losing oxygen, entering shock, or becoming unconscious. The medical officer must treat severity rather than volume.
Shock, confusion, panic, psychosis, and other psychological conditions can also affect crew safety. A patient may become irrational due to injury, poisoning, oxygen deprivation, medication, fear, infection, or an underlying psychological condition.
Do not assume every distressed crew member is merely being difficult.
Do not assume every difficult crew member requires sedation.
Assess the situation, reduce immediate danger, and use restraint or medication only when justified.
