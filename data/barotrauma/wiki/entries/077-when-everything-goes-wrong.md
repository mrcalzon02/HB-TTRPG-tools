## WHEN EVERYTHING GOES WRONG

There will be voyages during which several emergencies occur at once.
The hull breaches. The reactor begins overheating. Hostile creatures enter the ballast system. The medic is injured. The captain loses consciousness. Half the crew begins shouting contradictory reports over the radio.
When this happens, remember the order of survival:
1. Preserve breathable compartments.
2. Control flooding.
3. Stabilize the reactor and electrical systems.
4. Contain immediate hostile threats.
5. Recover and treat casualties.
6. Restore propulsion, navigation, and weapons.
7. Account for the crew.
8. Recover, reorganize, and resupply.
Do not run aimlessly between emergencies. Choose the most urgent problem you are capable of addressing and report that you are responding to it.
“Repairing aft breach.”
“Taking reactor.”
“Moving casualties to medical.”
“Reloading dorsal coilgun.”
Clear reports allow the captain to understand which problems remain unattended.
Panic spreads quickly through a submarine. So does competence.
Be the crew member whose arrival improves the situation.
This section fits after Leaving the Vessel and before the detailed medical and logistics chapters. It treats away missions as deliberate operations rather than spontaneous excursions.
