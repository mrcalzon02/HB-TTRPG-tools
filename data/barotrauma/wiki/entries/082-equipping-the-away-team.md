## EQUIPPING THE AWAY TEAM

The equipment carried should match the mission.
Carrying too little leaves the team unable to complete its work. Carrying too much deprives the submarine of supplies, slows the diver, and concentrates a dangerous amount of valuable equipment on a person who may not return.
A basic away-team loadout may include:
* A functioning diving suit.
* A full oxygen tank.
* At least one reserve oxygen supply when the mission warrants it.
* An underwater scooter for longer journeys.
* A handheld sonar or navigation aid.
* A radio headset.
* A light source.
* The tools required for the objective.
* Basic medical treatment.
* A weapon appropriate to the expected threat.
* Storage for recovered materials.
* Spare batteries or fuel where necessary.
A mining team may require plasma cutters and ore storage.
A salvage team may require cutting tools, crowbars, wiring equipment, and additional carrying capacity.
A security operation may require weapons, ammunition, restraints, breaching tools, and medical support.
An expedition into an alien ruin may require all of the above, along with substantially more caution than the average crew member believes necessary.
Before cycling the airlock, check:
* Suit condition.
* Oxygen level.
* Radio function.
* Weapon condition.
* Ammunition.
* Tool batteries or fuel.
* Medical supplies.
* Storage capacity.
* Team readiness.
Do not discover that your oxygen tank is empty after leaving the submarine.
Do not take every spare oxygen tank aboard because you are afraid of running out.
Carry a reasonable reserve and leave enough equipment for the other people who may need to respond if your team encounters trouble.
