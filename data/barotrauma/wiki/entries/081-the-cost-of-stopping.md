## THE COST OF STOPPING

A mining opportunity must be considered against the danger of remaining stationary.
A submarine that stops beside a cavern wall becomes easier for hostile wildlife to approach. The engines no longer provide the same ability to evade contact. Diving personnel outside the hull limit the captain’s freedom to manoeuvre. Weapon crews may have restricted firing angles because friendly divers are operating near the target area.
A rich mineral deposit can preserve the vessel’s finances.
It can also keep the submarine in one place long enough to attract every unnatural abomination within several kilometres.
The away team should therefore work deliberately.
Do not spend ten minutes discussing which person should mine the first piece of ore. Do not wander into side passages while the submarine waits. Do not mine three fragments and then begin a recreational tour of the cave system.
Recover the intended resources, remain aware of the situation, and return when recalled.
The captain may terminate the operation at any time because of:
* Approaching hostile contacts.
* Changing current or terrain conditions.
* Reactor or electrical problems.
* Hull damage.
* Loss of communication.
* Mission time constraints.
* Low ammunition.
* Another crew emergency.
* The discovery of a larger threat nearby.
A recall order is not a suggestion.
The mineral deposit will still be present if the submarine survives long enough to return.
