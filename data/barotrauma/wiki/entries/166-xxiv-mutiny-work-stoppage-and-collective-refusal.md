## XXIV. MUTINY, WORK STOPPAGE, AND COLLECTIVE REFUSAL

Union action is not automatically mutiny.
Mutiny seeks unlawful control of the vessel.
A labor action seeks enforcement or renegotiation of employment terms.
The distinction may become difficult when the reactor operator folds their arms beside the control panel, but it remains important.
A protected collective refusal may arise when:
* Wages have not been paid.
* The vessel is manifestly unsafe.
* Essential equipment is unavailable.
* The crew is ordered beyond agreed duties without negotiation.
* The captain refuses to address a serious contract violation.
* A union representative has been unlawfully detained.
* The employer attempts forced labor.
A work stoppage must not deliberately destroy the vessel or abandon innocent personnel to immediate danger.
Essential life-support functions should be preserved where possible.
Crew members may refuse to depart from port more safely than they can refuse to repair an active hull breach.
The ocean does not suspend pressure while labor and management resolve their differences.
Union law expects both judgment and solidarity.
