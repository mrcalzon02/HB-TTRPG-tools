## THE SUBMARINER BETWEEN FACTIONS

Ordinary submariners often find themselves operating between Coalition stations, separatist territories, corporate enclaves, cartel ports, union docks, Honkmother gatherings, and communities where the local authorities insist no cults are present despite the chanting audible through the walls.
The general crewman should therefore understand several principles.
First, clothing alone does not establish guilt.
Second, concealed parasites are more concerning than visible face paint.
Third, visible face paint does not make sabotage acceptable.
Fourth, political affiliation does not automatically make someone an enemy combatant.
Fifth, anyone attempting to disable life support, spread infection, seize the vessel, or kill the crew has moved beyond philosophical disagreement.
Captains and security officers should avoid treating every clown as a cultist, every dissident as a separatist agent, every religious person as a Husk adherent, or every shortage complaint as the beginning of mutiny.
Broad suspicion creates the exact isolation and resentment from which radical movements recruit.
Equally, the crew should not dismiss obvious warning signs merely because confrontation may be uncomfortable.
Warning signs include:
* Unexplained biological samples.
* Hidden syringes.
* Missing medical supplies.
* Deliberate infection.
* Repeated tampering with life support.
* Cult markings placed inside restricted compartments.
* Attempts to isolate vulnerable crew members.
* Sabotage described as ritual.
* Violence described as comedy.
* Claims that the submarine must be sacrificed.
* Claims that the crew must “hear the call” through depressurization.
The ocean already possesses enough mechanisms for killing submariners.
It does not require assistance from theology.
