## Poisoning

Europa offers a remarkable variety of substances capable of killing a human being.
Some are produced by local life. Others are carried aboard deliberately. Still others were fabricated by the medical department after someone discovered that the chemical synthesizer would permit it.
The medical officer should understand the common effects of the poisons, venoms, paralysants, and toxins carried or encountered by the crew. They must also know which antidotes correspond to which agents.
An antidote is not universally effective merely because the container contains the word “antidote.”
When poisoning is suspected, determine what substance may have been involved and how long ago exposure occurred. Administer the appropriate countermeasure and observe the patient for continuing deterioration.
Do not respond to an unknown toxin by injecting every antidote aboard the vessel in alphabetical order.
