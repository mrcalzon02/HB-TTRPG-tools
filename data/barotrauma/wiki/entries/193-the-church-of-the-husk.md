## THE CHURCH OF THE HUSK

The Church of the Husk presents a substantially darker problem.
Its members revere, accept, or seek union with the parasitic organism commonly called the Husk parasite.
The same organism has killed thousands of submariners directly and contributed to the deaths of many more through abandoned stations, infected wrecks, lost vessels, quarantine failures, and containment operations.
Exact casualty figures remain disputed because several early outbreaks were recorded as station failures, piracy, unexplained crew loss, or “biological irregularities,” which is administrative language for discovering that the dead have begun walking.
Husk infection may progressively alter the body, impair communication, compromise ordinary human behavior, and transform the victim into an active threat to surrounding personnel.
The organism is not misunderstood medicine.
It is not a harmless adaptation.
It is not a spiritual vaccination.
It is not an unusually committed dietary preference.
It is a lethal parasite.
Church adherents nevertheless describe infection as:
* Communion with Europa.
* Freedom from fear.
* Adaptation to the sea.
* The next stage of humanity.
* Release from the weakness of ordinary flesh.
* Proof that Europa has chosen them.
Medical personnel generally describe it as:
* Infection.
* Parasitic compromise.
* A quarantine emergency.
* A reason to lock the medical cabinet.
* A reason to ask why the assistant has several unlabeled syringes.
The Church is more secretive than the Honkmother movement.
Its members may conceal their affiliation, hide biological material, sabotage medical examinations, interfere with treatment, or deliberately expose other crew members without consent.
Some cells believe forced infection is an act of liberation.
Others view uninfected humanity as temporary, inferior, or spiritually deaf.
Still others appear less interested in religion than in using the Church as cover for assassination, sabotage, biological warfare, or personal experimentation.
Any crew member suspected of deliberate Husk exposure must be treated as both a medical and security concern.
This does not permit immediate execution of anyone who coughs.
It does require:
* Prompt medical evaluation.
* Isolation when justified.
* Control of biological samples.
* Inspection of medical storage.
* Protection of treatment supplies.
* Reporting unexplained symptoms.
* Preventing infected personnel from accessing crowded areas.
* Avoiding the traditional mistake of waiting until the patient has fully transformed because confronting them earlier felt impolite.
An infected person may still be a patient.
A cultist deliberately spreading infection is a hostile actor.
Medical and security personnel must understand the distinction, and must also understand the moment at which the first condition becomes the second.
A willingness to expose oneself to the Husk does not create the right to expose everyone else.
Consent does not spread through ventilation.
