## XLVII. FINAL DECLARATION

Beneath Europa’s ice, survival is collective.
Every pressure door was installed by someone.
Every fuel rod was mined, fabricated, transported, inspected, and loaded by someone.
Every station light depends upon a chain of workers stretching through the darkness.
Every submarine route exists because crews traveled it before charts were reliable and rescue was uncertain.
Standard Union 208 exists because those workers learned that isolated courage was not enough.
A single crew can be threatened.
A single captain can be cheated.
A single vessel can disappear.
A united maritime network cannot be ignored so easily.
The union has survived governments, corporations, cartels, wars, blockades, disasters, schisms, frauds, and generations of administrators confidently predicting that organized labor was no longer necessary.
It remains necessary because Europa remains Europa.
The ice is still thick.
The sea is still dark.
The pressure is still waiting.
The creatures remain hungry.
The contracts remain complicated.
The employers remain creative.
The crews still require food, air, fuel, medicine, pay, rescue, and the knowledge that someone will answer when they call.
Remember the Crampton refusal.
Remember the blacklist years.
Remember the Saville crewmen.
Remember the stations that believed they could function without the people who carried their food and repaired their machinery.
A station may threaten a submarine.
A cartel may threaten a crew.
An employer may threaten a worker.
But when Union 208 withdraws its hands, the cargo remains at the dock, the pumps remain broken, the reactor parts remain undelivered, and the station begins counting how many meals remain.
Do not trifle with the union.
Not because every member is powerful.
Not because every captain agrees.
Not because every station recognizes the same law.
Do not trifle with it because beneath the ice, civilization is a supply chain held together by working people.
When those people refuse to move, very little else does.
