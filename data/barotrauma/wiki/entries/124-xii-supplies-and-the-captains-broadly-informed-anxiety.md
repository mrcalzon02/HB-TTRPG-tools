## XII. SUPPLIES AND THE CAPTAIN’S BROADLY INFORMED ANXIETY

The captain should maintain a general understanding of the vessel’s stores.
They need not personally count every bandage.
They should know whom to ask and whether that person has recently been using the bandages as decorative streamers.
Important supplies include:
* Reactor fuel.
* Welding fuel.
* Oxygen.
* Ammunition.
* Medical treatment.
* Blood products.
* Mechanical components.
* Electrical components.
* Fabrication materials.
* Diving equipment.
* Batteries.
* Tools.
* Anything the crew insists is “mission critical” after purchasing it personally.
The captain should also know what can be fabricated aboard.
Minerals may become ammunition, medicine, fuel, explosives, tools, or electrical components.
They do not become those things through optimism.
The vessel still requires working machinery, power, trained personnel, and time.
A cargo hold full of minerals is not the same as a stocked submarine.
It is a stocked submarine after engineering has spent twenty minutes arguing about fabrication priorities.
