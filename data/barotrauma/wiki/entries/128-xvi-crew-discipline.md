## XVI. CREW DISCIPLINE

Discipline beneath the ice is complicated by distance, mixed jurisdictions, private contracts, debts, political allegiances, criminal associations, union rules, and the traditional Europan belief that every regulation is optional if the nearest authority is several kilometres away.
Not every disagreement is mutiny.
Not every refusal is sabotage.
Not every criticism is insubordination.
The captain must distinguish between:
* A legitimate safety objection.
* A department reporting that an order is impossible.
* A worker raising a compensation issue.
* Refusal of ordinary duty without cause.
* Deliberate obstruction.
* Theft.
* Assault.
* Sabotage.
* An organized attempt to seize command.
* An assistant wearing a clown mask and behaving suspiciously, which is statistically inconclusive.
Discipline should be proportionate.
Possible responses include:
* Verbal correction.
* Reassignment.
* Removal from sensitive duties.
* Confiscation of weapons.
* Detention.
* Dismissal at the next station.
* Transfer to local authorities.
* Emergency force when the vessel or crew is under immediate threat.
Execution is not the standard punishment for incompetence.
If it were, Europa would have run out of submariners during the first decade.
