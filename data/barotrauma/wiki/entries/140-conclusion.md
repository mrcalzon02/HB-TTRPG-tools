## CONCLUSION

The submariner’s trade is not built upon heroic individuals acting alone.
It is built upon ordinary people performing necessary work under extraordinary pressure.
The most valuable crew member is not always the finest shot, the most gifted engineer, the quickest medic, or the boldest diver. It is often the person who can be trusted to carry out an order, report a problem, conserve supplies, help a struggling crewmate, and keep their judgment when the alarms begin.
Do not operate equipment you do not understand.
Do not take supplies you do not require.
Do not leave the vessel without authorization.
Do not use weapons without identifying the target and what lies beyond it.
Do not interfere with the helm or chain of command for personal satisfaction.
Do not turn mistakes into feuds.
Do not make Europa’s work easier.
Learn your station. Maintain your equipment. Answer the radio. Watch the hatches. Return what you borrow. Help recover the wounded. Keep the decks clear. Respect the people whose lives are sealed inside the same hull as yours.
Beyond the glass is darkness, pressure, cold, radiation, and hunger.
Inside the hull is the crew.
Treat that distinction as sacred.
