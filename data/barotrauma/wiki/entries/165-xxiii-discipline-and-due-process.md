## XXIII. DISCIPLINE AND DUE PROCESS

Union rules do not prevent discipline.
They prevent arbitrary discipline.
Crew members may be corrected or punished for:
* Sabotage.
* Assault.
* Theft.
* Deliberate insubordination.
* Unauthorized weapons use.
* Reckless endangerment.
* Abandoning duty.
* Misuse of medical supplies.
* Tampering with the reactor.
* Unauthorized movement of the submarine.
* Repeated refusal of agreed work.
* Bringing an unidentified organism aboard and placing it in the ballast tank.
Discipline should be proportionate to the violation.
Possible measures include:
* Warning.
* Retraining.
* Reassignment.
* Loss of sensitive privileges.
* Temporary detention.
* Restitution.
* Dismissal.
* Transfer to station authorities.
* Emergency force when an immediate threat exists.
A crew member should be informed of the reason for serious discipline.
Evidence should be preserved when possible.
Witnesses should be consulted.
The accused should be permitted to respond unless the vessel is in immediate danger.
Summary execution because someone “looked suspicious” is generally disfavored.
This remains true even when the person is dressed as a clown.
