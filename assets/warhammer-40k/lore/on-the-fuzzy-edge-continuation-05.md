# On the Fuzzy Edge

## Continuation V — The Receipt That Wasn't

**Based on the work of:** *On the Fuzzy Edge.*

The burial office had seventeen inspection cards, sixteen expenditure warrants, and one argument about the missing warrant.

The argument had begun before Javard arrived. He knew because the clerk nearest the door was still holding a ruler as though he intended to settle the matter with it.

"Seventeen inspections," said the senior custodian. "Sixteen payments. There must have been a first year when the work was done without charge."

"Or the seventeenth warrant is missing," said the clerk.

"That is less charitable."

"To whom?"

Javard found a chair that had not yet been requisitioned as evidence.

"Which of you is responsible for the originals?"

The ruler descended.

"I am," said the clerk. "Merek. The custodian signs the annual allowance. I keep the cards."

"Then neither of you is to explain the other man's records to me. Show me what each record actually says."

Caldris stood beside the door with an expression of determined neutrality. He had spent the morning persuading the burial office that preserving its documents was not an accusation that its clerks had been murdering people. The burial office had accepted this in principle and resented it in practice.

Merek laid the cards in order. Their paper had yellowed unevenly. The oldest bore the same narrow stamp as the card Javard had seen beneath Ephram Sol's plaque.

**TRANSFER READINESS INSPECTION.**

Not interment. Not custody. Readiness.

"These were all issued by the maintenance servitor?" Javard asked.

"Stamped by it. The duty clerk removes each year's card after the Vigil of Saint Orphael and files it here."

"Does the clerk inspect the niche?"

"No. The unit reports deficiencies if there are any."

"Has it?"

"One security deficiency this year."

Serik, standing behind Javard, looked toward the ceiling.

"I have already been insulted by the machine," he said. "Must the record preserve it?"

"It has been preserved without our help," Javard said.

Merek showed them the last card. There, in a cramped additional field, was the notice about an unauthorized weapon. No human had initialled it.

"Who signs to certify that the transfer occurred?" Javard asked.

The custodian pointed to an empty rectangle on the oldest card.

"That would be the receiving officer."

"And who is the receiving officer?"

"The officer who receives the transfer."

"An enviable definition."

The custodian reddened. "I cannot give you a name the book does not contain."

"Good. Please continue not doing so."

---

The expenditure warrants were more revealing about the cathedral than about the dead.

Each year the burial office requested a modest allotment for the upkeep of condemned Lower Ossuary Nineteen. Each year a different administrator had complained that money ought not be spent on a closed precinct. Each year the expenditure had survived by being attached to a larger allowance for the maintenance of unused burial places.

The sixteenth warrant had been renewed by a clerk who was now dead. The oldest was signed by the previous Pontiff's bursar.

"Why is the first year absent?" Javard asked.

Merek brought out the burial office's accession register.

"Because it wasn't a payment," he said. "It was an instruction."

The first entry had been made seventeen years earlier, after Sol's death and the initiation of the pending transfer. It directed the office to prepare one destination and keep it serviceable until a properly authenticated delivery could be received.

The order was signed by the predecessor's bursar. A second signature field, marked **RECEIPT OF REMAINS**, was blank.

"Who authorized the preparation?" Javard asked.

"The bursar."

"Who authorized the transfer?"

Merek produced the separate transfer strip. Its issuing authority was obscured beneath a valid but unresolved Inquisitorial designation. Javard had already seen the strip. He did not allow the familiar seal to become more informative through repetition.

"Who accepted the remains?"

Neither clerk answered.

Caldris shifted his weight.

"Your predecessor may have assumed the transfer was certain," he told the custodian.

"He may," said Javard. "He might also have wanted to ensure that nobody could later say the cathedral had refused to prepare a place. We have a signed preparation order. We do not have his reasons."

The custodian looked at the seventeen cards.

"Then the inspections were lawful."

"They may have been properly ordered. That is not the same as proof that the transfer happened."

"But the niche looks occupied."

"Something in the niche produced organic residue when Sister-Medicae Halva tested the drainage gap. She did not identify it."

Merek looked up sharply. "You tested the niche?"

"Without opening it."

The custodian closed his eyes.

"Someone should have informed my office."

"Someone should have been able to tell me who was responsible for it."

The custodian opened his eyes again. It was not a fair answer to his complaint, and Javard knew it.

"You are right," Javard said. "You should have been informed. Caldris, have an authenticated account delivered before the next watch. The test, the limits of the result, and who was present. No suggestion of an identified body."

Caldris wrote it down.

The custodian's anger did not vanish. It became something Javard could work with.

---

Merek would not surrender the cards.

"I have no authority to release original records from this office."

"I have an Inquisitorial rosette."

"And I have a custody register," Merek said. "If you remove them, I need an entry saying what left, who took it, and on whose order."

Serik watched Javard carefully.

"Then we shall make one," Javard said.

Merek hesitated. He had expected the rosette to end the argument, and its failure to do so had left him without the speech he had prepared.

The originals were laid flat between clean boards and secured with the burial office's cord and wax. Merek wrote a numbered transfer memorandum. Javard authenticated the order for temporary examination. Caldris witnessed the sealing, but not the truth of anything written on the cards.

They made two inventories. One stayed with the burial office. One accompanied the bundle.

"Now you can take them," Merek said.

"Not yet. What happens to the other niches if your inspection unit is diverted to us?"

"The ordinary route will be delayed."

"How long?"

"One watch, perhaps two. The unit has already completed the Vigil circuit, but its wheel bearing requires service."

"Then service it. I have not ordered you to suspend maintenance."

"Someone has asked us to preserve its operating record."

"Preserve a copy of the record, not the unit."

The custodian said, "That will require a second clerk."

"Yes."

"We are already short one."

"Why?"

"He was assigned to the Pontiff's recovery office."

Caldris made a small, unhappy noise.

Javard looked at him.

"Can the recovery office spare him?"

"Not without delaying the acting celebrant's appointments."

"Use the standing delegation for the celebrant. Then return the clerk to his ordinary post."

Caldris stared at him.

"That procedure is becoming a personal grievance."

"An institution is entitled to grievances. It is not entitled to manufacture emergencies by refusing to use its own procedures."

Outside the office, a bell marked the midday rites. Nobody in the room had eaten.

Javard bought them an hour.

---

Halva's statement arrived in a plain envelope. It was three pages long and included a separate note objecting to the expectation that she would provide witness testimony during her patient's dressing change.

Javard approved of the objection. It made the limits of her statement easier to trust.

She had heard Montpclair ask for Javard by name after the Pontiff regained partial orientation. She had heard the question about Sol. She could not swear that nobody had spoken either name earlier in the ward. She had been attending to the patient's breathing, bleeding and damaged cranial fittings.

Caldris's statement was shorter.

He remembered hearing the Pontiff ask whether Javard had opened Sol, and remembered sending for the Interrogator. He had not heard a staff member prompt the question. He could not account for every conversation before he arrived.

The statements agreed about the words.

They did not establish how the words had reached the patient.

Serik read them at Javard's invitation.

"So neither of them knows."

"Neither claims to know."

"Is that an improvement?"

"Over a confident answer invented after the event? Considerably."

A runner from the medicae brought a third note. Montpclair had slept through the morning, woken in pain, and been ordered not to receive visitors. His dressing had required changing. His remaining hearing was adequate on the right, but Halva would not certify his fitness for administrative work.

Caldris read the note twice.

"The Pontiff will want to know what we found."

"He will be told when he can hear it without paying for the information in blood."

"That could be days."

"The records have waited seventeen years."

"That is an unkind comparison."

"Yes," Javard said. "It is."

Caldris folded the note and put it away. His irritation was genuine. So was his concern for the man upstairs. Javard made himself remember both.

---

The transfer book lived in a separate room.

The burial office recorded destinations. The transit office recorded movement. The two were linked by a reference number, not by the certainty of the men who worked in them.

The transit registrar had a scar across one knuckle and a habit of placing his hand over the open book whenever anyone leaned too close.

"Sol's reference?" he asked.

Javard gave it.

The registrar found the line.

**DELIVERY REQUESTED.**

Beneath it were spaces for carrier acceptance, departure, arrival, and recipient's receipt.

The first was marked with an office stamp. The other three were blank.

"Did a carrier accept the request?" Javard asked.

"The office accepted the request."

"Did a person accept the load?"

"Not recorded."

"Is there a separate manifest?"

"Not attached to this line."

"Could there have been one?"

"Yes. Some of the older carriers kept their own."

"Where would those records be?"

"With the carrier. If it still exists."

Javard looked at the date. Seventeen years had consumed companies, ships, clerks, seals, and at least one Pontiff. It had not obliged a single one of them to preserve his preferred chain of evidence.

"Does your office ever close an unfulfilled request?"

"After a formal cancellation."

"Has this been cancelled?"

"No."

"Why not?"

The registrar gave him the first answer all day that required no qualification.

"Nobody has authorized us to cancel it."

Serik leaned against the wall.

"So there is a place kept ready, and a request nobody can close, and no record of a delivery."

"That is what these offices can establish," Javard said.

"Then what is in the niche?"

"That is a different question."

The registrar put his hand over the book again.

"If you are going to open it, I would like notice."

"Why?"

"Because someone will eventually ask why my office allowed a transfer to be completed without a receipt."

Javard regarded him.

"Would you sign one now?"

"No."

"Even if I ordered you?"

"I would sign that you ordered me. I would not sign that I saw a body."

The registrar's knuckle whitened against the page.

Javard nodded.

"Good. Keep that distinction."

---

At dusk the records were back in the cathedral's custody, secured under dual inventory. Javard retained authenticated copies and the transfer office's signed statement of what its book did not show.

Caldris was exhausted. He had spent a day persuading clerks to preserve evidence that might eventually expose mistakes made by their predecessors, while keeping enough of the cathedral running for the faithful to notice nothing more unusual than the customary delays.

"Do we stop the inspections?" he asked.

"No."

"Do we tell the burial office the niche may be occupied?"

"We tell them exactly what was observed, and what was not established."

"Do we inform the Pontiff?"

"When Halva permits."

"And the cabinet?"

"Still sealed."

Caldris leaned against the records table.

"Seventeen years of work. For a receipt that doesn't exist."

"For a receipt we haven't found."

"You are determined to preserve even that possibility."

"I am determined not to improve the evidence by disliking it."

Serik, who had spent most of the day carrying bundles and discovering that the Inquisition required more witnesses than a commercial lawsuit, set down the final inventory.

"Where next?"

"The carrier's records, if they survive. The predecessor's instruction, if anyone kept its original. And the people who paid for sixteen years of work without ever asking to see a delivery."

"Do you think they knew?"

"Some of them knew there was a pending transfer. Some knew only that the budget renewed. We shall have to discover which."

From the adjoining chamber came the scratch and click of Archive Servitor Nine.

Javard did not go to it.

The machine had given him a warning. Montpclair had repeated a question. A sealed niche stood ready beneath the cathedral. None of those things had become less troubling because the burial office's paperwork was ordinary.

Ordinary paperwork, he had learned, could preserve a mystery for longer than any conspiracy.

He placed the signed transit statement beside the older burial strip. On one page a place had been prepared. On the other a journey had been requested. Neither contained a human acknowledgment that the journey had ended.

Between the two documents was a space wide enough for a body.

He closed the folder without filling it.
