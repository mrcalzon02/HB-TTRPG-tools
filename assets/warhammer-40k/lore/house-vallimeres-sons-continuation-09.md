# House Vallimere's Sons

## Continuation IX — The Hours Owed

The fleet instruction office had found a way to offer the Academy precisely what it had asked for, provided the Academy could supply what the fleet no longer possessed.

Renault read the answer twice before breakfast. He passed it to Prite without comment.

One independent counterpart assessment could be considered during the next review cycle. The fleet would nominate an officer and an assessor, but only after the two engine-room exercises displaced by the previous trial had been completed. The Academy would pay for additional observation and record custody. The fleet would not surrender another operational lieutenant during the present cycle. Nor would it accept Academy instructors as substitutes for independent evaluators.

There was a space for a proposed date.

It was empty.

"They've agreed," Prite said.

"They've agreed to consider agreeing," Renault replied.

Darcelle had already opened the earlier invoice. "They also want the original engineering assessments closed before they'll let the pressure rig go again."

"Can we pay for a second rig?"

"Can we build a second lieutenant?" Renault asked. "That might be cheaper."

Prite set the correspondence beside the seminar account. The Academy's two-cadet exercise had delivered two urgent pallets and evacuated four simulated workers. It had not delivered the other five pallets, repaired the berth, replenished the shuttle, or produced evidence that their teaching made better fleet officers.

The fleet was not disputing that the exercise had taught something. It was declining to spend a second working day finding out what.

"Then we find the hours," Prite said.

Renault tapped the blank date. "From whom?"

---

The gunnery instructor had kept a list of the cadets whose optional tactical review had been surrendered to make room for the seminar. There were two names, each with a failed problem and a request for a second attempt.

Prite found her in Hall Four, where she was supervising the replacement of a scorched emitter contact. The room smelled of warmed insulation. A maintenance technician had the casing open and was waiting for the current to be isolated before touching anything.

"You could give them the review next week," Prite suggested.

"I could. Next week is the live-fire calibration."

"After that?"

"After that they leave for the short navigation block. I can find them a console on the rest day, if the rest day is not already occupied by someone else's improvement."

He did not mistake the last word for praise.

"Would you take an additional instructor?"

"One who knows their previous attempts and can distinguish a bad firing solution from a damaged projector?"

"Yes."

"Then you have named my present instructor."

The technician announced the current was isolated. The gunnery instructor waited while he checked it himself.

Prite had spent his childhood in rooms like this, where a machine could be returned to service only after somebody had done the unglamorous work of proving it would not kill the next person to open it. His father had never called the proof an inconvenience. Prite had.

"The cadets should get their second attempt," he said.

"They should. They were promised the time before you needed the room."

She handed him the training register. The original review was still marked as postponed, not completed. Neither cadet's existing examination mark had changed. The instructor had refused to let the absence of a second attempt be entered as a failure.

"Don't fix the ledger," she said. "Fix the timetable."

He carried the register back to the workroom.

Renault studied the dates. "We can return the optional tactical review by using our next unscheduled demonstration period."

"That is where I meant to run another seminar."

"I know."

"We could shorten the seminar."

"We already shortened the gunnery review to make room for the first one."

Darcelle looked up from the maintenance account. "The technicians stayed two hours after the seminar to restore the consoles. One has been assigned a full shift tomorrow. The other has not had the owed time entered against anything."

Prite reached for the account.

"Not against maintenance," Darcelle said. "Against us. We used the machines."

The first seminar had ended with an exhausted simulated shuttle. Its real aftermath was a technician who could not be both off duty and on the next morning's repair roster.

Prite marked the demonstration period for the two gunnery cadets. He had been prepared to sacrifice a lesson. He was less pleased to discover the sacrifice belonged to him.

---

The Academy bursar received the professors with the strained courtesy of a man who had learned that genius usually arrived without a purchase order.

Renault brought a staffing table. Darcelle brought the simulator service record. Prite brought the fleet's conditional offer.

The bursar examined all three.

"Your table has four columns," he said.

"Personnel, hours, replacement cover and cost," Renault answered.

"It has no contingency."

"We haven't requested a contingency."

"You have requested people to leave their ordinary work. People become ill. Ships arrive late. Equipment fails. That is why there is a contingency."

Darcelle turned over his service record. The emitter contact in Hall Four had survived the seminar, but it had been removed from the following week's navigation exercise after inspection. The replacement was already requisitioned for the conventional course. Another independent counterpart trial would need a different hall or a confirmed repair before the fleet officer arrived.

The bursar put a finger on the line.

"Which appropriation pays for that?"

"Existing maintenance," Prite said.

"Maintenance would have replaced a worn contact eventually. The earlier use brought the work forward. You may not bill a replacement twice, but neither may you call the work free because the machine was old."

Renault drew a line through a duplicated charge. Darcelle corrected the technician-hours entry. Prite watched the projected balance become less agreeable.

They could cover the Academy's clerical sealing and one observer from the instructional reserve. They could not cover an additional fleet observer and an engineering assessor without postponing a scheduled conventional assessment. The bursar would not permit that postponement to be described as a harmless exchange of rooms.

"Then we request an allocation," Prite said.

"From the Headmaster."

"Yes."

"With the displacement stated."

"Yes."

The bursar nodded. It was the first encouraging thing he had done.

"You are learning how to ask for money."

"I had thought I was learning to teach officers."

"That is considerably more expensive."

---

The fleet evaluator joined the review by secured channel the following morning. She had not brought Lieutenant Morn. Morn had returned to her duties, and the evaluator saw no reason to detach her merely to explain that she could not be detached.

The Headmaster sat at the end of the table. The gunnery instructor was present at his request. Vane stood near the door, having checked the names of those admitted and the authority under which they were there.

Renault gave the figures. The Academy could fund the next assessment's local record custody and one observer without touching the conventional course. It could not yet fund the second observer or the fleet engineering assessor. The two displaced engine-room exercises remained unfinished, and one depended on a pressure rig booked for a damage-control examination.

"Would a later date solve the staffing problem?" the Headmaster asked.

"It would solve part of it," the evaluator said. "Not the qualification of the counterpart. We will not send a lieutenant merely because your calendar has an opening."

Prite had prepared an argument about the value of the exercise. He folded the page instead.

"What evidence would make another trial worth the detachment?"

"The same evidence you are trying to obtain. That is why the first trial was permitted. But it is not the only claim on our people. Give us a defined question, a funded observation plan, and a period when the postponed training has been restored. We can then decide whether the cost is justified."

"Could we supply a retired officer?"

"You could supply an Academy instructor. That would make it Academy instruction. It would not be an independent fleet counterpart."

Darcelle nodded before Prite could speak. He had spent too many nights constructing artificial opponents who seemed independent because he had made their answers unpredictable.

The evaluator continued. "I have not rejected the method. I have not endorsed its effect on fleet performance. Those are different positions."

The Headmaster turned to the gunnery instructor.

"Your two cadets?"

"Their original marks stand. Their review is owed. Professor Renault has offered a replacement period, but the technicians must have the room ready."

"And the maintenance hours?"

"Recorded," Darcelle said. "One owed rest period is still awaiting a roster amendment."

The Headmaster wrote the amendment into the decision slate and sent it to the duty office for acceptance. He did not call the matter settled while it remained unsigned.

"The independent assessment is deferred to the next review cycle," he said. "Not approved, not cancelled. The Academy will make no further request until the fleet's displaced exercises are restored and the bursar certifies a complete staffing plan. Our next demonstration period goes to the two gunnery cadets. The seminar remains a single non-graded instructional exercise."

Prite looked at the empty date on the fleet's offer.

"Can I ask again when those conditions are met?"

"You may. You may also be refused."

Renault looked at the unnumbered question on Prite's slate.

Prite closed it.

---

House Caldrake's observer had filed his formal request before the meeting. It asked for the command-channel transcript of the two-cadet seminar, the individual first accounts, and the later cross-brief debriefs. In a separate paragraph it challenged the Academy's description of the seminar as a response to the earlier fleet trial.

Vane did not treat the second paragraph as a threat. It was a fair question.

The observer met him in the records chamber with a clerk from the Academy's ordinary appeals office. The clerk was not part of Vane's protective detail, and the observer had insisted on that distinction.

"Your House has the signed independent findings," Vane said. "The Academy will also provide the Headmaster's signed seminar account, including the displaced review, the maintenance hours and the lack of any fleet-performance conclusion."

"The first accounts would tell us whether the professors altered the cadets' explanations afterward."

"They were sealed before the opposite briefs were released. The custody record establishes the order of sealing. The private contents are not within your observer agreement."

"An institution certifying its own custody is not independent proof."

"No," Vane said. "It is a record that can be challenged through the proper process. You have asked for an external witness to the seal log. That is a narrower request than access to the cadets' private accounts."

The appeals clerk confirmed that the observer could submit a challenge to the custody procedure without acquiring the protected debriefs. A designated reviewing officer could examine the seal sequence and report whether it complied with the existing assessment rules. The clerk made no promise that such a review would vindicate either party.

The observer read the Headmaster's signed account.

"It says two pallets were delivered."

"Yes."

"And five remained aboard."

"Yes."

"Why does the Academy's notice call this a successful seminar?"

Vane examined the notice. It had been prepared by a publicity clerk, not by the instructors. The phrase was there.

"I will have that referred to the Headmaster," he said.

"Will you strike it?"

"I do not have authority to amend his office's notice."

The observer seemed almost disappointed.

"Then my House will preserve the objection."

"You should."

He did. He also accepted the signed account and filed the narrower custody challenge. No private transcript changed hands. No new duel petition accompanied the exchange.

Vane entered the disposition in the security record. It was possible for an observer to dislike the Academy, exercise a lawful right and still be correct about a sentence. That was not a defect in the protection orders.

---

The publicity notice reached Prite with the disputed phrase underlined.

He had not written it. He had said that the cadets had learned something. The publicity office had translated that into a successful demonstration of the Academy's revised doctrine, and someone else had shortened the sentence again.

The Headmaster summoned the clerk who had issued it. The clerk produced the approval trail: an instructional summary, a request for a public account, and a routine compression of the wording to fit the notice board. No forged signature or malicious intervention was required.

"Was the exercise successful?" the Headmaster asked Prite.

"It was completed."

"That was not my question."

"It taught the cadets to identify information they had failed to exchange. It cost an optional review and maintenance hours. It did not establish that our doctrine improves fleet outcomes."

The Headmaster turned to the clerk.

"Print that."

"It will not fit the board, sir."

"Then use two boards."

The revised notice went up that afternoon. The old one was retained with the correction attached, not quietly removed from the archive. The cadets' original marks and sealed accounts were left alone. The custody challenge would proceed through the ordinary review channel, where it could succeed or fail on the seal record rather than the House's opinion of the professors.

Prite watched a pair of cadets stop to read the longer notice. One laughed at the expense table. The other asked why the Academy had spent so much to teach people to talk to each other.

Prite nearly answered. Then he remembered the fleet evaluator's question.

What had the Academy actually learned that justified taking another officer from duty?

Not enough, yet.

He let the cadets finish reading.

---

The two gunnery cadets received their postponed review in the recovered demonstration period. Their instructor supervised the attempt, the replacement contact passed inspection, and the maintenance technician's amended rest entry appeared on the duty roster before the room was released. Neither cadet was promised a better mark for having waited. Both were given the opportunity that had been owed.

Renault brought the completed timetable to the workroom. The conventional navigation block had not moved. The damage-control assessment retained its pressure rig. The fleet's two postponed engine-room exercises were still waiting on the fleet's own schedule, and the independent counterpart assessment had no date.

"One problem repaid," he said.

"Not all of them," Darcelle replied.

"No. I thought you would appreciate the novelty of a correct total."

Darcelle placed the sealed seminar custody receipt in its proper file. He did not attach the cadets' private accounts to the House observer response. The reviewing officer would receive what the procedure authorized when the challenge was accepted for examination.

Prite returned to the syllabus. The first questions remained where they had been. So did the unnumbered one.

**Who is allowed to say no?**

He had once understood it as a problem for the cadet facing another commander. Now he had an answer from the gunnery instructor, the bursar, the fleet evaluator, the Headmaster and a hostile observer who had been right about an overconfident notice.

None of them had prevented him from teaching. They had prevented him from spending other people's obligations without counting them.

"Are you writing Lesson Four?" Renault asked.

"No."

"Good. The printers will survive another day."

Prite entered the conditions for a renewed fleet request beneath the question: restored displaced training; certified staff and funding; independent authority; a question the next trial could actually answer.

He left the date blank.

Outside, Hall Four was occupied by the conventional course. The cadets inside were learning to plot an approach without destroying a ship. It was not new doctrine. It was still necessary.
