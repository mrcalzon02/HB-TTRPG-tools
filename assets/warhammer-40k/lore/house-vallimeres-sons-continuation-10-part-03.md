The examining officer's finding occupied three pages.

The two account seals matched the numbers recorded before cross-brief release. The cabinet's lock and access records showed no opening during the overnight interval. The witnesses' accounts agreed with the recorded movement. No evidence examined supported a claim that the professors altered the cadets' first accounts after the seminar.

But the receiving handover was incompletely documented. The review could not certify an uninterrupted named-person custody chain for the overnight transfer. It could certify the narrower seal and access facts. The defect belonged in the finding, not in a footnote hidden from the challenger.

The observer read the conclusion twice.

"So the Academy's claim of perfect custody was wrong."

"The Academy's records support a limited custody finding," the officer said. "If someone described that as perfect, the description was wrong."

"Will the cadets' accounts be excluded?"

"From what? The seminar was not graded, and there is no pending fleet performance assessment using those accounts as independent proof. They remain protected instructional records. Their evidentiary limitations must accompany any later authorized use."

"Will the Academy amend its process?"

"I have referred the missing handover to the records authority. I do not command its budget."

The observer accepted the certified finding and filed notice that House Caldrake reserved its existing rights. He had obtained a genuine adverse procedural point. He had not obtained the private debriefs, a new duel petition or proof of falsification.

Vane escorted him out without offering congratulations or a warning. Both would have been inappropriate.

---

The Headmaster received the finding before the morning's instructional review.

He had Prite, Darcelle and Renault present. The records custodian attended for the first ten minutes. The examining officer attended for the first twenty. Neither was asked to remain while the professors discussed their syllabus.

"We will require a named receiving signature for sealed assessment transfers," the Headmaster said.

The custodian looked at the existing form. "That requires a second person at the cabinet when the desk closes."

"Does it?"

"On some shifts. If the same clerk carries and receives, a second witness must countersign. Otherwise we're merely asking someone to certify their own transfer."

The Headmaster considered that. The records office was short two clerks during examination weeks. An additional witness could be assigned from the adjacent registry, but that office already carried the night inventory.

"Cost it," he said. "Until then, any transfer without a second person must be logged as a single-custodian movement, not described as independently witnessed."

The custodian wrote it down. No new clerk materialized from the order.

Prite looked at the review.

"Can we keep teaching with the slates?"

"Within their existing instructional purpose," the examining officer said. "You cannot advertise a chain of custody the records do not establish."

"I wasn't going to."

"You weren't the one who wrote the notice last time."

He accepted that.

The Headmaster authorized a correction to the seminar's custody summary. The correction would travel with the original finding, not replace it. The observer's objection would remain attached. The Academy's sealed cadet records would remain sealed.

After the others left, Renault said, "We have discovered that two signatures cost more than one."

"One of them costs an entire clerk," Darcelle replied.

Prite watched the Headmaster sign the limited order. He had expected to resent the Caldrake observer for finding fault. Instead he resented the part of himself that had wanted the review to produce a clean victory.

The missing name did not make the seminar worthless. Nor did the intact seals make the missing name appear.
