# The Long Patrol of Broussard — XIX: The Inland Bearing (working insert, part 2)

**Placement:** Immediately after part 1. Noncanonical working prose.

"The plate is taking the strain on one edge," the survey assistant said.

Sella inspected the seating. "Unload it."

The hauler crew did so without argument. They lifted the plate, corrected its bed and checked the angle again. Sella noticed that nobody suggested making the correction under tension. She considered this an improvement worth more than an apology.

The second proof rose through all three increments and held within the written limit. The assistant read the figures aloud. The survey chief checked them, repeated them and signed the new bearing slowly with his good hand.

"Thirty-one metres," he said. "On this alignment only."

The hauler chief held out his slate. "And now?"

"Now we inspect the cable guide and sign a new load ceiling," Sella said. "You have proved an anchor, not moved a ship."

The man looked upward for an authority less troublesome than the engineer.

The sky offered snow.

---

Holt received the convoy's accounting response while Broussard was inspecting the unloaded guide.

The quartermaster accepted the hauler's additional engine hours as a recovery expense, provided the next dispatch distinguished proof loading from actual ship movement. There was no new fuel issue. The replacement auger remained assigned to the scheduled supply vehicle, but no departure notice had arrived.

Kelm read the message twice.

"They've agreed the hauler worked and declined to pay for its next day's work."

"They've agreed to enter the hours," Holt said. "That isn't payment."

"Thank you, Holt. I was trying to remain angry at one person."

Holt kept the two proof attempts as separate entries. Combining them would have made the account shorter. It would also have concealed the first failed bearing and the correction that followed. Kelm saw the entries and did not ask him to change them.

Gabriel brought the infirmary's latest acknowledgment to the outer route. Etten remained under observation. The surgeon had made no new finding. Gabriel had already given the survey chief the exact wording, and the man had returned to his work rather than ask for certainty the message could not provide.

"Thirty-one metres certified," Gabriel said.

"One alignment certified," Broussard corrected.

"Will you move today?"

"The guide inspection isn't finished. Sella hasn't signed the load ceiling."

"Kelm won't enjoy that."

"He'll enjoy losing his refrigeration plant less."
