# Antegra Station

## Continuation X — The Debt

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The first thing to emerge from Substructure XVIII was a receipt.

It came up through the impossible corridor on a brass document trolley whose wheels squeaked with the stubborn regularity of machinery that had survived the extinction of its manufacturers and intended to complain about maintenance until the end of time.

Captain Ardan Krell raised his pistol.

Senior Auditor Selene Marr raised one hand.

"Wait."

"For what?"

"It may be properly filed."

Krell looked at her.

"I have spent the last six hours watching reality eat our census. I am going to shoot the trolley."

"Then I will have to record the destruction of evidence."

"You have just declared eight million people to be Talbor Varik."

"That was accounting. This is evidence."

Talbor Varik stepped between them before Imperial governance reached its traditional conclusion.

The trolley stopped.

Upon it rested a narrow case of black material.

No lock.

No hinge.

Only a shallow depression shaped exactly like a human hand.

His hand.

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse said, "Do not touch it."

Witness Lysa Nineteen whispered, "He already did."

Varik looked at the child.

"When?"

"Before."

"Before what?"

Lysa's blindfold was still stained with blood.

"Before you forgot."

Behind Varik's eyes there were no moons now.

Only a white city beneath a black sky, ashamed of something it had survived long enough to regret.

The Collector remained beyond Antegra, a silhouette made from absent stars.

Across the station, eight million citizens continued writing one another's names on walls and skin while every official system insisted they were one man.

Varik placed his palm into the depression.

The case opened.

Inside was a human skull.

Krell lowered his pistol by several degrees.

"That is disappointing."

Marr stared at him.

"You expected what?"

"Something less human."

Rhyse leaned closer without touching it.

The skull was old. Very old. Its surface had been polished almost smooth, but a small brass plate had been fixed to the brow.

Marr read the inscription.

TALBOR VARIK.

Nobody spoke.

Krell looked at Varik.

Then at the skull.

Then back at Varik.

"I have questions."

"Get in line."

Rhyse examined the plate.

"This is not Low Gothic engraving."

"It says my name."

"It says what our minds interpret as your name."

Solvik folded his arms.

"An important distinction."

Krell glanced at him.

"You have become very philosophical since drawing your pistol at a ledger."

"The ledger was behaving suspiciously."

The skull spoke.

Not aloud.

Every handwritten instance of TALBOR VARIK aboard Antegra changed at once.

I AM THE PORTION RETURNED.

Marr checked the official census.

Population: 2.

"Oh," she said.

Varik did not like the tone.

"What?"

"You have doubled."

---

Rhyse ordered the chamber sealed.

This was largely ceremonial. Antegra had spent most of the morning demonstrating that doors were philosophical suggestions.

The skull remained in its case.

Lysa would not approach it.

Varik would not look away.

The Collector transmitted again.

RETURN THE REST.

Rhyse said, "It believes the skull is part of what was stolen."

"It believes I am part of what was stolen," Varik replied.

Marr turned the census slate around.

Two legal Talbor Variks existed.

One had a pulse.

The other had seventeen outstanding ration chits and had somehow acquired responsibility for a dockside assault committed thirty-two years before Varik was born.

Marr frowned.

"The station is distributing your liabilities between you."

Krell said, "Can we assign all of them to the dead one?"

"Captain."

"I am trying to help."

Varik touched the brass plate on the skull.

Memory struck him.

Not the dead city's memory.

His.

Sixty-eight years earlier.

He stood in Substructure XVIII beside a younger Aveline Rhyse.

The vault was open.

The white city burned inside a machine too large to fit beneath Antegra.

Men and women screamed without making sound as memories passed between them like electrical current.

And in the centre of it all stood Talbor Varik.

Another Talbor Varik.

Older than he had been then.

Older than he was now.

The remembered Varik looked directly at him.

"Do not let it finish collecting us."

The memory broke.

Varik fell against the table.

Krell caught him again.

"I am developing concerns about your balance."

"There was another me."

Rhyse went still.

Varik saw it.

"You knew."

"I knew there was a second biological signature."

"Mine."

"Identical to yours."

"And you removed that from my memory."

"At your request."

Varik laughed once.

There was no humor in it.

"Of course. Apparently I spent my youth leaving administrative traps for my old age."

Marr said, "That does sound like you."

"Thank you, Auditor."

"It was not praise."

Rhyse finally spoke.

"The city did not steal an object."

Lysa whispered, "It stole people."

The Collector moved.

Every tactical display aboard Antegra showed it one degree closer without crossing the intervening space.

Rhyse looked toward the ceiling as if she could see it through kilometres of rock and armor.

"No. It stole copies."

---

The truth arrived in fragments.

The extinct city had discovered the Collector long before humanity reached the system.

It did not kill civilizations.

It reconciled them.

Every memory. Every identity. Every contradiction. Every divergent version of a person was gathered, compared and reduced to a single authoritative state.

Nothing was permitted to remain uncertain.

Nothing was permitted to be remembered differently.

The city had understood what that meant.

Perfect recollection was not immortality.

It was the death of possibility.

So the city had done the only thing that saved it.

It lied.

It copied itself imperfectly into biological minds, split histories into incompatible versions, and scattered mutually contradictory identities across generations.

The Collector could not reconcile what refused to agree with itself.

Humanity, arriving millennia later, had been ideal soil.

The Imperium maintained contradictory archives as naturally as lungs maintained respiration.

Antegra had become camouflage by accident.

Then Varik had opened the vault.

The city copied him.

Not once.

Thousands of times.

Each copy carried a slightly different version of the city-memory.

Most were destroyed during the original Severance action.

Some were hidden.

One had become the skull on the table.

And one had apparently walked away.

Krell said, "Where?"

Rhyse did not answer.

Varik looked at her.

"Where did he go?"

"We never found him."

"Sixty-eight years."

"Yes."

"An identical copy of me has been missing for sixty-eight years and nobody thought this merited a footnote?"

Marr cleared her throat.

"In fairness, your records contain several million footnotes."

Varik stared at her.

"Not now."

"Understood."

The skull's jaw moved.

This time sound emerged.

A dry scrape of bone.

Then Varik's own voice.

"He went to New Presidio."

Silence.

Rhyse closed her eyes.

Krell swore softly.

Varik looked at the skull.

"Why?"

"Because the Collector follows coherence."

"And New Presidio?"

The skull smiled without muscles.

"Was loud."

Marr said, "That is not an answer."

"It is the answer we had."

Varik felt cold.

New Presidio sat farther along the Cafarron Corridor, a place of broadcasts, traffic, bureaucracy, rumor and Imperial certainty shouted loudly enough to become indistinguishable from panic.

A perfect place to hide a contradiction.

A perfect place to spread one.

The Collector transmitted.

RETURN THE REST.

The skull answered before anyone else could.

"No."

Every light in Central Audit failed.

For three seconds the station existed only by emergency lumen and handwritten names.

Then the Collector removed the skull from history.

The case was empty.

Marr's census returned to one.

Krell stared at the table.

"What was there?"

Varik opened his mouth.

Nothing came.

He knew something had been there.

He knew it mattered.

He could no longer remember what it was.

Lysa began crying.

Rhyse seized a grease pencil and wrote across the wall before the knowledge disappeared completely.

NEW PRESIDIO.

Varik read the words.

Recognition returned like a knife pushed behind the eyes.

Not memory.

Direction.

The Collector had not recovered what it wanted.

It had recovered one receipt.

The rest of the debt was somewhere else.

Krell looked at the words on the wall.

"We are going there, aren't we?"

Varik picked up his father's saber.

"Eventually."

Marr looked toward the coherence displays.

"Eventually may be optimistic."

The station-wide mnemonic fragmentation had begun to fail again.

Population identities were reconnecting.

The dead city was returning.

The Collector was still outside.

And somewhere on New Presidio, if a message written by a thing that no longer existed could be trusted, another Talbor Varik had enjoyed sixty-eight years to become someone else.

Krell sighed.

"I preferred the tithe fraud."

Varik looked toward the impossible corridor.

"So did I."

Below them, Substructure XVIII printed a new document.

This one contained a destination.

And a date.

The date was tomorrow.
