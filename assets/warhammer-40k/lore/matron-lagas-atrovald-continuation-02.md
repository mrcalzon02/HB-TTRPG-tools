# Matron Lagas Atrovald

## Continuation II — The Third Witness

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The third witness arrived under guard.

This was not unusual.

The number of guards was.

Matron Lagas Atrovald counted twelve before the first one entered the Matronal chamber.

Then she counted the second twelve.

Then the third.

By forty-seven she stopped.

Arthus Pell did not.

"Fifty-three," he whispered.

Atrovald looked at him.

"You counted."

"I became concerned."

"At what number?"

"Nine."

"Sensible."

The first rank belonged to the Logos Historica Verita.

The second wore the dark robes of a Senatorum archival custody office Atrovald recognized but had never particularly liked.

The third wore no heraldry at all.

That was the rank she trusted least.

Between them moved a bier.

Not a casket.

Not technically.

A casket implied a body.

The object carried into her chamber was long enough for one, armoured enough for several, and surrounded by enough devotional machinery to suggest the distinction had been considered and rejected by committee.

Pell watched it pass.

"Is that the manuscript?"

Atrovald studied the suspension frame.

The object inside was hidden beneath layered black cloth, seal-chains, purity strips and a transparent field whose machine-spirit announced its displeasure in a constant low whine.

"Either that or Logos has developed an unusually formal way of returning furniture."

The lead Logos custodian stopped before her lectern.

No name was offered.

Good.

Names complicated depositions.

He presented a slate instead.

TRANSFER AUTHORITY: CONCORDANCE SEVEN-TAU.

OBJECT: PRE-UNIFICATION WITNESS.

TRANSCRIPTION: PROHIBITED.

AUTOMATED OPTICAL CAPTURE: PROHIBITED.

NOOSPHERIC RECORDING: PROHIBITED.

PSYCHOMETRIC CONTACT: PROHIBITED.

RUBBING, IMPRESSION, CASTING, TRACING, RECONSTRUCTION OR MEMORY-EXTRACTION: PROHIBITED.

Atrovald read the list twice.

"Reading?"

The custodian hesitated.

"Authorized."

"Comforting."

Pell leaned nearer.

"Touching?"

The custodian looked at him.

"Discouraged."

"That is not prohibited."

"No."

"Why?"

The custodian's expression suggested that Logos Historica Verita considered curiosity an occupational disease.

"Because the last person to touch the binding without gloves lost three fingerprints."

Pell looked at his hands.

Atrovald looked at the case.

"Chemically?"

"Administratively."

Silence.

Atrovald raised one eyebrow.

The custodian continued.

"The prints remained physically present. They ceased to match his identity records."

Pell put his hands behind his back.

"Discouraged," he agreed.

The bier settled onto the receiving table.

Forty or more guards adjusted position around it.

Atrovald regarded them.

"Are all of you staying?"

The lead custodian nodded.

"Physical chain-of-custody must remain continuous."

"You intend to stand in my chamber while I work."

"Yes."

"For how long?"

"Until concordance is complete."

Atrovald looked toward Pell.

"Cancel tomorrow."

"I already cancelled this afternoon."

"Expand your ambition."

The custodian did not smile.

Logos people often lacked practice.

Atrovald broke the first seal herself.

She did not enjoy it.

That was important.

People who enjoyed opening ancient sealed containers generally became examples in training manuals.

The first seal belonged to Logos.

The second belonged to the Senatorum custody office.

The third bore the personal cipher of a dead Lord Archivist.

The fourth bore no Imperial mark.

Atrovald stopped.

The wafer was dark red.

Not wax.

Not resin.

Something mineral, perhaps, fused around a cord that had once been pale.

A symbol had been pressed into it.

A circle.

Open at the bottom.

Inside it, three vertical lines.

Pell leaned over the table.

"Do we know it?"

"No."

"Should we?"

"That is a dangerous question."

The lead Logos custodian offered another slate.

"Seal predates confirmed provenance."

Atrovald looked up.

"You authenticated an object whose outer seal predates its provenance?"

"We authenticated the chain available to us."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

For the first time, she liked him slightly.

"Good."

She cut the cord.

Nothing happened.

Pell exhaled.

Atrovald looked at him.

"Did you expect lightning?"

"No."

"Daemons?"

"No."

"What, then?"

"I had not selected a preference."

"Wise."

They removed the black cloth.

The third witness was a book.

That was disappointing.

Atrovald had hoped for something stranger.

The universe, however, had learned that ordinary objects were more effective.

The binding was pale hide over thin metal boards.

No title.

No decoration.

No Imperial aquila.

No maker's stamp.

The surface had been repaired so many times that the original material survived only in islands between later stitches.

The spine was wrong.

Atrovald saw that before she touched it.

Not damaged.

Wrong.

Too broad for the number of leaves.

Pell saw it a moment later.

"There are pages missing."

"Probably."

"How many?"

"Do not count absences until we establish what is present."

Pell nodded.

He wrote nothing.

That was difficult for him.

Atrovald approved.

She donned gloves.

Not because the custody order demanded them.

Because she had become fond of her fingerprints.

The cover was cold.

The chill did not pass through the glove.

It passed around it.

One of the unmarked guards shifted.

Atrovald glanced at him.

"Problem?"

"No, Matron."

"You moved."

"Yes."

"Why?"

The guard's jaw tightened.

"Old habit."

"From what?"

He did not answer.

The Logos custodian answered for him.

"He has transported the witness before."

Atrovald looked back at the guard.

"How many times?"

The man swallowed.

"This is my first transfer."

Pell frowned.

"You just said—"

"I know what I said."

Atrovald kept her hand on the book.

"Do you remember another transfer?"

"No."

"Do you remember moving because of another transfer?"

The guard stared at the object.

"Yes."

The chamber became quieter.

Atrovald withdrew her hand.

"Record that."

The Logos custodian shook his head.

"Recording restrictions."

"Not the manuscript. The witness."

"The guard?"

"Yes."

The guard looked unhappy.

Atrovald understood.

Nobody enjoyed being reclassified as evidence.

She had never understood why.

It was one of the few reliable paths to institutional immortality.

Pell took a manual slate.

"Statement entered."

Atrovald opened the cover.

The first leaf carried no title.

Only a line of handwriting.

THE PRECIPE OF THOUGHT.

Pell leaned closer.

"Precipe."

"Yes."

"The later title is *The Precipice of Thought*."

"I am aware."

"Copyist error?"

"Perhaps."

Atrovald turned the page.

The next hand had added a correction.

PRECIPICE.

Below it, centuries later:

PRECEPT.

Then:

PRECIPICE.

Then:

PRECIPE.

Four titles occupying one line.

No erasures.

No single correction.

A conversation among dead custodians who had lacked the decency to settle the matter before dying.

Atrovald felt something very close to affection.

"Useful," she said.

Pell looked at her.

"How?"

"It disagrees immediately."

"That is useful?"

"It means Logos did not send us a tidy relic."

"You sound pleased."

"I am."

"Should I be worried?"

"Constantly."

The first folios were familiar.

Not identical.

Familiar.

The central argument survived: thought as a precipice between obedience and agency, civilization as a structure that required both memory and choice, authority as something that could preserve truth or merely outlive objections to it.

The version Predulum had called heretical was gentler.

That surprised Atrovald.

The pre-Unification witness was not more revolutionary.

It was less polite.

Where the Interlocutum Authorized Original described civil obedience as "the necessary geometry by which innumerable wills are aligned to common survival," the older witness used six words.

A cage can also preserve life.

Pell read it.

Then read it again.

"I see why Predulum wanted it destroyed."

"No."

Pell looked at her.

"You do not?"

"I see why he would want this sentence destroyed. That is not the same thing."

She turned the page.

The next sentence had been scored through with a blade.

Not inked out.

Cut.

The cut passed through the leaf in nine parallel lines.

Someone had wanted the words harmed.

Atrovald angled the folio under the lumen.

Pell moved instinctively toward a magnifier.

The Logos custodian raised one hand.

"No optical capture."

Pell stopped.

"I was going to magnify it."

"Prohibited."

"Magnification is not capture."

"It is on the secondary schedule."

"There is a secondary schedule?"

"There are nine."

Pell looked at Atrovald.

She did not help him.

Suffering was educational.

Atrovald examined the cut sentence by eye.

Enough ink survived between the scores.

She read aloud.

"'When the keeper determines which memory may survive, the keeper becomes author in all but confession.'"

Nobody in the chamber moved.

Forty or more guards became very interested in walls.

Pell looked at the Interlocutum seal on Atrovald's lectern.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

Atrovald turned the page.

"Matron."

"Yes."

"That sentence is absent from our Authorized Original."

"I know."

"Is it present in the Logos witness?"

"No."

"You remember?"

"Eleven disputed paragraphs begin at folio eighty-seven. This is folio twelve."

Pell looked ill.

"The divergence begins earlier."

"No."

"But—"

"This is not a divergence between the two later witnesses."

He understood.

Both later witnesses omitted the sentence.

Different custodians.

Different chains.

Same absence.

Atrovald looked at the cut marks.

An older removal.

A shared ancestor.

The sort of thing archivists dreamed about before waking in terror.

"Continue," she said.

The book grew worse.

Not because the prose became more heretical.

Because the history became more legible.

At folio nineteen, a margin note in archaic Low Gothic instructed a reader to "remove civic examples rendered obsolete by reunification."

At folio thirty-one, a different hand replaced a reference to elected ration syndics with "appointed keepers."

At folio forty-four, three leaves had been excised so carefully that only the stitch pattern revealed their absence.

At folio fifty-two, an Imperial-era custodian had inserted a devotional formula praising the Master of Mankind.

The ink was centuries younger than the page.

Pell stared at it.

"That cannot be original."

Atrovald did not look up.

"Do not use that word casually."

"Original?"

"Yes."

"I thought that was our entire purpose."

"Our purpose is to determine relationships between witnesses. 'Original' is what people say when they want the relationship to stop."

Pell considered this.

"That should be written over the entrance."

"It would attract historians."

"Right."

At folio sixty-eight, the book became physically thinner.

Atrovald closed it.

The Logos custodian stepped forward.

"Problem?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"The missing folios are not random."

Pell looked at the spine.

"You said not to count absences."

"Now we may."

They began.

Manually.

Page by page.

No machine.

No transcription.

Pell counted gatherings.

Atrovald counted cut stubs.

The Logos custodian counted their counting, which appeared to be his principal contribution.

Thirty-two leaves had been removed before any known Imperial custody mark.

Eleven more had been removed later.

Seven had been replaced.

Three replacement leaves had themselves been replaced.

One leaf was present twice with different wording.

Pell held both open side by side.

"Which one came first?"

Atrovald studied the inks.

"Unknown."

"Which one is correct?"

"Defective question."

Pell's mouth twitched.

"You have been spending too much time with Inquisitors."

"No. Inquisitors dislike defective questions. They prefer defective answers."

One of the guards laughed.

Only once.

The Logos custodian turned toward him.

The guard resumed being architectural.

Atrovald continued.

Folio eighty-seven arrived.

The known break.

The Interlocutum Authorized Original and the Logos witness had diverged here by eleven paragraphs.

The third witness contained neither version.

Pell did not speak for several seconds.

Then:

"Where are they?"

"Not here."

"Both later versions have text."

"Yes."

"Different text."

"Yes."

"But this one has nothing."

"Not nothing."

Atrovald pointed.

The folio carried a rectangular stain.

The shape of a pasted sheet removed long ago.

Beneath it, in faded pencil:

INSERT APPROVED CONTEXT AFTER SETTLEMENT.

No context followed.

No settlement was named.

Pell sat down.

He had not been invited.

Atrovald allowed it again.

This was becoming a bad habit.

"The eleven paragraphs were added later."

"At least two different sets of eleven paragraphs were."

"By whom?"

"Unknown."

"Why?"

"Unknown."

"When?"

"Different times."

Pell rubbed his face.

"Our Authorized Original contains a contextual insertion."

"Yes."

"Logos contains a different contextual insertion."

"Yes."

"Both claim descent from an earlier witness where the insertion point was blank."

"Apparently."

"So neither preserved the text."

Atrovald looked at him.

"Careful."

"What?"

"You are assuming the blank is earlier than every insertion."

"It is the oldest copy."

"Witness."

Pell exhaled.

"Oldest witness."

"Good."

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"No."

She turned the page.

Folio eighty-eight had been renumbered.

Originally ninety-four.

Pell saw it.

Then the Logos custodian.

Then several guards, because once an archival room realizes something is wrong, everyone becomes an expert.

Atrovald followed the renumbering backward.

Eighty-seven had once been ninety-three.

Eighty-six had once been ninety-two.

Six folios were missing immediately before the blank insertion point.

Not removed by later Imperial hands.

Removed before the current binding.

The cut edges had been folded into the spine and hidden beneath an ancient repair.

Pell whispered something devotional.

Atrovald frowned.

"Do not bring theology into pagination."

"I was seeking assistance."

"From whom?"

He thought about it.

"Fair."

They reached the final leaf shortly before midnight.

No colophon.

No author.

No date.

Only a custodial note.

The hand was old.

Older than the archaic Gothic annotations.

The language was not.

It had been translated directly onto the page by someone much later.

Atrovald recognized the translator's notation as early Imperial.

The original line beneath it was written in a script she did not know.

The translation read:

SIXTH SURVIVING WITNESS.

COMPARE AGAINST COPIES TWO, FOUR, AND SEVEN BEFORE CORRECTION.

Pell stopped breathing.

Atrovald looked at him.

"Again."

He inhaled.

"Thank you."

She reread the line.

Sixth surviving witness.

Not sixth copy.

Not sixth edition.

Witness.

The word choice might belong to the translator.

Or it might not.

Either way, it was unpleasant.

The Logos custodian spoke for the first time in nearly an hour.

"We did not see that."

Atrovald looked at him.

"You authenticated the object."

"Not its scholarship."

"Comforting."

"We were prohibited from transcription."

"So were we."

"Yes."

"Yet I found it."

The custodian accepted the rebuke with professional misery.

That improved her opinion of him.

Pell stood.

"Copies two, four and seven."

"Witnesses."

"Right."

"Do not let despair erode precision."

"Where are they?"

Atrovald looked at the book.

"Not here."

"Matron."

"That remains the most accurate answer."

Pell began pacing.

Atrovald allowed three circuits.

On the fourth she stopped him.

"You are making the guards nervous."

"There are fifty-three guards."

"Fifty-two."

Pell stopped.

He looked around.

Counted.

Fifty-two.

The Logos custodian counted too.

"Where is Custody Auxiliar Nine?"

Nobody answered.

Atrovald's chamber had one door.

It was sealed.

No alarms had sounded.

No guard had left.

Pell looked at the book.

Atrovald did not.

That would be melodramatic.

She looked at the custody slate.

Fifty-two names.

There had always been fifty-two.

She remembered Pell whispering fifty-three.

The memory remained.

The record did not.

The unmarked guard who had remembered transporting the book before stepped out of formation.

"Matron."

"Yes."

"There were fifty-three."

The Logos custodian checked his slate.

"No."

The guard's face hardened.

"There were."

"Name?"

The man opened his mouth.

Nothing came.

He tried again.

Atrovald watched the effort become fear.

"I don't know."

"Face?"

"I—"

His hands began shaking.

"No."

"Position?"

"Behind me."

The place behind him was occupied by a guard who had been there since arrival.

According to the slate.

According to the formation diagram.

According to every recorded handoff.

Pell whispered, "Administratively."

Atrovald looked at him.

"The fingerprints."

"The man who touched the binding."

Atrovald looked down at her gloves.

Still hers.

Probably.

She closed the book.

"Seal it."

The Logos custodian moved immediately.

For the first time, Atrovald approved of urgency.

Black cloth returned.

Seal-chain.

Field.

Custody prayers.

The old mineral wafer could not be restored, so the open circle with three lines remained unsealed beneath the newer layers.

Atrovald disliked that.

She disliked it more when the lead custodian's slate refreshed.

TRANSFER PARTY: FIFTY-TWO.

No discrepancy.

No missing name.

No alert.

Pell checked his manual notes.

He had written:

FIFTY-THREE GUARDS PRESENT.

The ink remained.

Atrovald took the slate.

"Do not correct this."

"I wasn't going to."

"Good."

"Why?"

"Because correction appears to be where people disappear."

Nobody laughed.

That was appropriate.

The Matronal chamber lights dimmed.

Not failure.

Power conservation.

The hive above them shifted into its false night, billions of lives continuing under layers of stone, smoke, law, hunger and records that assured distant authorities the population remained both productive and approximately countable.

Atrovald looked at the two later witnesses waiting in sealed cases.

The Interlocutum Authorized Original.

The Logos witness.

And the older volume on the bier.

Three texts.

Three claims.

One missing guard.

She felt tired enough to become philosophical.

This was dangerous.

"Open a concordance docket."

Pell moved to his lectern.

"Seven-Tau?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Seven-Tau compares two claimed authorities. We now have three witnesses and evidence of at least seven."

Pell hesitated.

"What classification?"

Atrovald thought.

The Interlocutum had classifications for forged texts.

Corrupted texts.

Dangerous texts.

Chronologically displaced texts.

Texts of uncertain authorship.

Texts whose authors had been declared heretics after publication, before publication, or posthumously for administrative convenience.

It had no classification for a book that appeared to remove custodians who became too certain about it.

That omission felt irresponsible.

"Open an S-Rank provenance fracture."

Pell went pale.

"The last one was—"

"I know."

"It led to three departmental dissolutions."

"I know."

"Two executions."

"I know."

"And the discovery that an entire sub-archive had been cataloguing the wrong century."

"That was embarrassing."

"Matron."

"Open it."

He did.

The Interlocutum did not alarm.

It inhaled.

That was how Atrovald thought of it.

Across the hidden archive, access permissions changed.

Doors sealed.

Vault servitors woke.

Archivists received orders to remain at stations they had already spent most of their lives occupying.

Deep below, data stacks isolated themselves from the general noosphere.

A thousand clerks lost access to files they had never known existed.

Several would spend the morning submitting complaints.

Atrovald found this reassuring.

The machinery of secrecy remained healthy.

Pell waited.

"Scope?"

Atrovald dictated.

"All witnesses, derivatives, quotations, paraphrases, doctrinal condemnations, destruction orders and contextual harmonizations associated with *The Precipice of Thought*, including title variants."

"All?"

"Yes."

"Matron, that could be millions of references."

"Then begin with the ones we can reach before the Imperium ends."

"Priority?"

She looked at the sealed third witness.

"Find copies two, four and seven."

Pell wrote.

"Surviving?"

"Do not assume."

He struck the word.

"Find references to copies two, four and seven."

"Better."

The Logos custodian cleared his throat.

"Logos will require joint authority over the provenance search."

Atrovald turned.

"No."

His face tightened.

"The third witness is in our custody."

"It is in joint concordance."

"Our mandate comes from the Lord Regent."

"Mine comes from nine thousand years of people being afraid to throw anything away."

"That is not equivalent authority."

"No."

Atrovald looked around the chamber.

"It is older."

The guards became very still.

Pell found something fascinating in his slate.

The custodian measured the distance between argument and jurisdictional suicide.

He chose survival.

"Joint consultation, then."

"Accepted."

He bowed his head by perhaps three millimetres.

A triumph.

A small one.

Imperial institutions survived on small triumphs because large ones usually required artillery.

Pell's lectern chimed.

Search response.

Too fast.

Atrovald's stomach tightened.

"Already?"

Pell read.

His face changed.

"Reference to Witness Four."

"Where?"

He did not answer.

Atrovald stood.

"Pell."

He turned the slate toward her.

INTERLOCUTUM HOLDING.

SUB-ARCHIVE: NULL-SEVEN.

ACCESS: S-RANK.

DEPOSIT DATE: CLASSIFICATION CONFLICT.

STATUS: PRESENT.

Atrovald read it again.

Null-Seven was inside the Interlocutum.

Not metaphorically.

Not historically.

Inside.

Four levels below the Matronal chamber.

She knew the vault.

She had inspected it six years ago.

It contained sealed astronomic atlases, two pre-Compliance civic registries, a falsified saintly genealogy, and a crate of navigational prayer wheels nobody had successfully dated.

No book.

No Witness Four.

Pell looked at her.

"You inspected Null-Seven."

"Yes."

"Was it there?"

"No."

"Could you have missed it?"

Atrovald stared at him.

He had the grace to look ashamed.

"No," he said. "Of course not."

The Logos custodian stepped closer.

"When was the holding added?"

Pell opened the metadata.

Then stopped.

"Matron."

"What?"

"The accession bears your authority."

The chamber became very quiet.

Atrovald took the slate.

ACCESSION AUTHORITY: MATRON LAGAS ATROVALD.

SEAL VALIDATION: CONFIRMED.

She had not signed it.

The seal was hers.

The authorization cipher was hers.

The biometric hash was hers.

The timestamp was tomorrow.

Pell made a small sound.

"No."

Atrovald looked at him.

He pointed.

"Tomorrow."

"Yes."

"That is impossible."

"That word has been having a difficult evening."

The Logos custodian looked from the slate to the sealed third witness.

"Chronometric contamination?"

Atrovald considered Lord Inquisitor Predulum.

Ordo Signora Temportum.

Repeatedly drawn back to the Interlocutum.

Repeatedly sanitized.

Always rediscovering the same trail.

He had left that morning demanding destruction of the manuscript.

She suddenly disliked the shape of his persistence.

Not because he was wrong.

Because perhaps he had been right at the wrong time.

"Pell."

"Matron?"

"Where is Predulum?"

"He departed."

"I know he departed. Where is he now?"

Pell checked.

No record.

He checked another channel.

Then another.

"His transport never cleared the spire."

Atrovald felt the first true fear of the night.

"Location?"

"Unknown."

The lead Logos custodian touched his comm-bead.

"Seal external transit."

Atrovald looked at him.

"Now you may be urgent."

The order went out.

Too late.

An alarm sounded deep beneath the chamber.

One tone.

Long.

Then two short.

Pell went white.

"Null-Seven."

Atrovald was already moving.

The guards moved with her.

Fifty-two.

Pell counted anyway.

They descended through the Interlocutum.

Past ranks most Imperial servants did not know existed.

Past vaults where history slept under stasis, prayer and armed suspicion.

Past doors that opened only after recognizing Atrovald's face, blood, code, gait and one private phrase she had always considered insultingly sentimental.

At Null-Seven, the door stood open.

No breach marks.

No override.

No damage.

Her authorization filled the access log.

Timestamp: tomorrow.

Inside, the vault had changed.

The atlases remained.

The civic registries remained.

The false genealogy remained.

The prayer wheels remained in their crate.

And in the center of the chamber stood a reading desk.

Atrovald had never seen it before.

On the desk lay a book.

Pale hide.

Metal boards.

No title.

Beside it stood Lord Inquisitor Predulum Crystallumtra von Termuth IV.

He was not wearing his hat.

This made him look ill.

More importantly, he was holding a page.

Pell whispered, "Oh, no."

Predulum looked up.

His face was grey.

"Matron."

Atrovald stopped three metres away.

"Put it down."

He looked at the page in his hand.

Then at the book.

"I did."

"No."

Predulum frowned.

"I did."

Atrovald looked at the desk.

A page lay there.

The same page.

Predulum was holding it.

Both were true.

The Logos custodian drew a sidearm.

Atrovald raised one hand.

"Do not."

Predulum smiled.

It was an awful smile.

Not mad.

Relieved.

"I remember now."

Atrovald wished he had said almost anything else.

"What?"

"Why you keep erasing me."

Pell's eyes widened.

Atrovald's voice remained level.

"We do not erase you."

"Sanitize. Excise. Reconstruct. Such lovely words."

"Predulum."

He held up the page.

"There are seven."

Atrovald looked at the book.

"Seven witnesses?"

"No."

His smile vanished.

"Seven attempts."

The chamber felt colder.

The Logos custodian spoke.

"Attempts at what?"

Predulum looked at him as though noticing Logos for the first time.

"At making one original."

Nobody moved.

The page on the desk turned itself.

Predulum's held page did not.

Two versions.

Atrovald stepped closer.

"Who made the attempts?"

Predulum laughed once.

"I don't know."

"Who ordered them?"

"I don't know."

"Who are you quoting?"

He looked down at the page.

"Me."

Pell whispered something.

Atrovald ignored it.

Predulum continued.

"I wrote this tomorrow."

Atrovald closed her eyes for one second.

Only one.

Then opened them.

"Of course you did."

The Inquisitor looked almost apologetic.

"I thought you would say that."

"Did I?"

"Tomorrow."

Behind them, somewhere four levels above, the sealed third witness began ringing.

Not a bell.

A vibration through the Interlocutum's stone.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

The book on the desk answered.

The two copies of Predulum's page began to blacken from the edges.

Atrovald moved.

She seized the one on the desk.

Predulum seized the one in his hand.

The text remained long enough to read one line.

DO NOT RECONCILE THE WITNESSES.

Then both pages became blank.

Predulum stared.

Pell stared.

The Logos custodian lowered his pistol.

Atrovald looked at the pale book in Null-Seven.

Witness Four.

Present.

Accessioned tomorrow.

Under her authority.

She had spent her life teaching that truth required custody.

Now custody itself had become part of the contamination.

She felt, unexpectedly, angry.

Good.

Fear was useful.

Anger was cheaper.

"Seal Null-Seven."

Predulum stepped forward.

"Matron—"

"Inside."

He stopped.

"You are sealing me in?"

"Temporarily."

"On what authority?"

Atrovald looked at the accession record bearing tomorrow's version of her seal.

"Apparently mine."

Pell made a strangled noise that might have been laughter.

Predulum did not.

"That is not amusing."

"No," Atrovald said. "But it is documented."

She backed out of the vault.

The Logos guards withdrew.

Pell followed.

Predulum remained beside Witness Four.

Atrovald paused at the threshold.

"Do not read further."

Predulum looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because every institution involved has spent millennia trying to decide which version of this text should be allowed to exist."

"And?"

Atrovald looked at the duplicate page that had gone blank in her hand.

"And I am beginning to suspect the text has opinions."

The vault door closed.

Locks engaged.

Not tomorrow's.

Hers.

Now.

Pell stood beside her in the corridor.

"Matron."

"Yes."

"Predulum is going to object."

"Of course."

"He is a Lord Inquisitor."

"I have noticed."

"He may order the door opened."

"Then we will ask which version of him is giving the order."

Pell looked at the sealed door.

"That seems dangerous."

"Everything tonight has been dangerous."

Atrovald turned back toward the lift.

"The difference is that this is precise."

Behind the vault door, Predulum began hammering.

Three knocks.

Then one more.

Fourth Order.

Pell looked at Atrovald.

She kept walking.

"Are we going to answer?"

"Eventually."

"When?"

Atrovald looked at the manual note in his hand.

Fifty-three guards.

The only surviving record of someone the custody slate insisted had never been present.

Then at the blank page she had taken from Null-Seven.

Then upward, toward the third witness waiting in her chamber.

"After we find out who disappears when we do."

The lift doors closed.

Deep beneath Spire Methelium Beta, the Interlocutum sealed itself around four competing versions of a book that should have had one.

Across the spire-way, Logos Historica Verita began filing emergency concordance notices.

Somewhere inside Null-Seven, a Lord Inquisitor remembered tomorrow.

And in the Matronal chamber, beneath fifty-two recorded guards and one unrecorded absence, the third witness lay under seal.

Its spine was too broad.

There were still pages missing.

One of them, Atrovald now knew, had not been removed yet.
