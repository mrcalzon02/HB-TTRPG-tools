# Antegra Station

## Continuation XIV — The Pending Case

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The Collector did not understand delay.

This was not immediately obvious, because the Collector did not possess a face with which to look confused, nor a mouth with which to object, nor any of the lesser organs by which humanity had spent several thousand years perfecting the expression of administrative despair.

It expressed confusion by changing the local laws of causality.

For eleven seconds every clock on Antegra Station displayed a different time.

Then every clock displayed PENDING.

Senior Auditor Selene Marr regarded the nearest chronometer with professional satisfaction.

"It has accepted jurisdiction."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse stared at her. "You cannot possibly know that."

"It changed the status field."

"It changed time."

"Yes. Into a status field."

Captain Ardan Krell lowered his pistol by perhaps half an inch. "Is that good?"

Marr considered the question.

"It is procedural."

"That was not what I asked."

"It is the best answer currently available."

Across Dock Fourteen, two Talbor Variks stood facing one another.

One was sixty-eight years older than the other.

Both looked exhausted.

Neither looked surprised.

That, Krell thought, was probably the most convincing evidence that they were the same man.

---

At 04:19—or what the station continued to call 04:19 despite the objections of several hundred clocks—the first appeal arrived.

It appeared simultaneously on every functioning terminal.

IDENTITY RESOLUTION REQUEST.

SUBJECT: TALBOR VARIK.

STATUS: DEFERRED.

GROUNDS FOR APPEAL: SELF-OWNERSHIP.

Marr read the final line twice.

"Interesting."

Old Varik leaned against the bulkhead. "It wants its name back."

"It says it owns itself."

"Wouldn't you?"

Marr looked toward the younger Varik.

"Your file contains three mutually exclusive declarations of legal personhood, one death certificate, two reinstatements and a disciplinary memorandum asserting that you are technically station infrastructure."

Varik sighed. "That memorandum was rescinded."

"Not correctly."

"Of course not."

Marr turned back to the appeal.

"Self-ownership is not sufficient. The disputed property has been incorporated into Imperial subjects."

Rhyse's expression hardened. "Alien property."

"Possibly."

"Definitely."

"Then the Imperium has a claim by conquest."

Rhyse stared at her.

Marr raised one eyebrow.

"Legate, if you wish to explain to the Administratum that possession does not establish Imperial title, I will gladly stand several systems away while you do so."

For the first time since arriving on Antegra, Rhyse almost smiled.

Almost.

Then every lumen dimmed.

A second line appeared.

COUNTERCLAIM: PRIOR TITLE.

Old Varik whispered, "It is learning."

---

The Collector's next argument was physical.

Deck Twenty-Seven ceased to exist for four seconds.

Not exploded. Not depressurized. Not translated through the warp.

It simply stopped being a location anyone could consistently describe.

Six thousand nine hundred and twelve people occupied Deck Twenty-Seven at the time.

When it returned, six thousand nine hundred and eleven remained.

The missing citizen was a sanitation clerk named Edrin Vale.

His wife remembered him.

His supervisor did not.

His ration account existed.

His birth record did not.

His boots remained beneath his bunk.

His bunk had never been assigned.

The Collector had filed evidence.

Krell read the casualty report in silence.

"It can do this to everyone?"

Old Varik nodded.

"Eventually."

"Then why hasn't it?"

"Because it is trying to be correct."

Marr looked up sharply.

Old Varik tapped the appeal screen.

"That is what the city learned. The Collector does not destroy contradiction because it hates contradiction. It destroys contradiction because contradiction prevents certainty. It cannot reconcile what it cannot define. Once it defines something, it makes the definition true."

Magos Hester Vhal's mechadendrites tightened around the edge of a console.

"Ontological enforcement."

"Call it what you like."

"I prefer accurate terminology."

"So does it. That is the problem."

Witness Lysa Nineteen had been silent since the two Variks met.

Now the blindfolded child spoke.

"It is reading the forms."

Everyone looked at her.

"Which forms?" Rhyse asked.

Lysa pointed upward.

Antegra possessed approximately one hundred and forty million active administrative documents.

It possessed considerably more inactive ones.

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Then Marr said, "Good."

Krell turned on her. "Good?"

"Captain, have you ever attempted to read all of Antegra's forms?"

"No."

"Exactly."

---

At 04:31, Antegra Station formally opened Case 7-OMNIA-NULL.

Marr insisted on the designation because the previous number assigned by the cogitator belonged to a dispute over sewage reclamation tariffs.

Krell suggested that the present emergency was more important.

Marr replied that this was precisely why it should not inherit sewage precedent.

The case acquired eight million interested parties.

Then seventeen million.

Then sixty-four million, because several departments registered dead citizens, unborn dependents, machine-servitors, dissolved guilds and three statues as potential stakeholders.

Rhyse demanded to know why the statues had standing.

Marr explained that one had received a tax assessment in M41.873 and had been paying it ever since.

Nobody volunteered to investigate further.

The Collector attempted to consolidate duplicate claimants.

Antegra objected.

The Collector attempted to define TALBOR VARIK as a singular identity.

Antegra produced two living claimants, eleven incompatible death records, thirty-four archived signatures, a disputed childhood baptismal certificate, and one maintenance requisition describing him as a load-bearing obstruction.

The Collector paused.

Old Varik laughed.

It was not a pleasant laugh.

"Sixty-eight years," he said. "I kept New Presidio alive by making myself impossible to finish."

Young Varik looked at him.

"And now?"

"Now you have eight million assistants."

---

The first real attack came at 04:44.

Every citizen on Antegra remembered the same childhood.

It lasted less than a second.

A red sky.

Seven moons.

A narrow street of black stone.

A hand holding theirs.

Then fire at the horizon.

Eight million people staggered simultaneously.

Hospitals overflowed within minutes. Transit stopped. Manufactoria alarms began screaming. Three dock cranes collided because their operators had remembered being children on a world that had died before humanity knew it existed.

And beneath the borrowed memory was a word.

Not spoken.

Not heard.

Known.

The Collector's missing self-reference.

Vhal severed her own auditory implants before realizing hearing was irrelevant.

Rhyse drew her pistol on Lysa, then on Old Varik, then lowered it because there was no useful target for a weapon designed to make holes.

Young Varik gripped the edge of the command table.

He almost understood the word.

That was worse than understanding it.

The dead city inside Antegra recoiled.

Old Varik shouted, "Do not complete it!"

"How?" Krell demanded.

"Think of something else!"

Krell stared at him.

"That is your plan?"

"It worked for sixty-eight years!"

Marr slammed both palms onto the audit console.

"No. We make them disagree."

She opened the station emergency channel.

"All departments. Immediate corrective action. Effective now, all personnel are ordered to dispute one fact about themselves. Any fact. Name, age, birthplace, rank, assignment, marital status, shoe size. Submit contradictory declarations through any available channel. Handwritten filings are acceptable. Illegible filings are preferred."

Krell stared at her.

"Shoe size?"

"Captain, this is not the moment to become doctrinaire."

Across Antegra, eight million frightened people began lying about themselves.

The shared childhood fractured.

One citizen remembered seven moons.

Another insisted there had been six.

A third filed a sworn declaration that moons were an ecclesiastical metaphor and therefore outside the jurisdiction of planetary survey.

A hab-worker claimed he had been born ninety years after his own birth.

A dockhand declared herself legally deceased but available for overtime.

Someone in Hydroponics registered as a municipal fountain.

The Collector tried to reconcile them.

The word broke apart again.

---

At 05:03, Case 7-OMNIA-NULL exceeded the capacity of Antegra's primary cogitator banks.

Vhal redirected archival load into obsolete payroll engines.

Then into transit control.

Then into devotional accounting.

At 05:07, the station's shrine network began processing identity appeals.

At 05:09, a machine-spirit responsible for incense allocation issued a temporary injunction against ontological consolidation.

Rhyse read the notice.

"This is absurd."

Vhal's optical cluster rotated toward her.

"The injunction is valid."

"According to whom?"

"The incense allocator."

Rhyse closed her eyes.

"The Emperor protects."

Marr glanced at the screen.

"At present, apparently through procurement."

Then the Collector answered.

Not with text.

With silence.

Every terminal went blank.

Every cogitator stopped.

Every servo-skull dropped from the air.

For three seconds Antegra possessed no central records at all.

The station became eight million people who knew one another imperfectly.

The Collector reached for the absence.

And found nothing coherent enough to seize.

The lights returned.

The terminals rebooted.

One message waited on every screen.

REQUEST FOR FINAL ADJUDICATION.

Marr frowned.

"It wants a judge."

Old Varik went pale.

"No."

Young Varik turned to him.

"What?"

"That is how it wins."

The dead city stirred inside them.

A memory surfaced: black towers, seven moons, a chamber filled with people arguing while something patient waited beyond the walls.

They had delayed it.

Contradicted it.

Confused it.

Then, eventually, someone had agreed to decide.

The Collector had not conquered the city.

The city had appointed an authority competent to define the truth.

And the moment that authority ruled, the Collector made the ruling absolute.

Rhyse understood.

"No judge."

Marr nodded.

"No final ruling."

Krell looked at the message.

"Can we refuse?"

Marr's expression became grim.

"A refusal is a decision."

The two Variks looked at one another.

For the first time, neither had an answer.

Then Lysa Nineteen raised her hand.

"I know someone who can decide without deciding."

Rhyse turned slowly toward her.

"Who?"

The child pointed toward the station's astropathic vault.

"Someone who isn't here."

At that moment, every astropath on Antegra began screaming the same coordinates.

Not New Presidio.

Not the seven-moon city.

A location elsewhere in the Cafarron Corridor.

A location Antegra's current charts marked with three mutually contradictory names.

The Collector immediately attempted to correct them.

Marr saw the correction request appear.

Then she smiled without humor.

"Oh," she said. "It has found another jurisdiction."

And for the first time since the Collector arrived, something beyond Antegra filed an objection.
