# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXV — The Recalled Witness

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The dock opened without admitting that it had opened.

This was, Selene Marr reflected, already more honest than most Imperial ports.

Beyond Antegra's observation blister, the nearest perimeter vessel had extended a narrow structure into the dark. Auspex called it a docking arm. Visual magnification called it a corridor. Magos Hester Vhal's instruments alternated between describing it as twelve metres long, nine kilometres long, and absent.

The brass hearing automaton stamped.

WITNESS RECALLED.

Old Talbor Varik stared at the stamp.

"I heard you the first time."

STAMP.

COMPLIANCE OUTSTANDING.

Young Varik looked almost sympathetic.

"Apparently contempt of court survives temporal duplication."

"Good to know," Old Varik said.

Captain Ardan Krell sealed the observation shutters.

"Nobody boards an unidentified vessel without an escort."

Marr lifted the evidence sleeve containing the forwarding order.

"It is not an unidentified vessel."

Krell looked at her.

"You have spent the last hour forbidding us to identify it."

"Correct. It is presently a venue."

"That is worse."

"Legally, much better."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse checked the charge on her pistol.

"I am going."

Marr shook her head.

"The order recalls a witness. Singular."

"Then amend it."

"I could. I won't."

Rhyse's eyes narrowed.

"Why?"

Marr looked at Old Varik.

"Because someone has been holding his place for seventy-one years. I would like to know whether they recognize the correct Talbor Varik before we helpfully provide alternatives."

Young Varik stopped smiling.

Old Varik sighed.

"You make friendship exhausting."

"I am your counsel. Friendship would be a conflict of interest."

The automaton stamped.

CORRECT.

Marr pointed at it.

"Nobody asked you."

The transit tube attached to Dock Fourteen at 07:29 station time.

No impact registered.

No pressure seal engaged.

No docking clamps fired.

Nevertheless, the outer hatch indicator changed from VACUUM to VENUE.

Krell read it twice.

"I dislike that word more every hour."

"Exposure improves comprehension," Vhal said.

"Exposure usually improves immunity."

"Not to law."

The hatch opened.

Beyond it waited a corridor built in an Imperial style that had gone out of use centuries before Antegra's construction. Ribbed iron. Riveted plates. Brass conduit covers. Niches for devotional candles instead of lumen strips. The deck bore the double-headed aquila, but its proportions were wrong in the subtle way of an old coin.

Dust lay along the edges.

Human dust.

The kind made by boots, cloth, skin and decades.

Old Varik stepped to the threshold.

He stopped.

Marr saw his hands shake.

"You remember it."

"No."

The automaton stamped behind him.

TESTIMONY INCONSISTENT.

Varik turned.

"I remember not remembering it."

The automaton considered this.

STAMP.

ACCEPTED PROVISIONALLY.

"I hate this station," Varik muttered.

"Which one?" Marr asked.

He gave her a look and crossed the threshold.

The hatch shut behind him.

For Antegra, eight seconds passed.

For Talbor Varik, according to the chronometer implanted in his left wrist, four hours and nineteen minutes passed.

When the hatch reopened he was standing exactly where he had disappeared.

His coat was wet.

There had been no water in the corridor.

He carried a paper docket.

There had been no paper in his hands when he left.

And there was blood beneath his fingernails.

There had been no blood on him either.

Rhyse moved first.

"Talbor?"

Old Varik looked at her.

Then at Marr.

Then at Young Varik.

"Close the hatch."

Krell did.

"What happened?"

"Close the inner hatch too."

Krell did that as well.

Varik waited until both indicators showed SEALED.

Then he handed Marr the docket.

The first page was headed:

FIRST HEARING — PETITIONER PRESENT.

The date field contained three dates.

One was seventy-one years ago.

One was nine hundred and eleven years ago.

One was tomorrow.

Marr did not comment on the last one.

She had learned restraint.

It had been an unpleasant morning.

"Who questioned you?"

Varik sat down on the deck.

"Nobody."

The automaton stamped.

TESTIMONY INCONSISTENT.

Varik glared at it.

"Nobody questioned me because the hearing already had my testimony."

Silence.

Marr crouched beside him.

"From seventy-one years ago?"

"From today."

Young Varik stepped closer.

"You were gone eight seconds."

"I was gone four hours."

"That isn't what she asked."

Old Varik looked at his younger self.

"I know."

Marr opened the docket.

Pages of testimony followed.

Talbor Varik described arriving at a place whose name had been struck from every copy. He described three contradictory coordinates. He described vessels waiting around an empty region of space. He described Antegra arriving there by forwarding order.

He described Marr refusing to name the place.

The testimony was verbatim.

Including statements Varik had made less than an hour ago.

Including statements he had not made yet.

Marr stopped reading.

"Did you say these things?"

"Some."

"Which?"

"The ones I remember."

"And the others?"

"I remember remembering them."

Krell rubbed both eyes.

"I would like one problem aboard this station that can be shot."

Rhyse glanced toward the sealed hatch.

"There may still be time."

Vhal took the docket and examined its fibres.

"Paper composition consistent with Imperial archival stock. Age indeterminate. Ink age ranges from fresh to approximately nine centuries."

"On the same page?" Marr asked.

"On the same letters."

"Useful."

"You use that word incorrectly."

"Frequently."

The printer in Dock Fourteen began operating.

A page emerged.

FROM: ORIGIN

WITNESS RETURNED INCORRECTLY.

Varik stood.

"No."

A second page.

PLEASE RETURN WITNESS.

"Absolutely not," Varik said.

A third page.

POSTAGE DUE.

Marr stared at it.

"There it is."

Rhyse looked at her.

"What?"

"The true horror."

The station klaxon sounded.

Not the attack alarm.

Not decompression.

Not warp breach.

Accounts receivable.

Across Antegra Station, eight million citizens discovered that their ration accounts had been assessed a fractional transit surcharge.

Krell's vox erupted with reports.

"They billed the station?"

Marr checked the forwarding order.

A new line had appeared beneath DESTINATION DISPUTED.

CARRIAGE: UNPAID.

"Apparently."

"For what?"

"Varik."

Old Varik looked offended.

"I walked."

"The Imperium has never considered that relevant to transport fees."

The automaton stamped.

COSTS RESERVED.

Varik swore at it.

Marr kept reading.

The docket's final pages were different.

No testimony.

No questions.

A seating plan.

The first hearing had possessed seven positions.

PETITIONER.

COUNSEL.

WITNESS.

ADJUDICATOR.

RESPONDENT.

RESPONDENT PRIOR.

And one position whose title had been blacked out so heavily the paper had become brittle.

Beside that final position someone had written:

ABSENT. PLACE HELD.

Marr looked toward the sealed hatch.

"Talbor. When you crossed over, who was waiting?"

Varik did not answer.

"This matters."

"Three people."

"The adjudicators?"

"No."

"Then who?"

Varik swallowed.

"Me."

Young Varik went still.

Old Varik continued.

"Three of me."

Marr said nothing.

"One was younger than him." Varik pointed at Young Varik. "One was older than me. One was dead."

Krell's hand moved unconsciously toward his sidearm.

"Dead?"

"He had been dead a long time. He was still seated."

"And they spoke?"

"The oldest one did."

Marr waited.

Varik looked at her.

"He said we were late."

The lights dimmed.

Beyond the shutters, something struck the perimeter.

Not Antegra.

Not yet.

Vhal opened the observation shield by two centimetres.

The stars outside were still wrong.

The defending vessels still occupied mutually exclusive positions.

The black edge of the dead-letter mechanism pressed against them.

But another light now burned beyond the perimeter.

Small.

Steady.

Imperial red.

Krell examined it through magnification.

"Beacon."

"Identification?" Rhyse asked.

"None."

Marr looked at Varik.

He had gone white.

"You know it."

"I know the pattern."

"Do not give me the name."

"I wasn't going to."

That frightened Marr more than if he had argued.

The beacon transmitted.

Vhal isolated the signal.

It was not addressed to Antegra.

It was addressed to Talbor Varik.

The message consisted of one line.

WITNESS, YOUR TESTIMONY REMAINS INCOMPLETE.

The brass automaton rolled forward.

STAMP.

WITNESS RECALLED.

Varik stared at it.

"Again?"

STAMP.

YES.

Marr took the docket from Vhal.

"No."

Everyone looked at her.

The automaton raised its stamp.

Marr raised one finger.

"The witness has returned with documentary evidence establishing that the recalling venue already possesses his testimony, including testimony not yet given. Further recall is therefore duplicative, potentially contaminating, and administratively wasteful."

The stamp hovered.

Marr continued.

"More importantly, the venue has invoiced Antegra for carriage without establishing tariff authority, contractual consent, point of origin, point of destination, distance travelled, or whether the witness was actually transported."

Rhyse slowly smiled.

Krell whispered, "Emperor preserve us."

"No," Marr said. "Accounts will do."

The automaton stamped.

RECALL STAYED PENDING COST REVIEW.

Old Varik laughed.

It became coughing.

Then laughter again.

Outside, the beacon flashed once.

The dock withdrew three metres.

Not closed.

Offended.

Marr turned to the final page of the docket.

There was handwriting beneath ABSENT. PLACE HELD.

Fresh handwriting.

Her handwriting.

She had not written it.

NOT ABSENT.

MISDELIVERED.

Below that, a routing mark appeared.

The same return-to-origin mark they had found inside the Collector.

Vhal's lenses focused.

"New ink."

"I can see that."

"No. New now. It is still forming."

They watched another sentence write itself.

DO NOT LET THE COLLECTOR TAKE THE EMPTY SEAT.

The station shuddered.

For the first time since Antegra had been forwarded, the Collector appeared on auspex.

Not outside the perimeter.

Inside it.

Between Antegra and the open dock.

Its return-to-origin marks burned across black glass like wounds.

The dead-letter darkness behind it stopped advancing.

Respondent Prior began hammering on the red maintenance door.

And the hearing automaton, which had spent the morning stamping with the confidence of a minor god, very carefully lowered its stamp without touching the paper.

Marr looked at the empty seventh place on the ancient seating plan.

"There," she said quietly. "That is why the first hearing never ended."

Rhyse drew her pistol.

"Because someone was missing?"

"No."

Marr watched the Collector move toward the dock.

"Because the wrong party has been trying to attend it for nine hundred and eleven years."
