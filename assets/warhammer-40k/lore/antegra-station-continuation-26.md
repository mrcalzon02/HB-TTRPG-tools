# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXVI — The Seventh Seat

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

Marr raised one finger.

The automaton stopped with its stamp three centimetres above the docket.

It was, Selene Marr thought, the first time she had ever silenced a court with a finger.

She intended to enjoy it for as long as circumstances permitted.

"Before the witness is recalled," she said, "the court will establish the capacity in which he is being recalled."

STAMP.

WITNESS.

"That is a label, not a capacity."

The stamp hovered.

Old Talbor Varik looked from Marr to the sealed hatch.

"Selene."

"Quiet. You are presently expensive."

"I think something is coming through the perimeter."

"Then it can wait its turn."

Captain Ardan Krell stared through the observation slit.

"I am increasingly convinced that is not how the universe works."

"The universe has had ample opportunity to file an objection."

The lights failed.

For half a second Dock Fourteen existed only by emergency strips, devotional candles, and the cold red pulse of the unidentified beacon outside.

Then the lights returned.

The Collector stood beside the outer hatch.

Nobody had seen it enter.

Nobody ever did.

It was taller than Marr remembered, if remembering height remained useful. Its black surfaces reflected Dock Fourteen incorrectly. In one reflection Young Varik was absent. In another Old Varik was seated. In a third Marr was standing on the opposite side of the room, holding a docket she had not yet touched.

Rhyse drew her pistol.

Krell drew his.

The Collector ignored both.

It looked at the paper seating plan.

More precisely, it looked at the seventh position.

The blacked-out title beneath Marr's thumb began to clear.

One letter appeared.

Then another.

Marr slammed the evidence sleeve shut.

"No."

The Collector took one step.

The brass automaton stamped so hard its little table jumped.

RESPONDENT APPROACHING RESERVED POSITION.

"On what authority?" Marr demanded.

The automaton hesitated.

It produced a second stamp from somewhere inside its chassis.

AUTHORITY DISPUTED.

"Good. Then stay it."

STAMP.

MOTION?

"Emergency motion to preserve seating pending identification of the absent party."

The Collector took another step.

The temperature dropped.

Every clock in Dock Fourteen advanced exactly one second.

For the first time in days, all of them agreed.

Marr hated it immediately.

"And," she added, "motion to prohibit reconciliation of the seating plan while identity remains disputed."

The automaton's gears clicked.

The Collector stopped.

Not because it chose to.

Because the floor between it and the seventh position ceased agreeing about distance.

Three metres became eleven.

Eleven became half a metre.

Half a metre became a corridor that had not been built yet.

Respondent Prior was still behind the red maintenance door.

It hammered once.

Twice.

Then three times.

The seating plan answered with three small knocks from inside the evidence sleeve.

Old Varik went pale.

"That happened over there."

Marr looked at him.

"At the first hearing?"

"Yes."

"Before or after you testified?"

Varik closed his eyes.

"Both."

"Of course."

Young Varik leaned against the bulkhead.

"Can we establish one thing that only happens once?"

Vhal considered him.

"Death usually performs adequately."

Old Varik opened his eyes.

"Not here."

Nobody contradicted him.

The unidentified Imperial beacon transmitted again.

This time it addressed the court.

SEAT SEVEN REMAINS RESERVED.

Marr stepped toward the vox pickup.

"For whom?"

No answer.

"State the party's name."

The beacon flickered.

Then:

NAME NOT YET FILED.

"Capacity?"

A longer pause.

The Collector's head turned toward the speaker.

The response arrived.

RECIPIENT.

Silence settled over Dock Fourteen.

Marr opened the seating plan again, carefully shielding the blacked-out title from the Collector.

"Recipient of what?"

The beacon answered immediately.

THE PETITION.

Old Varik sat down.

Not dramatically.

His knees simply ceased participating.

Marr read the six known seats again.

Petitioner.

Counsel.

Witness.

Adjudicator.

Respondent.

Respondent Prior.

Recipient.

The case had spent nine centuries arguing about who sent the petition, when it had been sent, whether Antegra existed when it was sent, and whether the Collector had the authority to reconcile any of it.

Nobody had asked who it had been sent to.

This was embarrassing.

Marr was professionally offended.

"The seventh seat is not vacant," she said.

The automaton stamped.

FINDING PROPOSED.

"It is reserved for a party not yet delivered."

STAMP.

FINDING PROPOSED.

"The Collector cannot occupy it because the Collector is already a respondent."

The Collector moved.

Every reflection in its body became the same reflection.

Dock Fourteen sharpened.

Young Varik and Old Varik blurred toward one another.

Marr felt, for one dreadful instant, that there had only ever been one Talbor Varik and that remembering otherwise was an administrative error waiting to be corrected.

She slapped both palms onto the docket.

"Objection. Duplicate capacity."

The automaton stamped.

SUSTAINED.

Reality loosened.

Young Varik staggered away from his older self.

Krell exhaled.

"Did you just prevent temporal annihilation with a conflict-of-interest rule?"

"No," Marr said. "That would be absurd."

Krell waited.

"I prevented joinder."

"Thank the Throne."

The Collector retreated one pace.

Outside, the dead-letter darkness pressed harder against the defensive perimeter.

One of the unidentified vessels vanished from auspex.

Then returned.

Then appeared twice.

Vhal watched the readings.

"Perimeter integrity declining."

"Time?" Krell asked.

"Indeterminate."

"Estimate."

"Between six minutes and seventy-one years."

"I preferred indeterminate."

The printer activated.

FROM: ORIGIN

DELIVERY ATTEMPT IN PROGRESS.

A second sheet followed.

RECIPIENT MUST ACKNOWLEDGE RECEIPT.

Marr stared at it.

"There."

Rhyse lowered her pistol by a fraction.

"There what?"

"That is why the hearing never ended."

She held up the page.

"A petition can be filed without being granted. It can be disputed. Lost. Misrouted. Appealed. Returned. Forwarded. But if this entire proceeding is a delivery—"

Old Varik finished it.

"Someone has to sign for it."

The red maintenance door shook in its frame.

Respondent Prior struck it repeatedly now.

Not warning them away.

Marr understood.

"It wants in."

Vhal looked at the door.

"Previous behavior indicated the opposite."

"Previous behavior was before we identified the missing capacity."

Rhyse frowned.

"Why would that matter?"

Marr looked at the Collector.

It had stopped advancing.

It was looking at the red door too.

"Because Respondent Prior preserves alternatives. The Collector reconciles them. The dead-letter mechanism destroys what cannot be delivered."

"And the recipient?"

Marr folded the seating plan.

"Chooses whether to accept the message."

The room became very still.

Krell said, "That sounds harmless when you say it quickly."

"It isn't."

The automaton stamped.

CORRECT.

The red door opened one centimetre.

Cold air entered Dock Fourteen carrying the smell of paper, machine oil, dust, and something older than all three.

A hand appeared around the edge.

Human.

Probably.

It wore an Administratum signet ring whose seal had been deliberately filed smooth.

The Collector recoiled.

Not far.

Enough.

Marr noticed.

So did Rhyse.

The hand placed a document on the deck and withdrew.

The door slammed shut.

Nobody moved for several seconds.

Then Krell said, "I assume shooting the paper would be frowned upon."

"Profoundly," Marr said.

"Good. I wanted the option formally excluded."

Marr picked up the document.

It was a receipt.

Not a petition.

Not an order.

A receipt.

The paper was older than Antegra Station.

The ink was fresh.

DELIVERY: PENDING.

RECIPIENT: ____________________

SIGNATURE: ____________________

Below those fields someone had written by hand:

DO NOT LET IT SIGN FOR YOU.

Marr knew the handwriting.

Her own.

Again.

She was beginning to resent herself across time.

The Collector extended one black hand.

The blank RECIPIENT line began filling itself.

C

O

L

Marr tore the receipt in half.

Every alarm on Antegra Station activated simultaneously.

The dead-letter darkness struck the perimeter.

The unidentified vessels fired outward.

The red door burst open.

Respondent Prior entered Dock Fourteen like a possibility finally granted standing.

And the brass automaton stamped both halves of the torn receipt.

DELIVERY REFUSED.

The Collector froze.

Marr looked at the stamp.

"I did not refuse delivery."

STAMP.

RECIPIENT UNKNOWN.

"Then correct the record."

The automaton clicked.

For once, it seemed uncertain.

Marr pointed at the seventh seat on the ancient plan.

"The intended recipient has not been identified. A third party attempted unauthorized acceptance. The document was destroyed to prevent fraudulent receipt."

The automaton's gears accelerated.

The Collector's fingers lengthened toward the torn paper.

Marr kept talking.

"Delivery remains pending. Seat Seven remains reserved. No substitute recipient may be recognized without proof of identity."

STAMP.

DELIVERY PENDING.

STAMP.

SEAT RESERVED.

STAMP.

SUBSTITUTION DENIED.

The Collector vanished.

Not dramatically.

One moment it occupied Dock Fourteen.

The next moment the universe had misplaced it.

Marr did not mistake that for victory.

Outside, the dead-letter mechanism withdrew by several kilometres.

The perimeter stabilized.

The station clocks resumed disagreeing.

Young Varik remained young.

Old Varik remained old.

The dead Varik, wherever he was, presumably remained inconvenient.

Marr allowed herself one breath.

Then the printer started again.

FROM: ORIGIN

DELIVERY ATTEMPT FAILED.

REASON: RECIPIENT IDENTITY DISPUTED.

A second page followed.

NEXT ATTEMPT SCHEDULED.

DATE: TOMORROW.

Old Varik looked at the first-hearing docket.

One of its three dates was tomorrow.

"That's bad," he said.

"Yes."

"You sound pleased."

"I know what tomorrow is for now."

Rhyse holstered her pistol.

"And the recipient?"

Marr looked through the observation slit at the nameless beacon, the nameless vessels, and the nameless place holding Antegra's place in a hearing that had never ended.

"Still missing."

The beacon transmitted one final line.

COUNSEL, PREPARE YOUR RECIPIENT.

Marr read it twice.

Then a third time.

"My recipient?"

The automaton stamped.

QUESTION DEFECTIVE.

Marr closed her eyes.

"Of course it is."

Across Dock Fourteen, the sealed red door answered with a single knock.

And somewhere beyond it, someone began filling in a name.
