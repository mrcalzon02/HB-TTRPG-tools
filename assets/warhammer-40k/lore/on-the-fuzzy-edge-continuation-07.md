## Continuation VII — The Name of the Office

The cathedral's answer arrived in a folder that had been repaired twice and addressed three times.

Sub-Dean Caldris put it on Javard's desk without sitting down. His brass fingers were holding the folder shut, though it had no fastening.

"Is it bad?" Javard asked.

"It is correct."

"That sounds worse."

"The Office of Temporal Properties confirms that the burial office is authorized to maintain Lower Ossuary Nineteen. It does not recognize the burial office as competent to cancel the transfer."

"We knew that."

"Now they know it."

Caldris opened the folder. The letter bore a present-day signature, a cathedral seal and the name of the office that had searched the records. It also contained a copy of a much older routing card. The card had been written in a hand Javard had not seen among the annual maintenance warrants.

Under *subject* was the name Ephram Sol.

Under *destination* was the cathedral's receiving point.

Under *controlling authority* were four words: **PRIOR DELEGATION — CUSTODY HOLD**.

There was no name, no office address and no authorization to open anything.

"Is that the designation?" Javard asked.

"The one our clerks have been carrying forward. They assumed it was the name of the office."

"Why?"

"Because the field says controlling authority."

Javard looked at the old card. It was an ordinary error to make. A column heading was not a witness. It was not even a reliable description of the information entered beneath it.

"Who wrote the first card?"

"The clerk who received the instruction is dead. The office has his duty roster, but not his statement. They have located the copy made for the first annual allowance."

"Not the instruction itself?"

"That remains in the financial archive. You saw it yesterday."

"I saw the bursar's instruction. I have not seen what he received."

Caldris's human hand moved toward the folder, then stopped.

"Neither have they."

The present-day reply distinguished maintenance from custody with admirable care. The cathedral could keep a destination ready, pay for it, inspect it and record deficiencies. It could not claim a corpse had arrived. It could not certify that the unnamed prior authority had released its hold. Nor could the Office of Temporal Properties identify a living successor who had accepted responsibility for that decision.

"Then their answer is that nobody owns it," Serik said from the window.

"No," Javard replied. "Their answer is that they cannot identify the owner."

Serik made a face.

"That distinction is going to cost us another morning."

"At least."

---

The Inquisitorial answer arrived after the midday bell.

It did not arrive in a black carriage or beneath an armed escort. A messenger from the district relay brought a narrow envelope to the El Shaban lodging office and required a signature from the factor named on the delivery docket. Serik signed. He had been doing that sort of thing for nine days and was getting uncomfortably good at it.

He carried the envelope to Javard without breaking the outer fold.

"The seal is genuine," he said.

"How did you determine that?"

"The messenger refused to let me touch the inner cover."

"A useful method of authentication. Did he know what was inside?"

"He said that if he had, he would be paid differently."

Javard checked the relay marks against his own request before opening it.

The response was from a records authentication desk attached to his Inquisitorial chain. It confirmed that the old custody-hold designation had once been accepted as a valid routing category for restricted ecclesiastical material. The desk could authenticate the category and the form of the surviving reference. It could not, from the reference alone, identify the officer who had issued the underlying order.

The designation had been retired from ordinary routing twelve years earlier.

Retired was not revoked.

The reply made that distinction twice, as though the clerk had already encountered someone who had failed to appreciate it.

An inactive routing category did not invalidate an old order. Equally, an old valid order did not authorize a new officer to perform an act merely because he possessed a newer rosette. The authentication desk had no authority to release the cathedral cabinet, order the opening of Sol's niche, or certify that either action had ever been permitted.

Javard read the last paragraph aloud.

"'The relevant original instrument, if retained, is held outside the present routing index. An accredited officer may petition for a supervised examination of its external custody metadata without seeking disclosure of its sealed contents.'"

Serik waited.

"Well?"

"Someone has offered us a way to look at the outside of another envelope."

"How many envelopes before we reach a person?"

"That is one of the questions."

Caldris took the reply when Javard offered it, but read only the part marked for cathedral acknowledgment. The restricted annex stayed under Javard's hand. Caldris had not asked to see it, and Javard had no reason to make the old clerk responsible for knowledge he did not require.

"The designation is real, then," Caldris said.

"The category was real. The person remains unidentified."

"Does the Inquisition still claim the hold?"

"The desk claims the reference can be authenticated. It has not claimed responsibility for the instruction."

Caldris looked at the cathedral reply beside it.

"So our office cannot release it, and yours will not."

"Mine has not yet determined who may."

"That is a poor arrangement."

"It has lasted seventeen years. Poor arrangements can be remarkably durable."

---

The senior burial custodian had not waited for Javard to finish his correspondence.

He had twenty-six families arriving for grave-tablet verification, a cracked wheel on the inspection servitor and a junior clerk who had confused two similarly named deceased men. The mistake had been caught before either inscription was approved, but the families had both been summoned and neither wished to go home without an answer.

Merek was in the public records hall, where a woman was holding a copy of her father's burial certificate as if someone might try to take it away.

"My father did not have a middle name," she told the clerk.

The clerk was young and frightened enough to keep looking at the wrong column.

Merek came around the counter, took the register and turned it sideways so the woman could see the two entries. He did not apologize on behalf of an abstraction called the office. He showed her which entry belonged to her father and where the error had occurred. He asked her to read the corrected inscription before signing.

She read it three times.

"Will the stone be right?"

"Not until the mason has copied this," Merek said. "You may return to inspect it before it is set."

The woman lowered her certificate.

"Then I'll come."

After she left, Merek noticed Javard at the end of the hall.

"We have finished nineteen," he said. "Seven remain. The mason will not have all the corrected proofs until tomorrow."

"How many would you have finished if I had taken your clerks?"

"Fewer."

"Then keep them."

Merek watched him for a moment.

"Your old transfer is still open."

"Yes."

"The annual maintenance office wants to know whether to renew its charge. The account is due for review."

"Can it be renewed without pretending the remains were received?"

"It has been for sixteen years."

"Then record the actual condition again."

Merek did not look pleased. He was not required to. A pending destination occupied space, consumed materials and made every annual reviewer ask a question to which he had no satisfactory answer.

"How long?"

"Until someone competent makes a different decision."

"And if nobody does?"

Javard looked toward the hall, where the remaining families were taking their places.

"Then the cost should remain visible."

Merek went back to work. The investigation had not won him another clerk, another servitor or an easier afternoon. It had at least stopped asking him to make the living wait so that the dead could be filed more quickly.

---

The two routing replies had a point of agreement.

They both cited the same old reference.

They did not agree on what it meant.

Javard laid the documents beside the original bursar instruction. The cathedral had inherited a custody-hold designation and used it to justify maintaining a destination. The Inquisitorial authentication desk recognized the designation as a historical routing category but would not attach an issuing officer to it. The bursar's own words prohibited a receipt without witnessed delivery.

Three offices had preserved three different portions of an instruction. None could presently certify the act everyone wished to discuss.

Serik traced the old reference with the blunt end of a pen.

"What if it was never an officer's order?"

"It may have been a clerk's description of one."

"Then we have spent a week investigating a heading."

"Not quite. The cabinet seal is authentic. The transfer is open. The destination has been maintained. Those facts do not disappear because the heading is poor."

"Can the designation tell us who sent the original?"

"Not by itself."

Javard compared the old card with the newer cathedral copy. The newer copy had reproduced the designation as a proper office title, with capital letters and a neat ruled box. The older card had not. Its letters were uneven, cramped against the margin. Whoever wrote it had fitted an awkward description into a field designed for a name.

He could see how the later mistake had occurred. He could not establish that it had occurred innocently.

"Someone transcribed it," he said. "Someone else promoted the transcription into an authority."

"Does that help?"

"It tells us where to stop trusting the copy."

Serik nodded. He had once assumed that a stamped record was a fact and an unstamped account was an opinion. Nine days with Javard had not made him love paperwork. It had made him more particular about which part of it deserved belief.

"Could we ask the clerk who made the second copy?"

"He's dead too."

"Convenient."

"He's been dead eight years. I don't think he arranged it for us."

Serik set down the pen.

"Then the external custody metadata."

"Yes."

"Another petition."

"Yes."

"Another delay."

"Yes."

"Would you like me to write those answers on a card so you can hand it to me next time?"

"Only if you can get it authenticated."

---

Sister-Medicae Halva had sent no invitation.

Javard did not mistake that for an oversight.

He found her at the ward's outer desk while she was signing for a fresh dressing kit. She looked at his empty hands before she looked at his face.

"No questions for him," she said.

"None."

"That is new."

"How is he?"

"Alive. Tired. Still unable to tolerate a full office. He attempted to read a devotional page yesterday and stopped when the light caused pain. We have not cleared him for visitors."

"Has anyone from the cathedral asked him to sign?"

"They have stopped asking me to carry the papers in. Whether they have stopped drafting them is another matter."

Javard put a sealed notice on the counter.

"This is for the office, not for him. It confirms that no answer from Montpclair is required for the current records request."

Halva read the outer heading.

"Will it keep Caldris away?"

"Caldris has learned to use the standing delegation."

"Then there is hope for him."

She signed receipt of the notice as an administrative handoff, not as medical testimony. Javard did not ask whether Montpclair had spoken of Sol again. Halva had already made the limits of her observations clear, and the patient's recovery had not become less important because two archives had answered a letter.

---

On the way out, Serik caught up with him.

"You could have asked."

"I could have."

"Would she have answered?"

"Probably."

"Then why not?"

"Because I have no new question that would justify disturbing him."

Serik walked beside him in silence until they reached the steps.

"I've been thinking about going back to the trade house."

Javard stopped.

"Do you wish to?"

"Not today. But you offered, and I want to know whether you meant it."

"I did."

"If this becomes an open-ended hunt through Inquisitorial records, I will need to tell the people paying me why their factor has vanished."

"Tell them you are completing a disputed shipping account. It will even be true."

"For how long?"

"We shall set a review at the end of this request. You may leave then without my calling it disloyalty."

Serik looked relieved, then faintly insulted by his own relief.

"And if I stay?"

"You will continue to be paid."

"At the rate agreed?"

"Unfortunately."

They resumed walking. It was not a vow of brotherhood. It was a workable arrangement between two men who knew rather more about each other's limits than they had a week earlier.

---

The records authentication desk's second message was shorter.

The original restricted instrument had not been located by the desk. Its custody index showed that a packet associated with the old designation had been transferred from routine authentication to a controlled register during the same administrative period as Sol's death. The entry carried an accession date and a receiving-office mark. It did not state the packet's contents, its author or whether the packet contained the instruction still governing the cathedral.

The desk would not release a facsimile on a routine query.

It would accept a petition for supervised examination of the packet's external register line. Such a petition required a defined purpose, a named officer accountable for the inspection and an undertaking not to use the result as authority to break any existing seal. A refusal would be recorded with its reason.

Javard read the conditions twice.

Serik watched him.

"Are you going to ask Kelford?"

"I will route the petition through the authority under which I am already serving. That is different from asking him to decide what the evidence means."

"Will it take long?"

"Longer than a crowbar."

"Less expensive?"

"Not necessarily."

Javard drafted the request without mentioning Graveheart, the disputed-reader mark or Vorn's fractured testimony. None of those facts was needed to justify identifying the custodian of an old restricted instrument. Including them would widen the inquiry before he knew who would receive it.

He identified the transfer reference, the cathedral's open receiving field, the original bursar instruction and the two independently returned routing replies. He asked to inspect only the external register line: who accepted the packet, under what office authority, and whether any subsequent transfer of custody was recorded.

He expressly disclaimed any request to open the cabinet, disturb the niche or cancel the transfer.

Caldris witnessed the cathedral portion. Serik witnessed the preparation of the commercial copies but not the restricted annex. Javard signed the Inquisitorial petition himself.

The authentication desk had given him an actionable route, not an answer. He could live with that.

When Caldris returned to the records chamber, Archive Servitor Nine was feeding parchment beneath the green lumen.

Scratch.

Click.

Scratch.

Click.

The red wax on the cabinet was still intact.

Javard did not ask Vorn another question. He had a printed warning from the man already, and had made no progress toward knowing whether the word *he* referred to Sol, Montpclair, a reader or someone not yet named. Repeating the phrase to a damaged witness would not improve it.

He placed the cathedral answer, the Inquisitorial answer and the new petition in separate folders. The old transfer remained open. The reserved carriage remained unrecorded as released. The grave-tablet work would finish tomorrow. Montpclair remained under Halva's care.

Caldris regarded the three folders.

"What do I tell the Office of Temporal Properties?"

"That you have preserved its answer."

"And the burial office?"

"That it should continue to record what it actually maintains."

"And when they ask who controls the hold?"

"Tell them we have identified the question's proper destination. We have not received its answer."

Caldris considered this.

"That will not satisfy them."

"No."

"Or you."

"No."

The old clerk picked up his copy and left.

Javard stayed beside the cabinet for a moment longer. He could have broken the wax. He had the rank, the curiosity and, in all likelihood, enough armed men to make the argument short.

He had none of the missing authority, and none of the missing facts.

He put his rosette away.

Somewhere in the cathedral's seventeen-year-old paperwork, a clerk had written a category where a person ought to have been. That was not proof of a conspiracy. It was not proof of an accident. It was the point at which an institution had ceased to know whom it was obeying.

Javard signed the outgoing register.

This time the next question would have an accountable recipient.

Whether that recipient would answer was another matter.
