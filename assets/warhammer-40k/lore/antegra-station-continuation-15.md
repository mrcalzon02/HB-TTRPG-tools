# Antegra Station

## Continuation XV — The Foreign Office

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The objection arrived in triplicate.

This impressed Selene Marr more than the screaming.

The screaming had become ordinary.

Antegra Station had endured alarms, astropathic seizures, contradictory childhoods, an extinct civilization remembering itself through eight million unwilling citizens, and an entity capable of editing causality with the temperament of an unusually patient registrar.

Triplicate, however, implied standards.

Three sheets crawled from the astropathic transcription engine.

They were identical except for the destination line.

The first named a place Marr did not recognize.

The second named a place she was certain did not exist.

The third contained only a departmental routing code seventeen digits long.

All three bore the same sentence.

FINAL ADJUDICATION DECLINED.

Captain Ardan Krell stared at them.

"That's it?"

Marr examined the seals.

"No."

"There are four words."

"There are three copies."

Krell waited.

Marr did not elaborate.

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse took the first page from her.

"Who sent this?"

Witness Lysa Nineteen tilted her blindfolded head toward the astropathic vault.

"They don't agree."

Old Talbor Varik laughed once.

Young Talbor Varik did not.

Neither reaction was reassuring.

---

At 05:17 station time, the Collector filed a jurisdictional challenge.

It did so by removing the concept of distance from Dock Fourteen.

For nine seconds the command dais was simultaneously beside the outer hatch, three decks below the astropathic vault, and approximately two metres to the left of itself.

Krell vomited into a waste receptacle that had not been there before and, once distance returned, was discovered in Hydroponics.

Nobody commented.

The terminal printed:

EXTERNAL AUTHORITY INVALID.

Marr keyed a response.

GROUNDS?

The answer appeared immediately.

IDENTITY UNRESOLVED.

Marr smiled.

Rhyse saw it.

"Why are you smiling?"

"Because it has made an error."

Old Varik pushed himself away from the bulkhead.

"It doesn't make errors."

"Everything makes errors. The interesting question is whether it recognizes them."

She tapped the message.

"It claims the external authority is invalid because its identity is unresolved."

Krell wiped his mouth.

"And?"

"And an unresolved identity is precisely what makes it competent in this matter."

Vhal's optical cluster rotated.

"Paradoxical jurisdiction."

"No. Ordinary jurisdiction. Paradox merely makes the paperwork more honest."

Rhyse looked at the three destination names.

"We still do not know who they are."

"Neither does the Collector."

Marr sat down.

"That is presently their strongest qualification."

---

The reply channel would not accept a conventional transmission.

Vox failed.

Astropathic sending failed.

Laser tightbeam returned to Antegra before it had been fired.

A courier servitor dispatched toward the communications annex walked through the same doorway six times and eventually submitted a resignation request despite possessing no recognized legal personhood.

Vhal approved it.

"Why?" Krell asked.

"Its petition was correctly formatted."

"It's a servitor."

"It was persuasive."

The servitor sat down and refused to move.

Antegra's labor office opened a vacancy.

Somewhere in the station, an unemployed deckhand applied.

The Imperium continued.

Then Lysa Nineteen took one of the triplicate sheets.

Rhyse caught her wrist.

"Do not."

"I haven't done anything."

"That has ceased to be reassuring."

Lysa turned the page over.

The reverse was blank.

She held it toward Old Varik.

"Write the wrong name."

Silence.

Young Varik frowned.

"Whose?"

"Theirs."

"We don't know it."

"Exactly."

Marr understood first.

She slid a stylus across the table.

"Governor."

Both Variks reached for it.

Both stopped.

Krell closed his eyes.

"Of course."

Marr pointed to the younger man.

"You first."

Young Varik wrote the first destination name exactly as the transcription engine had rendered it.

Old Varik took the stylus and deliberately misspelled it.

The page trembled.

A fourth destination appeared beneath the first three.

Then a fifth.

Then eleven more.

The Collector reacted violently.

Every name began correcting itself.

Lysa slapped both hands onto the sheet.

"Don't let it finish."

Marr opened the station channel.

"All departments. We require immediate assistance with foreign correspondence."

Krell looked at her.

"You're going to have eight million people misspell it."

"Captain, I am going to have eight million Imperial citizens exercise their natural gifts."

---

The response was magnificent.

Within forty seconds Antegra had generated twelve million spellings.

Several were obscene.

Two were theological accusations.

One was a recipe for sump fungus.

A manufactorum clerk submitted the same name nine thousand times with progressively angrier punctuation.

A hab-school class rendered it in finger paint.

Someone in Dock Control entered ORK.

Someone else corrected that to NOT ORK.

A third citizen filed an objection to both classifications on the grounds that neither party had completed the appropriate species declaration.

The Collector tried to reconcile the submissions.

It failed.

For the first time, the distant coordinates stabilized.

Not into one name.

Into a relationship.

Antegra could not determine what the place was called.

It could determine that something there was listening.

The astropathic vault went dark.

Every choir member inhaled at once.

Then a voice spoke through all of them.

Not one voice.

Several.

Layered badly.

One elderly.

One young.

One metallic.

One that sounded as though it had learned Low Gothic from a legal deposition.

"ANTEGRAN PETITION RECEIVED."

Marr leaned toward the grille.

"Identify authority."

The choir answered:

"REQUEST DENIED."

"Reason?"

"IDENTIFICATION WOULD PREJUDICE THE MATTER."

Marr sat back.

"Competent."

Rhyse stared at her.

"That is your assessment?"

"They understand disclosure."

The choir continued.

"STATE RELIEF SOUGHT."

Marr looked at Old Varik.

He shook his head.

Young Varik did the same.

Rhyse said, "Destruction of the xenos entity."

The choir screamed.

Not in pain.

In refusal.

Every lumen on Dock Fourteen turned red.

The Collector immediately printed:

PROPOSED REMEDY ACCEPTABLE.

Marr tore the page from the terminal before it finished emerging.

"No."

Rhyse's hand settled on her pistol.

"Auditor."

"Legate, it agreed with you."

Rhyse stopped.

That was enough.

Marr faced the grille.

"Relief sought: continued inability to reach a final determination."

Silence.

Then:

"CLARIFY."

"We require a ruling that does not resolve the dispute."

Krell muttered, "Emperor preserve us."

Marr continued.

"We require an authority that can accept jurisdiction while refusing competence, acknowledge standing while denying identity, and issue an enforceable order whose enforcement depends upon remaining provisional."

The layered voice answered after twelve seconds.

"THAT IS NOT A RULING."

"Correct."

"THAT IS ADMINISTRATION."

Marr smiled.

"Now you understand."

---

The distant authority requested the case file.

Antegra attempted to transmit Case 7-OMNIA-NULL.

The attempt lasted three seconds.

Then every cogitator on the station emitted a smell like burning dust.

Vhal severed the link.

"Data volume incompatible."

"How large is the file?" Krell asked.

"That question no longer has a stable answer."

"Give me an unstable one."

"Between four terabytes and eleven inhabited childhoods."

Krell looked away.

"I preferred tonnes."

The foreign authority offered a solution.

"SEND REPRESENTATIVE RECORD."

Marr selected a random file.

It belonged to Edrin Vale, the sanitation clerk the Collector had erased from Deck Twenty-Seven.

His record remained contradictory.

His wife remembered him.

His supervisor did not.

His ration account existed.

His birth record did not.

His boots remained beneath a bunk that had never been assigned.

Marr transmitted it.

The Collector reacted before the distant authority did.

Every screen on Antegra displayed:

RECORD INVALID.

The choir answered:

"OBJECTION NOTED."

RECORD INVALID.

"OBJECTION NOTED."

RECORD INVALID.

"OBJECTION NOTED."

The exchange repeated twenty-three times.

On the twenty-fourth, the foreign authority added:

"OBJECTION DUPLICATIVE."

The Collector stopped.

Old Varik began laughing.

This time Young Varik joined him.

Krell did not.

"What?"

Marr folded her hands.

"It has been told to stop repeating itself."

"And that's funny?"

"Captain, there are victories and there are miracles."

---

At 05:46 the foreign authority issued its first order.

It arrived on paper.

Nobody knew from where.

One moment Marr's desk was empty.

The next, a cream-colored sheet rested upon it beneath a brass weight bearing an unfamiliar seal.

The order read:

INTERIM FINDING.

THE PETITIONER HAS DEMONSTRATED A LIVE DISPUTE.

THE RESPONDENT HAS DEMONSTRATED CAPACITY TO ALTER EVIDENCE.

NO FACT ALTERED AFTER COMMENCEMENT OF REVIEW SHALL BE ACCEPTED AS DISPOSITIVE.

ALL PARTIES ARE DIRECTED TO PRESERVE THE STATUS QUO PENDING FURTHER REVIEW.

NO FINAL ADJUDICATION IS AUTHORIZED.

Marr read it twice.

Rhyse read it once.

Vhal scanned it fourteen times.

Krell asked the only question that mattered.

"Does it work?"

The Collector answered him.

It tried to remove Dock Fourteen.

Nothing happened.

It tried to consolidate the two Variks.

Nothing happened.

It tried to restore its stolen self-reference inside the minds of Antegra's citizens.

Eight million people flinched.

The word almost formed.

Then the memory stopped changing.

Not erased.

Not resolved.

Preserved.

The status quo.

Old Varik whispered, "Oh."

The Collector printed one line.

AUTHORITY?

The foreign choir answered:

"DISPUTED."

The Collector printed:

IDENTITY?

"DISPUTED."

JURISDICTION?

"DISPUTED."

VALIDITY?

"DISPUTED."

The Collector paused.

Then:

FINALITY?

The choir answered:

"DENIED."

For thirteen seconds nothing in Antegra Station changed.

After the preceding hours, this was terrifying.

Then the Collector withdrew.

Not departed.

Not defeated.

Withdrew.

The distinction mattered.

Its impossible presence receded from the station's clocks, bulkheads, records and memories like pressure leaving a wound.

Deck Twenty-Seven remained where it was.

Edrin Vale did not return.

New Presidio did not reappear.

The dead city remained inside them.

The two Talbor Variks remained two.

And Case 7-OMNIA-NULL remained open.

---

At 06:02 the first ordinary shift bell rang.

Nobody trusted it.

At 06:03 a second bell rang because the first had been filed under the wrong schedule.

That one everyone accepted.

Antegra resumed breathing.

Ration lines reopened.

Dock cranes moved.

The manufactoria restarted one by one.

Millions of citizens discovered that their emergency identity declarations had propagated into payroll.

The dead received wages.

Children received pension notices.

A municipal fountain was assigned a twelve-hour shift in Hydroponics.

Marr ordered all corrections suspended.

"For how long?" Krell asked.

"Until we know whether accuracy is safe."

"That could be years."

"Captain, this is the Administratum. Years are a rounding error."

Rhyse stood beside the foreign order.

"We need to know who issued this."

Lysa Nineteen shook her head.

"No."

Rhyse looked down at her.

"Explain."

"If we know who they are, so does it."

Old Varik nodded.

"The child is right."

"She usually is," Marr said. "It is becoming inconvenient."

Lysa smiled.

Then stopped.

Her face turned toward the astropathic vault.

The choir was whispering again.

Not the layered foreign voice.

Something else.

A human voice.

Weak.

Distant.

Repeating a routing code.

Marr compared it to the third destination line on the original objection.

It matched.

Rhyse moved closer to the grille.

"Message?"

Vhal translated the signal.

Her optical cluster contracted.

"Not a message."

"Then what?"

"A docket notice."

Marr held out her hand.

The transcription engine produced a narrow strip of paper.

She read it.

CASE 7-OMNIA-NULL.

VENUE TRANSFER REQUESTED.

PETITIONER: ANTEGRA STATION.

RESPONDENT: UNRESOLVED.

RECEIVING AUTHORITY: UNRESOLVED.

REASON: LOCAL JURISDICTION COMPROMISED.

PROPOSED HEARING LOCATION: WITHHELD.

ATTENDANCE REQUIRED.

Marr looked up.

"Required by whom?" Krell asked.

The paper advanced another line.

BY ALL PARTIES.

Old Varik went very still.

Young Varik looked toward the stars beyond Dock Fourteen.

Rhyse checked the charge on her pistol, which Marr considered touching in the way one might consider a child carrying an umbrella into orbital bombardment.

Lysa whispered:

"They want us to come there."

Marr folded the notice carefully.

"No."

Krell blinked.

"No?"

"We do not yet know where there is."

"So what do we do?"

Marr looked at the open case, the two governors, the blindfolded witness, the Inquisitor, the Magos, and the millions of contradictory citizens whose continued existence presently depended upon nobody becoming too certain about anything.

Then she looked at the destination field marked WITHHELD.

"We file for travel expenses."

For the first time that morning, the darkness beyond Antegra Station seemed almost merciful.

It did not answer.
