# Antegra Station

## Continuation XX — The Respondent

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The fourth stamp was followed by silence.

Not ordinary silence.

Ordinary silence aboard Antegra Station contained ventilation, pumps, distant boots, devotional machinery, arguments about requisitions and at least one person somewhere insisting that a valve had been like that when they found it.

This silence contained none of those things.

It waited.

Edrin Vale stared through the red maintenance door.

"I am not going in there."

The brass automaton stamped once.

WITNESS DECLINES ASSIGNMENT.

Vale looked offended.

"I didn't decline it. I said I'm not doing it."

Selene Marr rubbed the bridge of her nose.

"That is generally how declining works."

"Not in maintenance. Declining implies somebody asked."

Captain Ardan Krell kept his pistol trained on the darkness.

"Can we establish whether the distinction matters before whatever is in there establishes it for us?"

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse glanced toward the observation glass.

The Collector remained absent.

That absence had acquired weight.

Magos Hester Vhal extended a mechadendrite toward the threshold. The probe crossed three centimetres and stopped.

Not against a barrier.

Stopped.

Its polished tip remained visible, but the cable behind it slackened as if those three centimetres had become several kilometres.

Vhal retracted the probe.

It returned warm.

"Spatial metric indeterminate," she said.

"Meaning?" Krell asked.

"The door is far away while being here."

"I preferred it when the doors merely went to the wrong deck."

Vale nodded. "Those were good doors."

The elderly adjudicator did not share their nostalgia.

She stood behind the docket table, one hand pressed flat against its brass edge.

"Do not address the additional respondent."

Marr looked at her.

"It requested recognition."

"Yes."

"This is a hearing."

"Yes."

"Recognition therefore seems difficult to avoid."

The adjudicator's eyes hardened.

"Counselor, some petitions are dangerous because they can be granted."

Marr considered the darkness.

"And some respondents?"

"Are dangerous because they can answer."

A fifth stamp came from beyond the red door.

The brass automaton shuddered.

A strip of paper emerged from its mouth.

REQUEST FOR RECOGNITION RENEWED.

APPLICANT: —

Marr lifted the strip.

There was no blank after APPLICANT.

There was a dash.

Not missing information.

A declared absence of information.

"It has no name," Young Talbor Varik said.

Old Talbor Varik shook his head.

"No. It declined to provide one."

Rhyse looked at him.

"You know the distinction?"

"I have spent sixty-eight years surviving distinctions."

"And apparently three years before that," Marr said.

Old Varik gave her a sour look.

"Thank you."

"Accuracy requested implicitly."

Vhal's lenses brightened.

Marr glanced at her.

"Do not encourage me."

The darkness beyond the red door changed.

Something appeared there.

Not a figure.

A desk.

It was wooden.

That alone made it obscene.

Wood on Antegra was a luxury reserved for shrines, senior offices and officials who wished visitors to understand that several forests had died for their authority.

This desk was plain.

Behind it sat an empty chair.

On the desk rested a ledger.

Beside the ledger stood a stamp.

Vale whispered, "That's my desk."

Marr looked at him.

"You have a wooden desk?"

"No."

"Then it isn't your desk."

"It's the desk I keep asking for."

Krell lowered his pistol enough to stare at him.

"You've been requisitioning a wooden desk?"

"For twenty-two years."

"Why?"

"Because the metal one shocks me."

"Have you repaired it?"

Vale looked wounded.

"Captain, there are standards."

The ledger opened by itself.

Pages turned.

Slowly.

Each page was blank until it faced them.

Then writing appeared.

Marr could not read it from the threshold.

Vhal could.

"Maintenance orders."

Vale swore.

"Dates?" Marr asked.

"Negative values."

"How negative?"

Vhal paused.

"First entry predates Antegra Station by nine hundred and eleven years."

Nobody spoke.

The page turned.

"Second predates the Cafarron Corridor designation by approximately two millennia."

Another page.

"Third date is not representable in Imperial calendrical notation."

Vale folded his arms.

"Still overdue, I suppose."

The brass automaton stamped.

WORK ORDER STATUS: OVERDUE.

Vale pointed at it.

"See? This is why nobody likes administration."

Marr almost corrected him.

Then decided that the morning had already contained enough lies.

The elderly adjudicator stepped closer to the hatch.

"Close the ledger."

Nothing happened.

She repeated herself.

"Close the ledger."

The pages continued turning.

For the first time Marr heard fear enter the woman's voice.

Not panic.

Recognition.

"You have seen this before," Marr said.

The adjudicator looked at her.

"No."

Marr waited.

The adjudicator's mouth tightened.

"That is the problem."

Another page turned.

A diagram appeared.

Seven circles around a central mark.

The seven dead moons.

Antegra before Antegra.

The central mark was not a station.

It was a hole.

Old Varik stepped backward.

Young Varik did not.

"What was here?"

The adjudicator said nothing.

Marr turned toward the unnamed naval officer.

"You came to adjudicate a case concerning this location. You arrived carrying evidence older than our incident. You recognized Old Varik. You knew the red door was not Antegra evidence. I think the period in which withholding everything was charming has ended."

The naval officer's jaw tightened.

"We are not authorized to identify the jurisdiction."

"I did not ask you to."

"You asked what was here."

"Yes."

"That answer identifies the jurisdiction."

Marr sighed.

"Naturally."

The sixth stamp sounded.

The automaton printed:

RECOGNITION MAY BE GRANTED WITHOUT IDENTIFICATION.

Everyone stared at it.

Marr looked toward the darkness.

"Was that you?"

The adjudicator seized her wrist.

"Do not address it."

Marr looked down at the hand.

Then at the adjudicator.

"You are currently touching an officer of the Administratum without filing the appropriate notice."

The woman released her.

Krell made a noise that might have been a cough.

Rhyse's expression did not change, but something behind it approved.

Marr lifted the printed strip.

"It is correct. Recognition is procedural. Identity is evidentiary. We can recognize an additional respondent without deciding what it is."

"And then it has standing," the adjudicator said.

"It already has a stamp."

"You do not understand."

"Then explain."

The adjudicator looked through the red door.

"The Collector reconciles contradictions. It makes one answer where many answers exist."

"We have noticed."

"This does the opposite."

The ledger stopped turning.

The room became colder.

"It creates contradictions?" Krell asked.

"No," said the adjudicator. "It creates the possibility that there could have been another answer."

Marr frowned.

Vhal spoke first.

"Counterfactual generation."

"Not generation," the adjudicator said. "Permission."

The word sat badly in the chamber.

Imperial institutions understood permission. They understood it principally as something other people should not have.

Old Varik stared at the diagram of the seven moons.

"The Collector was drawn here because of it."

"Perhaps."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the only one we have survived."

Young Varik looked at the empty chair beyond the door.

"What do you call it?"

The adjudicator closed her eyes.

"We don't."

The seventh stamp sounded.

The automaton printed another strip.

PROPOSED DESIGNATION FOR PURPOSES OF PROCEEDING:

THE RESPONDENT PRIOR.

Marr read it twice.

"Acceptable."

"No," the adjudicator said.

Marr took her stylus.

"Provisional recognition only. Identity disputed. Nature disputed. Chronology disputed. Jurisdiction disputed. No finding of personhood, agency, causality, divinity, xenos classification, warp classification or tax status."

Krell looked at her.

"Tax status?"

"One should never close a useful avenue."

She signed.

The brass automaton stamped.

ADDITIONAL RESPONDENT RECOGNIZED PROVISIONALLY.

DESIGNATION: RESPONDENT PRIOR.

ALL ATTRIBUTES DISPUTED.

The Collector appeared in every reflective surface at once.

Not gradually.

Violently.

Every pane, lens, polished weapon, brass fitting and human eye showed the same impossible white geometry.

For the first time since Marr had encountered it, the Collector did not seem calm.

The chamber spoke with its voice.

RECOGNITION INVALID.

The red door answered with a stamp.

VALIDITY DISPUTED.

The Collector replied.

RESPONDENT DOES NOT EXIST.

Stamp.

EXISTENCE DISPUTED.

RESPONDENT PRECEDES CAUSE.

Stamp.

CAUSALITY DISPUTED.

RESPONDENT HAS NO IDENTITY.

Stamp.

IDENTITY DISPUTED.

Marr felt a terrible smile trying to happen.

She suppressed it.

Professionalism had limits, but one should occasionally pretend otherwise.

The Collector's geometry sharpened.

Then every reflection showed the red door closed.

The actual red door remained open.

Rhyse whispered, "It cannot reconcile it."

"No," the adjudicator said. "It never could."

The Collector spoke again.

REMOVE RESPONDENT.

The automaton printed the request.

Marr took it.

"Grounds?"

The Collector did not answer.

"Motion deficient."

Krell stared at her.

"You are asking that thing to justify itself?"

"Captain, if we abandon procedure the moment procedure becomes inconvenient, civilization collapses."

Rhyse looked around Antegra.

"Your confidence in civilization is ambitious."

"I said collapses. I did not claim it had succeeded."

The stamp beyond the door sounded once.

The ledger turned to a new page.

A single work order appeared.

Vale read it from the threshold.

"Oh, no."

Marr looked over.

"What?"

He pointed.

FAULT: EXCESS CERTAINTY.

LOCATION: ANTEGRA.

SOURCE: COLLECTOR.

CORRECTIVE ACTION: INTRODUCE ALTERNATIVE.

ASSIGNED: EDRIN VALE.

Vale stared into the darkness.

"I fix pipes."

The ledger added a line.

QUALIFICATION ACCEPTED.

Krell laughed.

It was brief, involuntary and close to hysteria.

Vale glared at the ledger.

"Don't you start."

The desk stamp moved by itself.

Once.

The red door widened.

Beyond it, the darkness acquired depth.

And in that depth appeared Antegra Station.

Then another Antegra Station.

Then another.

Hundreds.

Thousands.

Not stacked in space.

Possible.

One burned.

One was pristine.

One had never been built beyond its central spindle.

One carried Imperial heraldry from a dynasty nobody recognized.

One orbited six moons.

One orbited eight.

One was empty.

One was crowded with people who stood at every viewport looking back at them.

One was a wreck.

One was a cathedral.

One was something no human engineer would have admitted to constructing while sober, threatened or alive.

Vale whispered, "Those aren't histories."

Vhal's mechadendrites had gone completely still.

"Correct."

"What are they?"

"Alternatives."

The Collector screamed.

There was no sound.

Every clock aboard Antegra jumped forward one second and backward three.

Every lumen extinguished and relit.

For one instant Marr remembered dying as a child.

For another she remembered never joining the Administratum.

For another she remembered being Governor Marr of Antegra Station and ordering Talbor Varik executed for sedition.

Then the memories vanished.

Not erased.

Declined.

Marr gripped the docket table until her fingers hurt.

"Close it."

Vale looked at her.

"The adjudicator said—"

"Not the hatch. The alternatives."

"How?"

Marr looked at his slate.

"Complete your work order."

Vale stared at her as if she had suggested repairing a reactor with devotional pastry.

"It says introduce an alternative."

"Then introduce one."

"Which?"

"A small one."

"How small?"

Marr looked toward the impossible stations.

"Something boring."

Vale's expression changed.

Maintenance understood boring.

Boring was pressure holding.

Boring was pumps turning.

Boring was a shift ending without anyone being on fire.

He stepped to the threshold.

Rhyse caught his shoulder.

"If you cross that door, I cannot guarantee you remain yourself."

Vale looked at her hand.

"Lady, I work maintenance. I haven't been myself since the second night shift."

He crossed.

Nothing happened.

Then everything happened somewhere else.

Vale reached the wooden desk.

The chair remained empty.

He picked up the stamp.

The Collector filled every reflection.

DO NOT.

Vale looked back.

"Is that an order?"

Marr answered immediately.

"No recognized authority established."

"Good."

Vale stamped the work order.

CORRECTIVE ACTION:

FILTER ELEMENT 7-C REPLACED WITH FILTER ELEMENT 7-D.

Marr blinked.

Krell stared.

Vhal made a soft mechanical sound.

"Those components are functionally equivalent."

Vale nodded.

"Exactly."

The alternatives vanished.

All of them.

The red door remained.

The Collector's geometry fractured across the glass.

It tried to reconcile the change.

There was nothing meaningful to reconcile.

Antegra could have contained Filter 7-C.

Antegra could have contained Filter 7-D.

Both states were valid.

Neither mattered.

The difference existed.

That was enough.

The Collector withdrew from the reflections.

The silence broke.

Ventilation returned.

Somewhere far away, a pump coughed.

A worker swore.

Antegra became itself again, insofar as anyone was presently willing to make that allegation.

Vale stepped back through the red door.

His slate chimed.

DISCONTINUITY REPAIR: COMPLETE.

STATUS: TEMPORARY.

He frowned.

"Of course it is."

Marr read over his shoulder.

A new line appeared.

NEXT INSPECTION: 71 YEARS AGO.

Vale closed his eyes.

"I hate this station."

Young Varik said, "You have said that before."

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Good. Let's keep it that way."

The elderly adjudicator approached the threshold.

Her fear had not disappeared.

It had become older.

"Now you understand why we never named it."

Marr shook her head.

"No."

The adjudicator looked surprised.

"No?"

"I understand why you fear it. I do not understand why it is here."

The adjudicator looked toward the red door.

"Because someone asked a question."

"Who?"

"We do not know."

"What question?"

The woman hesitated.

The brass automaton stamped.

QUESTION ADMISSIBLE.

The adjudicator glared at it.

Marr waited.

Finally the woman spoke.

"Before there was Antegra Station, before the seven moons were dead, before the Collector arrived, something in this system asked whether what had happened was the only thing that could have happened."

Nobody moved.

"And?" Marr asked.

"Something answered no."

The red door closed.

This time normally.

Ancient hinges creaked.

The latch clicked.

Vale immediately placed a maintenance seal across it.

Marr looked at the seal.

"Will that help?"

"No."

"Then why?"

"Procedure."

Marr nodded solemnly.

"Of course."

The brass automaton printed the hearing record.

FIRST HEARING — INTERIM FINDINGS.

COLLECTOR: RECOGNIZED RESPONDENT.

RESPONDENT PRIOR: PROVISIONALLY RECOGNIZED.

RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN RESPONDENTS: DISPUTED.

ORIGIN OF PETITION: DISPUTED.

ORIGIN OF ANTEGRA: DISPUTED.

CAUSAL ORDER: DISPUTED.

EDRIN VALE: PRESENT.

EDRIN VALE: DECEASED.

EDRIN VALE: NOT YET ASSIGNED.

The final line appeared more slowly.

NEXT MATTER:

WHO ASKED THE FIRST QUESTION?

Marr read it.

Old Varik read it.

Young Varik read it.

Vale refused.

"No," he said.

Marr looked at him.

"No?"

"I'm maintenance. That's research. Different department."

For several seconds nobody said anything.

Then, from somewhere deep inside Antegra Station, a printer began running.

One printer.

Then ten.

Then hundreds.

Then thousands.

Across the station, abandoned terminals awakened.

Archive machines began ejecting paper.

Personnel offices printed files for employees who had never existed.

Navigation stations produced charts of systems never visited.

Ecclesiarchal cogitators printed prayers to saints not yet canonized.

Maintenance terminals produced work orders older than humanity.

And in Governor Varik's office, a machine disconnected from every network on Antegra printed a single page.

Young Varik reached it first.

At the top was the seal of the Cafarron Corridor administration.

Beneath it:

INQUIRY OF ORIGIN.

SOURCE: EXTERNAL.

DATE RECEIVED: 71 YEARS PRIOR.

DESTINATION: ANTEGRA STATION.

Young Varik turned the page over.

There was one sentence on the back.

DOES ANTEGRA STATION EXIST?

Below it were two boxes.

YES.

NO.

Both had been checked.

And beneath both marks, in handwriting Selene Marr recognized as her own, someone had written:

QUESTION DEFECTIVE.

PLEASE REFILE.
