The fleet instruction office's next dispatch arrived during the same week.

One of the two postponed engine-room exercises had been restored to the training roster and completed under its original instructor. Its report was signed. The other had not been held. The pressure rig was now available after the scheduled damage-control assessment, but the qualified instructor and maintenance team were committed to an operational inspection until the next duty rotation.

Renault compared the new schedule with the earlier invoice.

"They've found the machine," he said. "Now they need the people."

Prite reached for the requisition form.

Darcelle put a hand on it.

"Read the last paragraph."

The fleet would not accept Academy funding as authority to recall personnel from operational duty. It would review a new assessment request only after the second displaced exercise had been completed, its own staffing office had confirmed a qualified counterpart and assessor, and the Academy had certified funding without taking time from the conventional course.

The bursar's corrected table still lacked provision for the additional independent assessor. Prite could ask the Headmaster to transfer money from another instruction account. He could not make that account's existing commitments disappear.

"What if we wait?" he asked.

"Then the fleet finishes its training," Renault said. "Possibly."

"And our cadets finish theirs," Darcelle added.

Prite turned to the empty date in the assessment request.

He had once taken an empty field as a challenge to his ingenuity. He now knew it might represent somebody else's actual work.

He wrote a question for the next seminar instead: what can a commander know about an ally's refusal, and what must remain unknown until that ally chooses to say more?

He did not schedule the seminar.

---

The Caldrake observer received the Academy's corrected custody summary by ordinary dispatch. The accompanying letter did not ask him to withdraw his challenge. It acknowledged that the receiving signature was missing, that the review had not found evidence of alteration, and that future record transfers would carry a named-custodian limitation until a funded two-person procedure was approved.

He could accept the finding or pursue a further challenge through the established channel. He was not required to like it.

Vane filed the dispatch receipt with the protection office. Priority Omega still applied. The three professors were still the subject of noble grievances and had not become safer because one of those grievances had been processed without violence.

In Hall Four, Prite watched a navigation class finish its ordinary approach exercise. The instructor had rejected a cadet's request for a second attempt because the next class needed the consoles. She had offered a written review instead. The cadet was disappointed and took the offer.

Nobody called the refusal a strategic breakthrough.

Prite remained until the students had left. He checked the room booking, the maintenance slate and the unfinished request from fleet instruction. There was enough information to write another clever lesson. There was not yet enough authority to spend another officer's afternoon proving it.

For once, he left the room on time.
