# On the Fuzzy Edge

## Continuation III — Lower Ossuary Nineteen

**Based on the work of: On the Fuzzy Edge.**

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The cathedral possessed seven maps of its underworks.

None agreed.

Javard considered this encouraging.

A conspiracy could falsify a map. Age could produce seven.

Sub-Dean Caldris spread them across the records table while Archive Servitor Nine continued feeding parchment into its reader behind them.

Scratch.

Click.

Scratch.

Click.

The servitor had not spoken again.

The bell beneath the cathedral had not struck again either.

Javard disliked both facts equally.

"This one is oldest," Caldris said.

"How old?"

"Before the sump collapse."

"Useful."

"This one is newest."

"After?"

"After the condemnation."

"More useful."

Caldris frowned.

"The newer map omits the condemned galleries."

"Exactly."

The El Shaban factor beside Javard leaned over the plans.

His name was Serik.

Javard had known that for nine days and had deliberately avoided using it in the records chamber. Covers survived partly because everyone involved resisted the human urge to demonstrate familiarity.

Serik tapped a faded line.

"If this is the old maintenance gallery and this is the new retaining wall—"

"It isn't," Caldris said.

Serik looked at him.

"The wall?"

"The line. That is a drainage culvert."

"It says maintenance."

"The notation changed after the Third Foundation Survey."

"When was that?"

"Two hundred and twelve years ago."

Serik stared at the map.

"Was the culvert there then?"

"Yes."

"So why did they rename it?"

Caldris looked offended by the question.

Javard folded his arms.

"Because someone was paid to conduct a survey."

Caldris considered.

"Probably."

There were moments when the Imperium became almost comprehensible.

They selected three routes.

The first required opening a welded plague barrier.

The second passed through a collapsed cistern.

The third descended through a reliquary furnace, crossed an abandoned drainage gallery and reached the crypt level through what the oldest map called a bone lift.

Serik looked at Javard.

"Third?"

"Third."

"Because it is safest?"

"No."

"Shortest?"

"No."

Caldris said, "The plague barrier has an Ecclesiarchal seal."

Serik nodded.

"Third."

Progress.

---

Javard changed clothes.

Not dramatically.

The El Shaban coat stayed upstairs.

So did the commercial identification.

He wore plain field robes beneath a weathered protective mantle, respirator at his belt, lamp at his shoulder and his rosette under the inner fold where it could be reached without being advertised.

Serik carried a shotgun.

Caldris carried keys.

Javard considered this a fair distribution of institutional authority.

At the reliquary furnace, they acquired a fourth member.

Sister-Medicae Halva had grey hair, a scar across her lower lip and the weary expression of someone who had spent thirty years explaining to priests that incense did not sterilize surgical instruments.

"You are not going below without medicae support," she said.

Javard looked at Caldris.

"I did not request—"

"No," Halva said. "He did."

Caldris examined his keys.

Javard looked back at the medicae.

"Why?"

"Fungal bloom. Gas. Old sump water. Rust. Bone dust. Structural collapse."

"Anything dangerous?"

Halva stared at him.

Serik looked away.

Caldris coughed.

Javard nodded.

"Good. You were warned."

Halva handed him a respirator cartridge.

"This one works."

He inspected the seal.

"What about mine?"

"It is seventeen years past recertification."

Javard looked at Caldris.

The sub-dean looked at the furnace.

"Cathedral stores."

"Of course."

Javard replaced the cartridge.

The furnace access door opened on the fourth key.

Beyond it, a stair descended through heat-stained stone.

The air changed after twenty steps.

Incense disappeared first.

Then warmth.

Then the cathedral itself.

Javard had spent enough time inside Imperial buildings to know that large institutions developed geological layers. Offices became storerooms. Storerooms became sealed corridors. Corridors became walls. Walls became legends. Eventually someone discovered a room behind a shrine and declared it ancient, although the original construction order remained trapped in a filing cabinet three floors above.

The underworks were older than the current cathedral.

Not sacred old.

Functional old.

Brick channels.

Iron pipes.

Ceramic drains.

Maintenance alcoves with hooks where tools had once hung.

The Imperium had built holiness on top of plumbing and then forgotten the plumbing.

Halva checked the air every hundred metres.

"Acceptable."

Serik looked at the black growth along the wall.

"That?"

"Do not eat it."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Then it is acceptable."

Javard liked her.

They reached the first collapse forty minutes later.

The ceiling had dropped across the gallery in a wedge of masonry and corroded reinforcement.

Caldris consulted the oldest map.

"We go left."

There was no left.

There was wall.

He checked the newer map.

"Ah."

Javard waited.

"The corridor was moved."

"When?"

"Unclear."

"Before or after the map?"

Caldris looked at him.

"That is an unnecessarily philosophical question."

Serik found the passage behind a row of ossuary niches.

Someone had bricked over the original opening and cut another six metres farther down.

The newer masonry carried a date stamp.

Twenty-two years ago.

The sump collapse.

Javard touched nothing.

"Why close this one?"

Caldris studied it.

"Containment."

"Of what?"

"Sump water."

Halva crouched.

"No."

They looked at her.

She pointed at the mortar.

"Dry."

Serik said, "After twenty-two years?"

"Inside the joint."

She scraped the edge with a steel probe.

Dust fell.

No staining.

"No water came through this."

Javard looked at the blocked passage.

The wall had been built at the time of the sump collapse.

That did not mean the sump collapse had required it.

"Other side?"

Serik asked.

Caldris checked the map.

"Lower ossuary access."

Javard looked at the alternate opening.

"Convenient."

Nobody disagreed.

---

The bone lift was not a lift.

It had been one.

The cage was gone.

The shaft remained.

A chain ladder had been bolted into the wall sometime after the machinery was removed.

Halva shone her lamp down.

"Thirty metres."

Serik leaned over.

"That all?"

"Would you prefer more?"

"No."

"Then thirty."

Caldris did not look into the shaft.

"I will remain here."

Javard looked at him.

The sub-dean held up the ring of keys.

"If you require a door opened, call."

"From thirty metres below."

Caldris considered.

"I will listen carefully."

Javard descended first.

The shaft smelled of wet stone and iron.

Halfway down, he found the first bone.

A human finger wedged behind a ladder bracket.

Old.

Dry.

Not attached to anything.

He left it there.

At the bottom, the lift opened into a circular chamber lined with niches.

Most were empty.

The few occupied ones bore ceramic name plaques.

Halva descended next.

Then Serik.

Their lamps crossed the room.

LOWER OSSUARY XIX had been painted over the arch in black.

Below it, someone had added a second notice.

**CONDEMNED — SUMP CONTAMINATION — NO DEVOTIONAL ACCESS.**

Serik looked at the floor.

"Dry."

"Yes," Javard said.

There was dust.

Seventeen years of it, perhaps more.

Except down the center.

A narrow track had been disturbed.

Not footprints.

Wheels.

Halva crouched.

"Old."

"How old?"

She looked up.

"I treat bodies."

"Approximate."

"Not yesterday."

Javard accepted this.

The track led through the arch.

Lower Ossuary Nineteen contained four hundred and twelve wall niches.

Javard knew because each had a number.

Only one had a name.

**EPHRAM SOL.**

Serik stopped.

"That was easy."

"No," Javard said.

The plaque was clean.

Everything around it was grey.

He approached without touching it.

The niche was at chest height.

Its closure stone bore no devotional seal.

No transfer seal.

No date.

Only the name.

Halva shone her lamp across the edges.

"Opened."

"When?"

She gave him a look.

"Not yesterday."

"Useful."

"I try."

Javard examined the floor.

Wheel marks ended beneath the niche.

There were no tracks leading away.

"Body came here."

Serik said, "Pending."

Javard looked at him.

"The record says pending."

"Yes."

"But the body came here."

"Something did."

Serik adjusted his grip on the shotgun.

Javard noticed.

"Do not become imaginative."

"I wasn't."

"You moved your finger toward the trigger."

"Professional pessimism."

"Acceptable."

Halva inspected the adjacent niches.

"These are empty."

"All of them?"

She moved her lamp.

"Near this one. Yes."

Javard stepped back.

The pattern became visible.

Sol's niche occupied the center of a cleared section.

Twenty empty spaces around it.

Not random.

A working perimeter.

Someone had expected access.

"Serik."

"Yes?"

"Watch the corridor."

He did.

"Halva."

"Still here."

"Can you tell whether there is a body inside without opening it?"

She looked at the stone.

"Not with certainty."

"Without certainty."

She placed two fingers against the lower edge.

Then sniffed.

Then took a thin probe from her kit and inserted it into a drainage gap beneath the niche.

Javard waited.

She withdrew it.

"Organic residue."

"Human?"

"Probably."

"One?"

She stared at him.

"Do you want me to invent an answer?"

"No."

"Then probably human."

Good.

Javard looked at Sol's name.

The transfer order had remained pending upstairs for seventeen years.

Down here, something had been delivered.

The record and the physical world had separated.

That interested him more than either alone.

Serik called softly.

"Movement."

Javard turned.

"Where?"

"Corridor."

No one explained what a corridor was.

They extinguished two lamps.

Halva moved behind the niche wall.

Javard took the opposite side.

Serik kept the shotgun low.

A light appeared at the far end.

Weak.

Yellow.

It approached slowly.

Metal clicked against stone.

Then a figure entered the ossuary.

Not a monster.

A servitor.

Small.

Maintenance pattern.

Its lower body rode on four narrow wheels. One arm ended in a brush, the other in a brass stamp.

It stopped.

Its optical unit rotated toward them.

Serik exhaled.

The servitor rolled forward.

Javard watched.

It ignored them.

At Sol's niche it stopped.

The brush arm extended.

It cleaned the plaque.

Once.

Twice.

The brass stamp pressed against a small paper card fixed beneath the niche.

Javard had not noticed the card.

The servitor withdrew.

The stamp had left a date.

Today.

Beneath seventeen earlier dates.

One per year.

Serik whispered, "What is it doing?"

Javard read the printed heading.

**TRANSFER READINESS INSPECTION.**

The servitor turned.

Javard stepped into its path.

It stopped.

"Unit designation."

The machine clicked.

"OSSUARY MAINTENANCE SIX."

"Authority."

"BURIAL OFFICE."

"Task."

"MAINTAIN TRANSFER DESTINATION."

"Transfer of whom?"

The optical unit turned toward Sol.

"EPHRAM SOL."

"Status?"

"PENDING."

Javard looked at the niche.

"Is Ephram Sol here?"

The servitor clicked.

"TRANSFER PENDING."

"Has anything been placed in that niche?"

"TRANSFER DESTINATION MAINTAINED."

Not an answer.

Javard tried again.

"Who cleans the plaque?"

"OSSUARY MAINTENANCE SIX."

"Who installed the plaque?"

"TRANSFER DESTINATION MAINTAINED."

Serik shifted.

The servitor's optical unit snapped toward him.

"UNAUTHORIZED WEAPON."

Serik looked at Javard.

Javard said, "Ignore it."

The servitor stamped another card.

**SECURITY DEFICIENCY NOTED.**

Serik stared.

"Can I shoot it now?"

"No."

The servitor rolled away.

"Why not?"

"It has become useful."

"It called me a deficiency."

"Professionally."

Halva emerged.

"Are you going to open the niche?"

Javard looked at it.

"No."

Serik made a sound of disbelief.

"We came all the way down here."

"Yes."

"We found the place."

"Yes."

"The record says the body never arrived."

"Yes."

"The niche appears occupied."

"Yes."

"And we're leaving it closed."

"For now."

Serik looked at Halva.

She shrugged.

"He is the Inquisition."

Javard ignored this slander.

He crouched beside the inspection card.

Seventeen dates.

Annual.

Same day.

He compared the most recent stamp to his chronometer.

Today was not the anniversary of Sol's death.

Nor the date on the transfer order.

He knew both.

"What is today?"

Serik frowned.

"Today."

"Liturgical."

Halva answered.

"Vigil of Saint Orphael."

Javard looked at the card.

"Every year?"

She checked the older dates.

"Same vigil."

Caldris's voice came faintly down the shaft through Serik's comm-bead.

"Interrogator."

Javard touched his receiver.

"Yes."

"You should come back."

"Reason?"

"Montpclair is awake."

Javard looked at Sol's niche.

"Condition?"

"Angry."

"Encouraging."

"There is more."

Javard waited.

Caldris lowered his voice.

"He asked for you."

That was less encouraging.

"By name?"

A pause.

"Yes."

Serik looked at Javard.

Javard looked back at the sealed niche.

Montpclair had not known his name.

Not officially.

The Pontiff had been his intended contact. Javard had planned to approach him after the sermon.

That conversation had never happened.

"Exact words."

Caldris answered.

"He woke during augmetic revision. He asked whether Interrogator Javard had opened Sol."

Nobody in the ossuary moved.

Javard looked at the niche.

Still closed.

Good.

"Who heard him?"

"Three medicae attendants. A confessor. Me."

"Did you answer?"

"No."

"Good."

"What do I tell him?"

Javard considered.

"Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"He asked a question. Questions survive."

Caldris sighed.

"I will tell him you are unavailable."

"That is not nothing."

"Then come back and tell him yourself."

The channel closed.

Serik said, "He knows about Sol."

"Apparently."

"He knows you."

"Apparently."

"Does that change anything?"

Javard looked at the sealed niche.

"Yes."

"What?"

"We still don't open it."

Serik frowned.

Javard pointed at the annual inspection card.

"Someone has spent seventeen years keeping this destination ready."

Then toward the shaft.

"And a man I never introduced myself to woke from surgery knowing I might be standing in front of it."

He turned away.

"Curiosity can wait five minutes."

Halva looked at the niche.

"Five?"

"Imperial five."

"How long is that?"

"Unclear."

They climbed.

At the top, Caldris was waiting.

His face had the expression of a man who had been given a message and deeply regretted possessing literacy.

"Before you ask," he said, "yes. I am certain."

Javard stepped off the ladder.

"Good."

"Good?"

"Uncertainty would require more paperwork."

They returned through the underworks.

The false sump wall remained dry.

The black fungus remained uneaten.

The cathedral grew warmer around them.

By the time incense returned to the air, Javard had decided what bothered him most.

Not that Montpclair knew Sol.

A Pontiff could inherit secrets.

Not that he knew an Interrogator was investigating.

Institutions leaked.

Not even that he knew Javard's name.

The Inquisition was less invisible than it preferred to believe.

It was the verb.

Opened.

Not found.

Not reached.

Not investigated.

Opened.

Montpclair had asked whether Javard had opened Sol.

As if Sol were not a man.

As if Sol were a container.

At the records chamber, Archive Servitor Nine was still working.

Scratch.

Click.

Scratch.

Click.

Javard stopped beside it.

"Demas."

Nothing.

"Sol."

The feeder stopped.

Javard waited.

The servitor printed a line.

**DO NOT OPEN UNTIL HE ASKS.**

Javard read it.

Caldris read it over his shoulder.

Serik swore softly.

Halva did not.

She merely looked tired.

"Who?" Javard asked.

The servitor resumed feeding parchment.

Scratch.

Click.

Scratch.

Click.

Javard folded the printed strip.

Upstairs, Pontiff Montpclair was waiting.

Below, Ephram Sol was pending.

Between them stood seventeen years of people who had apparently known the difference.

Javard put the strip inside his coat beside the burial transfer.

"Take me to the Pontiff."
