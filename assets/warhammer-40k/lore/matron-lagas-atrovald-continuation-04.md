# Matron Lagas Atrovald

## Continuation IV — The Empty Place

**Based on the work of: Matron Lagas Atrovald.**

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The first answer to whether Hesk Venn was dead was that he had never been paid.

Atrovald found this unsatisfactory.

The second answer was that he had been issued a blanket.

She found that worse.

Arthus Pell stood across her desk with a requisition ledger and a paper personnel sheet whose edges had softened from handling. Personnel had never enrolled Venn. The barracks had no bunk for him. Stores had issued one blanket and insulated gloves against his transfer position.

"Recipient signature?" Atrovald asked.

"Quartermaster's mark."

"That establishes the quartermaster was present."

"She is very clear on that point."

"Did she see Venn?"

"She issued to the escort as a group."

Atrovald examined the entry. The ink matched twelve adjacent requisitions. The quartermaster's pen had split on the same downward stroke. Nothing about the line demanded a miracle.

"Leave the original in Stores. Put a witness beside it."

"She thinks she's under investigation."

"Tell her we are investigating a transfer. She is entitled to the distinction."

Pell wrote the instruction on paper.

"Now," Atrovald said, "the death register."

---

The Interlocutum maintained four records of death. Medicae recorded bodies. Personnel recorded vacancies. The mortuary recorded arrivals. The rites office recorded who had been released to the Emperor, whether or not the other three agreed.

Venn appeared in none.

He appeared, without a name, in the casualty *inquiry* register.

A temporary clerk had opened a report concerning custody position fifty-three. The report stated that an auxiliary had failed to return with the transfer escort. An unsigned withdrawal followed. The inquiry was closed for want of a complainant.

"Was a body found?" Atrovald asked.

"No."

"A death certified?"

"No."

"Who withdrew the report?"

"No authority recorded."

Atrovald turned the sheet over. Its fold marks were worn flat, although it had been filed that morning.

An old folder could explain that. She would not promote a crease into a revelation.

"Bring me the duty book. Then the clerk."

"The duty book was reconciled."

"Excellent. We can discover against what."

---

Archivist Meret Sorn arrived with an objection of her own.

"The Logos observer wants the Mortuary-Seventeen box."

"On whose authority?"

"Joint consultation."

"Consultation is not possession."

Sorn had brought the original shelf report about the grey ceramic container marked PROVISIONAL SECOND. Three years ago she had filed it after the box moved racks without a signed order. The report had vanished after being marked corrected.

It had returned to the closed-record tray.

Atrovald read the old report. Beneath Sorn's signature and the routine closure mark, another hand had written:

**DO NOT RECONCILE LOCATION WITH CUSTODY.**

"That's advice," Pell said.

"It is a sentence," Atrovald answered. "We have not established who gave it."

Sorn kept two fingers against the paper.

"I want it to remain in Mortuary-Seventeen."

"Then it remains."

"Should we move the box to a safer vault?"

"No. The seal is evidence. Its ordinary location is evidence. We do not improve one by destroying the other."

Atrovald ordered a separate physical concordance note, signed by Sorn, Pell and herself. The Logos observer would receive notice that a custody dispute existed under joint consultation, not the hidden inscription or the container's precise rack. Sorn did not look relieved. She understood the difference between protection and secrecy, and knew that both could become accusations.

---

The clerk who opened the missing-guard inquiry was alive and working two floors above.

Atrovald interviewed him in a small room rather than bringing him through three ranks of armed wardens. He had been accused of nothing.

"I opened it because a guard was missing," he said.

"Who reported that?"

"A man from the escort."

"Name?"

"He had a numbered custody chit with the officer's mark. I didn't ask."

"Did he see Venn disappear?"

"He said there should have been fifty-three and there were fifty-two."

"Did he identify the missing man?"

"Not by name."

"Why was the inquiry withdrawn?"

"An officer came back and said the original count was wrong. I told him to sign the withdrawal."

"Did he?"

"He took the chit and left."

"Then why did you close it?"

"I didn't."

The clerk examined the closure mark. He was elderly and offended that his handwriting might be mistaken for someone else's.

"Not mine."

"Do you remember the reporting man?" Atrovald asked.

"Cold. He kept rubbing his hands. Said the transit lift had no heat."

Pell stopped writing.

Atrovald did not look at him.

"Do you remember his face?"

"Perhaps. I was busy."

She thanked him and sent him back to his work. Outside, Pell caught up.

"Venn complained about cold."

"According to your memory."

He flinched.

"I remember him."

"I know. I need your memory intact, not defended."

They walked in silence.

"Find the lift maintenance log," she said. "A broken heater is more likely to leave a service record than a vanished guard."

---

Null-Seven remained sealed. Witness Four remained on its desk. Predulum had eaten and the medicae report recorded no change in his condition.

Atrovald opened the speaking grille.

"Predulum."

"Are you letting me out?"

"No."

"Then I would like better food."

"Have you eaten?"

"Yes."

"Good."

She kept the inquiry sheet behind her back. He had not been shown it.

"Do you recall the escort that brought the sixth witness?"

"Too many guards."

"How many?"

"Fifty-three."

"Why?"

Predulum frowned.

"An empty place when they formed up. Rear rank, left of the bier. Someone stepped sideways. There was room for another man."

"Did you see anyone leave?"

"No."

"Did anyone tell you there was a disappearance?"

"I don't know what I knew before you shut me in."

He leaned closer. For once, the theatrical Lord Inquisitor was absent.

"Are you going to erase me again?"

"No order for sanitization has been issued."

"That is a bureaucrat's answer."

"It is the answer I can prove."

He looked at her a long time.

"Then keep the paper."

She closed the grille without asking which paper he meant.

The custody clerk recorded the time. Atrovald had him add that no document had been displayed or described before Predulum spoke. Four people now remembered a missing place. They were not necessarily four independent witnesses; all had occupied the same institution, heard the same rumours, and stood under the same authority.

She wrote that down too.

---

The transit lift had never possessed a heater.

Its refrigeration baffle protected temperature-sensitive transfers on the adjacent route. A repair slip from the previous week described a fault that made the lift unpleasantly cold.

Ordinary cold. An ordinary complaint.

The kind of detail a real guard might remember.

The kind of detail someone inventing a guard might borrow.

Atrovald placed the repair slip beside the unsigned tomorrow-dated instruction returning Venn to Null-Seven.

"Tomorrow," Pell said.

"Yes."

"What if that instruction is signed when tomorrow comes?"

"Then we will have a signed instruction to examine."

"What if you're asked to sign it?"

"I have already refused."

"Perhaps that's why it is unsigned."

"Or why it exists."

She summoned witnesses from two separate custody offices. They sealed the unsigned instruction in a transparent sleeve and recorded the present time from two independently maintained clocks. One was mechanical. The other belonged to the rites office, whose custodian disliked the Interlocutum enough to keep an exact account of every minute spent there.

The clocks disagreed by eleven seconds.

Atrovald preserved both readings.

She issued a separate notice in the ordinary personnel chain: Hesk Venn was to be sought as a potentially missing custodian, neither presumed dead nor presumed alive. No transfer in his name was to be accepted without an identifiable living officer assuming responsibility. The notice did not mention tomorrow.

"Won't that alert whoever wrote the instruction?" Pell asked.

"Possibly."

"Then why issue it?"

"Because if a man is missing, an archive that merely admires its own contradiction has failed him."

She signed the present-day notice.

Not the future order.

In Mortuary-Seventeen the container remained unopened. In Null-Seven Predulum remained confined with Witness Four. Somewhere in Stores a blanket was still charged to a man no personnel register acknowledged.

Atrovald placed the two orders in separate drawers.

For the first time since the provenance fracture began, she had issued an instruction she could explain without invoking the history of a book.

Find the missing man.

If he existed, he was owed more than a footnote.

If he did not, someone was owed an account of why the Imperium had begun issuing him gloves.
