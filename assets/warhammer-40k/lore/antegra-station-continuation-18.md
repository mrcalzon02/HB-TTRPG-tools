# Antegra Station

## Continuation XVIII — The Original Petition

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

Selene Marr did not sign.

This surprised Captain Ardan Krell sufficiently that he checked whether she was still breathing.

"You stopped."

"I noticed."

"You had the stylus down."

"Also noticed."

"Marr, I have watched you sign evacuation orders while the room was on fire."

"The evacuation order was not dated seventy-one years before my birth."

Krell considered this.

"Fair distinction."

The brass automaton waited across the table.

It had no face with which to express impatience. Imperial manufacture had solved that deficiency by giving it a posture.

The form remained between them.

ORIGINAL PETITION FOR INTERIM PROTECTION.

DATE OF FILING: SEVENTY-ONE YEARS PRIOR.

PETITIONER: ANTEGRA STATION.

AUTHORIZED REPRESENTATIVE: SELENE MARR.

The signature line was blank.

Young Talbor Varik leaned over Marr's shoulder.

"If you sign it, we create the petition."

Old Varik shook his head.

"Or confirm that she always did."

"That is worse."

"Most accurate things are."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse took the document by one corner.

"Do not touch the date."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were looking at it professionally."

Marr looked up.

"That is how I look at everything."

"Precisely."

Magos Hester Vhal extended a probe over the page without making contact.

A lattice of green numerals trembled in the air.

"Material analysis: paper stock Antegra administrative issue. Manufacture date: today. Ink present in headings: today. Temporal registration encoded in venue seal: seventy-one years prior."

Krell frowned.

"So it is new paper pretending to be old paper."

"That description also applies to most archives," Marr said.

The automaton tapped the table once.

Seven minutes had become six.

Marr turned the form over.

The reverse was blank except for a filing instruction.

DO NOT DETERMINE CAUSE.

She read it twice.

Then a third time.

"Interesting."

Krell sighed.

"I hate when you say that."

"The petition does not ask us to explain why Antegra requires protection."

Rhyse looked at the line.

"It asks only that protection be requested."

"Exactly."

Old Varik understood first.

"The cause was never part of the original filing."

Marr nodded.

"Which means the petition can precede the incident without describing it."

Young Varik stared at him.

"That does not make it sane."

"No," Marr said. "It makes it admissible."

The Collector appeared in the black observation panes.

Not fully.

A suggestion of geometry. A shape made by every reflection agreeing slightly too much.

The docket printer woke.

RESPONDENT REQUESTS IMMEDIATE EXECUTION OF ORIGINAL PETITION.

Marr smiled without humor.

"There it is."

Krell looked from the strip to her.

"It wants you to sign?"

"It wants an origin."

Vhal's mechadendrites became very still.

"A completed original petition establishes a singular causal record."

Rhyse drew her bolt pistol.

The gesture was ceremonial. Everyone present knew shooting the paperwork would only create exhibits.

"Then don't sign it."

The docket struck again.

RESPONDENT NOTES FAILURE TO FILE INVALIDATES INTERIM RELIEF.

Old Varik laughed once.

It was an ugly sound.

"There is the trap."

Marr folded her hands.

"Yes."

Sign, and give the Collector a clean origin.

Refuse, and erase the procedural foundation protecting Antegra.

Krell stared at the ceiling.

"I miss tyranids."

"You have never fought tyranids," Rhyse said.

"I miss them hypothetically."

Five minutes.

The brass automaton produced a second stylus.

Marr ignored it.

Witness Lysa Nineteen stood near the sealed outer hatch, eyes unfocused.

"The other station is listening."

Young Varik turned.

"The prior Antegra?"

"All of them."

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Marr finally said, "Define all."

Lysa swallowed.

"I can't. Every time I count, I remember another one."

The deck lights dimmed.

For an instant Marr saw Antegra Station not as steel and void-shielding but as overlapping silhouettes.

Stations within stations.

Some larger.

Some broken.

One burning.

One dark.

One whose central spine had been replaced by something resembling a cathedral built inside the skeleton of an animal too large to have existed.

In every version, seven dead moons waited outside.

In every version, somewhere, Talbor Varik was governor.

Not always the same Talbor Varik.

The vision vanished.

Krell was gripping the table.

"Did everyone see that?"

"Yes," said Rhyse.

"Good. I dislike private madness. It feels elitist."

The docket printer chattered violently.

ADDITIONAL PETITIONERS ASSERTED.

NUMBER: UNRESOLVED.

The Collector's reflection sharpened.

Marr looked at the form again.

PETITIONER: ANTEGRA STATION.

Singular.

There was the error.

Or the opportunity.

She drew the form toward herself.

Rhyse raised the pistol.

"Marr."

"I'm not signing it."

"You are holding the stylus."

"I am amending it."

Krell groaned.

"That has never once made me feel better."

Marr struck through a single word.

STATION.

Above it she wrote:

STATIONS.

The brass automaton froze.

The Collector moved in every pane at once.

The docket printer began hammering.

RESPONDENT OBJECTS TO MATERIAL ALTERATION.

Marr added a notation.

IDENTITIES AND NUMBER OF PETITIONERS DISPUTED.

RESPONDENT OBJECTS.

She added:

OBJECTION NOTED.

The printer stopped.

Krell stared at her.

"Can you note your own objections?"

"Apparently."

"That seems corrupt."

"Captain, corruption requires a functioning standard. We are presently negotiating one."

Four minutes.

The venue returned.

The elderly adjudicator appeared in the chair opposite Marr without transition, as though she had merely been omitted from the previous moment.

The naval officer stood behind her.

Neither looked pleased.

The adjudicator examined the amendment.

"You altered the petitioner."

"The evidence altered the petitioner. I corrected the form."

"That is not how original filings work."

"Then the original filing was defective."

Silence.

Even the Collector seemed to appreciate the audacity.

The elderly woman looked at Marr for a long time.

"Do you understand what you are asserting?"

"That no singular Antegra can be established as the original petitioner."

"And therefore?"

"The case has no unique origin."

The woman's expression hardened.

"And therefore it may never close."

Marr glanced at the Collector.

"That appears preferable to the alternative."

The naval officer spoke for the first time.

"You would condemn every Antegra to an unresolved proceeding."

"They appear already condemned. I am attempting to improve the paperwork."

Krell covered his mouth.

Rhyse did not.

She almost laughed.

Three minutes.

The adjudicator pushed the amended petition back.

"It still requires authorization."

Marr looked at the signature line.

SELENE MARR.

Then at the date.

Seventy-one years ago.

"No," she said.

The Collector brightened.

The adjudicator's eyes narrowed.

Marr turned the page sideways.

"It requires authorization by the petitioners. I am not the petitioners."

"You are their representative."

"According to a form whose validity is the matter under review."

The docket printer emitted one small click.

Krell whispered, "I think you hurt it."

Marr continued.

"If I sign as representative, I establish my authority before the authority exists. That gives the respondent precisely the causal anchor it wants."

Vhal emitted a low binharic trill.

"Circular authorization."

"Yes."

Old Varik leaned forward.

"Then who signs?"

Marr looked toward the station around them.

Eight million people.

Contradictory records.

Duplicated names.

Dead workers still drawing ration allotments.

Children registered before their parents arrived.

Decks that disagreed over which century's numbering system they used.

Antegra had spent generations becoming an administrative catastrophe.

For once, this was an asset.

"Everyone," Marr said.

The adjudicator went pale.

"That is not practical."

"Excellent."

She turned to Vhal.

"Open the petition to distributed countersignature. No central ledger. No reconciliation. Local marks only."

Vhal's lenses contracted.

"Estimated signatures: eight million, subject to contradictory census records."

"Include the contradictory records."

"Estimated signatures: eleven million."

"Better."

The Collector struck the station.

Not physically.

Every clock on Antegra became 04:17.

Every chronometer.

Every shift bell.

Every machine cycle.

Every pulse counter in Vhal's body.

Time became unanimous.

The deck shuddered.

Lysa screamed.

The Collector had found certainty.

Marr looked at the synchronized clocks.

"Krell."

"Yes?"

"What time is it?"

He understood.

"04:12."

Rhyse said, "04:19."

Young Varik said, "03:58."

Old Varik closed his eyes.

"Seventy-one years ago."

Vhal began transmitting contradictory timecodes across every maintenance channel.

The station joined in.

Workers shouted different hours into vox pickups.

Clerks reset chronometers by hand.

Priests declared local noon by ecclesiastical authority.

Somebody in Hydroponics announced it was Tuesday.

It was not Tuesday.

This was considered helpful.

The clocks began to disagree.

The Collector recoiled.

The petition propagated.

Not as a document.

As a question.

DO YOU ASSERT THAT ANTEGRA REQUIRES PROTECTION WITHOUT AGREEING WHAT ANTEGRA IS?

Across the station, answers returned.

YES.

YES.

NO, BUT ONLY BECAUSE FORM 19-B WAS MISSING.

YES.

WHO IS ASKING?

YES.

REQUEST OVERTIME AUTHORIZATION.

YES.

One answer came from Deck Twenty-Seven under the name Edrin Vale.

YES.

Marr stopped breathing.

Edrin Vale had been erased.

The Collector noticed too.

Every reflection snapped toward the same nonexistent point.

The adjudicator stood.

"Do not reconcile that signature."

Vhal severed three data conduits with a plasma cutter.

"Done."

The counters continued.

One million.

Three million.

Seven million.

Ten million.

The number exceeded Antegra's population and kept rising.

Signatures arrived from the prior station.

From stations nobody remembered.

From New Presidio.

Marr saw that name only once before the system deliberately corrupted the routing header.

She did not open the attached transmission.

Some archives deserved patience.

The final count refused to settle.

The brass automaton stamped the petition.

AUTHORIZED BY PETITIONERS.

DATE OF FILING:

The stamp hesitated.

Then printed:

DISPUTED.

The Collector vanished from the windows.

Not defeated.

Denied an origin.

The elderly adjudicator sat down slowly.

"You understand this petition now exists at every point from which a petitioner asserted standing."

Marr nodded.

"Including seventy-one years ago."

"Including times that may not belong to your history."

"Yes."

"You have made the case retroactively distributed."

"Yes."

Krell looked between them.

"Is that bad?"

The adjudicator considered him.

"Captain, there are categories of bad for which your language has not yet developed adequate tenses."

"Wonderful."

The docket printer woke one final time.

ORIGINAL PETITION RECEIVED.

ORIGIN: DISPUTED.

PETITIONERS: DISPUTED.

CAUSE: DISPUTED.

RELIEF: INTERIM.

STATUS: OPEN.

Then another line appeared.

FIRST HEARING: PENDING.

Marr frowned.

"We just had the hearing."

The elderly adjudicator looked toward the sealed hatch.

"No."

A knock came from the other side.

Not three measured knocks this time.

One.

Weak.

Human.

The brass automaton produced a new docket slip.

WITNESS REQUESTS ENTRY.

IDENTITY: EDRIN VALE.

STATUS: DECEASED.

STATUS: NEVER EXISTED.

STATUS: PRESENT.

Krell drew his sidearm.

Rhyse raised hers.

Vhal powered the plasma cutter again.

Marr put down the stylus.

"Well," she said, "at least his paperwork is in order."

The hatch began to open.
