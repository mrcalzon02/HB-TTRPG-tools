# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXIV — The Forwarding Address

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The stars changed.

Selene Marr had always considered stars among the more dependable pieces of Imperial infrastructure.

They were remote, murderous, largely indifferent to paperwork, and generally remained where the charts claimed they were.

Antegra Station's new stars had failed the last requirement.

They were wrong.

Not unfamiliar.

Wrong.

Captain Ardan Krell stood at the observation blister with both hands behind his back and the expression of a man attempting to intimidate astronomy.

It was not working.

"Navigation?"

The auspex officer swallowed.

"No correspondence, Captain."

"None?"

"Not within the Cafarron Corridor. Not within any neighboring subsector. The cogitator has attempted stellar matching against every chart available aboard station."

Magos Hester Vhal's mechadendrites clicked against the deck.

"Correction. It has attempted matching against every chart it currently admits exists."

Krell looked at him.

"Is that distinction necessary?"

"On Antegra?"

Krell closed his eyes.

"Point conceded."

Marr entered carrying the forwarding form in a sealed evidence sleeve.

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse followed her.

Rhyse looked through the blister.

"Where are we?"

Marr placed the sleeve on the chart table.

"That is presently a defective question."

"I expected you to say that."

"Then you are learning."

Old Talbor Varik was already at the table.

Young Talbor Varik arrived four seconds later.

Both stared through the blister.

Neither spoke.

Marr noticed.

"You recognize something."

Old Varik shook his head.

"No."

Young Varik said, at exactly the same moment, "Almost."

They looked at each other.

Marr pulled out a chair.

"Excellent. We have begun with contradictory testimony before breakfast. The day may yet be salvageable."

The brass hearing automaton stamped.

LOCATION OF PETITIONER: DISPUTED.

Krell glared at it.

"We know."

STAMP.

ACKNOWLEDGED.

The station had not translated through the warp.

That was Vhal's first conclusion.

No Gellar transition had occurred. No navigator had reported passage. No warp alarms had sounded. The station's clocks had not skipped, duplicated, reversed, or begun counting in liturgical fractions.

By Antegra standards, the relocation had been almost offensively tidy.

Its orbit was gone.

Its local traffic was gone.

The seven dead moons were gone.

The Collector was gone.

Respondent Prior remained misfiled.

The faceless end of undelivered things was nowhere visible.

And eight million people had awakened beneath a sky nobody could name.

At 06:31 station time, the first external signal arrived.

It was not a distress call.

It was not a challenge.

It was not Imperial.

It consisted of a single burst of machine code.

Vhal translated only the protocol layer.

"Routing acknowledgment."

Marr looked at him.

"Addressed to whom?"

"Antegra Station."

"From whom?"

"Field absent."

"Origin?"

"Field absent."

"Destination?"

Vhal paused.

"Field disputed."

Marr rubbed her forehead.

"At least the local bureaucracy speaks our language."

A second transmission arrived.

This one was Low Gothic.

DO NOT STATE YOUR LOCATION.

Krell read it twice.

"We don't know our location."

Marr nodded.

"For once ignorance is operational security."

Rhyse pointed at the message.

"Who sent it?"

"Unknown."

"Can we answer?"

"No."

"Because answering constitutes address?"

"Because the last time we answered an impossible letter we acquired a station-wide metaphysical filing dispute. I am introducing a temporary policy of caution."

Krell raised an eyebrow.

"You?"

"Do not make me rescind it."

The third signal was visual.

A light appeared beyond the blister.

Then another.

Then nine more.

Ships.

At extreme range.

They emerged one by one from behind nothing Antegra's sensors could identify.

Vhal examined the returns.

"Mass readings inconsistent."

"How inconsistent?" Krell asked.

"The lead vessel masses between eight thousand and fourteen million tonnes depending upon which sensor is queried."

"Weapons?"

"Unknown."

"Drive signature?"

"Unknown."

"Imperial construction?"

Vhal's lenses narrowed.

"Question defective."

Marr smiled faintly.

"He's learning too."

The ships did not approach.

They arranged themselves around Antegra at enormous distance.

Not a blockade.

A perimeter.

Facing outward.

Krell saw it first.

"They're not watching us."

Rhyse followed his gaze.

"They're watching for something coming after us."

The observation blister darkened automatically as a pale flare crossed the distant stars.

For half a second, Marr saw three separate silhouettes where the lead vessel should have been.

Three hulls.

Three configurations.

Three possible names waiting at the edge of recognition.

Then the image stabilized.

Marr turned away before anyone could speak them.

"No identifications."

Old Varik looked at her.

"You saw it."

"I saw insufficient evidence."

"Selene—"

"No. We have spent the better part of a day preventing hostile reality from becoming singular. We are not going to undo that because a silhouette feels familiar."

The automaton stamped.

IDENTIFICATION DEFERRED.

Old Varik laughed once.

It was not a happy sound.

"You really can litigate anything."

"Only things that exist."

Young Varik coughed.

Marr corrected herself.

"Or can be made to appear on a docket."

At 06:44 the first citizen disappeared.

Not physically.

That would have been simpler.

Citizen Halden Prey remained seated at breakfast with his wife and two children.

His ration account vanished.

His habitation assignment vanished.

His employment record vanished.

His marriage remained valid but no longer named a spouse.

His children retained a father but their birth records refused to specify who he was.

The local medicae cogitator listed him as an unidentified visitor suffering acute bureaucratic distress.

At 06:46 it happened to twelve more people.

At 06:48, three hundred.

Marr reached Central Records as the first queue formed.

People were frightened.

Imperial citizens were accustomed to being hungry, overworked, taxed, conscripted, irradiated, condemned, misplaced, and occasionally eaten by things their superiors insisted did not exist.

They were less accustomed to becoming administratively hypothetical before lunch.

Clerk-Militant Osric Dane met Marr at the counter.

"We have a classification failure."

"How many?"

"Eight hundred and rising."

"Pattern?"

"None."

"There is always a pattern."

"Then it is withholding cooperation."

Marr approved of Dane.

He had survived twenty-nine years in Central Records without developing optimism.

Vhal arrived carrying a spool of fresh printout.

"Correlation identified."

Marr took it.

Every affected citizen shared one administrative property.

Their place of birth was outside Antegra Station.

Rhyse read over her shoulder.

"The station has been forwarded. They haven't."

Marr looked at the growing queue.

"No. Their records haven't."

The difference mattered.

People were still here.

Their paperwork was trying to remain behind.

The forwarding order applied to ITEM: ANTEGRA STATION.

It had never defined what Antegra Station contained.

Marr felt the familiar irritation that accompanied discovering an existential crisis caused by imprecise nouns.

"Seal Central Records."

Dane blinked.

"Against whom?"

"Everyone."

"Including us?"

"Especially us."

She turned to the automaton.

"Emergency motion. Define Antegra Station for purposes of forwarding as the station, all attached structures, all embarked vessels unable to depart safely, all inhabitants presently aboard, all recognized residents temporarily absent, all records necessary to establish those persons, all property necessary to sustain them, and all contradictory historical versions currently under protective order."

The automaton's stamp hovered.

Rhyse stared at Marr.

"That is broad."

"I am trying to move a city through a postal loophole. Precision has become expensive."

STAMP.

PROVISIONALLY GRANTED.

Across Central Records, cogitators screamed.

Halden Prey's name reappeared.

His ration account returned.

His marriage acquired a spouse.

His children reacquired a father.

His employer immediately issued him a notice for thirty-two minutes of unauthorized absence.

Marr examined the notice.

"Good."

Prey stared at her.

"Good?"

"You exist sufficiently to be punished. Within the Imperium, Mister Prey, that is nearly a certificate of citizenship."

The relief lasted eleven minutes.

At 07:03 the perimeter ships fired.

Not at Antegra.

Outward.

Space beyond them rippled.

The stars there did not disappear.

They were crossed out.

Black horizontal bars spread across the heavens one after another, exactly like the pale writing that had covered the faceless reflection in Annex Nine.

Krell's voice came over the vox.

"Marr. Observation blister. Now."

She arrived to find every weapon station aboard Antegra active.

"What are we looking at?"

Krell pointed.

Beyond the perimeter, the dark was becoming rectangular.

Vhal spoke quietly.

"The disposal mechanism followed the forwarding route."

Rhyse's hand rested on her holster.

"Can those ships stop it?"

One of the distant vessels vanished.

Not exploded.

Not destroyed.

Its sensor return changed to UNDELIVERABLE.

Then nothing.

Krell answered for everyone.

"Apparently not."

The printer behind Marr clicked.

Nobody had installed a printer in the observation blister.

There was one there now.

A page emerged.

FROM: ORIGIN

YOU WERE NOT SUPPOSED TO BRING THAT.

Marr read it.

"The instructions were incomplete."

Another page.

THAT IS NOT A DEFENSE.

"It has been for the Administratum for ten thousand years."

A third page emerged after a longer pause.

FAIR.

Krell stared at the exchange.

"Are you arguing with the past?"

"Yes."

"Is it helping?"

"Marginally."

The faceless darkness advanced through the stars.

The perimeter tightened.

Three distant ships became five.

Then two.

Then eleven.

The sensors could not agree how many defenders existed.

Marr understood why they had survived as long as they had.

They were doing exactly what Antegra had done.

Refusing to provide one answer.

Old Varik whispered, "This is the place."

Marr looked at him.

He did not say a name.

Good.

"The place with three names?"

He nodded.

"I think so."

"Think?"

"I remember arriving here. I remember never arriving here. I remember being told we had already left."

Young Varik had gone pale.

"I remember none of that."

"Also useful," Marr said.

The forwarding form inside its evidence sleeve began to burn.

Not with flame.

The ink vanished from the bottom upward.

DESTINATION DISPUTED disappeared first.

Then FORWARD.

Then the blank address lines.

The dead-letter end was erasing the route behind them.

Rhyse saw it.

"If it removes the forwarding order?"

"We become undeliverable again."

"And then?"

Marr watched another perimeter ship become nothing.

"I suspect we discover why the phrase dead letter contains the word dead."

The hearing automaton rolled into the blister under its own power.

It stamped the deck.

EMERGENCY HEARING RESUMED.

Marr looked down at it.

"On what matter?"

STAMP.

VENUE.

"Venue is disputed."

STAMP.

CORRECT.

A second stamp.

VENUE THEREFORE PRESERVED.

Marr froze.

Then smiled.

Rhyse recognized the expression.

"You found something."

"The destination is disputed."

"Yes."

"Which means the dispute itself is part of the destination."

Krell looked between them.

"Translate that into something a person with a weapon can use."

Marr pulled the forwarding form from its sleeve.

Half the text was already gone.

She wrote across the empty destination field.

VENUE: UNDER ACTIVE DISPUTE.

The erasure stopped.

The faceless darkness stopped with it.

Every perimeter ship stabilized at once.

For three seconds the universe appeared to hold its breath.

Then the Collector appeared in the observation blister.

Krell drew his pistol.

Rhyse drew hers.

Marr did not.

The Collector looked through the blister at the dead-letter end.

NOT DESTINATION.

Marr nodded.

"Correct."

NOT ORIGIN.

"Also correct."

NOT RECIPIENT.

"We are making excellent progress."

The Collector turned toward her.

WHAT IS IT?

Marr held up the form.

"Venue."

The Collector was silent.

The hearing automaton stamped.

VENUE DISPUTED.

The Collector looked at the automaton.

Then at the stars.

Then at the faceless darkness waiting beyond the perimeter.

For the first time, Marr saw something like comprehension pass through its impossible surface.

The dead-letter end could dispose of undeliverable things.

It could follow a return route.

It could erase an address.

But a venue was not an address.

It was where a dispute was being heard.

And until the dispute ended, the venue existed because the hearing required somewhere to occur.

Marr looked toward the darkness.

"Motion to maintain venue pending resolution of Case 7-OMNIA-NULL."

STAMP.

GRANTED.

The stars flickered.

The black bars withdrew one degree.

Not defeated.

Stayed.

Imperial civilization had spent ten thousand years proving that postponement could become a form of architecture.

Antegra had merely weaponized the principle.

The printer produced another message.

FROM: ORIGIN

THAT WILL NOT HOLD FOREVER.

Marr wrote beneath it.

NEITHER DOES THE IMPERIUM.

She fed the page back into the printer.

It vanished.

A minute later the reply arrived.

FROM: ORIGIN

ALSO FAIR.

Old Varik laughed again.

This time there was something almost human in it.

The perimeter ships began transmitting.

Not names.

Coordinates.

Three sets.

All different.

All describing Antegra's present position.

Vhal placed them side by side.

"Mutually exclusive."

"Excellent," Marr said.

Rhyse frowned.

"You still refuse to identify this place."

"Correct."

"Even now?"

Marr looked through the blister at the three impossible coordinate solutions, the surviving perimeter vessels, and the dark edge waiting for their procedural protection to fail.

"Especially now. A name would be an address."

The automaton stamped.

SENSIBLE.

Marr pointed at it.

"We have discussed this."

A final signal entered the station.

Unlike the others, it used an Imperial encryption protocol.

Old.

Very old.

Older than Antegra's current charter.

Older than New Presidio's surviving records.

Vhal isolated it without decoding the origin header.

The message body contained six words.

YOU FORWARDED YOURSELVES HERE AGAIN.

Marr read it twice.

"Again."

Old Varik sat down.

Young Varik did not.

Neither looked surprised enough.

The next line appeared slowly.

THE FIRST HEARING NEVER ENDED.

Then a third.

WE HAVE BEEN HOLDING YOUR PLACE.

Outside, one of the perimeter ships turned toward Antegra.

Its running lights blinked in a pattern Old Varik recognized.

He covered his face with both hands.

Marr did not ask for the name.

Not yet.

The hearing automaton stamped once.

WITNESS RECALLED.

And somewhere beyond the glass, in a place with three coordinates and at least three names, something that had been waiting seventy-one years finally opened a dock.
