# Antegra Station

## Continuation XIII — The Early Arrival

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

At 04:07, something knocked on the outside of Dock Fourteen.

There was nothing outside.

Dockmaster Pell verified this twice, because the first verification had produced an answer he disliked and the Imperium had trained him from childhood to believe that sufficiently repeated paperwork could eventually force reality into compliance.

The second auspex sweep returned vacuum.

The third returned vacuum.

The fourth returned the outline of a man standing on the exterior side of the pressure hatch.

Then it returned vacuum again.

Pell stopped requesting additional sweeps. This was not because he had become satisfied. It was because he had become educated.

Talbor Varik arrived with Captain Ardan Krell, Senior Auditor Selene Marr, Magos Hester Vhal, Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse and Witness Lysa Nineteen at 04:08.

The knocking continued.

Three knocks. A pause. Three knocks. A pause.

Marr looked at the chronometer above the hatch.

"The warrant says 04:17."

Krell checked the charge on his pistol. "Perhaps it is early."

"Official transit is never early."

"That is what concerns you?"

"At present it is the most obviously impossible detail."

Rhyse stepped toward the inner hatch. "Nobody opens it."

Pell looked relieved.

"Nobody opens it," Rhyse repeated, "until we know whether opening it matters."

Pell's relief departed.

The knocking stopped. Every lumen in Dock Fourteen dimmed.

Lysa tilted her blindfolded face toward the sealed hatch.

"It isn't outside."

Krell swore. "Where is it?"

The child pointed at Varik.

"Almost here."

---

At 04:09, Dock Fourteen received a customs declaration.

No vessel had arrived. No courier had delivered it. The declaration emerged from a printer disconnected during Emergency Order Nine-Seventeen.

Marr caught the paper before it touched the deck and read it twice.

Her expression became professionally offended.

"This form is obsolete."

Varik stared at her. "Selene."

"Revision thirty-two was withdrawn forty-one years ago. It also lists the issuing authority as the Antegra Governor's Office."

Varik held out his hand.

PASSENGER: TALBOR VARIK.

POINT OF ORIGIN: NEW PRESIDIO.

POINT OF ENTRY: ANTEGRA STATION.

DECLARED GOODS: NONE.

UNDECLARED GOODS: ONE MEMORY, IMPERIAL PROPERTY, DISPUTED.

PURPOSE OF ENTRY: RETURN.

AUTHORIZED BY: TALBOR VARIK.

The signature beneath it was not the one Varik used now. It was the one he had used sixty-eight years earlier.

Krell leaned over his shoulder. "Forgery?"

Marr answered first. "No. I have spent the last three days studying his fraudulent signatures."

Varik looked at her. "There is a sentence I did not expect to hear during my governorship."

"You are no longer legally governor."

"Thank you."

"You are welcome."

The hatch knocked again. Four times.

Vhal's optical cluster contracted. "Pattern change."

"Meaning?" Krell asked.

"It has learned we are counting."

---

At 04:11, Varik ordered Dock Fourteen evacuated.

At 04:11:30, Marr countermanded him because Talbor Varik was not legally governor.

At 04:11:42, Rhyse countermanded Marr because she was an Inquisitorial legate and therefore possessed the ancient Imperial privilege of ending arguments by making them substantially worse.

The dock emptied. Varik, Krell, Marr, Vhal, Rhyse and Lysa remained.

The outer hatch status rune changed from SEALED to OCCUPIED.

There was still no pressure in the lock. No mass. No heat. No atmosphere.

But the lock insisted that someone was inside it.

Vhal interfaced with the dock cogitator.

"Identity challenge has begun."

"Against what?" Varik asked.

"You."

A second line appeared beneath OCCUPIED.

IDENTITY CONFIRMED: TALBOR VARIK.

Then a third.

IDENTITY CONFIRMED: TALBOR VARIK.

Then:

IDENTITY CONFLICT.

RESOLUTION REQUIRED.

Lysa began to cry quietly.

Rhyse knelt beside her. "What do you see?"

"Not see. Remember."

"What do you remember?"

"Being him."

Lysa turned toward Varik.

"Both of you."

---

The memory arrived before the man did.

Varik remembered New Presidio.

He had never been there.

He remembered standing beneath a vaulted transit concourse while emergency shutters descended one by one. He remembered a station announcement repeating his name. He remembered signing the customs declaration Marr now held.

He remembered sixty-eight years of waiting.

He remembered dying eleven times.

He remembered none of the deaths agreeing.

Varik staggered. Krell caught him.

"Talbor."

"I'm fine."

"You have said that during gunshot wounds."

"Then I am consistent."

"That is not reassuring."

The memory deepened.

New Presidio had not been hiding the duplicate Varik.

The duplicate had been hiding New Presidio.

For sixty-eight years he had split records, corrupted timetables, contradicted witness statements, altered docking histories and repeatedly made himself impossible to reconcile.

But New Presidio was too coherent. Too connected. Too well indexed.

Eventually the station had begun using him as a template.

Not copying his body.

Copying his contradiction.

Varik opened his eyes.

"New Presidio isn't broadcasting a message."

Vhal's mechadendrites went still. "Clarify."

"It is broadcasting a disagreement."

Marr understood first. "With whom?"

Varik looked at the hatch.

"The Collector."

The knocking stopped.

At 04:14, the inner hatch opened by itself.

There was no decompression.

Inside the pressure lock stood Talbor Varik.

Older. Thinner. His governor's coat had faded almost grey, one sleeve repaired with New Presidio station cloth. His hair was white. His left eye was a crude augmetic.

Marr checked the declaration.

"You are three minutes early."

The old man smiled. "No."

He stepped across the threshold.

The dock chronometer changed to 04:17. Every other clock still read 04:14.

"I have been exactly on time for sixty-eight years."

Krell raised his pistol. "Are you Talbor Varik?"

The old man considered this.

"That depends which department is asking."

Marr exhaled through her nose. "Unfortunately convincing."

Rhyse did not lower her weapon. "What did you bring back?"

The old Varik looked toward the deck, toward Substructure XVIII.

"The thing the city stole."

The younger Varik felt the inherited memories recoil.

"What is it?"

"A name."

"Whose?" Krell asked.

Old Varik looked at him.

"The Collector's."

Vhal made a sound like a prayer being strangled by mathematics.

"The city discovered that the Collector could reconcile anything it could correctly identify," old Varik said. "So they stole the one identifier it could not afford to lose. Not a title. Not a designation. Its own self-reference. They broke it into memories and hid the pieces inside themselves."

Rhyse's face hardened. "And when the city died?"

"The pieces survived in the people who remembered it."

Antegra.

Eight million contradictory human beings carrying fragments of an alien civilization that carried fragments of something older still.

Imperial recordkeeping had accidentally become a vault.

Marr looked faintly insulted by the theological implications.

"Then what was at New Presidio?"

Old Varik tapped his temple. "The index."

The younger Varik understood.

"You."

"Us."

The lights went out.

For one second Dock Fourteen vanished.

In the darkness, something vast noticed that a missing word had returned to the same place as its definition.

The lights came back.

Every display showed:

IDENTITY RESOLUTION AVAILABLE.

ACCEPT.

REFUSE.

Krell stared at the screens. "That seems unusually straightforward."

Marr drew her data-stylus. "Which means it is almost certainly fraudulent."

Then every display added a third option.

It was not written by the Collector. Vhal knew because the machine-code underneath it was Antegra's own.

DEFER PENDING REVIEW.

Marr smiled.

"There we are."

Rhyse looked from one Varik to the other. "What happens if we choose it?"

Marr touched the third option.

"We become the Imperium."

Across Antegra, eight million contradictory records opened at once.

The Collector attempted to resolve Talbor Varik.

Antegra filed an objection.

And somewhere beyond the station, something that had crossed stars to reclaim its stolen name encountered the oldest weapon humanity had ever perfected.

A pending case.
