# Antegra Station

## Continuation XII — Dead Air

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

New Presidio began broadcasting.

Antegra did not listen.

This required considerably more effort than the sentence suggested.

At 03:58:07 local station reckoning, every receiver on Antegra had activated. At 03:58:09, Senior Auditor Selene Marr ordered Central Audit isolated from the station vox-net. At 03:58:12, Captain Ardan Krell ordered the same thing, louder and with firearms. At 03:58:16, Magos Hester Vhal informed them that neither order mattered because the signal was not using the vox-net.

At 03:58:20, Talbor Varik pulled the power coupling from the nearest speaker with both hands.

The speaker continued talking.

It used his voice.

Older. Tired. Amused.

The same voice that had warned him not to come.

Then it began to say something else.

Witness Lysa Nineteen screamed.

Not in fear.

In command.

"DON'T HEAR IT."

The words struck the room harder than gunfire.

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse moved first. She seized a brass ceremonial censer from the wall and smashed the speaker grille until the voice became a wet metallic rasp.

It continued through Marr's data-slate.

Krell shot the slate.

Marr stared at the fragments.

"That was indexed."

"You may audit me later."

"I intend to."

The ceiling annunciator came alive.

Vhal tore its cable trunk open.

The voice moved to the emergency lumens.

Not through sound.

Through the faint electrical whine of their regulators.

Varik understood.

"It doesn't need equipment."

Vhal's mechadendrites froze.

"Correct."

"Then what is broadcasting?"

Lysa had both hands clamped over her ears.

"The station."

Nobody asked which station.

That had become another question with too many correct answers.

---

At 03:59, Antegra went deaf by decree.

Varik issued Emergency Order Nine-Seventeen, composed in thirty-two seconds and written by hand because every cogitator in Central Audit had begun displaying fragments of a transmission nobody had authorized them to receive.

NO CITIZEN SHALL KNOWINGLY RECEIVE, REPEAT, TRANSCRIBE, INTERPRET, SUMMARIZE, INDEX, CATALOGUE, ARCHIVE, TRANSLATE, CHANT, SING, MIME, OR OTHERWISE RENDER THE CURRENT NEW PRESIDIO BROADCAST.

Marr added MIME.

Krell had asked why.

"Because someone will."

Nobody argued.

The order spread by runners.

Bells were rung in hab districts where bells still existed. Where they did not, wardens struck pipes with tools. Dockmasters killed public address systems with cutting torches. Priests ordered congregations into hymns loud enough to drown anything that might speak between verses. Factory overseers discovered, with something approaching religious ecstasy, that they finally possessed legal authority to make machinery noisier.

Across Antegra, eight million people were instructed not to listen to a message they had not been told existed.

This produced predictable results.

By 04:03, seventeen thousand people had attempted to find it.

By 04:05, the Department of Necessary Omissions had begun issuing contradictory descriptions of the forbidden transmission.

One notice claimed it was an agricultural tariff revision.

Another described it as a devotional address concerning proper boot maintenance.

A third warned that unauthorized reception could result in retroactive reassignment to the corpse-starch reclamation guild.

That one proved unexpectedly effective.

Marr approved all three.

"Disinformation?" Krell asked.

"Public administration."

"There is a difference?"

"In theory."

---

The shuttle waited in Dock Fourteen.

Its flight plan said NEW PRESIDIO.

Its navigation cogitator said NEW PRESIDIO.

Its fuel allocation, departure warrant, docking authority, cargo manifest and emergency recovery instructions all agreed that Governor Talbor Varik was traveling to New Presidio.

The shuttle itself was pointed at an empty maintenance anchorage twelve thousand kilometres from Antegra.

Marr inspected the paperwork.

"This is fraud."

Varik tightened the seal on his coat.

"Yes."

"Good fraud."

"Thank you."

"I did not compliment you. I classified it."

Krell stood at the hatch with four armsmen.

"You are still not going alone."

"I am not going anywhere."

"Your paperwork disagrees."

"That is the point."

Rhyse arrived with Lysa and Vhal.

The child had strips of waxed cloth packed over her ears beneath the blindfold.

It did nothing.

She was trembling.

Varik crouched in front of her.

"What is the broadcast saying?"

Rhyse's expression sharpened.

"Do not ask her that."

"I am asking whether she can identify its purpose, not its content."

Lysa answered before Rhyse could stop her.

"It wants to be remembered correctly."

The dock seemed colder.

Varik looked toward the hull beyond which New Presidio lay somewhere across the Corridor.

"The other me?"

"No."

"The city?"

"No."

Lysa turned her covered eyes toward him.

"New Presidio."

Krell swore softly.

Rhyse did not.

Inquisitors generally preferred to save profanity for doctrinally significant occasions.

"Stations do not want things," Rhyse said.

Lysa smiled without humor.

"Antegra does."

Nobody had a useful reply.

---

At 04:00 exactly, the false shuttle departed.

Varik remained on the dock.

The shuttle carried no crew.

Its passenger compartment contained a governor's coat, a sealed blood sample, three handwritten pages in Varik's hand, a vox recording of him complaining about municipal heating allocations, and enough authenticated administrative metadata to convince an Imperial archive that Talbor Varik had personally boarded.

Marr had insisted on the complaint.

"Why that recording?" Krell had asked.

"Identity markers."

"He has official speeches."

"Nobody sounds like themselves in an official speech."

Varik had been forced to concede the point.

The shuttle cleared Antegra's docking envelope.

For seven seconds, nothing happened.

Then every impossible reception on the station stopped.

Silence arrived so suddenly that Central Audit seemed to inhale.

Vhal checked three instruments.

"The shared-state linkage has shifted."

"To the shuttle?" Varik asked.

"Partially."

Marr looked pleased in the severe manner of an auditor whose forged document had survived inspection.

"We lied to it."

Rhyse looked at her.

"Do not celebrate."

"I am not celebrating."

"You are smiling."

"That is professional satisfaction."

"It is disturbing."

"Thank you."

Then the shuttle vanished.

No flash.

No warp signature.

No explosion.

One moment it occupied Dock Control's tactical display.

The next, its marker appeared beside NEW PRESIDIO.

Not at the system edge.

Not at a translation point.

Docked.

The timestamp remained 04:00:07.

Krell stared at the display.

"That is impossible."

Vhal made a clicking sound through her respirator.

"We should retire that phrase."

The broadcast resumed.

Only this time it came from the empty shuttle's transponder.

Across a distance it had not crossed.

Varik's older voice spoke once.

"That was clever."

Marr whispered, "Damn."

The voice continued.

"It was clever last time too."

Then the transponder died.

---

Varik did not remember doing it last time.

That was becoming less informative by the hour.

Rhyse ordered the old Severance archive reopened under her personal seal. Vhal searched for transit anomalies. Marr searched for warrants. Krell searched for something he could shoot.

Krell finished first.

There was nothing.

Marr finished second.

There was a warrant.

Of course there was.

She placed it on Varik's desk.

SPECIAL MOVEMENT WARRANT 88-THETA-VARIK

The same warrant.

The departure field now read:

COMPLETED.

The destination field had changed.

It no longer said NEW PRESIDIO.

It said ANTEGRA STATION.

Varik read it twice.

"We sent the shuttle away."

"Administratively," Marr said.

"Physically."

"Apparently less relevant."

Rhyse leaned over the document.

A new line formed beneath their hands.

RETURN PASSENGER: TALBOR VARIK

Krell immediately drew his pistol.

"Finally."

"Captain," Varik said.

"Something is returning you to a station you never left. I have been patient."

"By your standards."

"Exactly."

Lysa began crying.

Quietly.

Rhyse knelt beside her.

"What do you see?"

"Nothing."

"What do you hear?"

"Nothing."

"Then why are you crying?"

The child pointed toward Antegra's main observation blister.

"Because New Presidio stopped."

Varik felt the meaning before he understood it.

Not the broadcast.

New Presidio.

Vhal turned toward the long-range auspex.

The station's position was still present in the navigation tables.

Its beacon was still registered.

Its orbital solution remained valid.

Its mass shadow remained exactly where physics insisted it should be.

But every telescope looking toward New Presidio showed empty stars.

Every astropath asked to identify it returned the same answer.

There was no silence.

Silence was something.

There was simply no New Presidio to perceive.

Marr looked at the warrant.

The ink changed again.

RETURN PASSENGER: TALBOR VARIK

ORIGIN: NEW PRESIDIO

STATUS: IN TRANSIT

ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 04:17

Krell checked the chronometer.

04:06.

Eleven minutes.

Varik stared at the empty place where another Imperial station had been.

He had spent sixty-eight years preserving Antegra by making it difficult to remember.

Somewhere along the way, another version of him had apparently done the same thing to New Presidio.

Only better.

The dry thought came uninvited.

There was always someone better at one's job.

Usually they were younger.

Occasionally they were oneself.

"Seal Dock Fourteen," Varik said.

Krell smiled for the first time that night.

"Now?"

"Now."

"Weapons free?"

Varik looked at the warrant.

At the line promising his own return.

At the empty stars where New Presidio had been.

"Not until we know which of me arrives."

Marr picked up her pry-bar.

"And if neither of you knows?"

Varik drew his father's saber.

"Then we shall have to compare paperwork."

At 04:07, something knocked on the outer hatch of Dock Fourteen.

There was nothing on the other side.
