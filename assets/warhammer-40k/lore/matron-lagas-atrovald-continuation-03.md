# Matron Lagas Atrovald

## Continuation III — The Record That Waited

**Based on the work of: Matron Lagas Atrovald.**

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

Atrovald did not answer the fourth knock.

She answered the seventh.

Not because seven had acquired significance. She refused to permit numbers that satisfaction.

The speaking grille beside Null-Seven clicked open.

"Predulum."

There was a pause.

"Matron."

"You have been striking an S-Rank vault door with your hand."

"I had noticed."

"Stop."

Another pause.

"Why?"

"Because if you damage yourself, I will have to document it."

Silence.

Then, faintly, "Cruel."

Atrovald closed the grille.

Pell stood beside her with three slates and the expression of a man who had spent the last hour discovering new categories of administrative despair.

"Well?"

"He has stopped."

"I meant the search."

"I know."

They walked.

The corridor outside Null-Seven had acquired a temporary custody station, two Logos observers, six Interlocutum wardens, a medicae servitor, and a clerk whose only assigned duty was to write down the time whenever anyone opened the speaking grille.

Atrovald had approved the clerk personally.

Digital records had become impolite.

She no longer trusted them to remain in the room after an argument.

Pell handed her the first slate.

"Witness Two."

"Reference?"

"Possibly."

"Then say possibly."

"Possible reference to Witness Two."

"Better."

They entered a side chamber normally used for classification disputes. Someone had removed the chairs.

Atrovald looked at Pell.

"Why?"

"Security."

"Chairs?"

"One had an unsecured hollow frame."

"All chairs have hollow frames."

"That was my position."

"And?"

"They removed all of them."

Atrovald remained standing.

The Imperium was safest when deprived of furniture.

Pell activated the slate.

The record was old enough that half its metadata had been migrated through four incompatible archival systems. The surviving text concerned a disposal action.

Not a book.

A container.

**OBJECT: TEXTUAL WITNESS, PROVISIONAL SECOND.**

**DISPOSITION: DESTROYED UNDER CONTEXTUAL AUTHORITY.**

Atrovald read the line below.

**DESTRUCTION CERTIFIED BY MASS.**

"Mass?"

Pell brought up the attached figure.

Atrovald stared.

"That is too heavy."

"Yes."

"How much?"

"For a codex of the recorded dimensions? Nearly three times."

"Container?"

"Excluded."

"Bindings?"

"Included in the estimate."

Atrovald scrolled.

The destruction had been conducted by furnace.

Ash collected.

Ash weighed.

Ash sealed.

No surviving textual fragments.

No picts.

No transcription.

No named authorizing Matron.

Naturally.

"Where is the ash?"

Pell changed panes.

"Still here."

Atrovald looked at him.

"Of course it is."

"Sub-Archive Mortuary-Seventeen."

"Why?"

"Someone classified it as human remains."

Atrovald waited.

Pell consulted the note.

"The ash contained calcium phosphate."

"Paper can contain calcium."

"I know."

"Did they?"

"Apparently not."

Atrovald handed the slate back.

"Good."

"Good?"

"It survived."

"As ash."

"That is a condition, not an argument."

Pell opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Progress.

They took the old stairwell.

Officially, it still did not exist.

Atrovald had begun to find this comforting. The stairwell's nonexistence was stable. Nobody had rewritten it since breakfast.

Mortuary-Seventeen occupied a narrow vault behind an ossuary index. The custodian on duty was a woman Atrovald knew by sight and not by name.

This was unacceptable.

"Name."

The woman stiffened.

"Archivist Second Class Meret Sorn, Matron."

Atrovald looked at Pell.

"Write it down."

Pell did.

Sorn watched this with concern.

"Have I done something?"

"No."

This did not reassure her.

"Show us disposal remains tagged Precipice of Thought."

Sorn's face emptied.

Not fear.

Recognition.

Atrovald noticed.

"You know the tag."

Sorn looked at the vault.

"I know the box."

"Why?"

"It keeps being moved."

Pell stopped writing.

Atrovald did not.

"By whom?"

"No one."

"That answer is usually expensive."

"I mean no one signs for it."

Sorn led them between mortuary shelves.

Human remains had an institutional advantage over books: people were reluctant to edit them.

The box sat on a lower rack.

Grey ceramic.

Lead seal.

Old wax.

A brass plate identified the contents as cremated remains of an unknown servant of the Senatorum Imperialis.

Beneath that, barely visible under later lacquer, was a second engraving.

**PROVISIONAL SECOND.**

Pell crouched.

"That was not in the index."

"No," Sorn said.

Atrovald looked at her.

"How often does it move?"

Sorn swallowed.

"I noticed it three years ago. It was on rack nine. Then rack eleven. Then six. I thought the servitors were mis-shelving."

"Did you report it?"

"Twice."

"What happened?"

"The first report closed as corrected."

"And the second?"

Sorn looked embarrassed.

"I couldn't find it."

"The box?"

"The report."

Atrovald felt the familiar cold pressure behind her ribs.

"Do you remember filing it?"

"Yes."

"Who else?"

"My assistant. He transferred out last year."

"Name."

Sorn gave it.

Pell wrote that down too.

Atrovald pointed at the box.

"Nobody touches it."

Sorn nodded.

"Pell."

"Matron?"

"Bring scales."

"We have scales."

"Bring old scales."

He understood.

Mechanical balance.

No noospheric link.

No automatic correction.

The sort of instrument that could be wrong honestly.

It took twenty-three minutes.

Atrovald spent them reading the physical shelf ledger.

The box had indeed moved.

Not often.

Once every few months.

Sometimes a year.

The handwriting changed with custodians. The rack number changed with it.

Nobody had signed the movements.

More interestingly, the movements were always recorded.

The archive knew where the box was.

It simply did not know why.

Pell returned with two servitors carrying a brass balance older than the current floor.

Sorn stared.

"Does that still work?"

Atrovald looked at her.

Sorn corrected herself.

"Has it been calibrated?"

"Recently enough."

They weighed the box.

Then weighed it again.

Then changed weights.

Then moved the balance.

Then weighed it a fourth time because Pell had become personally offended by the result.

The mass matched the ancient destruction certificate.

To within six grams.

"Open it?" Pell asked.

"No."

He nodded immediately.

Sorn looked surprised.

Atrovald saw the question.

"If the seal is part of the evidence, breaking it is also part of the evidence."

"What do we do?"

"We look before we alter."

Pell leaned closer to the brass plate.

"Matron."

He pointed to the lacquer.

There were scratches around the word SECOND.

Not random.

Letters beneath it.

Atrovald requested oblique lumen.

Sorn provided a hand lamp.

No machine-spirit.

Atrovald approved of her more with every minute.

The hidden inscription emerged slowly.

**PROVISIONAL SECOND — DO NOT DESTROY UNTIL FIRST IS PRESENT.**

Nobody spoke.

Pell finally said, "The destruction record says it was destroyed."

"Yes."

"The box says not to destroy it."

"Yes."

"The mass is the same."

"Yes."

"Do you think the book is in there?"

Atrovald looked at him.

He sighed.

"Defective question."

"Improving."

Sorn stared at the box.

"Should I move it to S-Rank?"

"No."

Both archivists looked at her.

Atrovald folded her hands.

"The box has survived here because everyone believed it was a dead clerk."

Pell understood first.

"Leave it dead."

"Precisely."

Sorn looked less certain.

"What about the index?"

"Do not correct it."

That word again.

Atrovald disliked how useful it had become.

"Add a sealed Matronal concordance note outside the ordinary index. Physical ledger only. You, Pell, and I sign."

Sorn hesitated.

"Logos?"

"No."

Pell glanced at Atrovald.

She saw the objection.

"Not yet."

That satisfied neither of them, which was appropriate.

The Logos Historica Verita had joint consultation.

It did not have automatic possession of every thought in Atrovald's head.

Atrovald had learned something from Predulum after all.

Jurisdiction mattered.

They returned to Null-Seven near second watch.

The clerk recorded the grille opening.

Predulum was sitting on the floor when Atrovald looked through.

Witness Four remained on the desk.

He had obeyed her order not to read further.

This surprised her enough that she did not mention it.

"Predulum."

He looked up.

"Have I been released?"

"No."

"Then I dislike the direction of this conversation already."

Atrovald held the slate where he could see it through the grille.

Not close enough to read.

His eyes narrowed.

"What?"

"We found a possible reference to Witness Two."

Predulum stood.

"Where?"

"Elsewhere."

"Matron."

"You are confined with a chronologically disputed accession under my seal dated tomorrow. Your entitlement to precise geography is temporarily reduced."

He accepted this with visible effort.

"What does it say?"

"Destroyed."

Predulum's expression sharpened.

"And?"

Atrovald almost approved.

"And the evidence is inconsistent."

"How?"

"No."

He stared at her.

She stared back.

Pell shifted his weight.

Predulum pointed toward Witness Four.

"You expect me to tell you what I remember while you withhold what you find."

"Yes."

"Why would I agree?"

"Because tomorrow you apparently trusted me enough to use my seal."

"Your seal."

"My point."

Predulum rubbed both hands over his face.

When he lowered them, the theatrical Lord Inquisitor had receded. The tired man underneath was less impressive and considerably more useful.

"I remember a furnace."

Atrovald did not move.

Pell's stylus did.

Predulum looked at it.

"Do not write yet."

Pell stopped.

Atrovald said, "Why?"

"Because I don't know if I remember it."

"Explain."

"I can see it. I can smell hot metal. There is ash on someone's gloves."

"Whose?"

"I don't know."

"Where?"

"I don't know."

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Then what do you know?"

Predulum looked back at Witness Four.

"That I am afraid of it."

Atrovald waited.

He continued.

"Not the furnace. The sentence."

"What sentence?"

Predulum's jaw tightened.

"'Wait until the first is present.'"

Pell's stylus made a small sound against the slate.

Atrovald turned her head.

He had not written.

Good.

Predulum saw her face.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That was not nothing."

"No."

"Matron."

Atrovald closed the grille.

Predulum swore behind it.

The clerk wrote down the time.

Pell followed Atrovald down the corridor.

"You're not telling him."

"Not yet."

"He remembered the inscription."

"He remembered a sentence resembling an inscription we found."

"That's significant."

"Yes."

"So—"

"So we preserve the distinction."

Pell stopped.

Atrovald turned.

He looked frustrated.

Good. Frustration was evidence that he understood the stakes.

"If he knows we found the sentence," she said, "then tomorrow he remembers knowing we found it. If he doesn't, we still have two independent paths to the same words."

Pell's frustration vanished.

"Independent."

"For the moment."

He looked toward Null-Seven.

"Do you think his memory came from the box?"

"I think the word *from* is doing dangerous work."

They continued.

At the Matronal chamber, a clerk waited with a paper packet.

Not a slate.

Paper.

Atrovald had issued that order after the missing guard.

"Priority S-Rank response," the clerk said.

"Source?"

"Internal personnel reconciliation."

Atrovald opened the packet.

One page.

A name.

**CUSTODY AUXILIAR HESK VENN.**

Pell leaned closer.

"Who?"

The clerk answered.

"Transferred into Logos escort complement this morning."

Atrovald read the attached notation.

Fifty-third position.

Temporary assignment.

Manual roster only.

No corresponding digital personnel record.

No pay account.

No barracks allocation.

No birth world.

No service history.

No biometric file.

Only a transfer signature.

Atrovald recognized the hand.

"Pell."

He had gone pale.

"That's mine."

"Yes."

"I signed him in?"

"Apparently."

"I don't remember his name."

"Do you remember the man?"

Pell closed his eyes.

"Behind Custody Auxiliar Eight."

Atrovald waited.

"Average height. Scar on the chin."

"Anything else?"

"He complained about the cold."

The clerk looked confused.

"It isn't cold in the Matronal chamber."

"No," Pell said.

"It wasn't."

Atrovald took the packet.

At the bottom of the page was a routing instruction.

**RETURN AUXILIAR VENN TO NULL-SEVEN CUSTODY DETAIL UPON COMPLETION OF TRANSFER.**

The authorization field was blank.

The effective date was tomorrow.

Atrovald sat down.

Pell remained standing.

The clerk wisely said nothing.

For several seconds, the only sound was the old ventilation system and, somewhere far below, the machinery that kept four impossible witnesses separated from one another.

Atrovald read the instruction again.

Tomorrow.

Not signed.

Not yet.

She reached for a pen.

Pell caught his breath.

Atrovald stopped before touching the page.

"Matron?"

She looked at the empty authorization field.

Then at her own hand.

"No."

Pell exhaled.

Atrovald folded the paper once.

"New order."

He readied his slate.

"Paper."

He put the slate down and took out a notebook.

"Go."

"No future-dated instruction is to be executed merely because it bears, predicts, anticipates, resembles, or appears to require my authority."

Pell wrote.

"Any such instruction is evidence until its issuing event occurs in ordinary sequence."

He wrote faster.

"No one corrects a discrepancy solely to make tomorrow agree with today."

The clerk swallowed.

Atrovald continued.

"Null-Seven remains sealed. Predulum remains alive, fed, observed, and irritated."

Pell looked up.

"That last part?"

"Operationally inevitable."

He wrote it anyway.

Atrovald almost told him to strike it.

Almost.

Instead she looked at the personnel sheet.

Hesk Venn.

A man without a past, remembered by position and complaint, whose return order waited for a signature she had not given.

The archive had spent millennia correcting history.

Tonight, Atrovald would attempt something much harder.

She would allow it to remain contradictory.

"One more thing."

Pell waited.

"Find out whether Hesk Venn is dead."

The clerk stared.

Pell did not.

He had learned.

"Before or after tomorrow?"

Atrovald looked at him.

For once, the question was not a joke.

"Both."
