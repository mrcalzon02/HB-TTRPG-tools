# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXI — The Inquiry of Origin

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

Selene Marr read the sentence three times.

QUESTION DEFECTIVE.

PLEASE REFILE.

The handwriting remained hers.

This was irritating.

There were, Marr reflected, many acceptable ways for the universe to become impossible. It might tear open. It might fill with daemons. It might reveal that causality was merely a local custom observed by provincial minds with insufficient imagination.

Using her handwriting without authorization was simply rude.

Young Talbor Varik held the page by its corners.

"You wrote this."

"Apparently."

"Seventy-one years ago."

"Apparently."

"You were not alive."

Marr looked at him.

"Governor, I appreciate the evidentiary summary."

Old Varik leaned over his younger self's shoulder.

"Is it exactly your hand?"

"No handwriting is exact."

"You know what I mean."

"Unfortunately."

She took the page.

The pressure marks were right. The long downstroke on the M was hers. The final stroke of REFILE hooked left because she had been taught by a tutor who regarded penmanship as a moral problem.

Even the irritation was hers.

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse watched her from across the chamber.

"Can handwriting be compelled?"

"Everything can be compelled," Marr said. "The question is whether the result remains admissible."

Captain Ardan Krell sighed.

"I miss shooting things."

"You shot the Collector."

"It did not improve matters."

"That has rarely prevented the Imperium from repeating a method."

Magos Hester Vhal extended a scanner toward the page.

Marr pulled it away.

Vhal's lenses narrowed.

"Obstruction of analysis."

"Preservation of evidence. Your instruments have spent the morning reporting mutually exclusive truths."

"That is not a defect in the instruments."

"That is exactly what instruments say."

Edrin Vale stood near the red maintenance door, arms folded.

The maintenance seal he had placed across it remained intact.

Nobody trusted this.

Vale trusted it least of all.

His slate chimed.

He looked down.

"No."

Marr glanced over.

"What?"

"New work order."

"Relevant?"

"They are never relevant until somebody dies."

He read it.

His face tightened.

"Printer inspection."

Krell looked toward the distant clatter still echoing through Antegra.

"Which printer?"

Vale turned the slate around.

ALL.

Marr nodded.

"Efficient."

"You and I have very different definitions of that word."

The brass automaton at the docket table stamped once.

FIRST HEARING RESUMED.

MATTER: INQUIRY OF ORIGIN.

The elderly adjudicator returned to her chair.

The unnamed naval officer and the robed clerk took their places beside her.

The adjudicator looked at the page in Marr's hand.

For the first time since arriving, she appeared unsurprised.

Marr noticed.

"You expected this."

"No."

"That answer has become less persuasive with repetition."

"Expectation would imply knowledge of the sequence. We possess records of sequences. That is not the same thing."

Marr placed the inquiry on the docket table.

"Then let us discuss records."

The Collector appeared faintly in the polished brass edge.

Not enough to dominate the reflection.

Enough to listen.

The red door remained closed.

Respondent Prior did not stamp.

That absence bothered Marr more than the Collector's presence.

The automaton printed:

EXHIBIT ADMITTED PROVISIONALLY.

DOCUMENT: INQUIRY OF ORIGIN.

SOURCE: EXTERNAL.

SOURCE IDENTITY: WITHHELD.

DATE: DISPUTED.

RESPONSE AUTHOR: SELENE MARR.

AUTHOR STATUS AT DATE: NOT BORN.

Marr took out her stylus.

"Amend the last line."

The adjudicator stared at her.

"On what grounds?"

"Prejudicial phrasing."

"You were not born."

"Allegedly."

Old Varik smiled despite himself.

Rhyse did not.

"Counselor."

Marr looked at her.

"We currently have three versions of Governor Varik, one dead man who never existed, a station with multiple histories, a respondent older than the question that recognized it, and a hostile entity attempting to enforce singular truth. I am not conceding my own birth certificate merely because it has had a good morning."

The automaton stamped.

AUTHOR STATUS AT DATE: DISPUTED.

Marr inclined her head.

"Thank you."

Krell muttered, "It likes you."

"Captain, do not say things like that."

The adjudicator folded her hands.

"The Inquiry is the earliest surviving external reference to Antegra Station."

"Earlier than construction?"

"Yes."

"Earlier than the system survey?"

"Yes."

"Earlier than Imperial occupation?"

The adjudicator hesitated.

"Yes."

Vhal's lenses clicked.

"Chronological discrepancy magnitude?"

"Unknown."

"Estimate."

"The oldest physical copy we possess predates the current station by seventy-one years. The text may be older."

Young Varik frowned.

"How much older?"

"We do not know."

Marr tapped the paper.

"Who sent it?"

"Withheld."

"By your jurisdiction?"

"No."

That changed the room.

Marr stopped tapping.

"You did not send this."

"No."

"The Collector?"

The Collector brightened in the brass.

NO.

Marr glanced at it.

"Noted, not accepted."

The reflection dimmed.

"Respondent Prior?"

No stamp came from the red door.

The adjudicator shook her head.

"We believe not."

"Belief is cheap."

"So is certainty, here."

Marr disliked the answer because it was good.

Vale's slate chimed again.

He swore.

"Printer inspection updated."

"How?"

"It has a fault location now."

LOCATION: RECORDS ANNEX NINE.

Marr knew the annex.

Everyone who worked in Antegra administration knew Annex Nine.

It had burned forty-six years ago.

Then it had flooded twelve years later.

Then an audit had established that it had never been constructed.

The station still paid three clerks to work there.

Nobody had ever found them.

Marr stood.

"We are going to Annex Nine."

Krell checked his pistol.

"Finally."

"You will not shoot the records."

"I make no promises regarding clerks."

"Those are Imperial personnel."

"Allegedly."

Marr gave him a cold look.

Krell smiled.

"I am learning."

The adjudicator rose as well.

"The hearing travels with the petitioner."

"Of course it does."

The brass automaton unfolded six narrow legs from beneath its body and stepped down from the docket table.

Vale stared at it.

"It could walk this whole time?"

Marr gathered the files.

"Apparently."

"Then why did we carry it from Dock Fourteen?"

The automaton stamped.

TRANSPORT ASSISTANCE APPRECIATED.

Vale pointed at it.

"I hate that thing."

The journey to Annex Nine took twenty-three minutes by lift, two minutes by corridor, and an additional forty-one minutes because the first lift insisted Annex Nine did not exist.

Marr filed an objection.

The lift reconsidered.

Antegra's architecture had become increasingly responsive to paperwork, which Marr regarded as either encouraging or evidence that reality had finally surrendered.

They reached a sealed bulkhead on Deck Thirty-One.

The sign beside it read:

RECORDS ANNEX EIGHT.

Below it, in older paint:

RECORDS ANNEX TEN.

There was no Nine.

Vale checked his slate.

"Here."

Krell examined the wall.

"There is no door."

"Maintenance disagrees."

"Maintenance has been dead for seventy-one years."

Vale looked at him.

"That has never stopped management."

He removed a panel.

Behind it was a brass handle.

No hinges. No frame. Just a handle set into solid plasteel.

Vale pulled it.

The wall opened.

Annex Nine smelled of paper, dust and old electrical fires.

Rows of shelving disappeared into darkness.

Green-shaded lamps burned over desks.

Three clerks sat at them.

All three looked up.

Marr stopped.

The clerks were old.

Not elderly.

Old in the way portraits were old.

Their uniforms belonged to an Administratum pattern retired before Antegra Station had been commissioned.

The first clerk frowned.

"You're late."

Marr recovered first.

"For what?"

"The response."

The clerk held out his hand.

"Inquiry of Origin."

Marr did not move.

"When did you receive it?"

The second clerk consulted a ledger.

"This morning."

"Date."

He read it.

Seventy-one years ago.

Young Varik whispered something devotional.

Old Varik did not.

He was staring at the third clerk.

The third clerk stared back.

"Governor."

Old Varik went pale.

"I know you."

"Yes."

"From where?"

"You haven't been there yet."

Marr stepped between them.

"No."

The clerk blinked.

"Counselor?"

"We are not doing that today."

"Doing what?"

"Providing tantalizing fragments in lieu of useful testimony."

The clerk looked almost offended.

"I am a records clerk."

"Then behave like one."

That worked.

The man straightened.

"Very well."

Marr placed the Inquiry of Origin on his desk.

"Source."

He opened a ledger.

The page was blank.

"External correspondence."

"Origin."

"Not recorded."

"Transit route."

"Not recorded."

"Courier."

"Not recorded."

"Authorization code."

The clerk paused.

"There is one."

Marr leaned closer.

"Read it."

He did.

Vhal made a sharp mechanical click.

Young Varik frowned.

Krell looked at Rhyse.

Nobody else understood.

Marr did.

It was her authorization code.

Not her current one.

The code she would receive only if promoted to Senior Adjudicative Counsel.

A promotion that did not exist.

Yet.

Rhyse spoke softly.

"Could someone have derived it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because half the sequence is assigned by personnel lottery at promotion."

Vale shrugged.

"Lottery could repeat."

"It is generated from the employee's service record."

"Your service record is currently disputed."

Marr looked at him.

"You are becoming unhelpful."

"Occupational development."

The Collector appeared in every green lamp shade.

AUTHORIZATION INVALID.

The third clerk reached beneath his desk and produced a stamp.

AUTHORIZATION ACCEPTED AT TIME OF RECEIPT.

The Collector brightened.

INVALID.

The clerk stamped again.

DISPUTE NOTED.

Marr looked at the elderly adjudicator.

"Your people?"

"No."

"Respondent Prior?"

"No."

"Collector?"

"Obviously not."

Marr looked at the three clerks.

"Who employs you?"

The first clerk answered immediately.

"Antegra Station."

"Which Antegra Station?"

All three clerks looked confused.

"There is only one."

Every lamp in Annex Nine went dark.

The Collector filled the black glass.

Marr felt the air tighten.

Old Varik shouted, "Withdraw the statement."

The first clerk stared at him.

"What?"

"Say you were mistaken."

"I was not."

"Say it anyway."

The shelves began to creak.

Thousands of paper files shifted toward alignment.

Dates corrected themselves.

Names standardized.

Ink crawled across pages.

The Collector had found a declaration of singularity.

Marr slammed both hands onto the clerk's desk.

"Objection."

The brass automaton stamped.

OBJECTION RECEIVED.

"Grounds?" asked the adjudicator.

"Witness lacks foundation."

The clerk sputtered.

"I work here!"

"That is not proof of competence."

Vale nodded solemnly.

"She's got you there."

Marr continued.

"The witness asserts one Antegra without establishing which Antegra, which date, which jurisdiction, which construction history or which petitioner. Statement is conclusory, prejudicial and catastrophically convenient to an adverse respondent."

The Collector spoke through the lamps.

ONE STATION.

Marr pointed at Young Varik and Old Varik.

"Two governors."

She pointed at Vale.

"One deceased maintenance worker."

Vale raised a finger.

"Present maintenance worker."

"Disputed."

"Fair."

Marr pointed at the sign outside.

"Two annex numbers and one missing annex."

The adjudicator lifted her stamp.

"Objection sustained provisionally."

The clerk swallowed.

"I amend my answer."

The shelves stopped moving.

"Antegra Station employs us. Identity of Antegra Station disputed."

The lamps relit.

The Collector vanished.

Everyone breathed again.

Krell looked at Marr.

"I hate that this works."

"Captain, civilization is mostly things that should not work being maintained by people who are too tired to admit it."

Vale looked pleased.

"Finally, doctrine I can support."

The third clerk opened another ledger.

"There is an attachment to the original inquiry."

Marr froze.

"Why was it not produced?"

"You did not ask."

Marr closed her eyes.

There were moments when the destruction of mankind seemed less like tragedy and more like an overdue filing correction.

"Produce it."

The clerk withdrew a thin envelope from beneath the ledger.

It bore no seal.

No address.

No date.

Only one line:

FOR SELENE MARR, UPON HER FIRST REFUSAL.

Marr did not touch it.

Rhyse moved beside her.

"First refusal of what?"

"Unknown."

The elderly adjudicator whispered, "Do not open it."

Marr looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because I remember you opening it."

Silence.

Marr's expression hardened.

"When?"

"Seventy-one years ago."

Old Varik stared at the adjudicator.

"You said she was not there."

"She wasn't."

"Then how do you remember it?"

The adjudicator's hands trembled.

"Because after she opened it, she wasn't there either."

The red maintenance door appeared at the far end of Annex Nine.

Vale's seal was still across it.

Impossible distance had become merely inconvenient distance.

The seal tore itself in half.

One stamp sounded from beyond the door.

The brass automaton answered.

Then a third stamp sounded from inside Marr's unopened envelope.

The Collector appeared in every reflective surface.

For once, it said nothing.

Marr studied the envelope.

Then she picked up a blank evidence bag.

She placed the envelope inside without opening it.

The adjudicator exhaled.

"You refused."

Marr sealed the bag.

"No."

"You did not open it."

"That is not the same thing."

She wrote across the evidence label:

DECISION DEFERRED PENDING DETERMINATION OF WHETHER RECIPIENT EXISTS AT TIME OF DELIVERY.

The automaton stamped.

The red door stamped.

The envelope stamped.

Three acknowledgements.

The Collector withdrew.

Marr looked at the Inquiry of Origin again.

DOES ANTEGRA STATION EXIST?

YES.

NO.

Both boxes checked.

QUESTION DEFECTIVE.

PLEASE REFILE.

Now, beneath her old impossible handwriting, new text was appearing.

Not ink.

Pressure marks rising from the paper as though written from the other side.

Marr turned it over.

A second question had been filed.

Not seventy-one years ago.

Nine hundred and eleven years before Antegra Station.

It read:

IF ANTEGRA STATION DOES NOT YET EXIST,

WHO IS ANSWERING?

Below it was a signature line.

The name had been removed so thoroughly that even the blank felt censored.

But beside it was a routing mark.

Not a name.

Not coordinates.

A Cafarron Corridor transit classification.

Old Varik recognized it.

His face changed.

Marr saw recognition and fear arrive together.

"You know that mark."

Old Varik said nothing.

"Governor."

He looked at her.

"I know where I saw it."

"Where?"

He swallowed.

"Before New Presidio."

Marr waited.

Old Varik looked toward the sealed evidence bag.

"Before Antegra."

The brass automaton stamped.

NEXT MATTER:

PROVENANCE OF ROUTING MARK.

SOURCE LOCATION: DISPUTED.

WITNESS: TALBOR VARIK.

Old Varik closed his eyes.

"Of course."

Vale checked his slate.

"For what it's worth, I have worse news."

Nobody wanted to ask.

Marr did anyway.

"What?"

Vale turned the slate around.

PRINTER INSPECTION: COMPLETE.

FAULT NOT FOUND.

PRINTERS FUNCTIONING CORRECTLY.

OUTPUT ORIGIN: NOT ANTEGRA.

Marr looked across Annex Nine.

Thousands of shelves waited in the green light.

Somewhere beyond them, another printer began.

Slowly.

One page at a time.

The first clerk listened.

His face went pale.

"That printer isn't in the annex."

Krell drew his pistol.

"Where is it?"

The clerk looked upward.

"According to the inventory?"

"Yes."

"Nine hundred and eleven years ago."

The printer continued.

And somewhere impossibly far behind the sound, someone began sorting the pages.
