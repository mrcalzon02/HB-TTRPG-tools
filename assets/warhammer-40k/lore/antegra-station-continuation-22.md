# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXII — The Routing Mark

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The printer continued to operate nine hundred and eleven years before it had been manufactured.

Selene Marr considered this discourteous.

Not impossible. Impossible had become an administrative category before breakfast.

Discourteous was worse.

Records Annex Nine listened to the distant clatter of pages arriving from a machine that, according to every inventory available to Antegra Station, would not be installed for another eight centuries.

Old Talbor Varik stared at the routing mark printed beneath the Inquiry of Origin.

Marr watched him stare.

"You recognize it."

Varik said nothing.

"Governor."

"I was a governor."

"You are presently three governors and a jurisdictional problem. I am using the shortest available title."

Young Varik leaned closer to the page.

"I don't recognize it."

"You wouldn't."

"Why?"

Old Varik looked at his younger self.

For an instant Marr saw something almost paternal in his expression.

Then it became fear.

"Because I learned it later."

Captain Ardan Krell rested one hand on his holstered pistol.

"At New Presidio?"

"Before New Presidio."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse's attention sharpened.

"Where?"

Old Varik looked toward the green lamps.

The Collector was absent from their reflections.

That did not reassure anyone.

"I don't know what the place was called."

Marr frowned.

"That is a suspiciously convenient failure of memory."

"It isn't failure. I remember three names."

Nobody spoke.

Varik continued.

"All three were correct. All three were on the same chart. The chart changed depending on which copy you opened."

Marr took out her stylus.

"Do not say the names."

Rhyse looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because we have spent several hours preventing a hostile intelligence from converting ambiguity into ammunition. I see no advantage in loading the weapon ourselves."

The brass hearing automaton stamped.

LOCATION IDENTITY: WITHHELD PENDING FOUNDATION.

Marr nodded.

"Good machine."

Edrin Vale looked offended.

"You told me never to encourage it."

"Circumstances have deteriorated."

"That sentence should be painted over every hatch on the station."

Magos Hester Vhal extended a mechadendrite toward the routing mark without touching it.

"Describe function."

Old Varik swallowed.

"It wasn't a destination code."

"Then what?"

"A return code."

Young Varik frowned.

"For ships?"

"For records."

Marr stopped writing.

Old Varik tapped the mark.

"Documents carrying this symbol were supposed to return to the office that originated them if the receiving authority could not establish jurisdiction."

"Supposed to?"

"None ever did."

"Where did they go?"

"Nobody knew."

Vale's slate chimed.

He looked at it.

"We may now have an answer."

PRINTER INSPECTION UPDATED.

FAULT TYPE: RETURN LOOP.

AGE: 911 YEARS.

RESPONSIBLE SECTION: RECORDS ANNEX NINE.

Vale rubbed his face.

"I would like it entered into the record that Maintenance was not operating nine hundred and eleven years ago."

The automaton stamped.

ASSERTION DISPUTED.

Vale pointed at it.

"See? This is why I hate you."

The third clerk of Annex Nine rose from his desk.

"There is a return ledger."

Marr turned slowly.

"Of course there is."

The clerk disappeared between the shelves.

He returned carrying a book large enough to injure a junior administrator and therefore of standard Imperial dimensions.

Its leather was cracked. Its brass corners were green with age.

The cover read:

UNDELIVERABLE CORRESPONDENCE

RETURNED TO ORIGIN

The date beneath the title was nine hundred and eleven years old.

Krell stared at it.

"That book is older than the station."

The clerk nodded.

"Yes."

"Why is it here?"

"It was returned."

"From where?"

The clerk looked mildly puzzled.

"Here."

Krell closed his eyes.

Marr almost sympathized.

The ledger contained thousands of entries.

Most were mundane.

Tax assessments addressed to settlements that would not exist for centuries. Munitorum requisitions for regiments not yet founded. Death notices for people whose ancestors had not yet been born. A complaint regarding inadequate corpse-starch allotment filed by a citizen who, according to the margin note, subsequently became his own great-grandfather through a clerical correction nobody had possessed the courage to reverse.

The Imperium, Marr reflected, had finally discovered a way to make genealogy worse.

Then Vhal found the first relevant entry.

INQUIRY: DOES ANTEGRA STATION EXIST?

DISPOSITION: RETURNED.

REASON: RECIPIENT STATUS INDETERMINATE.

RETURN AUTHORITY: SELENE MARR.

Marr read it twice.

"No."

The clerk waited.

"That is not an evidentiary objection," Rhyse said.

"It is an emotional one."

"Noted."

Beneath the entry was another line.

RETURN FAILED.

ORIGIN NOT FOUND.

HELD PENDING ORIGIN.

Old Varik whispered, "That's what the mark means."

Marr looked at him.

"You said it meant return to origin."

"It does. But if origin cannot be found, the record stays in transit."

The elderly adjudicator went very still.

"For how long?"

The clerk answered.

"Until origin exists."

The red maintenance door at the end of Annex Nine stamped once.

Respondent Prior had been quiet since the envelope.

Now the seal Vale had repaired trembled.

The Collector appeared in the glass of every lamp at once.

RETURN RECORD INVALID.

Marr looked at the reflection.

"Objection noted."

NO ORIGIN.

"That appears to be the problem."

NO ORIGIN MEANS NO RECORD.

The elderly adjudicator stood.

"No."

The Collector brightened.

The woman continued.

"No origin means no authority to destroy the record."

The automaton stamped so hard the desk shook.

RECORD RETAINED PENDING ORIGIN.

The Collector vanished.

Not faded.

Vanished.

Old Varik stepped backward.

Marr noticed.

"What?"

"It knows this."

"The ledger?"

"The rule."

"How?"

Varik stared at the return mark.

"Because that's where I saw it."

Marr waited.

He did not elaborate.

She permitted exactly four seconds of dramatic silence.

"Governor, I have already prohibited tantalizing fragments."

Old Varik gave a humorless laugh.

"I saw that mark on the Collector."

No one moved.

Young Varik said, "Where?"

"Inside it."

Krell's hand closed around his pistol.

"Explain."

"At the place with three names. Before New Presidio. Before Antegra. We found something in a vault. Not a machine. Not exactly. A field of black glass with writing beneath the surface. The mark was repeated thousands of times."

Vhal's voice dropped into machine-flat precision.

"Associated text?"

"One phrase."

Marr raised a hand.

"Do not quote it if quoting it identifies the location."

"It didn't."

"Then proceed."

Old Varik looked at the Collector's empty reflection.

"RETURN FAILED. ORIGIN NOT FOUND."

The red door stamped twice.

The automaton stamped once.

Then the ancient ledger opened by itself.

Pages turned.

Hundreds.

Thousands.

They stopped at the first page.

The first entry was not about Antegra.

It was not Imperial.

The script was unknown to Marr, but a translation had been added beneath it in several hands across several centuries.

Vhal read aloud.

"Record type: petition."

Rhyse moved beside him.

"Petitioner?"

"Unknown."

"Respondent?"

Vhal's lenses contracted.

"Unknown."

"Subject?"

The Magos hesitated.

Marr disliked machines that hesitated. They usually did it immediately before becoming religious.

"Existence," Vhal said.

Below the translation was the same routing mark.

RETURN FAILED.

ORIGIN NOT FOUND.

HELD PENDING ORIGIN.

The date field contained no date.

Instead it contained a question.

WHO ASKED FIRST?

The distant printer stopped.

For the first time since the Inquiry of Origin began, Antegra Station was completely silent.

Then something nine hundred and eleven years in the past tore a sheet of paper from the printer.

Everyone in Annex Nine heard it.

A second sound followed.

A pen scratching.

The printer started again.

One page emerged from a machine on Antegra in the present.

Vale caught it before it reached the floor.

He read it.

His expression changed.

Marr held out her hand.

"Give me that."

Vale did.

The page contained a single routing instruction.

TO: ANTEGRA STATION

FROM: ORIGIN

ATTENTION: SELENE MARR

Marr read the final line.

Then she read it again.

The message was brief.

STOP RETURNING OUR MAIL.

Krell made a sound that might have been a laugh.

Nobody joined him.

Because beneath the sentence, slowly appearing in wet black ink, was a second line.

YOU HAVE ATTRACTED ITS ATTENTION.

The lamps went dark.

The Collector did not appear in them.

Something else did.

And for the first time that morning, Respondent Prior began hammering on the red door from the other side.
