# The Long Patrol of Broussard

## XIII. THE THING UNDER THE ICE

**Author:** Mrcalzon02 / Christopher Vardeman  
**Based on the work of:** *The Long Patrol of Broussard*

*An original non-commercial Warhammer 40,000 fan chronicle. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The first useful thing about a predator was that it wanted something.

The second was that, if you were fortunate, it wanted the obvious thing.

Captain Broussard climbed down from the *Hesperant Vale* hoping High Presidio had decided to be obvious.

The wind hit him hard enough to make the hatch slam against its stop behind him.

Snow moved sideways across the ice. The stranded freighter rose above the recovery perimeter like a dark cliff, work lamps burning along its damaged starboard quarter and temporary cables hanging beneath the hull in armored loops. Beyond it, the four Chimeras sat in a broken arc with their engines awake and exhaust freezing into pale clouds.

Nobody was standing around to watch.

Good.

The port-side perimeter sergeant met Broussard behind the nearest Chimera.

"Marker twelve. Then fourteen."

"Direction?"

The sergeant pointed with two fingers.

"Coming across us. Slow."

"Still under?"

"Yes."

Broussard looked past him.

The marker line was a series of black poles drilled into the ice beyond the vehicles. Between twelve and fourteen, perhaps two hundred metres out, the snow had settled into a shallow trough.

It had not been there six hours ago.

The trough moved.

Not quickly.

That was worse.

Fast things were usually committed to a mistake.

Slow things were still deciding.

Gabriel came down the ramp behind Broussard and buttoned the throat of his greatcoat.

"Anything visible?"

"The ground is thinking."

Gabriel watched the moving depression.

"I preferred it when the ground lacked initiative."

The sergeant handed Broussard a field glass.

The ice beyond the markers looked intact until the trough reached marker fifteen. Then a crack opened with a sound like a distant rifle shot.

One black pole leaned.

Stopped.

Then disappeared straight down.

Nobody fired.

Broussard lowered the glass.

"Good."

The sergeant glanced at him.

"Captain?"

"Nobody fired at a pole."

"High standards."

"I've seen lower."

He keyed the hard-line set clipped to the Chimera's hull.

"All perimeter elements. Hold fire until you have a body. Driver crews mounted. Recovery hauler stays cold unless ordered otherwise."

Acknowledgements came back one by one.

The third Chimera reported last.

Broussard looked toward it.

Its turret was already tracking the trough.

"Three, stop chasing it with the gun."

The turret stopped.

"Sorry, captain."

"It doesn't know you're nervous until you tell it."

Gabriel looked at him.

"Do High Presidio predators understand turret behavior?"

"No."

"Comforting."

"The crew does."

That was enough.

The trough continued toward them.

At one hundred and fifty metres the ice rose.

Not broke.

Rose.

A long white ridge lifted beneath the snow, twenty metres from end to end and still moving. Powder slid down its sides. Something struck the underside again and the whole sheet flexed beneath Broussard's boots.

The sergeant stopped smiling.

"That's bigger than the last one."

Broussard did not ask which last one.

High Presidio produced enough large hungry things that taxonomy was often less useful than ammunition.

"Thickness here?"

"Two-point-eight metres when we drilled."

"Near the ship?"

"More. Hull's weight compressed the snow but the ice held."

Broussard looked back at the *Hesperant Vale*.

Temporary power conduits crossed the ground beneath the damaged quarter. Two external work parties had already withdrawn through the belly access. The freighter could not move. The recovery hauler could, but only slowly, and its tow rig was laid out across the starboard side like an invitation to expensive disaster.

The thing under the ice did not need to eat the ship.

It only needed to break what was keeping it alive.

"Bring One and Four ten metres inward. Leave Two and Three where they are."

The sergeant frowned.

Then understood.

"Channel."

"Yes."

Orders went out.

Two Chimeras reversed toward the hull, tracks biting through wind-packed snow. The other pair held their positions. The perimeter opened into a shallow funnel with empty ice at its center.

Gabriel watched the vehicles settle.

"And if it prefers the occupied side?"

"Then it has disappointed me personally."

"I imagine that will trouble it."

The moving ridge reached a hundred metres.

Stopped.

The ice settled.

Nothing moved.

The wind hissed over the hull.

A loose chain struck metal somewhere beneath the freighter.

Once.

Again.

The silence stretched.

Broussard knew this part.

Men began inventing things inside silence. A shape in blowing snow. A sound under a track. A vibration that had always been there but now possessed teeth.

He keyed the line.

"Nobody shoots the weather."

Someone laughed once over the circuit.

Good.

Then the ice beneath Chimera Two exploded.

The vehicle lurched sideways as a black mass punched through ten metres beyond its left track. Ice slabs cartwheeled into the air. Something pale and jointed unfolded among them, too large to fit the hole it had made and therefore enlarging it by violence.

Broussard saw a hooked forelimb.

A wet black plate.

A mouth opening sideways.

That was sufficient body.

"Fire."

Chimera Two fired first.

The multilaser struck the creature across the upper mass in a line of white flashes. Steam burst from ice and flesh. Chimera Three joined half a second later, then One and Four from the inward positions.

The predator screamed through the ice.

Broussard felt it in his ribs before he properly heard it.

It surged upward.

For one impossible moment most of it stood above the surface.

The sergeant had been wrong.

It was not bigger than the last one.

It was considerably bigger than whatever estimate had produced that sentence.

Its body was long and low beneath overlapping plates, with too many load-bearing limbs arranged along the underside and two heavier forelimbs terminating in black hooks that could have opened a Chimera if given time. Frost steamed from seams between the plates. One side of its head had been burned open by multilaser fire.

The other side remained committed to the project.

It struck Chimera Two.

The hook hit the frontal plate and skidded across armor with a noise that made every tooth in Broussard's head hurt. The Chimera rocked backward.

"Two, reverse. Three, keep its head off them."

The driver did not need encouragement.

Chimera Two backed hard, one track climbing broken ice before finding purchase. The predator followed the movement and exposed more of its flank.

That was why Broussard had moved One and Four.

"One. Four. Flank."

Their multilasers crossed.

Armor blackened.

A plate burst.

Dark fluid hit the snow and immediately began freezing at the edges.

The animal twisted toward the new pain.

Chimera Three's gunner found the damaged head again.

The second burst went through.

The predator dropped.

Not dead.

Down.

Its forelimbs thrashed against the surface and widened the hole beneath it. One hook punched through the ice, vanished, then tore upward carrying a slab the size of a field table.

The slab struck Chimera Two's side and shattered.

"Two, status."

Static.

Then: "Mobile. Left vision block gone. Nobody hurt."

"Stay moving."

The creature tried to turn beneath the guns.

Its rear half slid back into the water.

Broussard saw the mistake before it completed it.

"Cease."

The firing stopped raggedly.

Gabriel turned to him.

The predator sank.

For a second there was only churned black water inside the broken white field.

Then the water moved toward the *Hesperant Vale*.

"It's going under us," the sergeant said.

"Yes."

The thing had learned where the pain was.

It had also learned where the guns were not.

Broussard keyed the circuit.

"All vehicles, do not pursue onto broken ice. Repeat, do not pursue. Engineering, shut down external starboard feeds you can lose without killing the ship."

Kelm answered from inside.

"Define can lose."

"Quickly."

"Helpful."

The freighter's exterior lights flickered.

Three went dark.

A pump changed pitch beneath the hull.

The black water vanished under the freighter's shadow.

Broussard looked at the recovery hauler.

Then at the tow gear.

Then at the line of temporary conduits.

The creature struck from below.

The *Hesperant Vale* moved.

Not much.

A ship that size did not need to move much to make everyone nearby reconsider their religion.

Snow fell from the lower hull in sheets. One temporary cable snapped free and whipped across the ice, throwing blue sparks before its breaker opened.

Inside the ship, an alarm began.

"Again," Gabriel said.

"It will."

Broussard ran toward the starboard side.

The sergeant followed.

Gabriel did not ask whether he should.

They reached the recovery hauler as the second impact came.

This one was farther aft.

The animal was moving under the hull, testing.

Not attacking randomly.

Testing.

Broussard disliked competent wildlife.

"Hauler driver. Start it."

The engine coughed awake.

"Tow rig?"

"Still anchored, captain."

"Release the ship end. Leave the ground tackle."

The driver stared through the hatch.

"We'll lose alignment."

"I'd rather realign it than have something pull the hauler through the freighter."

The hatch shut.

Men moved.

Fast, but not frantically.

Pins came free. A powered shackle opened. The heavy tow assembly sagged onto the snow.

The third impact never came.

Instead the ice split beneath the loose rig.

The predator erupted exactly where the hauler had been attached.

Its remaining forehook caught the tow frame.

For an instant animal and machinery became one very expensive mistake.

The hauler driver accelerated.

The released rig stayed behind.

The predator hauled backward.

Steel screamed.

Then the ground tackle tore loose and vanished into the hole with it.

The sergeant watched several tonnes of recovery equipment disappear.

"Convoy command is going to ask about that."

"Tell them it was eaten."

"They'll want a form."

"They have one."

Gabriel looked at Broussard.

"You sound very certain."

"This is the Imperium."

The predator surfaced again thirty metres from the hull.

This time it brought the tow frame with it.

The steel assembly was hooked across its forelimb. One length of chain dragged behind like a tail.

It thrashed, trying to rid itself of the machinery.

Broussard raised the field set.

"One and Four. Same wound."

The guns fired.

The damaged head came apart.

The creature convulsed.

One forelimb struck the ice twice, slower the second time.

Then it stopped.

Nobody cheered.

Broussard waited.

Thirty seconds.

A minute.

The body settled halfway into the broken ice.

Steam rose from it.

"Hold positions," he said. "Nothing approaches the carcass."

The sergeant looked at the embedded tow frame.

"Recovery gear?"

"Still there tomorrow."

"Maybe."

"Then tomorrow's problem has simplified itself."

He walked back toward Chimera Two.

The left-side vision block was shattered. The frontal armor carried four long gouges, one deep enough to expose bright metal beneath the paint. The track had shed two outer pads but remained seated.

The commander leaned out of the hatch.

"Told you nobody was hurt."

"I believed you."

"You came to check."

"I believe in inspection too."

The commander looked at the dead predator.

"Think there's another?"

Broussard looked across the white horizon.

The correct answer was yes.

The useful answer was different.

"Assume there is."

The commander nodded and disappeared back inside.

Gabriel joined Broussard beside the track.

"One damaged vision block, two track pads, one temporary cable and most of the tow alignment."

"Could be worse."

"You should not say that here."

Broussard looked at him.

Gabriel was serious.

"Fair."

Kelm came over the hard-line before Broussard could tempt the planet further.

"Captain."

"Here."

"You cut one temporary feed. We lost a bypass pump for forty-three seconds. It's back."

"Hull?"

"Still attached."

Sella's voice appeared behind his on the circuit.

"Stop asking that."

Broussard looked up at the dark mass of the freighter.

"Repair estimate?"

There was a pause.

He could almost hear Kelm regretting the question on his behalf.

Sella answered.

"Thirty-seven hours."

"It went up."

"Something hit the ship."

"I noticed."

"Then we're both current."

The line clicked dead.

Gabriel smiled faintly.

"She sounds recovered."

"Don't tell her."

They spent the next hour moving the perimeter.

Not outward.

Inward and sideways.

The dead predator had left a broken field of ice across the port approach, so Broussard treated the whole section as unusable. Chimera Two shifted to a protected angle near the bow. The other vehicles covered the intact approaches without crossing the fracture lines. Portable markers went down around the new holes. Nobody went near the carcass.

The recovery hauler parked beneath the freighter's lee with its remaining gear secured.

The missing tow tackle remained attached to the dead thing.

That offended the recovery crew more than the attack had.

Broussard understood.

Men could forgive nature for trying to kill them.

Equipment loss was personal.

By the time he returned to the bridge, the alarm panels had gone quiet.

Holt was still at the secondary station.

The impact recorder still showed its fourteen-second absence.

Broussard did not stop beside it.

There was nothing new there.

He went to the current status board instead.

Thirty-seven hours.

One Chimera damaged but mobile.

No military casualties.

No additional *Hesperant Vale* casualties.

Perimeter contracted.

Tow recovery delayed.

Predator dead.

The ship still stranded.

It was not victory.

It was another hour purchased from a planet that sold them expensively.

Kelm came onto the bridge carrying two cups.

He handed one to Broussard.

"Tea."

Broussard took it.

"Voluntary?"

"No. Sella sent it."

"Why?"

"She said if you intend to keep attracting wildlife, you should at least stay warm."

Broussard drank.

It was strong enough to remove paint.

He approved.

Outside, work lamps came back on one by one along the damaged hull.

The repairs resumed.

High Presidio had tried to eat the recovery operation.

For the moment, it had only eaten the tow gear.

Broussard considered that a negotiation.
