# The Long Patrol of Broussard

## XII. THE WORK AFTER

**Author:** Mrcalzon02 / Christopher Vardeman  
**Based on the work of:** *The Long Patrol of Broussard*

The dead went aft under canvas.

Nobody made a ceremony of it.

There would be one later, if the *Hesperant Vale* survived long enough to afford one.

For now Jerrik Noll, Edren Vale and Pallas Dorn were carried out of the forward service corridor on insulated stretchers, past the hard-line repeaters Broussard's search party had left at each junction, and into a ship that had already resumed trying not to die.

The change was almost offensive in its speed.

A hatch closed behind the bodies.

A warning plate went over the dead cross-feed.

Somebody fetched a fresh cable loom.

Somebody else wanted the portable lamps back.

The forward ventilation remained isolated, so two deckhands began dragging temporary ducting down the cargo spine while arguing about whose turn it had been to inspect the blower bearings three weeks earlier.

Grief had entered the ship.

Maintenance had refused to make room for it.

Broussard understood the arrangement.

He had served in the Guard long enough to know that the universe rarely stopped breaking things merely because people were mourning the previous breakage.

Captain Kelm waited beside the cargo-spine hatch as the last stretcher came through.

He put one hand on the canvas over Pallas Dorn's shoulder.

Only for a moment.

Then he stepped aside.

"Where's Sella?"

"Sitting down."

Kelm looked toward the forward passage.

"Voluntarily?"

"No."

That earned the faintest movement at one corner of Kelm's mouth.

It disappeared before becoming a smile.

"Good."

Broussard nodded toward the bodies.

"Your people have somewhere cold?"

"Port stores. Compartment twelve."

"Use it."

Kelm looked at him.

"I was going to."

"Then we're in violent agreement."

Gabriel came through the hatch behind them, carrying one end of the last folded stretcher. He passed it to a deckhand without comment.

Kelm rubbed both hands over his face.

"I need to tell the rest of the crew."

"Yes."

He waited, perhaps expecting more.

Broussard had none.

Kelm went aft.

Gabriel watched him leave.

"You could have offered something comforting."

"I told him to put his dead somewhere cold."

"Your pastoral gifts remain undiscovered."

"Good. I'd hate to be reassigned."

They returned to engineering.

The compartment had become louder since Broussard had last seen it. Temporary power had restored one bank of pumps, and the pumps objected to their resurrection with a steady hammering vibration through the deck. Two recovery crewmen were feeding a replacement conduit through an overhead tray while a *Hesperant Vale* engineer shouted measurements from inside an access cavity.

The Magos stood in the middle of it all with three mechadendrites inside an open distributor cabinet.

He did not turn as Broussard approached.

"Auxiliary cross-feed A-Seventeen through C-Nine remains mechanically isolated."

"Keep it that way."

"That is the present intention."

Broussard looked at the exposed cabinet.

"Repair estimate?"

"For the cross-feed?"

"For the ship."

A pause.

"Thirty-one to forty-six hours."

That was better.

Not good.

Better.

"What changed?"

"We completed inspection of the secondary distributor bank. Damage propagation was less extensive than projected."

"Anything else waiting to surprise us?"

The Magos finally looked at him.

"Yes."

Broussard waited.

"Unknown failures are, by definition, not presently enumerable."

Gabriel coughed into one fist.

Broussard decided not to reward him by looking over.

"Known failures."

"Three gravitic control assemblies remain degraded. Two coolant loops require pressure testing. Forward environmental circulation is operating through temporary bypass. Starboard steering response remains within emergency tolerance but outside commercial certification. The primary telemetry recorder remains incomplete for the impact interval."

There it was.

Not new.

Not forgotten either.

Broussard looked at the status board rather than at Gabriel.

"And the auxiliary bus?"

"Preserved."

That had been the point of choosing the slower repair.

The ship still had somewhere to go when the next system failed.

"Good."

He moved on.

No one followed him with an explanation of the missing telemetry. No one needed to. The gap was still there. So was the damaged ship. So were several hundred kilometers of frozen ocean between them and the convoy.

There was work in front of the mystery.

For the next six hours, Broussard let the mystery wait.

He inspected the perimeter teams outside the hull and found them cold, irritable and competent. The wind had strengthened across the ice, pushing powdered snow against the lee side of the stranded freighter until the drifts reached the lower skirts of the recovery hauler.

The four Chimeras had been repositioned into a wider arc around the *Hesperant Vale*. Their engines idled in rotation to conserve fuel while keeping enough systems warm to move without warning.

One crew had rigged a canvas windbreak between two vehicles.

Another had stolen it.

Broussard resolved the dispute by ordering them to make a second one.

This was received as an unreasonable demand upon the military profession.

The second windbreak existed twenty minutes later.

Beyond the perimeter, High Presidio remained white, flat and hungry.

Nothing moved on the ice.

That meant nothing.

The planet had demonstrated a talent for hiding large predators beneath landscapes that appeared incapable of concealing a boot.

Broussard checked ammunition, fuel, engine readiness and the recovery hauler's tow gear.

Then he went back inside.

The *Hesperant Vale* smelled different now.

The burned ozone was fading from the forward sections. Hot metal and sealant had replaced it. Somewhere aft, someone had started cooking.

The smell was mostly onions.

Given the cargo, this was either supper or structural damage.

He found Sella sitting on an overturned equipment case outside engineering with a cup in both hands.

She looked at him.

"You said twenty minutes."

"I did."

"It's been thirty-two."

"Terrible."

"I could have been working."

"You are now."

She looked down at the cup.

"This is tea."

"Recover aggressively."

Sella took a drink with the expression of someone complying under protest.

Broussard leaned against the bulkhead.

He did not ask if she was all right.

She wasn't.

She knew she wasn't.

He knew she knew.

There was no useful military outcome hidden inside making her say it again.

"Magos says thirty-one to forty-six."

"Optimist."

"He didn't sound optimistic."

"That's how you can tell."

"Your estimate?"

Sella considered.

"If the pressure tests hold, thirty-six. If they don't, forty-eight or worse."

"Steering?"

"Ugly but usable."

"Gravitics?"

"Same."

"Hull?"

"Still attached to the ship."

"I like that in a hull."

She drank again.

For a few seconds they listened to the hammering pumps.

Then Sella stood.

Broussard looked at the cup.

It was empty.

"Fine."

"Thank you."

"Don't make me regret it."

"Captain, everything on this ship makes everyone regret something."

She went back into engineering.

Broussard watched her for a moment, then continued aft.

Gabriel found him near the bridge access ladder.

"Perimeter?"

"Quiet."

"That's considerate of it."

"It'll apologize later."

They climbed.

The bridge was operating on reduced lighting. Holt sat at the secondary station with a cable bundle running from the console to a portable diagnostic unit on the deck. One display still carried the impact telemetry trace.

The missing interval remained a clean absence in a field of ugly data.

Broussard stopped beside it.

Holt glanced up.

"Still nothing."

"I can see that."

Holt looked back to his work.

Good.

No recital followed.

Gabriel stood on the other side of the station.

"Recorder itself?"

"Magos says it's intact enough to read."

"Which means?"

Holt shrugged.

"Which means what's missing is missing."

Broussard studied the trace.

Fourteen seconds.

A ship could do a great many things in fourteen seconds.

Mostly it could fail to do the one thing that mattered.

He had already learned that the gap did not explain three dead men in a service corridor. That problem had possessed its own stupid, sufficient cause.

The separation bothered him more than if the two problems had fitted neatly together.

Neat explanations were comforting.

High Presidio had not shown much interest in comfort.

Kelm entered the bridge behind them.

His face had settled back into the expression of a working captain.

"Crew knows."

Broussard nodded.

Kelm looked at the telemetry display, then at Holt.

"Anything?"

"No."

"Fine. Leave it running."

He moved to the main status board.

That was all.

No one accused anyone.

No one explained the gap again.

No one tried to make the dead men part of it because they happened to have died on the same ship.

The bridge returned to work.

Broussard remained beside the recorder for another minute, watching numbers crawl across the diagnostic display.

The current stream was complete.

So was the previous hour.

And the hour before that.

The machine had no trouble remembering the present.

Whatever had happened before impact remained fourteen seconds of nothing.

He left it there.

There would be time to dig when the *Hesperant Vale* could move under its own power.

Until then, the priority was brutally simple.

Keep the ship alive.

Keep the people alive.

Keep the perimeter intact.

Then ask why the record had a hole in it.

Outside, something struck the ice far beyond the port perimeter.

Every head on the bridge turned toward the sound.

A low concussion rolled through the hull a second later.

Holt looked at Broussard.

Broussard was already reaching for the hard-line vox.

"Perimeter. Report."

Static answered first.

Then a Guardsman, calm and breathless.

"Movement under the ice, captain. Far side of the marker line. Big one."

Broussard looked at Gabriel.

The commissar sighed.

"It was considerate for nearly six hours."

"Record achievement."

Broussard keyed the vox again.

"All vehicles warm. Nobody leaves cover. Track it and wait for my order."

He handed the set to Holt and headed for the ladder.

The *Hesperant Vale* still needed thirty-six hours.

High Presidio had apparently heard the estimate.
