# Antegra Station

## Continuation XI — Tomorrow's Post

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The document from tomorrow was a transit authorization.

This disappointed everyone.

Captain Ardan Krell had expected a threat, a prophecy, perhaps an anatomically inventive death sentence from the impossible intelligence beneath the station. Senior Auditor Selene Marr had expected an invoice. Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse had expected nothing at all, which was the professional habit of people who had survived long careers by refusing to give the universe ideas.

Talbor Varik read the page twice.

ANTEgra STATION — CIVIL TRANSIT AUTHORITY
SPECIAL MOVEMENT WARRANT
DESTINATION: NEW PRESIDIO
PASSENGER: TALBOR VARIK
DATE OF ISSUE: TOMORROW

Below that was his seal.

Not the seal currently hanging from his neck.

The older one.

The one he had surrendered sixty-eight years ago when the Severance investigators had temporarily removed him from office.

Krell leaned over his shoulder.

"Convenient."

"No."

"No?"

"Convenience is when the quartermaster accidentally gives you ammunition before you request it. This is evidence that tomorrow has started filing paperwork."

Marr held out her hand.

"Let me see it."

Varik gave her the warrant.

She inspected the fibers, the stamps, the registration marks and finally the serial number.

Her expression became unpleasantly thoughtful.

"It is genuine."

Krell said, "It cannot be genuine."

"Those are different statements."

"It was printed by a machine underneath the station that should not exist."

"That describes several departments."

"Auditor."

"Captain."

Varik looked past them toward the impossible corridor.

The Collector remained outside Antegra.

It had not moved again.

That was worse than movement.

Predators moved.

Creditors waited.

Across Antegra, the emergency fragmentation measures continued to hold the station's population apart. Names were written by hand. Shift rosters contradicted housing records. Parish clerks copied births into books that no cogitator was permitted to index. Dock workers had been reassigned to departments whose offices had never existed. Three hundred thousand citizens had received temporary middle names, and an unfortunate hab-block on Level Forty-Seven had been legally transferred to a moon that had been destroyed four centuries earlier.

The coherence index remained below the threshold.

Barely.

Witness Lysa Nineteen stood beside Rhyse.

The blindfolded child tilted her head toward the warrant.

"It isn't from tomorrow."

Varik looked at her.

"It says tomorrow."

"It says that because you need tomorrow to understand it."

Krell rubbed his face.

"I miss enemies that occupy space."

Rhyse ignored him.

"What is it from?"

Lysa pointed toward Substructure XVIII.

"From the other him."

Nobody asked which other him.

They had already exhausted the useful versions of that question.

---

Marr found the warrant's registration entry forty-three minutes later.

It was in the station archive.

It had always been in the station archive.

The record showed that Special Movement Warrant 88-Theta-Varik had been issued sixty-eight years earlier, voided sixty-eight years earlier, reissued tomorrow, received yesterday, and closed as completed twenty-two years from now.

There were six signatures.

Five belonged to Talbor Varik.

The sixth belonged to Senior Auditor Selene Marr.

Marr stared at it.

"I have never signed this."

Varik stood beside her terminal.

"Apparently you will."

"I dislike being assigned work retroactively."

"You are an auditor."

"Exactly. We prefer to assign it to others."

The signature was hers.

Not merely her name. The tiny upward cut on the second stroke, the pressure break caused by an old injury in her index finger, the private verification flourish she used only on sealed findings.

Krell looked between them.

"Can we burn it?"

Marr and Varik answered together.

"No."

Krell sighed.

"There are moments when I understand heresy."

Rhyse had spent the interval interrogating the old Severance records.

Most were still damaged by deliberate deletion. Some had been destroyed so thoroughly that the absence itself had classification markings.

But one fragment survived.

A transit log.

Sixty-eight years ago, during the original incident, a shuttle had departed Antegra without authorization.

Its destination field was blank.

Its passenger manifest listed one individual.

TALBOR VARIK.

The shuttle's transponder appeared again nineteen days later at New Presidio.

Then vanished.

Varik read the fragment without speaking.

Rhyse watched him.

"I did not know this."

"You knew there was another me."

"I knew a duplicate escaped the vault. I did not know where it went."

"The skull knew."

Krell frowned.

"What skull?"

Silence followed.

Varik looked at him.

Krell's face tightened.

"There was a skull."

"Yes."

"I knew that."

"For a few minutes."

Krell swore.

The Collector had taken more than the object.

It had taken the certainty around it.

Marr immediately wrote SKULL on the wall.

Then, beneath it, she added IMPORTANT.

Krell said, "That seems insufficient."

"We have limited wall space."

"We live inside an asteroid."

"Then requisition more wall."

---

The station's first transmission from New Presidio arrived three hours later.

It was not addressed to Antegra.

It was not addressed to Varik.

It was not, technically, a transmission.

A maintenance vox in Dock Twelve switched itself on and began receiving a broadcast on a frequency the receiver was physically incapable of tuning.

A deckhand heard twelve seconds before the set burned out.

He wrote down what he remembered.

The message contained no voice he could identify.

Only a station-identification chime, a burst of static, and four words.

DO NOT ANSWER HIM.

The deckhand could not explain who "him" meant.

Neither could the vox recorder.

The recorder insisted nothing had happened.

Varik ordered every unsanctioned receiver on Antegra disconnected from centralized routing.

Rhyse countermanded him.

"No."

Varik turned.

"No?"

"If New Presidio is already involved, silence may be exactly what the Collector wants."

"Or exactly what the other Varik wants."

"Yes."

"Excellent. We have reached the stage where every course of action may be collaboration with an enemy whose identity is uncertain."

Marr looked up from the warrant.

"That is normal governance."

Varik stared at her.

"You are enjoying this."

"I am coping."

The second impossible reception occurred in a chapel.

The third came through a child's toy auspex.

The fourth appeared as text on a ration dispenser.

All carried fragments.

DO NOT ANSWER HIM.

HE IS NOT YOU.

HE THINKS HE IS.

The fifth message was different.

It appeared simultaneously on every handwritten public noticeboard in Central Audit.

Ink bled upward through old paper.

Letters formed themselves.

TALBOR,

YOU TOOK YOUR TIME.

Varik stood before the message for a long while.

Krell quietly checked his pistol.

Marr checked the time.

Rhyse watched Lysa.

The child was smiling.

That frightened Varik more than the Collector.

"Why are you smiling?"

"Because he remembers you."

"The other Varik?"

"No."

Lysa pointed beyond the station hull.

"New Presidio."

---

They did not answer.

Not yet.

Varik refused to allow a reply until they understood what channel was carrying the messages.

The Mechanicus spent two hours proving that no such channel existed.

Hester Vhal delivered the conclusion personally.

"There is no transmission medium."

Krell said, "We received transmissions."

"Correct."

"Then there is a medium."

"Incorrect."

Krell looked at Varik.

"May I shoot the Magos?"

"No."

Vhal continued without apparent offense.

"The messages are not crossing distance. They are exploiting shared state."

Marr frowned.

"Explain that without using a phrase that requires another explanation."

The Magos considered this.

"Antegra and New Presidio remember the same thing."

Varik felt the cold return.

The dead city.

Or him.

Perhaps there was no longer a useful distinction.

Vhal displayed two coherence maps.

Antegra's was fractured deliberately into millions of disconnected administrative islands.

The second map was reconstructed from the impossible receptions.

New Presidio's was not fractured.

It was dense.

Brilliant.

Interconnected.

A blazing knot of repeated identity, broadcast traffic, archived voices, civic records, rumor, military reports and public declarations.

The perfect place to hide a contradiction had become something else over sixty-eight years.

A perfect place to amplify one.

Rhyse whispered, "How long?"

Vhal answered.

"Unknown."

"Estimate."

"The pattern is mature."

Varik looked at the display.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning New Presidio may have been remembering the city longer than Antegra has."

The Collector moved.

Not toward Antegra.

For the first time since its arrival, the absence in the stars changed orientation.

Every long-range auspex on the station lost New Presidio for exactly one second.

When it returned, Substructure XVIII printed another document.

Marr retrieved it.

A receipt.

Of course it was a receipt.

She read it once.

Then handed it to Varik.

TRANSFER ACKNOWLEDGED.

ORIGIN: ANTEGRA STATION
DESTINATION: NEW PRESIDIO
CARGO: ONE CONTRADICTION
STATUS: DELIVERED

The date was sixty-eight years ago.

The signature was Talbor Varik's.

Varik looked at Rhyse.

"I sent him."

"Perhaps."

"My duplicate."

"Perhaps."

"To New Presidio."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Lysa answered.

"To make it louder than you."

The meaning settled slowly.

Sixty-eight years ago, Varik had not merely hidden one duplicate.

He had exported one.

A decoy.

A contradiction deliberately planted elsewhere in the Cafarron Corridor, somewhere noisy enough to draw the Collector away if Antegra's quarantine ever failed.

Krell understood first.

"We used New Presidio as bait."

Rhyse's face hardened.

"You used millions of Imperial citizens as bait."

Varik looked at the old signature.

He could not remember making the choice.

That did not make it less his.

"Apparently I did."

The dry certainty in his own voice sickened him.

Marr took the receipt back.

"There is another possibility."

"Which is?"

"The duplicate volunteered."

Varik looked at her.

"Would I?"

Marr considered him for a moment.

"Yes."

That was worse.

---

The warrant changed at midnight.

The word TOMORROW vanished.

In its place appeared TODAY.

A second line formed beneath the destination.

DEPARTURE WINDOW: 04:00–04:17 LOCAL STATION RECKONING.

Krell read it.

"Seventeen minutes."

"Apparently reality has a timetable," Marr said.

"Can we miss it?"

Rhyse answered.

"Probably."

"Good."

"We do not know what happens if we do."

"Less good."

Varik looked toward the coherence display.

Antegra was holding.

New Presidio was bright enough to hurt the eyes.

The Collector was turning toward it.

And the old Talbor Varik—the man Varik had been before he ordered his own memories cut away—had apparently designed all of this.

Not to save Antegra.

Not entirely.

To choose which population would be noticed first.

The Imperium contained many forms of governance.

Some involved taxation.

Some involved execution.

The oldest involved deciding who would be permitted to die somewhere else.

Varik folded the warrant.

"Prepare a shuttle."

Krell stared at him.

"Absolutely not."

"Captain."

"You are legally eight million people, intermittently two people, and according to Auditor Marr's emergency finding you were never governor. I believe this gives me unusual latitude."

Marr raised one finger.

"Technically he is governor again."

Krell closed his eyes.

"Of course he is."

Varik almost smiled.

Almost.

"Prepare the shuttle. We are not going to New Presidio."

Rhyse studied him.

"Then where?"

Varik looked at the impossible warrant.

"As far as the flight plan is concerned? New Presidio."

Marr understood.

"You intend to lie to the route."

"It worked on the census."

"That is not remotely the same thing."

"Auditor, today I discovered that sixty-eight years ago I mailed a copy of myself to another station as bait for a thing that eats certainty. My standards for remotely the same thing have deteriorated."

Marr considered this.

"Fair."

They began drafting a false departure.

At 03:58, two minutes before the window opened, every receiver on Antegra activated.

This time the voice was clear.

Talbor Varik's voice.

Older.

Tired.

Amused.

"Talbor," it said. "Do not come here."

Varik stood very still.

The voice continued.

"You already did."

Then New Presidio began broadcasting.
