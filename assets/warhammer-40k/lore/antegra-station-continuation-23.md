# Antegra Station

## Continuation XXIII — The Dead Letter Office

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The lamps went dark.

Selene Marr disliked darkness for several reasons.

It concealed evidence.

It encouraged imagination.

And, on Antegra Station, it usually meant somebody had found a new interpretation of the electrical code.

The thing reflected in the dead lamps had no light of its own.

That was the first problem.

The second was that reflection normally required light.

The third was that Marr could see it anyway.

It stood behind everyone in Records Annex Nine and nowhere in the room.

Tall.

Narrow.

Wrapped in what might have been robes if cloth could be folded around absence.

Where a face should have been was a rectangular darkness crossed by pale horizontal lines.

They looked like writing.

They also looked like teeth.

Captain Ardan Krell drew his pistol.

Marr sighed.

"Captain."

"It is behind us."

"No. Its reflection is behind us."

"That distinction does not improve my morning."

"Nevertheless, shooting a reflection remains an inefficient ammunition policy."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse had already drawn her own weapon.

"For once, I agree with him."

"Naturally. This station has spent the last six hours demonstrating that agreement is dangerous, and now everyone has discovered teamwork."

At the far end of the annex, Respondent Prior hammered on the sealed red maintenance door.

Once.

Twice.

Again.

Not to get out.

Marr realized.

To get in.

Edrin Vale reached the same conclusion.

"It wants the door opened."

Magos Hester Vhal's lenses contracted.

"Correction. It requests sanctuary."

The brass hearing automaton stamped.

REQUEST FOR EMERGENCY SHELTER RECEIVED.

Marr stared at it.

"From Respondent Prior?"

STAMP.

YES.

"Against what?"

The automaton's arm lifted.

Stopped.

Trembled.

For the first time since Marr had met the infernal machine, it did not stamp.

The dark reflection moved.

Not forward.

Across.

It passed from one dead lamp to the next without occupying the distance between them.

Every sheet of paper in Annex Nine turned face down.

The ancient return ledger slammed shut.

Old Talbor Varik whispered, "Don't let it read anything."

Marr looked at him.

"You know it?"

"No."

"Then how do you know that?"

"Because the Collector was afraid of the return mark. Respondent Prior is afraid of that. And every record in the room just hid its face."

Young Varik swallowed.

"Reasonable inference."

Marr disliked reasonable inference almost as much as darkness.

It was how people began improvising doctrine.

The printer clicked.

One line emerged.

FROM: ORIGIN

DO NOT ADDRESS IT.

Marr read the sentence.

Then she folded the paper in half.

Krell glanced at her.

"That's all?"

"It specifically advised us not to address it."

"I meant the message."

"So did I."

The reflection stopped moving.

The pale lines across its faceless rectangle rearranged themselves.

Marr could not read the script.

Vhal could.

The Magos made a noise through his vox grille that Marr had previously heard only when a servitor had presented him with a cogitator repaired using kitchen cutlery.

"Do not translate," she said.

Vhal froze.

Rhyse turned.

"Why?"

"Because translation is a form of address."

The pale lines stopped.

Marr felt, with absolute certainty, that the thing was looking at her.

The printer began again.

FROM: ORIGIN

GOOD.

Marr narrowed her eyes.

"I dislike being congratulated by people nine centuries dead."

Vale looked toward the printer.

"We don't know they're dead."

"That was optimism, Mister Vale. Please refrain from it in an evidentiary space."

The red door shook under another blow.

Respondent Prior stamped from the other side.

Three impacts.

Pause.

Three impacts.

Pause.

The hearing automaton suddenly answered.

Three stamps.

The red door went still.

A line appeared on the automaton's docket strip.

EMERGENCY MOTION: RESPONDENT PRIOR REQUESTS TEMPORARY NONEXISTENCE.

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Krell finally said, "Can it do that?"

Marr took the strip.

"It can request anything."

"Can you grant it?"

"That is a considerably worse question."

Rhyse holstered her pistol.

"Why would a thing whose entire function is preserving alternatives ask not to exist?"

Old Varik answered.

"Because alternatives can be found."

The dead lamps reflected the faceless figure.

One by one, the pale lines across its face became legible.

Not because anyone translated them.

Because the words changed into Low Gothic.

UNDELIVERABLE.

The ancient return ledger shuddered.

A second word appeared.

UNCLAIMED.

The shelves creaked.

A third.

UNRESOLVED.

Every loose document in Annex Nine lifted from its desk.

They did not fly toward the figure.

They simply became thinner.

Ink faded.

Paper yellowed.

Edges crumbled.

A tax assessment nine centuries early vanished first.

Then a Munitorum requisition for a regiment not yet founded.

Then a death notice for a man not yet born.

The thing was not reading the records.

It was disposing of them.

Marr understood.

"Dead letters."

Vhal turned toward her.

"Clarify."

"Records with no deliverable recipient. No valid origin. No authority willing to claim them."

Rhyse looked at the faceless reflection.

"A disposal mechanism."

"Perhaps."

The return ledger's brass corners blackened.

Old Varik backed away.

"The Collector couldn't find its origin."

Marr nodded.

"And therefore it remained in transit."

Young Varik finished the thought.

"Until something noticed the backlog."

The printer hammered hard enough to tear its paper.

FROM: ORIGIN

IT IS NOT A PERSON.

IT IS NOT A GOD.

IT IS THE END OF UNDELIVERED THINGS.

Marr folded that message too.

"That is irritatingly poetic for an office memorandum."

Vale stared at her.

"You're calling that an office?"

"I am calling it nothing."

The pale lines on the reflection vanished.

For a moment the face was blank.

Then one new word appeared.

ANTEGRA.

The station groaned.

Not the annex.

The station.

A vibration passed through the deck plates like something enormous had placed a hand against the hull.

Vhal's mechadendrites snapped upright.

"External pressure unchanged. Structural stress increasing."

Krell looked upward.

"How?"

"Unknown."

Marr looked at the word.

ANTEGRA.

The thing had found a dead letter with eight million occupants.

The hearing automaton stamped frantically.

JURISDICTIONAL OBJECTION.

JURISDICTIONAL OBJECTION.

JURISDICTIONAL OBJECTION.

The reflection did not react.

"It doesn't recognize the court," Rhyse said.

"No," Marr replied. "Worse."

She picked up the original Inquiry of Origin.

"It recognizes the filing system."

The station groaned again.

Across Antegra, bells began ringing in departments that had no bells.

Cogitators printed closure notices.

Habitation ledgers marked entire families RETURNED.

Docking manifests changed ships from ARRIVED to UNDELIVERABLE.

Three thousand citizens briefly lost their registered addresses while remaining physically inside their apartments.

The Administratum responded with the speed expected of an institution confronted with apocalypse.

It issued a revised form.

Marr received a copy thirty-eight seconds later.

APPLICATION FOR CONFIRMATION OF DELIVERABLE STATUS.

She stared at it.

"Of course."

Vale read over her shoulder.

"Can we use it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because confirming Antegra is deliverable requires us to identify a valid recipient."

Rhyse understood first.

"Which reconciles the station."

"Yes."

Old Varik looked toward the lamps.

"And the Collector wins."

"Potentially."

"So if we do nothing, that thing disposes of us. If we prove we belong somewhere, the Collector gets an origin."

Marr tapped the form against the desk.

"Imperial administration is often criticized for creating false choices. It is refreshing to encounter two genuinely terrible ones."

Respondent Prior hammered again.

This time Marr answered the door herself.

Not by opening it.

She placed one palm against the red metal.

"Motion for temporary nonexistence denied."

The hammering stopped.

Krell stared at her.

"You denied it?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because if it ceases to exist, our number of alternatives decreases."

Rhyse almost smiled.

"And the Collector benefits."

"Precisely."

Marr raised her voice toward the door.

"Amended relief granted. Respondent Prior is temporarily classified as misfiled."

The automaton stamped.

RESPONDENT PRIOR: LOCATION UNKNOWN.

STATUS: MISFILED.

The red door disappeared.

Not opened.

Not destroyed.

It simply ceased to be located in Annex Nine.

The dark reflection twitched.

Marr saw it.

So did Varik.

"It lost it," he whispered.

"Good."

The pale face turned fully toward Marr.

SELENE MARR.

Krell raised his pistol again.

"It has your name."

"Names are cheap. The Administratum issues them at birth and misspells them thereafter."

A second line appeared.

RECIPIENT.

Marr's stomach tightened.

The unopened envelope addressed FOR SELENE MARR, UPON HER FIRST REFUSAL lay on the desk behind her.

The thing had not come for Antegra.

Not originally.

It had followed the mail.

Rhyse saw Marr looking at the envelope.

"Don't."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking about it."

"Legate-Inquisitor, if thoughts were admissible evidence, your profession would have ended civilization millennia ago."

The printer began screaming.

Its mechanism ran faster than paper could feed.

Vale tore open the maintenance panel and struck the emergency release.

The machine continued without power.

Sheets poured out.

Each bore the same message.

NOT HER.

NOT HER.

NOT HER.

Hundreds.

Then one different page.

Marr caught it.

FROM: ORIGIN

THE RECIPIENT IS THE ONE WHO REFUSED FIRST.

Marr looked at Old Varik.

Varik looked at the ancient ledger.

Vhal opened it.

The first petition waited on the first page.

Petitioner unknown.

Respondent unknown.

Subject: existence.

WHO ASKED FIRST?

Beneath it, ink appeared where no ink had been before.

DELIVERY ATTEMPT: ONE.

RECIPIENT REFUSED.

RETURN INITIATED.

RETURN FAILED.

ORIGIN NOT FOUND.

Marr felt the shape of the trap.

Nine hundred and eleven years ago was not the beginning.

Antegra was not the beginning.

New Presidio was not the beginning.

The place with three names was not the beginning.

They were stops in a failed return route.

The Collector had been following the record backward.

Respondent Prior had been keeping alternate routes open.

And this new thing—this end of undelivered things—had finally followed the accumulated correspondence far enough to reach the current address.

The lamps flickered.

For one instant the Collector appeared beside the faceless reflection.

They did not attack each other.

They regarded one another.

The Collector spoke first.

NOT RECIPIENT.

The faceless thing answered by changing one word across its face.

UNDELIVERABLE.

The Collector vanished.

Marr exhaled.

"That was informative."

Krell stared at the lamp.

"It called the Collector undeliverable."

"Yes."

"Can it dispose of it?"

"Possibly."

"Would that be bad?"

Marr looked around Annex Nine.

At the ancient ledger.

At the impossible printer.

At the unopened envelope.

At eight million lives presently in danger of being treated as clerical residue.

"Captain, when two incomprehensible entities disagree over which one has authority to erase reality, I recommend against developing a favorite."

The hearing automaton stamped.

SENSIBLE.

Marr glared at it.

"Do not encourage me."

A final page emerged from the printer.

Unlike the others, it was clean.

Heavy stock.

Imperial watermark.

Current date.

Current station seal.

Marr's authorization code.

It was a routing form.

One field had already been completed.

ITEM: ANTEGRA STATION.

STATUS: UNDELIVERABLE.

The destination field was blank.

The origin field was blank.

The recipient field contained a single word.

REFUSED.

Below it waited two boxes.

RETURN TO ORIGIN.

DESTROY AS DEAD LETTER.

Neither was checked.

A third box had been added in handwriting.

Marr recognized the handwriting.

Her own.

It read:

FORWARD.

Beneath it, in smaller script, someone had written:

DESTINATION DISPUTED.

Marr smiled without humor.

"There you are."

Rhyse leaned closer.

"You have a plan?"

"No."

"Then what?"

Marr took up her stylus.

"I have a form with an unauthorized option."

She checked FORWARD.

The faceless reflection recoiled.

Every lamp on Antegra Station came back on at once.

The printer stopped.

Somewhere that was not Annex Nine, Respondent Prior stamped once.

The Collector appeared in no reflection at all.

The routing form shuddered beneath Marr's hand.

New text printed itself under DESTINATION DISPUTED.

FORWARDING ADDRESS REQUIRED.

Marr's smile disappeared.

Old Varik looked at the three blank lines that followed.

Three lines.

Exactly three.

He went pale.

Marr capped her stylus.

"No."

The automaton stamped.

CLARIFY.

"We are not filling those in until somebody establishes what those three names actually mean."

The distant printer, nine hundred and eleven years in the past, produced one last sheet.

A hand tore it free.

A pen scratched.

Antegra's present printer answered.

Vale lifted the page.

"It's from Origin."

Marr held out her hand.

He passed it over.

The message contained only four words.

YOU HAVE BEEN FORWARDED.

Then the station's stars changed.
