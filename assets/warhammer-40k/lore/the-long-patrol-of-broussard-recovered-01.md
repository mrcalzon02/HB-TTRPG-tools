# The Long Patrol of Broussard

## The Hesperant Vale

**Author:** Mrcalzon02 / Christopher Vardeman  
**Based on the work of:** *The Long Patrol of Broussard*

*An original non-commercial Warhammer 40,000 fan chronicle. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

High Presidio spent half the year pretending its oceans were roads.

For long months the seas froze hard enough that commerce stopped sailing and began crawling. Personal skimmers crossed the white in quick, low arcs. Freight carriers dragged themselves over the ice in convoys visible for kilometers. Tankers moved like patient iron whales, broad-bellied and slow, carrying fuel and industrial liquids between the few great cities clinging to High Presidio's coasts.

The ice was never actually safe.

Every driver knew that.

Every convoy commander knew it better.

There were places where the frozen ocean groaned for days before splitting. There were pressure ridges tall enough to roll a careless transport. There were things under the ice that had learned the vibration of engines meant food.

High Presidio had snarloffs. It had the seal-like predators convoy crews called screwheads, infamous for the violent death-tumble they used once their teeth found purchase. It had crustalloids and decayvores that could reduce the dead to stripped remnants with alarming enthusiasm. There were larger things too, creatures known mostly by damaged vehicles, missing patrols and the stories told after the recaf had become sufficiently medicinal.

Captain Broussard's orders, fortunately, were simple.

Follow the tanker.

Protect the tanker.

Kill anything attempting to eat the tanker.

He liked simple orders.

Simple orders survived contact with weather, wildlife and junior staff officers better than elaborate ones.

The convoy stretched across the ice ahead of him, engines throwing heat haze into the frozen air. Broussard rode in a Chimera assigned to the escort element, its tracks biting into the snow-scoured surface while the tanker column crawled northward. The crew had been doing this long enough that boredom had become a kind of discipline. Men checked weapons because there was nothing else to do. Drivers cursed ice conditions by habit. Vox operators listened to static with the grave concentration of priests awaiting revelation.

Then the diversion order arrived.

A freighter had gone down ahead of them.

Not crashed.

Not entirely.

Broken.

Which, on High Presidio, was often worse.

The damaged vessel was the *Hesperant Vale*, a heavy surface freighter stranded on the frozen route after an ice impact tore into her lower forward starboard hull. Initial reports were ugly enough to justify intervention and vague enough to make Broussard suspicious.

The convoy command aboard the *Saint Malderson* reassigned him a recovery package.

Four Chimeras.

One utility recovery hauler fitted with a manipulator rig.

Mechanicus support, including a Magos authorized to assess whether the freighter could be repaired in place or had become an expensive new landmark.

Temporary command authority over the recovery detachment.

Evacuation priorities if the hull could not be made safe.

Cargo securing instructions.

And the usual quiet understanding that if wildlife came through the ice while men were outside their vehicles, the wildlife was to be killed before anyone began discussing whose jurisdiction the corpse belonged to.

By the time the *Hesperant Vale* appeared through the blowing white, Broussard had already decided that he disliked her.

She sat crooked against the ice, weight borne unevenly, one flank dusted with frozen debris. The freighter was too large to look helpless and too damaged to look anything else.

The vox cracked.

"Freighter *Hesperant Vale*, bridge receiving."

The voice belonged to a junior officer.

Broussard looked toward the Commissar seated inside the Chimera with him.

Gabriel Hamden Jalesthesian did not look much like the caricature of a Commissar soldiers preferred to fear.

He was young.

Too young, some men thought, until they watched him long enough.

He had a chiseled face, an easy smile and the sort of immaculate black, red and gold greatcoat that suggested either religious devotion to tailoring or a staff of terrified orderlies. His cap remained precise even after hours inside a troop carrier. He had been born on High Presidio, roughly two hundred kilometers southeast of the planetary capital, and graduated with the first full class of the Regilian Third Continental Schola.

The numbers behind that accomplishment were less decorative.

One hundred eighty-six entered.

Twenty-three completed the full course.

Thirty-one died during training.

For Jalesthesian's final qualification, the Schola ordered him to execute Josef Arlen, the trusted partner with whom he had trained, on fabricated charges of compromise.

Jalesthesian obeyed.

Quickly.

Cleanly.

The lesson he carried from the institution was one he expressed without drama.

"Life is hard. The Imperium is harder."

Broussard had expected a political officer.

He had instead received a man who listened.

Jalesthesian learned the crew's names. He understood the convoy routes. He knew when Broussard was making a regulation decision and when he was making the decision that would actually keep men alive. He joked without trying to become one of the soldiers, watched without making men feel constantly accused, and had the rare commissarial talent of allowing competent officers to finish speaking.

He had been assigned to Broussard's Chimera.

By now the crew had stopped treating that as an omen.

Mostly.

Broussard keyed the vox.

"Where is Captain Kelm?"

A pause.

"Occupied, sir."

Jalesthesian looked at the speaker grille.

Broussard looked at him.

Neither said anything.

The junior officer on the *Hesperant Vale* amended the answer.

"Captain Derrovan Kelm is coordinating internal response."

"Then tell him Captain Broussard is coming aboard."

Another pause.

"Yes, sir."

The recovery hauler moved first.

Its manipulator assembly crawled toward the torn section of hull while survey equipment swept the damage. The breach had ripped across two gravitic suspension grids. A distributor bank had been exposed. One power-storage reservoir had discharged catastrophically, leaving the compartment blackened by an electrical arc violent enough to turn insulation into smoke and metal fittings into slag.

The Magos lowered a boarding gantry.

Broussard crossed with Jalesthesian beside him.

The interior smelled of burned wiring, cold metal and the particular stale air of a ship whose circulation system had recently discovered mortality.

A junior bridge officer introduced himself as Holt.

Second Bridge Officer.

Young enough to still look uncomfortable when superior officers asked questions for which the true answer might be politically inconvenient.

Holt led them inward.

Broussard noticed the crew before he noticed Captain Kelm.

They were working.

They were afraid.

Those were not the same observation.

Freighter crews on High Presidio were familiar with accidents. Ice broke. Power failed. Machinery maimed people. Cargo shifted. Men died in stupid ways and were mourned briefly because commerce had schedules.

This crew watched their own corridors.

Jalesthesian noticed it too.

He said nothing.

That was one reason Broussard liked him.

Captain Derrovan Kelm met them near engineering.

Kelm had the strained look of a man whose ship had become the property of several competing emergencies. The Magos was already examining telemetry and power architecture, and within minutes the engineering discussion became an argument.

One repair path was fast.

It would bring the damaged systems back toward operating condition sooner, but at the cost of stressing or sacrificing auxiliary distribution capacity that the *Hesperant Vale* might need if anything else failed.

The other repair was slower.

Thirty-six to fifty-four hours.

More controlled.

More labor.

More time sitting exposed on the ice.

But it preserved the auxiliary bus.

Kelm wanted movement.

The Magos wanted survivability.

Broussard wanted the ship to remain a ship after they finished rescuing it.

Then they found the telemetry gap.

There was a brief irregularity in the telemetry immediately before the impact.

Not long enough to explain the wreck by itself.

Long enough to bother Broussard.

Broussard looked at Kelm.

Kelm looked at the Magos.

The Magos looked at the data as though human discomfort were an inefficient biological side effect.

Jalesthesian watched the crew.

Broussard made the repair decision.

"Slow repair."

Kelm began to object.

"Thirty-six to fifty-four hours," Broussard said. "Preserve the auxiliary bus."

"We can be moving sooner."

"You can be broken again sooner too."

The Magos gave the smallest possible movement that might have been approval.

Kelm's jaw tightened.

Broussard continued.

"We have four Chimeras, a recovery hauler, Mechanicus support and enough firepower to make the local wildlife reconsider its theology. We can hold the ice. I will not trade the only surviving secondary power route on this freighter for a faster departure because your schedule has become emotional."

Jalesthesian's easy smile appeared for half a second.

Kelm noticed.

It did not improve his mood.

The order stood.

Repair teams spread through the freighter.

The escort vehicles established a defensive pattern outside. Cargo was inspected and shifted where necessary. Evacuation priority lists were prepared in case the Magos changed his verdict from repairable to abandonable.

The hours acquired the slow shape of a siege.

High Presidio's cold pressed against every hull seam. Mechanics moved between vehicle and freighter in insulated gear. Soldiers watched the ice. The convoy's larger mission continued beyond them, tankers proceeding under other escorts while Broussard's detachment remained with the crippled ship.

A minor ice-grazer encounter interrupted the work without becoming the battle everyone had quietly expected. Whatever lived out on the frozen route was enough to remind the soldiers why Chimeras had been assigned to a repair operation in the first place, but not enough to stop the repairs.

The more dangerous development was social.

The two crews began becoming one.

Not formally.

Imperial organizations could spend three months formalizing the transfer of a spoon.

But men shared tools.

Ammunition was checked across unit lines.

Hot drink appeared where it had not been requisitioned.

The *Hesperant Vale* crew learned which Guardsmen swore constantly and which ones became quiet when nervous. Broussard's soldiers learned which freighter hands could improvise a power reroute and which ones should never be trusted with anything more dangerous than a broom.

Sella was one of the useful ones.

Wiry.

Tools ready.

The kind of crew member who appeared where trouble had begun five minutes before anyone thought to request her.

After one reroute held, someone asked whether the system was going to survive.

Sella looked at the indicator.

"Still flying."

On High Presidio that qualified as optimism.

Broussard kept returning to the telemetry.

Not merely the short irregularity nearest the impact. Something in the ship's older record did not sit cleanly beneath it.

So did Jalesthesian.

Not obsessively.

That would have been easier.

They returned to it because everything around it felt slightly wrong.

The hull strike was real.

The damage was real.

The repair was necessary.

But fear aboard the *Hesperant Vale* seemed older than the breach.

Broussard did not accuse Kelm.

Jalesthesian did not begin interrogations.

Men spoke less freely when they thought the Commissar had arrived at a conclusion.

So Gabriel listened.

He watched pauses.

He watched who deferred to Kelm.

He watched which questions Holt answered immediately and which ones made the Second Bridge Officer look toward closed doors.

The repair clock continued.

Then a routine inspection stopped being routine.

Jerrik went forward with two riggers to inspect the cargo holds.

Hours passed.

They did not report back.

At first nobody treated it as an emergency.

Vox inside the forward structure had always been unreliable.

There were damaged conduits.

Bulkheads.

Interference from repair work.

People lost track of time.

Then Broussard asked where they were.

Holt answered.

"Jerrik's vox is unreliable."

"How unreliable?"

Holt shifted.

"He knows to check in."

Jalesthesian turned his head toward him.

"Unreliable vox does not explain feet failing to return."

That ended the discussion.

Broussard ordered their route pulled.

Last confirmed position.

Access points.

Damage reports.

Anything between the missing men and the rest of the ship that had changed since they went forward.

The cargo itself should not have been especially dangerous.

The forward holds carried food.

Fruit.

Vegetables.

Loose bins.

Not stacked cargo crates locked under heavy netting. Not munition pallets. Not machinery capable of crushing a man because a gravitic compensator coughed.

Loose produce bins could shift if the hull moved badly enough.

They could spill.

They could obstruct a passage.

They should not make three men disappear.

Which meant the cargo was probably not the problem.

Broussard stood over the route display.

The line marking Jerrik's inspection path entered the forward hold complex and ended.

The Magos had engineers elsewhere.

Kelm had his damaged ship.

Holt had the expression of a junior officer who had finally realized the thing everyone had avoided saying aloud was about to be said anyway.

Jalesthesian rested one gloved hand near the fastening of his greatcoat.

Still smiling faintly.

Not because anything was funny.

Because some men smiled when they were angry and some when they were afraid, and Commissar Gabriel Hamden Jalesthesian had been trained too thoroughly to offer strangers either expression honestly.

Broussard looked toward the forward bulkhead.

Three men were missing.

The repair remained underway.

The *Hesperant Vale* still had a history Broussard did not trust.

And outside, beneath kilometers of winter road, High Presidio's frozen ocean continued making the slow sounds of something enormous turning in its sleep.
