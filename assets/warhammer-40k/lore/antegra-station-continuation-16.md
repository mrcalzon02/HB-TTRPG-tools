# Antegra Station

## Continuation XVI — The Travel Warrant

*An original Cafarron Corridor chronicle continuation. Warhammer 40,000 and associated marks and setting elements are the property of Games Workshop. This non-commercial fan work is not endorsed by or affiliated with Games Workshop.*

The travel request was rejected in nine seconds.

Selene Marr considered this encouraging.

Most requests on Antegra Station required three days to be rejected, seven to be appealed, and a further fortnight to discover that the office responsible had been destroyed two administrations earlier and continued functioning from habit.

Nine seconds implied urgency.

The paper strip read:

DESTINATION REQUIRED.

Marr placed it beside the venue notice.

PROPOSED HEARING LOCATION: WITHHELD.

Captain Ardan Krell read both.

"We need a destination to request travel to a destination they refuse to identify."

"Correct."

"And attendance is mandatory."

"Also correct."

"Good. I wanted to establish whether the universe had become stupid or merely hostile."

Old Talbor Varik leaned against the command rail.

"It can be both."

Young Varik looked at him.

"You sound experienced."

"I am you with sixty-eight additional years of disappointment."

Legate-Inquisitor Aveline Rhyse ignored them.

"Can the routing code be used as coordinates?"

Magos Hester Vhal rotated three lenses toward the strip.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because it is a routing code."

Krell sighed.

"Thank you, Magos."

"Precision is a courtesy."

"Not always."

Witness Lysa Nineteen stood beside the astropathic grille, head tilted.

"It isn't telling us where to go."

Marr looked at her.

"What is it telling us?"

"Who has to arrive."

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Marr picked up the venue notice again.

BY ALL PARTIES.

She read the line twice.

Then she smiled.

Krell saw it and swore quietly.

---

At 06:31 station time, Antegra Station filed Form Transit-Null/7.

The form did not exist.

This was not an obstacle.

Marr drafted it, Vhal assigned it a checksum, and Old Varik backdated its enabling regulation by sixty-eight years.

Young Varik signed the regulation as its originating authority.

The resulting document had therefore been authorized by the same governor twice, separated by most of a human lifetime and one unresolved question of identity.

The Administratum cogitator accepted it immediately.

Krell stared at the approval seal.

"That worked?"

"Captain," Marr said, "the system has been waiting centuries for paperwork dishonest enough to understand it."

Transit-Null/7 did not request passage to a place.

It requested passage to a hearing.

The distinction proved important.

Vhal fed the routing code into Antegra's navigation cogitators without identifying it as spatial data.

The cogitators objected.

She classified the objection as nonbinding.

They objected again.

She promoted one machine-spirit and reassigned another to waste reclamation.

The objections ceased.

A route appeared.

Not on the star chart.

On the station docket.

CASE 7-OMNIA-NULL

CURRENT VENUE: ANTEGRA STATION

NEXT VENUE: IN TRANSIT

Marr frowned.

"That is not a destination."

Lysa whispered, "It doesn't need one."

The deck moved.

Not physically.

Antegra remained in orbit around the same tired star, its docks full, its manufactoria smoking, its millions of contradictory citizens arguing over breakfast and pension notices.

But Dock Fourteen acquired a new legal status.

IN TRANSIT.

Every chronometer aboard it lost eleven seconds.

The outer observation panes went black.

Krell reached for the command rail.

"What did you do?"

"Filed for travel expenses," Marr said.

"I gathered that."

"They were approved."

"That is not reassuring."

"It was not intended to be."

---

The black beyond Dock Fourteen was not void.

Void contained stars.

This contained omissions.

The observation panes showed patches where something should have been, separated by thin lines of ordinary space. Once, for less than a second, Marr saw a station rotating beyond the glass.

Then it was a ship.

Then a city built along the inside of a cylinder.

Then nothing.

She did not name any of them.

Neither did Vhal.

That restraint probably saved them.

Rhyse drew her pistol.

Marr looked at it.

"Against what?"

"Contingencies."

"Does it shoot jurisdiction?"

"If necessary."

"I begin to understand the Inquisition."

Old Varik moved to the glass.

His face had gone pale.

"I've seen this."

Young Varik stiffened.

"At New Presidio?"

The older man did not answer.

That was answer enough.

Marr did not press him.

The original New Presidio transmission remained sealed behind too many absences already. She had no intention of manufacturing certainty merely because everyone in the room was frightened.

The Collector had demonstrated the cost of that habit.

A bell rang.

One tone.

Vhal checked the docket.

"Party status changed."

RESPONDENT: PRESENT.

The temperature fell.

Krell whispered, "Where?"

The black observation pane reflected the room.

Marr.

Krell.

Rhyse.

Vhal.

Lysa.

Two Variks.

And, between them, an eighth figure.

It had no face.

It had no uniform.

It had no body except where the reflection insisted a body ought to be.

Nobody turned around.

The docket advanced.

RESPONDENT IDENTITY: UNRESOLVED.

STATUS: PRESERVED.

Marr exhaled.

"The injunction still holds."

"For now," Rhyse said.

The reflected figure inclined its head.

Words appeared backward across the observation pane.

VENUE INVALID.

Marr took out her stylus.

"Objection noted."

The reflection did not move.

VENUE INVALID.

"Duplicative."

For one astonishing instant, nothing happened.

Then the eighth reflection vanished.

Krell began laughing.

Rhyse stared at him.

"Captain?"

"Sorry. I think I've finally become unwell."

"That would simplify the paperwork," Marr said.

---

At 06:44, Dock Fourteen received visitors.

They did not enter through the hatch.

Three chairs simply became occupied.

The first held an elderly woman in a severe black coat with no insignia Marr recognized.

The second held a young man wearing a naval uniform whose rank tabs changed whenever Krell looked directly at them.

The third held a brass automaton carrying a stack of folders tied with red cord.

Rhyse raised her pistol.

The elderly woman raised one eyebrow.

"If you discharge that weapon, Legate, you will be required to explain why."

Rhyse lowered it.

Marr decided she liked the woman immediately.

"Receiving authority?" Marr asked.

"Disputed."

"Identity?"

"Withheld."

"Location?"

"Inappropriate question."

Marr nodded.

"Competent."

The young naval officer looked toward Krell.

"Captain Ardan Krell?"

"Yes."

"For present purposes, no."

Krell closed his eyes.

"Of course."

The brass automaton placed the folders on Marr's desk.

Each was labeled CASE 7-OMNIA-NULL.

Each contained a different number of pages.

"Why are you here?" Rhyse asked.

The elderly woman answered.

"Because you complied with the summons."

"We did not go anywhere."

"Correct."

"Then how did we comply?"

"You entered transit."

"Transit to where?"

The woman looked genuinely offended.

"If we told you, we would have to determine it."

Old Varik laughed softly.

The woman turned to him.

Her expression changed.

Not fear.

Recognition.

Then professional irritation.

"You again."

Old Varik stopped laughing.

Young Varik looked between them.

"You know him?"

"That question is before the hearing."

The woman opened the first folder.

Inside was a photograph.

Marr could not see the location shown in it.

She could see Old Varik standing in the foreground.

He was younger.

Much younger.

Beside him stood the same elderly woman.

The image was dated seventy-one years ago.

Three years before Antegra's original incident.

Old Varik whispered, "No."

The woman closed the folder.

"Governor Varik, your objection is premature."

"I have never met you."

"That," she said, "is one of the disputed facts."

The docket printed another line.

PETITIONER IDENTITY: DISPUTED.

Young Varik stared at it.

"We are the petitioner."

"Apparently," Marr said, "that has become ambitious."

The elderly woman folded her hands.

"This hearing is not principally about the Collector."

Silence settled over Dock Fourteen.

Even the machinery seemed to understand that this was bad news.

Rhyse spoke first.

"Then what is it about?"

The woman looked at the two Talbor Variks.

"Standing."

Marr felt the shape of the trap before anyone else did.

If Antegra could not establish that it was the petitioner, the injunction protecting it might fail.

If it established that fact too completely, the Collector might reconcile the station through the definition.

A perfect Imperial proceeding.

Proving the truth could kill them.

Failing to prove it could also kill them.

The brass automaton distributed forms.

Marr read the heading.

DECLARATION OF CONTINUING EXISTENCE.

She looked at the elderly woman.

"Deadline?"

"Before arrival."

Krell glanced at the black windows.

"Arrival where?"

The woman smiled without warmth.

"Exactly."

Then every bell on Dock Fourteen rang at once.

The docket changed.

NEXT VENUE: APPROACHING.

For the first time since entering transit, something appeared beyond the observation panes that did not change when they looked at it.

A light.

Small.

Distant.

Steady.

Vhal measured it.

Her lenses contracted.

"We are not approaching the light."

Marr watched it grow.

"No."

The elderly woman closed the case file.

"It is approaching the hearing."

The light became two.

Then four.

Then hundreds.

Not stars.

Windows.

Whatever lay behind them remained hidden.

The docket printed one final line.

ALL PARTIES PRESENT.

And somewhere beyond the glass, something vast knocked politely before entering.
