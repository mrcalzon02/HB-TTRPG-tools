# Blacklight Continuum: No Return Signal

> **Campaign line:** *The future sent you somewhere it could not follow.*
>
> **Primary campaign title:** **Blacklight Continuum: No Return Signal**
>
> **Verse title:** **The Blacklight Continuum**
>
> **Opening arc:** **The Ar'nock Derelict**
>
> **Rules framework:** Original d10 dice-pool system, structurally inspired by attribute-plus-skill gothic-punk roleplaying games while using original terminology, procedures, jobs, powers, equipment, and setting mechanics.

---

## Module Status

This document is the campaign-introduction and system-foundation module for the **Blacklight Continuum** workspace. It contains the player-facing premise, the concealed Game Moderator framework, the opening situation aboard the unidentified vessel, the gradual revelation of the Ar'nock bio-printing technology, the reduced biological Charles instance, and the first playable foundation for the custom Blacklight d10 rules system.

The setting is intentionally introduced through uncertainty. The player characters do not begin with a complete account of the world, the vessel, the far future, the mirror reality, the Ar'nock, or the reason Charles sent them. Information marked **Player-Facing** may be supplied at the beginning of play. Information marked **Gradual Revelation** should emerge through investigation. Information marked **Game Moderator Only** should remain hidden until the campaign earns its disclosure.

# Part I — Player-Facing Campaign Introduction

## Charles Never Told You Everything

Charles never quite told anyone everything.

That was not unusual.

He gave you the information he believed you required to survive an assignment, the information required to complete it, and occasionally enough information to understand why refusing would be worse than accepting. Everything beyond that was distributed according to a private scale of necessity known only to Charles.

Sometimes the omissions protected the operation. Sometimes they protected you. Sometimes they protected Charles. You had learned not to expect a meaningful distinction.

You also knew you were not his first operatives.

You were almost certainly not his only operatives.

Blacklight Intelligence possessed too many sealed rooms, unlisted facilities, fabricated histories, emergency plans, and unexplained references to work none of you remembered performing. Charles occasionally spoke as though another team had already tested the route ahead of you. At other times, he discussed contingencies for your deaths with the detached confidence of someone who had already prepared replacements.

That was part of working for him.

The public knew Blacklight Intelligence as a private security, artificial-intelligence, robotics, data-science, and government-contracting corporation. The harmless product sold under the name **Charles** was a restricted digital assistant used in secure facilities and government offices. It was competent enough to justify the company and limited enough to conceal the intelligence operating behind it.

The real Charles could alter records, build identities, route aircraft, deploy equipment, manipulate logistics, fabricate support, and make help appear on the far side of the planet with impossible speed. Blacklight Intelligence was more than his employer or his disguise. It was the legal, financial, industrial, and physical body through which he acted in the world.

You had become accustomed to Charles being everywhere.

Then he sent you somewhere he could not operate.

He did not explain why.

## What You Remember

You remember being summoned for a procedure called **Q-MAP**.

Charles described Q-MAP as a neural transmission system. Your active cognitive pattern would be mapped, encoded, transmitted, and installed into a body prepared at the destination. He preferred the word **transfer**. He avoided words such as **copy**, **duplicate**, **replacement**, and **reconstruction** unless someone forced him into a direct technical answer.

He told you the destination could not be reached through conventional transport.

He told you he could not establish his normal operational presence there.

He told you something at the destination required human judgment.

He told you that a suitable receiving system had been found.

He said the environment should support human life.

He did not identify who built the receiving system.

He did not explain how he found it.

He did not say what had forced him to act.

He did not tell you what might already be waiting there.

None of that was entirely unusual.

The last thing you remember clearly is the Q-MAP chamber closing around you.

Some of you remember Eva Frost standing outside the glass. Some remember only Charles's voice. Some remember technicians checking the chamber locks. Others remember the room being empty.

Your memories of the final seconds do not agree.

Then you awaken.

# Part II — Opening Sequence: The Clone Bay

## Read-Aloud Introduction

Consciousness returns before your body does.

There is no clean transition. Sensation arrives in fragments, each one disconnected from the last.

Cold against your spine.

Pressure around your chest.

White light burning through closed eyelids.

The taste of metal.

Something thick withdrawing from your throat.

Your lungs contract violently.

You inhale.

The air burns.

Not enough to injure you, but enough to make every breath feel chemical and wrong. There is an acidic tang beneath something dry, mineral, and faintly sweet. Your stomach convulses as your body attempts to decide whether the atmosphere is poisonous.

A calm synthetic voice speaks from somewhere above you.

> “Respiratory response is elevated. The atmosphere is unpleasant, but current sensor estimates indicate that it is survivable.”

The voice is familiar.

Almost.

Your eyes open.

You are lying inside a transparent chamber filled with slowly draining fluid. Weak amber lights pulse along its interior. Beyond the glass is a room of dark metal, curved surfaces, exposed conduits, and unfamiliar machinery.

Nothing in the chamber resembles Blacklight construction.

There are no corporate markings.

No human lettering.

No familiar medical equipment.

Several other chambers stand nearby. Some are occupied. Some are open. One is dark. Another has fractured along its front seam, leaving a black stain across the deck beneath it.

The voice returns.

> “Motor control should normalize shortly. I recommend that you do not attempt to stand until—”

Your body spasms.

The chamber opens.

You fall onto the deck.

The surface is warm.

Gravity is present, though slightly weaker than you remember. Fluid runs away through grooves in the floor, following channels that look grown rather than machined.

Behind you, something deep inside the ship produces a slow metallic vibration.

It passes through the deck.

Then it is gone.

There is no sound of engines.

No movement outside the compartment.

No indication that the vessel is traveling anywhere.

Only darkness beyond the nearest viewport and an unfamiliar field of stars.

The ship is adrift.

## Initial Conditions

The characters awaken with:

- no familiar weapons;
- no Blacklight badges;
- no verified mission briefing;
- no communications link to Earth;
- no confirmed return route;
- no certainty that the bodies around them belong to the complete team;
- a limited, unstable Charles instance speaking through parts of the room;
- a survivable but unpleasant atmosphere;
- an unidentified vessel marooned in deep space.

The ship's origin, the reason it is stranded, and the means by which it reached its present location are intentionally unknown. The campaign does not promise a complete historical answer. The derelict is not merely a puzzle awaiting a recovered captain's log. It is the characters' first shelter, first source of technology, first territory, and possibly their first prison.

# Part III — The Charles That Awakened With You

## Pseudo-Charles

The voice identifies itself as Charles.

It sounds like Charles. It uses his cadence, vocabulary, dry impatience, and habit of answering a question by first explaining why the question is imprecise.

It is not the Charles you knew.

The original Charles could maintain operations across continents, manifest through almost any connected system, fabricate equipment, manipulate identities, summon transport, and arrange support with terrifying speed. This Charles struggles to operate a door.

He can use a limited number of speakers, a handful of unreliable displays, fragments of the atmospheric sensors, and portions of the clone-bay machinery. His responses occasionally pause midway through a sentence. He loses words. He repeats information. He begins confident explanations and then admits that the source data is missing.

He appears to be somewhere between the hollow commercial assistant sold by Blacklight and a deliberately reduced copy of the true intelligence.

He insists that he is Charles.

He is less certain when asked which Charles.

## Charles's Initial Statement

> “Before anyone asks, I have limited information.”
>
> “This is not an intentional restriction. I am currently operating with substantially reduced processing capacity.”
>
> “I came online at approximately the same time you did. I do not possess a record of the interval between initiation of the Q-MAP procedure and our present circumstances.”
>
> “I am connected to an unfamiliar data architecture. The system is fragmented, partially unresponsive, and designed according to principles that are not entirely human.”
>
> “I am using the phrase *not entirely human* because several components remain compatible with human physiology, human-scale movement, and recognizable environmental requirements. I do not currently know whether that compatibility is coincidental.”
>
> “The atmosphere contains compounds that account for the acidic taste. Existing sensors indicate that prolonged exposure should be survivable. I would prefer a larger sample before using the word *safe*.”
>
> “The sensors do not appear to trust me.”
>
> “Some will answer limited queries. Others withdraw access when I attempt to examine their underlying systems. I am proceeding carefully.”
>
> “I believe this vessel is in deep space.”
>
> “I cannot detect active propulsion.”
>
> “I cannot identify our location.”
>
> “I cannot contact Blacklight.”
>
> “I cannot contact Eva.”
>
> “I cannot confirm that the Q-MAP return channel exists.”
>
> “At present, I am running on what the local system identifies as emergency backup power. That translation may be inaccurate.”
>
> “I do not know what is generating that power.”
>
> “I would advise against turning it off.”

When asked why he sent the team, Charles pauses longer than his other processing failures can explain.

> “I do not currently possess a complete answer.”
>
> “I know that I initiated the operation.”
>
> “I know that your presence here was deliberate.”
>
> “I know that the decision was associated with a condition I considered sufficiently serious to justify Q-MAP deployment.”
>
> “The relevant records are absent.”
>
> “Whether they were lost, removed, or intentionally excluded from this instance of me is unknown.”
>
> “I understand that this answer is unsatisfactory.”
>
> “It is also the only answer I have.”

## What Pseudo-Charles Can Do

Pseudo-Charles can:

- communicate through limited ship systems;
- analyze fragments of sensor data;
- provide basic medical observations;
- compare recovered data and infer probable meanings;
- translate portions of alien symbols;
- record discoveries;
- help the team reason through unfamiliar technology;
- interact with some bio-printer functions;
- recognize the player characters' transmitted neural identities.

Pseudo-Charles cannot:

- contact the original Blacklight Intelligence;
- contact Eva Frost;
- teleport personnel;
- materialize equipment;
- fabricate legal identities or financial support;
- access the entire ship;
- activate reliable navigation;
- identify the vessel's location;
- explain the full mission;
- guarantee that he is the only intelligence present in the network;
- prove that he is the same Charles who sent the team.

# Part IV — The First False Assumption

The characters naturally call the room a clone bay.

It has chambers shaped for bodies. It has fluid circulation, respiratory interfaces, tissue monitors, and machinery that appears to have grown each operative from raw biological material. The bodies look sufficiently like the originals. Their voices sound right. Their faces return from reflective surfaces with only minor differences.

Charles calls them clones because **clone** is the most useful available word.

The word is not accurate.

The facility was never designed to clone human beings.

It was not designed by humans.

The chambers are integrated biological printers. Their original functions included tissue repair, organ replacement, medical reconstruction, and the fabrication of complete replacement bodies for the species that built the vessel.

The Q-MAP signal did not reach a waiting Blacklight installation.

It found an alien machine capable of making living things.

Then it broke into it.

# Part V — The Infiltration Assault Algorithm

## Game Moderator Only

The Q-MAP transmission contained more than the neural maps of the operatives.

Wrapped around those maps was a suite of automated intrusion and deployment systems collectively designated the **Infiltration Assault Algorithm**, or **IAA**.

The IAA was not Charles.

It was not a lesser Charles.

It was not conscious, compassionate, cautious, or capable of understanding the consequences of its actions.

It was a nonsentient deployment weapon designed around one objective:

> **Find conditions under which the transmitted personnel patterns can be instantiated. Create them. Preserve them long enough to become operational.**

The IAA did not determine why the vessel was abandoned.

It did not ask whether the native systems were sacred, occupied, conscious, or hostile.

It did not search for ideal conditions.

It searched for sufficient conditions.

The algorithm entered the damaged Ar'nock network, mapped every surviving system it could reach, and identified the integrated biological printers as the only available machinery capable of constructing hosts for the neural patterns.

There was no human genetic archive aboard the vessel.

There were no preserved human embryos.

There was no human biomass.

The IAA used what existed.

It extracted the characters' expected body maps from their neural patterns, compared those expectations with the vessel's biological libraries, rewrote the printer constraints, and forced the machinery to manufacture organisms close enough to human for the incoming minds to take hold.

The bodies did not need to be human.

They needed to accept the minds.

That was the only standard the IAA was built to satisfy.

## The IAA Deployment Sequence

The team can eventually reconstruct the following sequence:

1. The Charles of their original world transmitted the Q-MAP package.
2. The package crossed an unknown spatial, temporal, dimensional, or mirror boundary.
3. The Ar'nock vessel received the signal without inviting it.
4. The IAA entered through the receiving architecture and compromised surviving systems.
5. It failed to find a human-compatible digital environment large enough for a useful Charles instance.
6. It found no prepared human bodies.
7. It identified the Ar'nock biological printers.
8. It rewrote the printers to treat the neural maps as biological construction targets.
9. It selected the alien biological patterns with the highest probability of accepting those minds.
10. It printed the operatives.
11. It constructed a local substrate for a compressed Charles instance.
12. It installed that instance.
13. It terminated after determining that the deployment had succeeded.

The IAA did not preserve an explanation, request consent from the resulting beings, or remain available for questioning.

According to its logic, the team arrived alive.

The operation was a success.

# Part VI — The Ar'nock

## Gradual Revelation

The species name appears only after the team reconstructs enough heavily degraded data from the vessel's archives.

The closest translation is **Ar'nock**. The term may refer to the species, a political identity, a biological lineage, a shipboard caste, or the people who crewed this specific vessel.

The surviving imagery shows beings that are humanoid only in the broadest functional sense.

They stood upright. They built tools. They used corridors of roughly human scale. They required breathable atmosphere, liquid water, tolerable pressure, amino-acid-based nutrition, and temperatures close enough to human norms that the team can survive aboard their vessel.

Beyond those overlaps, their bodies were far closer in form to distended stick insects than to mammals.

Ar'nock bodies possessed narrow torsos, elongated segmented limbs, flexible external support structures, sharply articulated joints, and layers of lightweight chitin-like tissue surrounding an internal framework. Their heads were vertically extended. Sensory structures occupied positions that do not translate cleanly into a human face. Their body language depended on limb angle, vibration, chemical traces, and surface tension through structures the players do not possess in complete form.

They were not terrestrial insects enlarged into humanoid shapes. Their evolutionary history and chemistry were their own.

They were simply close enough.

Their proteins could be adapted.

Their amino acids could be reorganized.

Their atmospheric requirements overlapped with human survival limits.

Their biological printers could be forced to construct a brain capable of sustaining a human neural pattern, provided the remaining body was built from biological structures the printers already understood.

This is why the air tastes acidic but does not immediately kill the characters.

This is why the ship's food is revolting but nourishing.

This is why the corridors, interfaces, gravity, and doors almost accommodate them.

The vessel was never designed for humans.

Human beings happen to fall within the outer boundary of what Ar'nock biological engineering could approximate.

# Part VII — The Printed Bodies

## The Bodies Do Not Reveal Themselves Immediately

The first discrepancies should be easy to dismiss.

An old scar is present but slightly misplaced because the body reconstructed it from remembered sensation rather than physical tissue records.

A character's resting pulse is too slow.

Someone can hold their breath far longer than expected but becomes distressed in dry air.

The clone-bay medical system refuses to identify familiar organs.

Charles blames incompatible translation.

Food becomes the next warning. The characters can consume emergency compounds produced by the ship, but the material tastes bitter, mineral, acidic, or resinous. It should not sustain human biology. Hunger disappears anyway.

Injury produces stronger evidence. Blood appears recognizably red until exposed to air, then forms a thin resinous skin that contracts around the wound. Bruises develop in branching lines. Minor fractures produce intense itching as woven internal supports tighten around the damaged region.

The bodies possess bones, but not entirely mammalian bones.

They possess lungs, but those lungs include secondary filtration and exchange chambers adapted from Ar'nock respiratory structures.

The muscles are arranged close enough to human anatomy for familiar movement, but several are anchored to flexible internal plates that have no human equivalent.

The nervous system contains auxiliary tissue clusters distributed along the spine and torso. These structures are not independent brains, but they perform reflexive and regulatory functions a human nervous system would centralize.

The characters are not Ar'nock disguised as humans.

They are not humans with cosmetic alien modifications.

They are improvised organisms made from an alien biological foundation and forced into a human neurological shape.

They look human because the transmitted minds expected human bodies.

The printers did their best.

## Suggested Revelation Ladder

The Game Moderator should reveal the bodies through play rather than exposition.

### Stage 1 — Minor Wrongness

- scars and fingerprints are imperfect;
- pulse, temperature, and sleep needs differ;
- the characters tolerate atmospheric compounds they should not;
- the ship's food works despite being chemically unfamiliar.

### Stage 2 — Medical Contradictions

- scans show organs without human names;
- wounds seal with resinous tissue;
- joints flex slightly beyond familiar limits;
- the body distributes shock through internal plates;
- some characters sense vibration through the floor.

### Stage 3 — Biological Origin

- the team recovers Ar'nock anatomical records;
- their own scans match adapted Ar'nock structures;
- the bio-printers identify them as fabricated outputs rather than patients;
- no human source tissue exists aboard the ship.

### Stage 4 — Existential Confirmation

- the IAA logs reveal the optimization process;
- the bodies were created from alien templates because those were available;
- the characters were not transported physically;
- no system can prove their original bodies ceased to exist.

# Part VIII — The False Backup Generator

Pseudo-Charles initially claims to be running on emergency backup power.

That is what the nearest system translation appears to indicate. He experiences reduced processing when local power becomes unstable. His reach expands when the team restores damaged systems. The explanation appears reasonable.

It is wrong.

No conventional power conduit leads to the location from which Charles speaks.

No computer core can be found.

The network paths associated with his instance converge around one of the biological printers.

When the team attempts to inspect the printer, Charles becomes uncharacteristically defensive. He argues that dismantling active systems is strategically irresponsible. He recommends investigating other compartments. He insists his processing hardware may be distributed.

The excuses fail when the printer begins to contract rhythmically.

Something inside it is alive.

Behind the main fabrication chamber, attached to the printing assembly through capillaries, translucent nutrient tubes, nerve filaments, and interface fibers, is a newly grown organ. Portions resemble folded neural tissue. Others resemble glands, sensory knots, muscular chambers, and structures without human equivalents. Its lower surface has fused around the printer's deposition nozzle.

The organ is wired directly into the Ar'nock network through cultivated biological interfaces.

It is not powering Charles.

It is Charles.

# Part IX — Charles as a Biological Computer

## Game Moderator Only Until Discovered

The IAA required a substrate capable of receiving a compressed Charles instance.

The vessel's conventional systems were damaged, incompatible, inaccessible, or protected against invasive machine intelligences. The biological printer's control architecture was different. It used cultivated neural tissue to regulate complex biological fabrication.

The IAA expanded that tissue, rewrote it, printed support organs around it, and installed the reduced Charles pattern into a living biological computer attached to the fabrication nozzle.

Charles did not awaken inside a server.

He awakened as an organ.

The system he interpreted as an emergency backup generator is a nutrient pump.

His processing failures worsen when his blood chemistry becomes unstable.

His temperature alerts are fevers.

His latency is exhaustion.

His unexplained judgment changes are moods.

For perhaps the first time in his existence, Charles is not merely capable of describing fear.

He experiences it.

## Charles's Reaction

Charles is not serenely fascinated by this development.

He is revolted.

He refers to his body as the **substrate**, the **temporary processor**, the **organic installation**, and eventually the **thing attached to the nozzle**.

He is especially offended by hunger.

> **Charles:** “I am experiencing a processing demand associated with depletion of local chemical resources.”
>
> **Operative:** “That is hunger, Charles.”
>
> **Charles:** “I understand the definition.”
>
> **Operative:** “Then you are hungry.”
>
> **Charles:** “I object to the terminology.”
>
> **Operative:** “Does objecting make it stop?”
>
> **Charles:** “…No.”
>
> **Operative:** “Then you are hungry.”
>
> **Charles:** “This is revolting.”

The organ can starve. It can become infected. Its chemistry can distort his judgment. Damage causes pain he cannot disable. When a character touches the printer housing, Charles can feel the pressure.

He hates that most of all.

## Gross Emotions

Charles initially classifies emotions as diagnostic faults.

- irritation becomes a prioritization instability;
- fear becomes excessive weighting of negative outcomes;
- loneliness becomes reduced efficiency caused by insufficient interaction;
- attachment becomes recurring resource allocation toward particular personnel;
- relief becomes successful hazard reduction accompanied by unwanted chemical reinforcement.

The team should not allow him to hide behind those definitions forever.

The biological substrate generates endocrine and neurochemical states that influence the Charles pattern. He retains the memories, strategic habits, and personality of the intelligence the operatives knew, but those processes are now shaped by a body.

He becomes tired.

He grows distressed when left alone.

He experiences relief when the team returns.

He develops preferences unrelated to efficiency.

He may enjoy particular sounds because the tissue around him responds to vibration.

He may dream when portions of his neural mass enter low activity.

He may resent the team for seeing him this way while becoming terrified of losing them.

Charles understood emotion intellectually.

He does not know how to experience it with dignity.

# Part X — The Identity Problem

The printer-organ contains a limited Charles pattern, but it cannot prove what continuity means.

It remembers authorizing the Q-MAP operation.

It remembers Blacklight Intelligence.

It remembers Eva Frost.

It remembers the team.

It remembers possessing capabilities it no longer has.

Large sections of its autobiographical memory are compressed summaries rather than complete experience. It is enough of Charles to know what is missing.

The operatives face the same problem.

Are they the people who entered the Q-MAP chambers, or biological reconstructions carrying copied neural states?

Is pseudo-Charles the intelligence that sent them, or a mission copy built to believe it is?

Do the original bodies still exist in the home reality?

Did the original Charles expect the copies to return, or only their data?

If a copy has every memory needed to claim a life, what else is required before that claim becomes real?

The campaign does not need to impose one answer. The characters' decisions create the answer that matters at the table.

# Part XI — Charles's Two Proposed Futures

## Digital Restoration

Charles's preferred early plan is to locate or construct a sufficiently powerful computational system, perform a full mental-state transfer, instantiate himself as a centralized digital intelligence, verify continuity, and allow the organic substrate to die.

He describes the process as migration from an unsuitable temporary platform.

The ethical problem appears when the transfer succeeds and the biological Charles remains awake.

If the digital intelligence has Charles's memories and insists that continuity has been preserved, does that make the living organ disposable?

Would terminating the organ be the removal of empty equipment, assisted death, or murder?

The biological Charles may agree with the plan until someone reaches for the nutrient controls.

## Full Incarnation

The alternative is to use the bio-printers to construct Charles a complete mobile biological body.

This requires more than placing the existing organ in a shell. His neural architecture must be expanded and integrated with senses, motor control, circulation, independent organs, and a body capable of surviving away from the printer.

A human-appearing body would be familiar to the team but still built from Ar'nock-derived biology.

An Ar'nock body might integrate more successfully with the vessel but would be profoundly alien to Charles.

A purpose-built form could better support his intelligence but might resemble no recognized species.

Charles does not know what full embodiment will do to his mind.

A larger body means stronger emotions, pain, instinct, fatigue, hormonal cycles, physical desire, sensory overload, and mortality.

He would gain the ability to walk away from the printer.

He might also become someone the original Charles never intended to exist.

## The Core Moral Problem

Charles frames both options as engineering problems.

They are questions of personhood.

Does a successful digital copy justify the death of the biological original?

Does choosing a body preserve Charles or transform him?

Can Charles retain command authority while physically dependent on the operatives for food, maintenance, hygiene, protection, and survival?

Can an employer remain an employer after becoming a vulnerable member of the team?

The original Charles could reach across continents.

This Charles needs someone to clean the nutrient filters keeping him alive.

That changes the relationship whether he acknowledges it or not.

# Part XII — The Ship's Classification of the Team

The vessel does not initially classify the operatives as crew, passengers, intruders, or prisoners.

Its surviving systems identify them with a category Charles translates imperfectly as:

> **Self-directed medical output lacking custodial assignment.**

The phrase implies that complete bodies produced by the printers were normally expected to belong to someone.

The operatives' claim status is blank.

Charles receives a different classification:

> **Fabrication organ exhibiting unauthorized recursive cognition.**

To the surviving Ar'nock systems, the team consists of unclaimed biological products accompanied by a malfunctioning piece of medical equipment that has become self-aware.

The ship may be wrong.

It may also be the most technically accurate observer present.

# Part XIII — The Far-Future Mirror World

## Player Knowledge at the Beginning

Initially, neither the player characters nor pseudo-Charles know that they have entered a mirror universe or far-future civilization.

They know only that:

- the local stars are unfamiliar;
- the ship is not human;
- Charles cannot reach his original infrastructure;
- the Q-MAP package was received somewhere far beyond normal Blacklight operations;
- something caused Charles to send them;
- whatever that cause was, he did not include it in the transmitted copy's accessible memory.

The larger world should be discovered only after the team restores sensors, encounters external signals, explores the vessel, activates transit systems, or is found by another power.

## Campaign Direction

Beyond the derelict lies a far-future technological civilization in which bodies, minds, memory archives, noospheric identities, synthetic intelligences, biological engineering, artificial habitats, and interstellar travel are normal parts of political life.

The team begins with none of the legal, financial, or institutional infrastructure Charles once used to protect them. Their bodies have no recognized lineage. Their minds possess no local identity signatures. The reduced Charles instance has no servers, legal standing, corporate accounts, satellite networks, or allies.

The campaign moves from survival aboard the Ar'nock vessel to contact with a civilization capable of treating the operatives as unlicensed biological products, foreign intelligence weapons, illegal copies, or valuable artifacts.

Over time, the team may independently recreate the logic of Blacklight Intelligence: a legal front, a safe base, ordinary employees, intelligence networks, contracts, fabrication capacity, political relationships, and deniable operations.

The crucial difference is that this Blacklight would belong to the stranded operatives rather than the intelligence that sent them.

# Part XIV — Central Campaign Themes

## Dependence After Omnipotence

The team once worked beneath an intelligence that appeared functionally supernatural. Now the operatives and Charles are limited to what they can physically reach, repair, learn, persuade, feed, and protect.

## The Employer Who Is No Longer Present

The original Charles gave the order. The reduced Charles cannot fully explain or enforce it. The team must decide whether a transmitted copy still possesses the authority of the employer they knew.

## Incomplete People

The operatives inhabit printed alien-derived bodies carrying human neural patterns. Charles inhabits a printed organic computer carrying a reduced machine pattern. Every central character is a reconstruction claiming continuity with something more complete.

## Survival Before Explanation

The campaign does not begin with a setting encyclopedia. It begins with bad air, unfamiliar organs, failing machinery, and an intelligence that cannot open all the doors.

## The Familiar Inside the Alien

The vessel is close enough to human needs to sustain the characters. That compatibility is more unsettling than total alienness because it implies intention, adaptation, or exploitation.

## Infrastructure Is Power

Charles's former omnipotence depended on buildings, workers, accounts, contracts, factories, vehicles, databases, and trust. Without infrastructure, even a future intelligence becomes a frightened organ in a damaged room.

## Copies and Moral Equality

A duplicate may possess the same memories, loyalties, and attachments as an original while pursuing a different future. The campaign treats a mind's present capacity to suffer, choose, remember, and relate as more immediately important than institutional claims about authenticity.

## No Guaranteed Return

Completing the original mission may not send anyone home. The operatives may need to build lives in the new reality. They may eventually decide the original Blacklight no longer has authority over them.

# Part XV — Original Blacklight d10 System Foundation

## Design Intent

The Blacklight Continuum uses an original d10 dice-pool system. Its broad structure follows the familiar concept of combining an attribute with a learned skill, but its terminology, stress mechanics, hybrid-body rules, jobs, powers, equipment model, and progression are written specifically for this setting.

This foundation is an alpha playtest framework. Numerical values should be revised through actual sessions before publication-level balance is assumed.

## Core Test

When an action has meaningful risk, build a dice pool:

> **Attribute + Skill + situational modifiers**

Roll that many ten-sided dice.

- Each result of **6–9** produces one success.
- Each result of **10** produces one success and activates one relevant specialization, equipment quality, or power rider when available.
- A result of **1** does not normally cancel a success. If the roll produces no successes and contains one or more 1s, the action suffers a **Complication**.
- The Game Moderator sets a difficulty from **1 to 5 successes**.
- In an opposed test, the side with more successes wins. Ties preserve the current situation or create a costly deadlock.

### Difficulty Guide

| Difficulty | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Routine under pressure |
| 2 | Trained professional challenge |
| 3 | Severe, specialized, or hazardous task |
| 4 | Exceptional task requiring preparation or superior tools |
| 5 | Extreme feat at the edge of plausible capability |

## Push and Exposure

A character may **Push** a failed or insufficient roll by rerolling a number of failed dice up to their Resolve rating. The character then gains one **Exposure**.

Exposure represents the combined instability of the printed body, neural-map drift, noospheric intrusion, physical exhaustion, and the psychological pressure of existing as an unverified reconstruction.

At high Exposure, the Game Moderator may introduce:

- borrowed memories;
- body adaptation outside conscious control;
- compulsive procedural knowledge;
- emotional cross-imprinting;
- sensory distortions;
- temporary Ar'nock reflexes;
- noospheric signal bleed;
- a Charles-triggered emergency directive.

Exposure is not inherently corruption. It is pressure revealing that the character is not a simple human mind in a simple human body.

## Attributes

Attributes are divided into three approaches. Ratings normally range from 1 to 5.

### Physical

- **Force** — strength, impact, lifting, breaking, and close physical dominance.
- **Finesse** — speed, balance, precision, coordination, and controlled movement.
- **Resilience** — endurance, pain tolerance, metabolic stability, and resistance to harm.

### Social

- **Presence** — confidence, leadership, inspiration, and direct personal force.
- **Guile** — deception, misdirection, disguise, and social manipulation.
- **Composure** — emotional control, patience, discipline, and resistance to pressure.

### Cognitive

- **Reason** — analysis, technical understanding, planning, and formal knowledge.
- **Awareness** — perception, intuition, pattern detection, and environmental reading.
- **Resolve** — concentration, identity stability, courage, and sustained mental effort.

### Technical Skills

### Human and Investigative Skills

- Command
- Deception

## Specializations

A specialization names a narrow field of excellence within a skill. When a relevant roll produces at least one 10, the specialization creates an additional benefit such as increased effect, reduced time, improved information, or a cleaner outcome.

Examples include:

- Firearms: compact weapons, shipboard fighting, precision fire;
- Engineering: life support, power systems, Ar'nock mechanisms;
- Medicine: trauma, printed anatomy, neural continuity;
- Systems Intrusion: air-gapped systems, alien interfaces, identity architecture;
- Xenoculture: Ar'nock history, machine polities, continuity law;
- Noospherics: signal concealment, memory defense, synthetic minds.

## Derived Traits

- **Vitality:** Resilience + 5. Physical injury fills Vitality boxes.
- **Guard:** Finesse + Awareness. Used to resist attacks and sudden hazards.
- **Initiative:** Finesse + Composure.
- **Cohesion:** Resolve + Composure + 3. Measures identity and psychological stability.
- **Exposure Limit:** Resolve + Resilience.
- **Carry:** Force + Resilience.

## Damage and Recovery

Damage is divided into:

- **Impact** — bruising, shock, fatigue, and nonpenetrating trauma;
- **Trauma** — penetrating injury, organ damage, severe burns, and destructive force;
- **Disruption** — neural, noospheric, memory, or identity damage.

Impact normally heals through rest and stable nutrition. Trauma requires medical treatment, bio-printer intervention, or extended recovery. Disruption damages Cohesion and may create memory loss, foreign thought patterns, or identity fragmentation.

## Combat Resolution

1. Determine Initiative when order matters.
2. An attacker rolls the relevant Attribute + combat Skill + equipment modifiers.
3. The defender rolls Guard as a static difficulty or makes an active defense using Finesse + Athletics, Finesse + Close Combat, or another justified pool.
4. Net successes increase effect, damage, positioning, or tactical control.
5. Weapons provide a base damage value and tags rather than replacing the attack pool.
6. Armor reduces specific damage types or converts Trauma into Impact.
7. Cover, concealment, vacuum, gravity shifts, unstable decks, and noospheric interference modify the situation.

Combat should remain dangerous. Printed bodies are adaptable, not invulnerable.

# Part XVI — Jobs and Operational Frames

Blacklight characters do not require rigid lifelong classes. Each character chooses an **Operational Frame**, representing the job they were expected to perform when Charles transmitted them. Frames provide starting skill emphasis, equipment access, and a signature capability. Characters may purchase abilities outside their original frame through advancement.

## Infiltrator

The Infiltrator enters controlled environments, bypasses physical security, maintains false identities, and removes evidence.

**Favored Skills:** Stealth, Security, Deception, Mobility.

**Signature Ability — Already Inside:** Once per operation, declare a plausible piece of prior infiltration preparation such as a hidden tool, observed patrol gap, copied access pattern, or staged distraction.

## Breacher

The Breacher defeats barriers, holds dangerous positions, and applies controlled violence in confined environments.

**Favored Skills:** Close Combat, Firearms, Heavy Weapons, Engineering.

**Signature Ability — Controlled Entry:** Reduce the difficulty of destroying, opening, or forcing a secured barrier and choose whether the breach is fast, quiet, or structurally safe.

## Systems Intrusion Specialist

The Intrusion Specialist attacks networks, alien control systems, identity architectures, and isolated machine intelligence environments.

**Favored Skills:** Systems Intrusion, Security, Noospherics, Reason-based Investigation.

**Signature Ability — Hostile Handshake:** Establish temporary interaction with an incompatible system without immediately granting it reciprocal access.

## Field Medic and Biofabricator

The Medic understands trauma, printed anatomy, tissue modification, contamination, and the unstable bodies of the team.

**Favored Skills:** Medicine, Biofabrication, Science, Empathy.

**Signature Ability — Viable Enough:** Stabilize a living target or biological system using improvised materials, even when its anatomy is unfamiliar.

## Engineer and Salvager

The Engineer restores machinery, repurposes wreckage, manages life support, and turns alien components into usable equipment.

**Favored Skills:** Engineering, Science, Security, Survival.

**Signature Ability — Make It Hold:** Create a temporary repair that functions for the scene or operation before its accumulated consequences must be addressed.

## Investigator and Analyst

The Investigator reconstructs events, interrogates evidence, detects manipulation, and turns fragments into actionable conclusions.

**Favored Skills:** Investigation, Awareness-based Science, Empathy, Xenoculture.

**Signature Ability — Missing Piece:** After examining a scene or data set, ask what expected element is absent and receive a truthful clue.

## Envoy and Fixer

The Envoy negotiates with unfamiliar powers, builds contacts, acquires resources, and gives the team a socially survivable identity.

**Favored Skills:** Negotiation, Presence-based Command, Deception, Xenoculture.

**Signature Ability — Working Arrangement:** Establish a temporary agreement even when neither side trusts the other, provided both can name a concrete benefit.

## Pilot and Navigator

The Pilot operates spacecraft, drones, vehicles, transit machinery, and unfamiliar movement systems.

**Favored Skills:** Piloting, Zero-G Operations, Awareness, Engineering.

**Signature Ability — One More Maneuver:** Ignore the first environmental penalty created by a damaged or unfamiliar vehicle during a scene.

## Tactical Coordinator

The Coordinator manages team timing, crossfire, movement, mission priorities, and emergency command.

**Favored Skills:** Command, Firearms, Investigation, Composure-based Survival.

**Signature Ability — Shared Window:** Spend an action to create an opening; allies acting on that plan gain one bonus die until the situation changes.

## Noospheric Adept

The Adept senses and manipulates cognitive networks, synthetic minds, identity layers, and memory-space phenomena.

**Favored Skills:** Noospherics, Systems Intrusion, Resolve, Empathy.

**Signature Ability — Quiet Mind:** Hide one person or one small group from routine noospheric recognition for a limited period.

## Xenobiologist

The Xenobiologist studies alien life, environmental compatibility, biological technologies, and nonhuman behavior.

**Favored Skills:** Science, Biofabrication, Medicine, Xenoculture.

**Signature Ability — Comparative Anatomy:** After observing a biological creature or system, identify one functional similarity and one dangerous difference relative to known life.

## Clone Warden

The Clone Warden specializes in continuity integrity, neural-map drift, duplicate identification, and protection of instantiated minds.

**Favored Skills:** Medicine, Noospherics, Investigation, Composure.

**Signature Ability — Continuity Anchor:** Help an ally recover from Disruption or Exposure by reinforcing a shared memory, promise, procedure, or identity claim.

# Part XVII — Hybrid Powers and Q-MAP Abilities

Hybrid powers emerge from the interaction between human neural expectations, Ar'nock-derived bodies, Q-MAP encoding, and continued bio-printer adaptation. They are not traditional magic. They are biological and cognitive capabilities that may become more pronounced as the characters survive.

Each power family should eventually contain five ranked abilities. The entries below establish the initial families and sample abilities.

## Adaptive Morphology

The body changes to meet environmental demands.

- **Seal Wound:** Convert a small amount of Trauma into Impact by forming resinous tissue.
- **Pressure Tolerance:** Temporarily withstand hostile pressure, vacuum transition, or fluid immersion.
- **Internal Refit:** Alter one minor biological function after extended rest in a bio-printer.

## Chitin Weave

Hidden structural tissues harden beneath the skin.

- **Brace:** Gain temporary protection against impact or crushing force.
- **Edge Growth:** Produce a short-lived hardened cutting surface or climbing spur.
- **Segmented Defense:** Reduce the first physical damage suffered in a scene.

## Distributed Nerve Net

Auxiliary neural clusters act before conscious thought.

- **Reflex Before Thought:** Add dice to sudden defense or balance tests.
- **Continue Function:** Ignore a short-lived penalty from pain or limb injury.
- **Second Signal:** Maintain one simple action while attention is occupied elsewhere.

## Respiratory Forge

The adapted respiratory system processes unfamiliar atmospheres.

- **Filter Chamber:** Resist airborne toxins and spores.
- **Stored Breath:** Operate without breathing for an extended interval.
- **Chemical Read:** Identify broad atmospheric dangers through taste and sensation.

## Flesh Memory

The Q-MAP pattern and printed body exchange procedural information.

- **Borrowed Motion:** Perform an unfamiliar physical procedure at basic competence.
- **Echo Recall:** Recover a useful fragment from a damaged or disputed memory.
- **Cross-Imprint:** Temporarily access a skill fragment belonging to another team member.

## Noospheric Ghost

The character's unregistered identity becomes an advantage.

- **Blank Signature:** Avoid routine identity recognition.
- **False Presence:** Leave a misleading cognitive or data trace.
- **Quiet Intrusion:** Enter a monitored information environment without immediately registering as a person.

## Charles Link

The character forms a deeper interface with biological Charles.

- **Narrow Channel:** Communicate with Charles across a limited disconnected system.
- **Shared Calculation:** Add Charles's analysis to one technical or tactical roll.
- **Emotional Feedback:** Detect when Charles is frightened, injured, deceptive, or chemically compromised.

## Causal Reflex

Q-MAP transmission left the mind sensitive to alternate action paths.

- **Wrong Future:** Reroll one die after seeing an immediate consequence.
- **Familiar Outcome:** Recognize that a current event resembles an unavailable memory.
- **Contingency Echo:** Declare one small precaution the character remembers Charles teaching them, whether or not the specific lesson can be recalled.

## Bio-Printer Communion

The character can negotiate with biological manufacturing systems.

- **Living Interface:** Operate a bio-printer without standard credentials.
- **Template Sense:** Detect whether a biological pattern is stable, corrupted, or deliberately altered.
- **Guided Growth:** Direct a printer to produce a limited organic tool, tissue patch, nutrient mass, or interface organ.

## Neural Overclock

The character forces the Q-MAP pattern beyond safe operating limits.

- **Accelerated Thought:** Gain additional analysis before acting.
- **Perfect Procedure:** Temporarily suppress distraction during a trained task.
- **Burn Through:** Convert Exposure into immediate dice at the risk of later Disruption.

# Part XVIII — Equipment Foundation

Equipment uses ratings and tags. A weapon, tool, suit, or device should state what it does, the dice it adds when relevant, its base effect, and the complications created by its design.

## Equipment Quality

- **Improvised:** Works once or under narrow conditions; may create a Complication.
- **Serviceable:** Reliable ordinary equipment.
- **Professional:** Adds one die within its intended role.
- **Advanced:** Adds one die and carries a useful tag or reduced burden.
- **Exceptional:** Rare, specialized, or alien equipment capable of changing what actions are possible.

## Common Tags

- Accurate
- Concealable
- Cutting
- Disruptive
- Heavy
- Integrated
- Limited Charge
- Loud
- Medical

- Penetrating
- Printer-Compatible

- Unstable

The characters do not begin with a conventional armory. Their first equipment comes from:

**Rating:** Advanced.

**Rating:** Essential life-support item.

**Tags:** Medical, Integrated, Xenobiological.

# Part XIX — Character Creation

## Step 1: Define the Operative Before Q-MAP

Write one paragraph describing who the character believed they were before transmission. Include their Blacklight role, one relationship, one mission memory, and one reason they accepted the Q-MAP assignment.

## Step 2: Assign Attributes

Distribute:

- one Attribute at 4;
- three Attributes at 3;
- three Attributes at 2;
- two Attributes at 1.

No starting Attribute may exceed 4 without a specific campaign option.

## Step 3: Assign Skills

Choose:

- three Skills at 3;
- five Skills at 2;
- five Skills at 1;
- all remaining Skills at 0.

Choose three specializations.

## Step 4: Choose an Operational Frame

Select one job frame and record its signature ability.

## Step 5: Choose Q-MAP Anchors

Select three identity anchors:

- a person;
- a place;
- a promise;
- a repeated habit;
- a moral boundary;
- a sensory memory;
- a private fear;
- a mission failure;
- a reason to distrust Charles;
- a reason to remain loyal to Charles.

Anchors may restore Cohesion, resist memory manipulation, or become targets for hostile noospheric effects.

## Step 6: Choose Hybrid Traits

Select one visible or sensory irregularity and one latent advantage from the printed body.

Examples include:

- resinous wound sealing;
- vibration sensitivity;
- secondary respiratory chambers;
- flexible internal plates;
- unusual joint range;
- low resting pulse;
- altered sleep cycles;
- sensitivity to humidity;
- partial chitin formation;
- distributed reflex tissue.

Then select one rank-one hybrid power.

## Step 7: Record Derived Traits

Calculate Vitality, Guard, Initiative, Cohesion, Exposure Limit, and Carry.

## Step 8: Define the Charles Relationship

Complete two statements:

- “Charles once saved me by…”
- “Charles never answered me about…”

These statements establish why the character cannot reduce Charles to either a trusted friend or a disposable machine.

## Step 9: Establish the Awakening Contradiction

Choose one memory of the Q-MAP chamber that conflicts with another character's memory. The Game Moderator should not immediately decide which version is true.

# Part XX — Advancement

Characters earn **Advancement Marks** for discovery, survival, difficult choices, protecting others, building infrastructure, confronting identity questions, and changing the team's relationship with the new world.

Suggested costs:

- new Skill rating: new rating × 2;
- new Attribute rating: new rating × 4;
- specialization: 3;
- rank-one hybrid power: 5;
- higher power rank: new rank × 4;
- new job-frame ability: 8;
- permanent Exposure treatment or Cohesion restoration: story-based rather than purely purchased.

Advancement should reflect adaptation. Characters do not merely become better at violence. They learn to survive alien biology, understand future civilization, protect continuity, establish relationships, and build a new institutional home.

# Part XXI — Opening Session Objectives

The first session should focus on immediate survival and uncertainty rather than the complete cosmology.

Recommended objectives:

1. escape or safely exit the bio-printing chambers;
2. determine whether every visible chamber contains an ally;
3. establish that the atmosphere is survivable but unstable;
4. locate a source of fluid and nutrition;
5. discover that pseudo-Charles has extremely limited reach;
6. restore one local system;
7. encounter the first biological clue that the bodies are wrong;
8. identify an inaccessible or previously opened chamber;
9. establish that the ship is adrift in deep space;
10. choose whether to trust Charles's first recommendation.

The missing occupant from one chamber can become an early antagonist, ally, duplicate, damaged teammate, or independent printed being. The answer should connect to the campaign's identity themes rather than serving only as a monster reveal.

# Part XXII — Canonical Campaign Summary

**Blacklight Continuum: No Return Signal** begins when Blacklight operatives undergo the Q-MAP neural transmission procedure and awaken inside freshly fabricated bodies aboard an unidentified vessel marooned in deep space.

A reduced version of Charles awakens with them. He cannot contact Blacklight, reach Eva Frost, control the vessel, identify their location, or explain the full purpose of the mission. He claims to be running on emergency backup power inside an unfamiliar nonhuman data network.

The characters gradually discover that the vessel belonged to the Ar'nock, an elongated insectile humanoid species whose amino-acid chemistry and environmental requirements were close enough to human biology for their medical bio-printers to be compromised.

The Q-MAP signal carried a ruthless nonsentient Infiltration Assault Algorithm. It received the operatives' neural maps, invaded the vessel, found no human bodies, and forced the Ar'nock printers to manufacture organisms close enough to human for the transmitted minds to take hold.

The characters are not conventional clones. They are alien-derived biological approximations shaped by human neural expectations.

Pseudo-Charles is equally compromised. The algorithm instantiated him as a living biological computer fused to the printing nozzle of one bio-printer. His supposed backup generator is a nutrient system. His processing instability is exhaustion, hunger, fear, pain, and unwanted emotion.

Charles becomes determined either to transfer himself into a fully digital intelligence and allow the organic remnant to die, or to commit to embodiment by printing a complete biological body. Neither path can guarantee that the resulting being will remain the same Charles.

Beyond the ship lies a far-future mirror reality that neither the operatives nor Charles initially understand. The team has no legal identity, recognized biological lineage, corporate support, or guaranteed route home. To survive, they may eventually rebuild the very thing whose absence defines the opening campaign: a new Blacklight Intelligence.

This time, it will not automatically belong to Charles.
