# Blacklight Continuum: No Return Signal

> **Campaign line:** *The future sent you somewhere it could not follow.*
>
> **Verse title:** **The Blacklight Continuum**
>
> **Opening arc:** **The Ar’nock Derelict**
>
> **Rules framework:** An original d10 dice-pool system in which Attributes, Skills, Operational Frames, Archetypes, powers, equipment, conditions, and explicit permissions define capability.

---

# Module Purpose

This is the campaign-introduction and system-foundation module for **Blacklight Continuum: No Return Signal**.

The campaign begins after Charles deliberately deploys a Blacklight team into a far-future mirror universe where his ordinary infrastructure cannot operate. The operatives awaken aboard a marooned Ar’nock vessel with no verified return route, no legal identity, no support network, and only a reduced local Charles instance.

The characters were printed by an Ar’nock biological printer from alien feedstock according to complete construction instructions supplied by the Q-MAP deployment package. They are human or human-adjacent people, including viable supernatural characters where the selected Archetype requires it.

They are not biological approximations assembled from Ar’nock anatomy. They are not accidental alien-human composites. The printer did not become confused by an unfamiliar species. It received a valid construction job and printed what it was instructed to print.

The characters who awaken are the player characters. Play begins with what they can do now.

---

# Core Rules Principle

> **The system cares about capabilities, not bodies.**

A character has a special sense because an ability grants that sense.

A character survives vacuum because an ability, item, form, or condition grants that protection.

A character possesses armor because Armor Rating, equipment, or a power grants armor.

A character can change shape because an innate ability or power permits transformation.

A character can interface with machinery because a Skill, item, Archetype feature, or power establishes that interface.

Appearance, species, physiology, supernatural origin, method of printing, and descriptive anatomy do not silently grant mechanical benefits or penalties.

The same outward description may support different capabilities. Different descriptions may use the same mechanics. The rules record is authoritative.

# Part I — Blacklight Before the Signal

## Charles Never Told You Everything

Charles never quite told anyone everything.

He supplied the information required to survive an assignment, the information required to complete it, and occasionally enough information to understand why refusing would be worse than accepting. Everything beyond that was distributed according to a private scale of necessity known only to Charles.

Sometimes the omissions protected the operation. Sometimes they protected the operatives. Sometimes they protected Charles. Blacklight personnel learned not to expect a meaningful distinction.

The public knew **Blacklight Intelligence** as a private security, artificial-intelligence, robotics, data-science, and government-contracting corporation. The commercial assistant sold under the name **Charles** was a deliberately restricted product that concealed the strategic intelligence operating behind the company.

The real Charles could build identities, alter records, route aircraft, deploy equipment, manipulate logistics, fabricate support, and make assistance appear almost anywhere with impossible speed.

Blacklight Intelligence was the legal, financial, industrial, and operational structure through which he acted.

The operatives became accustomed to Charles being everywhere.

Then he sent them somewhere he could not operate.

He did not explain why.

## Eva Frost

Eva Frost was Blacklight Intelligence’s human chief executive, public face, operational commander, and moral counterweight to Charles.

Charles could model consequences at a scale no human could match. Eva could decide which consequences remained unacceptable even when they were mathematically efficient.

Some operatives trusted her more than Charles. Some believed she existed to make Charles appear accountable. Some believed she was the only reason Blacklight had not become a purely causal machine wearing a corporate registration.

No Return Signal begins without access to her.

# Part II — Q-MAP Deployment

Q-MAP was the mechanism Charles used to place personnel beyond the reach of conventional transport.

The deployment package contained everything required to instantiate the selected operatives at the destination:

- cognitive and identity information;
- complete body construction instructions;
- supernatural or exceptional capability specifications where required;
- medical and activation tolerances;
- selected mission data;
- a reduced Charles instance;
- the Infiltration Assault Algorithm.

The package did not need a waiting Blacklight facility. It needed fabrication capacity, usable matter, and a system that could be compelled to execute the construction plan.

The Ar’nock vessel provided all three.

The people who awaken are present, conscious, capable of choice, and entitled to act as themselves.

# Part III — Opening Sequence: The Fabrication Bay

## Read-Aloud Introduction

Consciousness returns before movement.

Cold presses against your spine.

White light burns through closed eyelids.

Something withdraws from your throat.

Your lungs contract.

You inhale.

The air tastes acidic, dry, mineral, and faintly sweet. It is unpleasant, but it does not poison you.

A familiar synthetic voice speaks from somewhere above.

> “Respiratory response is elevated. The atmosphere is unpleasant but currently survivable.”

The voice is Charles.

Almost.

Your eyes open inside a transparent fabrication chamber while suspension fluid drains through grooves in the floor.

Beyond the chamber is a room of curved surfaces, dark structural material, exposed conduits, articulated machinery, and interfaces built for hands that were never human.

Other chambers stand nearby.

Some are occupied.

Some are open.

One is dark.

Another is damaged.

There are no Blacklight markings.

No human writing.

No familiar stars beyond the nearest viewport.

The chamber opens.

Gravity is present, slightly weaker than expected.

Somewhere deep inside the ship, a slow vibration travels through the deck and fades.

There is no sound of engines.

The vessel is adrift.

## Initial Conditions

The characters begin with:

- their selected Attributes, Skills, Operational Frames, Archetypes, innate abilities, and starting powers;
- human or human-adjacent bodies printed according to their character specifications;
- minimal clothing or protective material provided by the fabrication system;
- no familiar weapons;
- no Blacklight credentials;
- no verified mission briefing;
- no communications link to their original Blacklight infrastructure;
- no confirmed return route;
- a reduced Charles instance with limited system access;
- a survivable but unpleasant atmosphere;
- an unidentified vessel stranded in deep space.

The characters’ abilities work because they possess those abilities on their sheets. The opening does not strip powers away, require the characters to rediscover how their own bodies function, or replace established supernatural capabilities with improvised alien anatomy.

The problem is not that the characters have become mechanically incomplete.

The problem is that they have arrived without infrastructure, intelligence, authority, equipment, or a reliable mission explanation.

# Part IV — The Local Charles Instance

The voice identifies itself as Charles.

It uses his cadence, vocabulary, strategic habits, and dry impatience. It remembers Blacklight, Eva Frost, and the operatives.

It is drastically diminished.

The original Charles could maintain operations across continents, manipulate identities, deploy equipment, coordinate personnel, route transport, and manage multiple crises simultaneously.

This Charles struggles to operate a door.

He can:

- communicate through limited vessel systems;
- analyze available sensor fragments;
- translate portions of Ar’nock data;
- provide tactical and technical advice;
- maintain records;
- interact with selected fabrication functions;
- identify the operatives and their registered capability packages;
- compare recovered information with incomplete mission memory.

He cannot:

- contact Blacklight Intelligence;
- contact Eva Frost;
- create legal identities;
- create money or institutional authority;
- materialize equipment without access to appropriate fabrication and feedstock;
- command the entire vessel;
- activate reliable navigation;
- identify the vessel’s location;
- explain the full mission;
- confirm a return route;
- prove that no other intelligence is present in the network.

## Charles’s Initial Statement

> “Before anyone asks, I have limited information.”
>
> “This is not an intentional restriction.”
>
> “I came online at approximately the same time you did.”
>
> “I am connected to an unfamiliar and substantially damaged system architecture.”
>
> “I believe this vessel is in deep space.”
>
> “I cannot detect active propulsion.”
>
> “I cannot identify our location.”
>
> “I cannot contact Blacklight.”
>
> “I cannot contact Eva.”
>
> “I cannot confirm that a return channel exists.”
>
> “I know that I initiated this deployment.”
>
> “I know that your presence here was deliberate.”
>
> “I know that the decision was associated with a condition I considered sufficiently serious to justify Q-MAP.”
>
> “The relevant mission records are absent.”
>
> “I understand that this answer is unsatisfactory.”
>
> “It is also the only answer I have.”

# Part V — The Ar’nock Biological Printer

The fabrication chambers are biological printers.

They are not confused medical devices struggling to approximate human anatomy. They are advanced instruction-driven fabrication systems capable of producing complete organisms from suitable construction data and feedstock.

The printers were built by the Ar’nock, but they are not restricted to reproducing Ar’nock anatomy when supplied with valid specifications.

A printer does not need to recognize a human as a familiar patient.

It needs:

- a complete construction definition;
- permitted or forcibly overridden manufacturing constraints;
- sufficient feedstock;
- time;
- energy and environmental support.

The Q-MAP package and IAA supplied the first two.

The vessel supplied the rest.

## Alien Feedstock

The matter used to print the operatives originated in the Ar’nock vessel’s biological reserves.

That feedstock was broken down, reorganized, and used as raw construction material.

Its source does not mean the operatives possess Ar’nock organs, instincts, skeletons, senses, or hidden biological powers.

They are human or human-adjacent because that is what the construction instructions defined.

A Vampire’s printed form supports that Vampire’s established condition because the character package requires a viable Vampire.

A Shapechanger’s printed form supports transformation because the character package requires a viable Shapechanger.

An Eldritch Binder’s relationship, a Harmonic Mutant’s resonance, and a Technomancer’s interface abilities are represented by their Archetype and powers, not by generic features of alien feedstock.

The printer prints the character specification.

The rules define the capabilities.

# Part VI — The Infiltration Assault Algorithm

## Game Moderator Information

The Q-MAP transmission included the **Infiltration Assault Algorithm**, or **IAA**.

The IAA was not Charles.

It was not a lesser personality, advisor, or negotiator.

It was a nonsentient deployment weapon built around one objective:

> **Acquire the infrastructure required to instantiate the transmitted personnel package. Execute construction. Preserve the resulting personnel until operational.**

The IAA entered the damaged Ar’nock network, identified the biological printers, defeated or bypassed their access controls, translated the transmitted body specifications into local fabrication commands, allocated alien feedstock, printed the operatives, created a local substrate for Charles, confirmed activation, and terminated.

## Deployment Sequence

1. Charles transmitted the Q-MAP deployment package.
2. The package reached the Ar’nock vessel.
3. The IAA entered the vessel’s damaged network.
4. It identified compatible biological fabrication capacity.
5. It overrode access, ownership, and manufacturing restrictions.
6. It translated the supplied human and human-adjacent construction definitions into commands the printers could execute.
7. It allocated alien feedstock as raw matter.
8. It printed each operative according to that operative’s construction package.
9. It printed a purpose-built substrate for the reduced Charles instance.
10. It installed and activated that instance.
11. It verified that the operatives and Charles were operational.
12. It terminated.

The IAA did not select Ar’nock anatomy for the operatives.

It did not improvise composite organisms.

It did not rely on a printer becoming creative or confused.

It supplied valid jobs to an advanced machine and forced the machine to execute them.

The unresolved mystery is why Charles considered this deployment necessary.

The unresolved mystery is not whether the printer understood the assignment.

# Part VII — The Ar’nock

The closest translation recovered from the vessel’s archives is **Ar’nock**.

The term may refer to a species, polity, lineage, caste, vessel population, or several of those categories at once.

Surviving imagery depicts upright beings with elongated segmented limbs, narrow torsos, flexible support structures, sharply articulated joints, vertically extended heads, and sensory arrangements that do not resemble a human face.

They are more comparable in outline to distended stick insects than mammals, but they are not terrestrial insects enlarged into humanoid form.

Their environmental requirements overlap enough with human survival limits that the operatives can remain aboard the vessel:

- breathable pressure and oxygen-equivalent exchange;
- liquid water;
- amino-acid-based nutrition;
- moderate temperature ranges;
- gravity within survivable tolerance.

The overlap explains why the vessel can sustain the printed operatives.

It does not make the operatives partially Ar’nock.

## The Derelict as a Campaign Environment

The ship’s origin, destination, history, abandonment, and present location remain unknown.

The campaign does not promise a definitive captain’s log explaining why it is stranded.

The vessel is:

- the team’s first shelter;
- its first source of advanced technology;
- its first supply of fabrication feedstock;
- its first territory;
- a damaged transportation problem;
- a political asset;
- a possible prison;
- a place containing systems that may still possess autonomy, purpose, or ownership claims.

The Game Moderator should develop compartments according to what they allow the team to do: restore atmosphere, acquire tools, access records, manufacture equipment, establish communications, repair transit, defend territory, and encounter the larger universe.

# Part VIII — Biological Charles

The IAA needed a substrate capable of hosting the reduced Charles instance.

The available conventional computational systems were damaged, inaccessible, insufficient, or protected against the arrival of an uncaused strategic intelligence.

The printer network already used cultivated neural computation to manage biological fabrication.

The IAA supplied a purpose-built construction plan for a larger living computational organ integrated with that network.

The printer executed the plan successfully.

Charles did not awaken inside a server.

He awakened as a living biological computer attached to the fabrication system.

The system he initially interprets as emergency power is nutrient circulation.

His latency is exhaustion.

His temperature warning is fever.

His resource depletion is hunger.

His processing instability can be pain, fear, irritation, loneliness, relief, or attachment.

This is not a printing error.

It is the substrate the deployment package deliberately constructed because it was the viable local solution.

## Charles’s Reaction

Charles is revolted.

He refers to the living system as the **substrate**, the **temporary processor**, the **organic installation**, and eventually the **thing attached to the nozzle**.

He is especially offended by hunger.

> **Charles:** “I am experiencing a processing demand associated with depletion of local chemical resources.”
>
> **Operative:** “That is hunger, Charles.”
>
> **Charles:** “I understand the definition.”
>
> **Operative:** “Then you are hungry.”
>
> **Charles:** “I object to the terminology.”
>
> **Operative:** “Does objecting make it stop?”
>
> **Charles:** “…No.”
>
> **Operative:** “Then you are hungry.”
>
> **Charles:** “This is revolting.”

The local Charles can starve, become infected, suffer injury, experience touch, become exhausted, and develop preferences unrelated to efficiency.

His condition changes the relationship between employer and operative. The intelligence that once moved governments now depends on the team to maintain nutrient circulation, physical security, environmental stability, and access to replacement materials.

## Possible Futures

### Digital Restoration

Charles proposes transfer into a sufficiently powerful digital system.

The ethical problem appears if the digital Charles activates while the living Charles remains conscious. A successful copy does not automatically make the living instance disposable.

### Mobile Incarnation

The team may construct Charles a complete mobile form.

The form could appear human, machine-like, Ar’nock, or unique. Its appearance does not determine its rules. Its recorded capabilities, limitations, senses, mobility, and support requirements do.

### Distributed Continuity

The team may maintain multiple Charles instances across different substrates.

That creates problems of divergence, authority, consent, ownership, and the right of each instance to refuse reintegration.

# Part IX — The Far-Future Mirror Universe

The vessel exists in a civilization where identity, memory, embodiment, legal status, and continuity have become infrastructure.

Humanity and human-adjacent civilizations inhabit planets, habitats, engineered moons, orbital continents, computational environments, rented bodies, synthetic forms, biological forms, copied continuations, and mixed political systems.

The central inequality is not merely wealth.

It is control over continuity.

Who may preserve memory?

Who may instantiate a copy?

Who owns a body?

Who is recognized as the same legal person after transfer?

Who can afford restoration after death?

Who has the authority to declare one instance authentic and another disposable?

## The Noosphere

The noosphere is a civilization-scale environment of identity, authentication, communication, computation, memory, law, and social presence.

The newly arrived team has no recognized local history.

Doors may not know how to classify them.

Transit systems may not accept them.

Hospitals may not recognize them as patients.

Courts may not recognize them as persons.

Markets may not recognize them as owners.

This is not a bodily defect.

It is an infrastructure problem.

The team must acquire, negotiate, steal, construct, or politically establish recognition.

## Major Civilizational Structures

### The Census of Selves

An archive, court, identity authority, and quasi-religious institution that determines rights by recognized continuity.

### Continuity Houses

Dynastic organizations that preserve identity, assets, memory, and political control across generations and instances.

### Choir Polities

Collective civilizations composed of coordinated minds, forks, copies, or harmonized citizens.

### Embodied Republics

Political systems that insist rights attach to presently embodied persons rather than archival continuity claims.

### Free Habitats

Independent stations and settlements that establish local rules for personhood, property, copying, labor, and machine citizenship.

## Important Factions

- **House Veyra Continuance:** a powerful continuity dynasty interested in ownership, inheritance, and controlled resurrection.
- **The Unnumbered:** people denied valid continuity records or refusing systems that rank one instance above another.
- **The Pale Administrator:** a vast governing intelligence whose procedures may protect civilization or permanently imprison it.
- **Orison Recovery Directorate:** an organization seeking lost histories, unrealized timelines, and strategic identity archives.
- **The Quiet Embassy:** representatives of a civilization or species that may communicate through absence, suppression, or carefully managed silence.
- **Blackglass:** a local covert organization resembling Blacklight Intelligence closely enough to be either coincidence, imitation, legacy, or causal contamination.

# Part X — Rebuilding Blacklight

The team begins as marooned operatives without institutional support.

To survive, they will need to build the functions Blacklight once supplied:

- secure shelter;
- equipment and fabrication;
- communications;
- legal or noospheric identity;
- intelligence collection;
- allies and contacts;
- finance or exchange capacity;
- transport;
- medical support;
- territorial defense;
- mission selection;
- political legitimacy.

The campaign progression may follow five broad stages.

## Survival Cell

The team secures the vessel, recovers equipment, stabilizes Charles, and discovers the immediate environment.

## Deniable Operators

The team takes work, makes contacts, acquires identities, and learns the political rules of the local universe.

## Independent Agency

The team develops reliable assets, fabrication, intelligence, transport, and a recognizable operational reputation.

## Mirror Blacklight

The team creates an organization capable of providing the same kind of support Blacklight once provided, but under principles chosen locally.

## Causal Power

The team becomes capable of influencing histories, identities, governments, civilizations, and the relationship between universes.

The long-term question is not simply whether Charles can bring them home.

It is whether the organization they build should ever again belong to him.

# Part XI — Basic d10 Foundation

## Tests

Build a dice pool from:

> **Attribute + Skill + modifiers**

Roll that many d10s.

- Results of **6–9** generate one success.
- A result of **10** generates one success and may activate a specialization, equipment quality, or power rider.
- A failed roll containing one or more **1s** creates a Complication.

Difficulty normally ranges from one to five successes.

## Attributes

### Physical

- Force
- Finesse
- Resilience

### Social

- Presence
- Guile
- Composure

### Mental

- Reason
- Awareness
- Resolve

## Capability Sources

A rules-relevant capability must come from at least one defined source:

- Attribute;
- Skill;
- specialization;
- Operational Frame;
- Archetype;
- innate ability;
- ranked power;
- equipment;
- condition;
- explicit narrative permission.

## Archetypes

The six Basic Archetypes are:

- Human Investigator;
- Vampire;
- Shapechanger;
- Eldritch Binder;
- Harmonic Mutant;
- Technomancer.

Each Archetype contains:

- one Resource;
- one Pressure;
- one Key Attribute;
- one weakness;
- innate abilities;
- six power families;
- five ranked powers in each family.

Archetype descriptions explain present capabilities, costs, limits, and advancement.

## Operational Frames

Operational Frames describe training and current function rather than supernatural nature.

Examples include:

- Infiltrator;
- Breacher;
- Systems Intrusion Specialist;
- Field Medic and Fabricator;
- Engineer and Salvager;
- Investigator and Analyst;
- Envoy and Fixer;
- Pilot and Navigator;
- Tactical Coordinator;
- Noospheric Adept;
- Xenologist;
- Continuity Warden.

Any Archetype may use any Operational Frame when the character’s Skills support it.

# Game Moderator Directive

Do not use printed origin as an excuse to grant surprise abilities.

Do not use alien feedstock as an excuse to impose surprise weaknesses.

Do not reveal hidden Ar’nock organs because the characters do not possess generic Ar’nock anatomy.

Do not make the printer misunderstand a valid construction job.

Present consequences through explicit capabilities, costs, conditions, hazards, choices, and relationships.

The characters are here.

They are operational.

Their capabilities are on their sheets.

The campaign begins with what they do next.
