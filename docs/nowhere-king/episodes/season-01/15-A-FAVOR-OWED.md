# Season One, Episode 15 — A Favor Owed

**Status:** Committed episode prose  
**Season:** One — The Return to Edselsto  
**Episode:** 15 of 32  
**Reveal ceiling:** Seven candles remain lit. The sealed stair remains closed behind its temporary barrier and is not explored further. No containment truth, prison truth, castle intelligence, buried-entity terminology, or supernatural mechanism is confirmed.  
**Primary dramatic purpose:** Force Luceran to obtain a necessary service by granting a real, bounded political concession. Make personal access and state bargaining overlap without making affection itself corrupt, and establish a debt that survives the episode.

---

Lord Edmund Moncler wanted Pellin Joss.

This was inconvenient because Pellin Joss did not belong to the king.

Luceran discovered both facts before breakfast.

Avarren put the Moncler letter beside his plate.

"They have amended their request."

Luceran buttered bread.

"More money?"

"A person."

"That is usually more expensive."

Avarren did not smile.

Luceran read.

Moncler's claims factor had assembled the documents demanded after the grain inquiry: wage rolls, interrupted contracts, tool inventories, carriage charges, unfinished stonework, timber already cut for the mansion Luceran had stripped of workers, and statements from craftsmen whose schedules had been broken when the Crown took precedence.

At the bottom was the request.

Pellin Joss, whose reconstruction of the grain accounts had become inconveniently famous, should review the claim.

"No," Luceran said.

Avarren waited.

"That was quick."

"Pellin works stores."

"Yes."

"He is supervising Olan's reconstruction."

"Yes."

"Tomas is already gone to the river because of us."

"Also yes."

Luceran pushed the letter away.

"Then no."

Avarren picked it up.

"What shall I tell Moncler?"

"That Pellin is occupied."

"They know."

"Then why ask?"

"Because they believe the standard you praised is only useful if the person applying it is not one of the people who caused their loss."

Luceran stopped eating.

"Did you cause their loss?"

"In the legal sense?"

"Avarren."

"No. In the administrative sense, Crown officers did. In the political sense, you did."

Luceran looked at the bread.

"I needed the workers."

"Yes."

"I paid them."

"Yes."

"Moncler still had a half-built mansion."

"Yes."

Luceran sighed.

"You enjoy this."

"Not before breakfast."

---

Pellin refused before Luceran finished asking.

They stood in the lower stores among barrels of beans, lamp oil, salt fish, and three clerks who had suddenly become fascinated by inventory marks.

"No."

Luceran blinked.

"You could let me finish."

"You want me to review Moncler."

"How do you know?"

"Their factor asked me yesterday."

Luceran stared.

"Directly?"

"Yes."

"And you said no."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Pellin pointed at the desk behind him.

Olan Vey sat there surrounded by reconstructed grain orders.

Olan raised one hand without looking up.

"Still being punished, Majesty."

Pellin lowered his voice.

"Tomas is at the river. Havel is covering two issue books because the kitchens keep borrowing my people. Olan cannot certify his own reconstruction. If I leave for Moncler, somebody else checks this."

"Who?"

"Exactly."

Luceran looked around.

"Can I assign someone?"

"Of course."

"Good."

"Do you want someone competent or someone available?"

Luceran frowned.

Pellin shrugged.

"Those are different lists."

"How long would Moncler take?"

"If their papers are good? Two days. If their papers are bad? Longer."

"And if I order you?"

Pellin looked at him.

The clerks became even more interested in barrels.

"Then I go."

"But?"

"But you asked me to tell you when a number was true even if the true number was inconvenient."

"This is not a number."

"It is staffing."

Luceran rubbed his forehead.

"Fine. What makes it possible?"

Pellin thought.

"Give me someone who can read accounts and is not frightened of telling Olan he is wrong."

Olan said, "I would also prefer that."

"Nobody asked you."

"I know."

Pellin continued.

"And Moncler does not get me as their man. I review for the Crown. My notes remain Crown records. Their factor may answer questions. He does not direct the inquiry."

"Reasonable."

"And I return when the claim is done."

"Obviously."

Pellin gave him a look.

Luceran corrected himself.

"In writing."

Pellin nodded.

Episode thirteen had apparently survived.

---

The available people were not competent.

The competent people were not available.

By noon this had become the governing principle of Edselsto.

Selise Varo could read accounts but belonged to the archive and had already embarrassed Ysabet by volunteering archive capacity in front of the king.

Avarren had clerks, but Moncler had specifically objected to a review performed entirely by offices involved in the original seizure.

Clavard had people who understood contracts and none who knew stores well enough to catch a false physical receipt.

Miren knew household books and refused the job before anyone offered it.

"Why are you looking at me?"

Luceran said, "I wasn't."

"You were."

"You can read accounts."

"So can half the court."

"Not well."

"That sounds like a problem you should solve."

They were walking through the south gallery, where workmen had removed three cracked panes and replaced them with oiled cloth. Wind pressed the cloth inward with every gust.

Luceran said, "Pellin needs someone."

"Pellin needs three someones."

"Yes."

"And you need Pellin because Moncler wants proof that your new fairness applies to them."

"Yes."

Miren glanced at him.

"So what are you buying?"

"I am not buying anything."

"Then you are not solving it."

Luceran stopped.

Miren stopped too.

"You sound like Iven."

"Iven was right about one thing. You need grain."

"I hated that."

"You can hate true things."

A carpenter carrying a sash frame waited behind them.

Miren noticed first and pulled Luceran against the wall.

The carpenter passed.

"Majesty. My lady."

They waited until he was gone.

Luceran said, "Kaelmont offered engineering people."

"For the stair."

"For buried rock and retaining walls."

"Ardel offered them."

"House Kaelmont offered them through Ardel."

Miren's face changed by very little.

Luceran saw it because he was learning to.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That was something."

"It was me deciding whether I wanted to say something."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

She folded her arms.

"If Ardel gives you engineers because she likes you, and you give Kaelmont something because you like her, nobody will believe those are separate facts just because you insist they are."

Luceran felt heat climb his neck.

"I have not—"

"I know."

"You know what?"

"I know you have not promised her anything privately. I know you have not slept with her. I know she is attracted to you. I know you are not blind."

Luceran looked down the gallery.

"This is a very public corridor."

"Then don't shout."

"I'm not shouting."

"Good."

He lowered his voice anyway.

"Are you jealous?"

Miren considered the question rather than rescuing him from it.

"Yes."

That answer landed harder than accusation.

"Oh."

"Not enough to pretend Kaelmont's roads are bad."

Luceran laughed despite himself.

Miren did not.

"That is the part I need you to understand. I can be jealous and still tell you their engineers are useful. Ardel can want you and still make a good bargain for her house. You can want someone and still owe the kingdom a clean contract. None of those truths cancel the others."

Luceran looked at her.

"Do you think I want her?"

"I think you enjoy being wanted."

That was worse.

"Cruel."

"Accurate."

He thought of Nella saying the same thing to Jory.

Edselsto was becoming repetitive in its insults.

Miren stepped closer.

"I am not asking you to dislike her."

"What are you asking?"

"If you make a bargain with her, make one you can read aloud with me standing beside you."

Luceran said nothing.

Miren touched his hand.

"That is my price for not making this simpler than it is."

"Your price?"

"Personal price. Not state price. Learn the distinction."

Then she kissed his cheek and walked away.

Luceran remained in the gallery with the unpleasant suspicion that everyone around him had become better at politics while he was busy becoming king.

---

Ardel Kaelmont arrived carrying three maps and no engineer.

"You asked for Master Renn."

"Yes."

"No."

Luceran stared at her.

"This is becoming fashionable."

Ardel smiled.

"What?"

"Telling me no before I finish."

"Would you prefer I waste time?"

"Occasionally."

She spread the maps across a table in the small west audience room.

Avarren attended.

Clavard attended.

Mara Henn attended because one of the maps crossed village land.

Tessa Rane attended because Mara had told her it did.

That had not been Luceran's plan.

He was learning that plans involving land acquired people.

Ardel tapped the first map.

"Master Renn is at White Steps bridge. The north abutment moved after the last rain. If I pull him, forty carts a day cross a bridge his deputy says is safe and he says is not yet proven safe."

Luceran said, "Keep him there."

Ardel's eyes flicked up.

"Good."

She tapped the second map.

"I can send Lysa Quill and two surveyors. Quill apprenticed under Renn. Retaining walls, drainage cuts, quarry faces. She is not a deep-mine engineer."

Mara said, "I don't need a mine engineer."

Ardel looked at her.

"You might if your stair goes where everyone hopes it goes."

Mara said, "I don't hope it goes anywhere."

"That makes one of you."

Luceran ignored that.

"What do you want?"

Ardel smiled again.

"There he is."

"Ardel."

"Five years. Nonexclusive carriage privilege on the Kaelmont north road for Crown works moving dressed stone, lime, and structural timber into Edselsto."

Clavard said, "No."

Ardel turned.

"I wasn't asking you."

"You were if you wanted a valid contract."

"Four years."

"Still no."

Luceran said, "Why?"

Clavard pointed to the map.

"Because 'Crown works' will mean every chair, barrel, window, paving stone, and mistress's wardrobe within a year if we do not define it."

Ardel looked wounded.

"I would never."

Tessa laughed.

Everyone looked at her.

"Sorry. Continue."

Ardel tapped the table.

"Dressed stone, lime, structural timber, iron brackets, slate."

Mara said, "No water works."

Ardel looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because the moment you tie Crown carriage privilege to drains, culverts, or spring stone, every village water dispute becomes a Kaelmont contract dispute."

"That seems dramatic."

"You have not met Bren Vess when someone says spring."

Tessa said, "She has a point."

Ardel considered.

"Fine. No water works unless separately contracted."

Luceran looked at Avarren.

"You are quiet."

"I am waiting for the favor."

Ardel's smile faded slightly.

"The favor is speed. Quill can leave tomorrow."

"That is service. Not favor."

She looked at him.

Avarren continued.

"House Kaelmont wants a concession. The Crown wants engineering labor. That is a bargain. If there is a favor, it is elsewhere."

Luceran suddenly understood why Ardel had brought no engineer.

"What else?"

She met his eyes.

"A seat."

"Where?"

"At the next three restricted works conferences concerning roads, retaining walls, quarry supply, or buried structural hazards affecting the west side. Observer only where Kaelmont has no contract. Speaking right where we do."

Clavard frowned.

"Access."

"Expertise."

"Access."

"Both."

There it was.

Not a bedroom.

Not a secret promise.

A chair near decisions.

Luceran thought of rooms becoming power.

Ardel watched him think.

That was part of the bargain too.

"Why three?" he asked.

"Because one can be ceremonial. Two can be accident. Three becomes habit."

Avarren almost smiled.

"Thank you for saying the quiet part aloud."

"I assumed you had already written it down."

He had.

---

Luceran did not agree.

Not then.

That mattered.

He sent everyone away except Mara and Tessa.

Ardel paused at the door.

"You are keeping the village and dismissing me."

"Yes."

"Should I be offended?"

"Probably."

She laughed and left.

Mara looked at the maps.

"Quill is good."

Luceran looked at her.

"You know her?"

"By work. She set the retaining wall above South Fork after the slide."

Tessa nodded.

"Still standing."

"Kaelmont wall?"

"Kaelmont engineer. Village labor. Different thing."

Luceran leaned over the map.

"Do we need her?"

Mara took time.

"For the stair? Not yet. Derrin can tell us whether the opening stands. Nella can tell us about hardware. Jory can tell us about timber."

"Then no."

"For the west retaining cuts and the drainage load above them before winter?"

She tapped three marks.

"Yes."

"Why didn't you ask?"

"Because Kaelmont offered before I had to."

That answer bothered him.

"Would you have?"

"Eventually. Or Quorin. Or Root and Flint. Depends what failed first."

"What fails first?"

Mara looked at him.

"If I knew, I would fix that one."

Tessa pointed at the lower track.

"And if Kaelmont gets carriage privilege, village carts?"

"Not excluded," Luceran said.

"Not excluded is not protected."

He looked at her.

She held his gaze.

"What do you want?"

Tessa's expression changed.

"You ask that more now."

"People keep wanting things."

"Yes."

She tapped the map.

"No exclusive road use. No priority that displaces food, firewood, or village water repair. Local carriers can bid on Crown loads. Existing private rights stay private."

"That is four things."

"You asked what I wanted, not how many."

Luceran laughed.

"Put it in writing."

Tessa glanced at Mara.

Mara said, "Growth."

Luceran pointed at the door.

"Both of you out."

---

The person Pellin needed came from somewhere Luceran had not looked.

Olan found her.

Her name was Nera Vos, and she kept the tool issue for Royal Works.

Luceran knew her face and not her name.

That embarrassed him more than he expected.

Pellin brought her to the stores office after supper.

"She reads quantities," he said.

Nera looked at him.

"I read."

"You know what I mean."

"Then say what you mean."

Pellin looked at Luceran.

"She will do."

Nera had spent the move to Edselsto recording chisels, mallets, saws, wedges, nails, braces, rope, lamp oil, and the steady disappearance of all of them into a construction project large enough to eat iron.

She knew which foremen returned tools clean, which crews returned half, and which noble factors treated Crown property as though a crest made borrowing invisible.

"Can you check grain orders?" Luceran asked.

"No."

Pellin said, "Yes."

Nera turned on him.

"I can check whether quantities move. I don't know grain contracts."

"You don't need to. Olan does."

Olan looked up from his desk.

"Unfortunately."

Pellin continued.

"You check that his reconstructed physical movement agrees with issues, receipts, and transfers. He explains the commercial terms. If you don't understand one, you stop."

Nera looked at Olan.

"Will you lie to me?"

Olan went very still.

"Not deliberately."

"Bad answer."

"It is the true one."

She considered him.

"Better."

Luceran said, "What do you want for two days?"

Nera looked surprised.

"Pay."

"You already receive pay."

"For tools."

Pellin said, "Temporary duty rate."

Nera said, "And somebody covers my issue desk."

"Who?"

"My brother."

Luceran blinked.

"Does he work for us?"

"He unloads stone. He can count hammers."

Pellin said, "Can he refuse a foreman?"

Nera smiled.

"He refuses me."

"Good enough."

Luceran realized that an hour of royal discussion had failed to solve what Olan had solved by knowing who actually worked beside him.

That lesson also stayed.

---

The bargain with Kaelmont took until midnight.

It was not five years.

It was three.

It was not a general Crown carriage privilege.

It covered dressed structural stone, lime, slate, structural timber, and ironwork specifically ordered for Edselsto royal works.

It was nonexclusive.

Village carriers and other qualified carriers could bid.

Food, fodder, fuel, water works, household goods, military stores, and private noble freight were excluded unless separately contracted.

No Kaelmont priority could displace emergency food, firewood, medical carriage, or village water repair.

Private land and water rights were untouched.

Rates would be posted by route and season rather than negotiated load by load in corridors.

Lysa Quill and two surveyors would arrive under a twenty-day Crown engagement paid at the ordinary specialist rate, not as unpaid gifts.

Kaelmont received two observer seats, not one house seat, at the next three works conferences when the agenda materially concerned roads, retaining walls, quarry supply, or west-side buried structural hazards. The seats expired automatically. They carried no vote, command, key, archive access, or right to enter the sealed stair.

If Kaelmont wanted them renewed, Kaelmont would ask again.

Ardel read the draft.

"You have murdered it."

Clavard said, "Then why are you smiling?"

"Because the corpse is profitable."

Avarren said, "Put that in the official copy."

"I will not."

"Coward."

Luceran watched them.

Then he looked at Ardel.

"One more condition."

"You have already found seventeen."

"The observer seats are named."

Her smile disappeared.

"To whom?"

"You and whoever you designate for technical work."

"Why me?"

"Because you asked for access."

"House Kaelmont asked."

"Through you."

Ardel studied him.

"And if I cannot attend?"

"You may designate both seats for that meeting, in writing. No permanent substitute."

Avarren looked at Luceran.

Luceran knew what he was thinking.

Personal access.

State access.

Entangled.

Not hidden.

Luceran said, "I want the person who negotiated the privilege answerable for how the privilege is used."

Ardel's expression softened.

"That is almost flattering."

"It is mostly administrative."

"Cruel."

"Accurate."

She laughed.

"Agreed."

They signed.

Mara witnessed the engineering terms.

Tessa witnessed the village protections.

Clavard witnessed the commercial terms.

Avarren sealed the Crown copy.

Ardel sealed Kaelmont's.

Nobody kissed anyone.

That also mattered.

---

Pellin left for Moncler's temporary claims office at dawn.

Nera Vos sat in his chair.

She hated it.

"Too high."

Olan said, "There is another."

"This one has the light."

"Then keep it."

She opened the first reconstructed grain order.

"Explain this."

Olan leaned over.

"Commercial transfer at depot."

"That is not movement."

"No."

"Then why is it in the movement column?"

Olan stared.

Pellin, halfway through the door, stopped.

Luceran saw his face.

"Go," Luceran said.

Pellin hesitated.

"She found one."

"Yes."

"Already."

"Yes."

Nera looked up.

"Are you leaving or haunting the doorway?"

Pellin left.

Luceran followed him into the corridor.

"You trust her?"

"No."

Luceran frowned.

Pellin adjusted the satchel under his arm.

"I trust her to ask when she does not know. That is enough for two days."

Luceran thought about that all the way upstairs.

---

Miren was waiting in his rooms that evening.

She had removed her shoes and stolen his better chair.

"Well?"

Luceran handed her the Kaelmont copy.

She read it.

All of it.

He waited.

"Three meetings."

"Yes."

"Named access."

"Yes."

"No stair key."

"No."

"No archive access."

"No."

"No water works."

"Unless separately contracted."

"You paid the engineers."

"Yes."

Miren reached the signatures.

"Tessa witnessed it."

"She threatened me until I protected firewood."

"Good."

"Mara witnessed it."

"Also good."

"Avarren wrote most of it."

"Less romantic."

Luceran sat on the edge of the table.

"Is this the part where you tell me you are not jealous anymore?"

Miren looked up.

"No."

"Oh."

"That was not the bargain."

He smiled.

"No."

She folded the copy.

"This I can stand beside."

Something in his chest loosened.

He had not realized how much he wanted her to say it.

Miren noticed that too.

She stood and came between his knees.

"Do not make that face."

"What face?"

"The one where you think I have absolved you."

"I was not."

"You were close."

"Cruel."

"Accurate."

He put his hands at her waist.

"Everyone says that now."

"Maybe everyone is right."

She kissed him.

This one was not brief.

When they separated, Luceran rested his forehead against hers.

"Does this have a political price?"

Miren's hands settled behind his neck.

"Everything around you has political consequence. That is not the same thing as price."

He was quiet.

She continued.

"If I ask you for a bridge tomorrow, you still have to ask who owns the riverbank."

"What if it is a very small bridge?"

"Especially then. Small things are how people stop checking."

Pellin would have approved.

Luceran decided not to say so.

---

Moncler's claim did not resolve in one day.

That was useful.

Pellin returned after dark with dust on his coat and three disputed tool inventories.

"Are they lying?" Luceran asked.

"Somebody is wrong."

"Moncler?"

"Maybe."

"Royal Works?"

"Maybe."

"Both?"

"Probably."

Luceran sighed.

"How long?"

"Tomorrow."

"And then?"

"Then you will owe Moncler money."

"How much?"

"Less than they asked. More than Avarren hoped."

Avarren, beside the fire, said, "I am here."

Pellin looked at him.

"I know."

Luceran almost laughed.

"Will they accept it?"

"If they want the standard more than the number."

"Do they?"

Pellin shrugged.

"Ask Moncler."

That was tomorrow's problem.

Tonight's problem was that Kaelmont had a remembered claim.

Three years of defined carriage opportunity.

Three works conferences of bounded access.

Twenty days in which Lysa Quill would inspect retaining walls and drainage cuts alongside people who did not work for Kaelmont.

It was not corruption.

It was not charity.

It was not friendship.

Ardel's attraction had not vanished because the contract was clean.

Miren's jealousy had not vanished because she approved the terms.

Tessa's suspicion had not vanished because she witnessed the protections.

Kaelmont's profit had not vanished because its engineers were genuinely useful.

The bargain had not made any of those things simpler.

It had merely made them legible.

Luceran was beginning to understand that this might be the best government could do.

---

The next morning, Lysa Quill rode into Edselsto with two surveyors, four measuring rods, a coil of line, three plumb bobs, a leather case of levels, and the expression of a woman already disappointed by the mountain.

Mara met her at the lower road.

"You came quickly."

"Lady Ardel said the king paid for twenty days and would become curious if I wasted one."

"He will."

"Where is the buried stair?"

Mara pointed uphill.

"Locked."

Quill looked at her.

"Then why am I here?"

Mara pointed west.

"Because that retaining cut is wet, the lower road is carrying more carts than it was built for, and winter does not care about buried stairs."

Quill followed her finger.

Then smiled.

"Good."

They went to work.

Above them, the sealed stair remained sealed.

At the river depot, Tomas Reed created the first independent Crown receiving copy and complained in writing that the roof leaked.

In stores, Nera Vos found two more places where commercial ownership and physical movement had been recorded as though they were the same thing.

Olan corrected them under supervision.

At Moncler's office, Pellin Joss made a noble claims factor prove the difference between a missing chisel and a chisel the Crown had actually taken.

At the Vess pasture, the observation watch again saw nothing worth reporting.

Jeron Bellisar still could not ride.

The false horn remained evidence without a sponsor.

The old family records remained incomplete.

The west stair remained unexplained.

And in the nave, seven candles burned.

Luceran had obtained what he needed.

So had Kaelmont.

That was what made it a favor worth remembering.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 15 converts existing pressures into a bounded bargain rather than inventing a new emergency.

### Durable changes

- Moncler requests Pellin Joss as a Crown reviewer under the evidentiary standard established in Episode 13. Pellin agrees only after replacement capacity is found and only as a Crown reviewer, not a Moncler agent.
- Olan Vey identifies Nera Vos, Royal Works tool-issue keeper, as a competent temporary physical-record checker. Nera receives temporary-duty pay; her brother covers her tool desk. This promotes another low-status worker through established occupational competence rather than invented expertise.
- Pellin begins the Moncler compensation review. The claim is not instantly resolved; his preliminary finding is that the Crown owes real compensation, less than Moncler claimed and more than Crown officers hoped.
- House Kaelmont receives a three-year, nonexclusive carriage opportunity covering defined Edselsto structural materials. Food, fodder, fuel, water works, household goods, military stores, and private noble freight are excluded absent separate contract.
- Village carriers retain bidding rights. Emergency food, firewood, medical carriage, and village water repair cannot be displaced by Kaelmont priority. Private land and water rights remain untouched.
- Kaelmont supplies Lysa Quill and two surveyors under a paid twenty-day Crown engagement. They are tasked first with west retaining cuts, drainage load, and road/structural conditions, not with unauthorized stair exploration.
- Kaelmont gains two bounded observer seats for the next three relevant works conferences. The access expires automatically and grants no vote, command, key, archive access, or stair-entry right.
- Ardel Kaelmont is personally named as one observer or must designate substitutes in writing, making her accountable for the access she negotiated rather than hiding personal access inside a house privilege.
- Miren explicitly acknowledges jealousy regarding Ardel while refusing to convert jealousy into bad state advice. She establishes a personal boundary: bargains involving intimate or attracted parties must be clean enough to be stated openly. Luceran accepts that intimacy has political consequence without treating affection as a purchasable state favor.
- Mara Henn and Tessa Rane materially constrain the Kaelmont bargain through drainage, village-carriage, emergency-supply, land, and water protections. Their interests remain distinct from Crown and Kaelmont interests.
- Lysa Quill arrives and begins mundane structural work. The sealed stair remains locked and unexplored.
- Living hunt tester and sponsor remain unidentified; Bellisar injury, Vess rights, Royal Companion review, Moncler compensation, Damaris depot reform, family-history mysteries, and stair authorization remain active obligations.
- Seven of seven candles remain lit. No confirmed supernatural behavior occurs.
