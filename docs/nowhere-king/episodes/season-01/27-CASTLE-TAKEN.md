# Season One, Episode 27 — Castle Taken

By dawn, everyone in Edselsto knew there were two castles.

There was the castle on the maps: gates, stairs, halls, offices, kitchens, chapel, archive, royal apartments.

Then there was the castle people could actually cross.

Bram Tolland's Edselsto was six defended thresholds joined by runners who might or might not arrive. Sir Aven Marr's Edselsto was a chain of rooms he could claim in the name of an emergency council, provided the men behind him obeyed when he said council. Ardel Kaelmont's Edselsto ended at the stone carts across the village road. Calienne Orsanne's was the chapel floor, where blood made political vocabulary seem childish.

The conspirators had more people.

The loyalists had more locked doors.

Neither advantage was permanent.

---

Bram reached the crown room before the first attack on its outer stair.

It was not a treasury. The ceremonial pieces were stored in a long upper wardrobe chamber because Edselsto had been built by people who assumed crowns, mourning mantles, swords, seals and robes belonged near the bodies that wore them.

The crown sat inside an iron-bound oak coffer.

Edrik looked at it.

"We can move it."

"Where?"

"The archive."

"Then they attack the archive."

"Family crypt."

"Then we tell them where the family crypt is."

Edrik swore softly.

Bram understood the problem. A crown was easier to defend than a rumor about a crown. Move it through a castle full of uncertain corridors and every servant who saw the escort became another possible map.

"Keep it here."

"Four of us?"

"Six."

"We have four."

"Then we have four."

Below them, someone shouted the new challenge.

"Boot."

A loyal guard answered automatically.

"Tack."

Then the shooting started.

Bram's face changed.

"They heard it."

"How?"

"Doesn't matter anymore."

He opened the door and shouted down the stair.

"No challenges. Faces only. If you don't know them, the door stays shut."

The security system had lasted less than a night.

That did not mean it had failed.

It meant the enemy was learning too.

---

Sir Aven Marr learned the same lesson from the other side.

A man named Cale Rusk presented himself at the old council antechamber with twenty-three armed retainers and a letter authorizing him to reinforce the restoration of lawful government.

Aven read it twice.

"Whose men?"

"Concerned houses."

"Which houses?"

"You know how these matters work."

"I know exactly how they work. Which houses?"

Rusk smiled as if Aven were being ceremonial.

"Several."

Aven folded the letter.

"No."

Rusk's smile remained.

"We already hold the lower gate."

"We hold the lower gate."

"Do you?"

That was when Aven understood that the coup had acquired a second command structure.

He had believed the emergency council would seize Edselsto, secure the Crown regalia, summon senior peers, and compel Luceran to return to constitutional negotiation.

Rusk wanted the same rooms.

He did not want the same government.

Aven could not now restore the old morning by announcing he had discovered unpleasant colleagues.

He stationed six Companions across the council chamber doors and told Rusk that no armed body entered the inner castle without his authority.

Rusk bowed.

Then he went around him.

---

At the upper service junction, Anja Pell counted bolts.

Nine crossbow bolts.

Five guards who could still stand.

Mikel Orr lay behind a flour chest with his shoulder bandaged and his face grey.

"You are not dying," Anja told him.

"I hadn't planned to."

"Good. Plans are unreliable today."

A kitchen boy crawled through from the east passage.

"Receiving's gone."

"We knew."

"They've got the pantry stair."

That mattered.

The pantry stair did not lead to the crown.

It led to the corridor behind Anja.

She looked at the five guards.

"Two stay. Three with me."

Mikel tried to rise.

"No."

"My post."

"Your blood is already doing enough work."

She took the junction key ring from him.

Mikel watched her go with the particular fury of a competent person forced to become cargo.

---

Pera Crewe spent the night under roots.

The kitchen drain descended below the retaining wall, crossed beneath a collapsed garden terrace and emptied into a narrow runnel that village children had used for years because adults considered it disgusting.

Pera considered adults insufficiently committed to useful architecture.

She crawled until her knees bled, climbed through wet pine roots, and reached the lower wood before moonset.

Then she discovered that carrying a royal warning did not make her know where the king was.

She knew the Northbridge road.

She knew Black Pine scouts used ridge marks cut into bark.

She knew her uncle Olan would murder anyone who hurt her, provided he survived long enough to learn about it.

That was the total of her strategic education.

At dawn she found a Black Pine trail mark.

By full light she found a rider.

The rider leveled a bow at her.

Pera raised both hands.

"Boot."

The rider's expression hardened.

"Tack," Pera said quickly. "I know. It's already old."

She pulled the leather from her sleeve.

The scout read it.

"Who gave you this?"

"Bram Tolland."

"When?"

"Before supper."

"Where is the king?"

"If I knew that, I wouldn't be asking you."

The scout laughed once, without humor.

"Fair."

He put her on his spare horse.

The warning had escaped Edselsto.

Whether it would arrive in time was now somebody else's problem.

---

At the village road, Ardel's stone carts stopped three separate groups.

The first was a household wagon carrying frightened servants downhill. She let it through after every person climbed out and Olan inspected beneath the boards.

The second carried six men claiming to be Kaelmont retainers.

Ardel knew four of them.

She did not know two.

"Names."

They gave them.

One was wrong.

Not a false name. A real Kaelmont name belonging to a man Ardel had seen three days earlier with his wrist broken under a timber cradle.

She pointed.

"Take him."

The other unknown man drew a knife.

Olan hit him with a mallet before the knife cleared his sleeve.

The four real retainers looked horrified.

"We were told they were from the upper estate."

"By whom?"

"A courier with your seal."

Ardel's seal was still on her hand.

She showed it to them.

"Congratulations. You have learned something."

The third group did not pretend.

Eight mounted men came down the road at speed.

Ardel's workers tipped the lead stone cart.

Half-dressed blocks rolled across the road.

The horses stopped because horses had more sense than politics.

The riders withdrew uphill.

Ardel did not pursue.

Her objective was not victory.

It was keeping the village from becoming a second gate.

---

The crown stair fell shortly after noon.

Not because Bram forgot a door.

Because the pantry stair put Rusk's men behind the upper service junction, forcing Anja to choose between being surrounded and abandoning it. She abandoned it.

That released enough bodies to pressure the family corridor.

Bram pulled two guards from the crown approach to keep the corridor open long enough for children, two elderly servants and three wounded people to cross toward Calienne's chapel.

Those two guards were the difference.

Rusk's men reached the crown stair while Bram had four defenders instead of six.

The first assault failed.

The second failed.

The third used a dining table as a shield and smoke from damp straw pushed up the stairwell.

Edrik coughed until he vomited.

Bram took a cut along his thigh.

Guardsman Lio Venn died with his hand still wrapped around the inside latch.

They withdrew because staying would have converted defense into four corpses and a crown taken ten minutes later.

Bram made the decision.

"Out."

Edrik stared at him.

"The crown—"

"Out."

They went through the robing room, broke a narrow connecting door into an unfinished gallery, and barred it behind them.

Rusk's men opened the coffer.

The crown was still there.

No thunder answered.

No candle went dark.

No law changed.

But downstairs, men began shouting that the Crown had been secured.

The phrase moved faster than the object.

---

Aven arrived after the coffer was opened.

Rusk held the crown in both hands.

Aven stopped.

"You were ordered to wait."

"By you."

"Yes."

Rusk looked around the room at the dead guard, the smoke, the overturned coffer.

"You wanted the castle taken."

"I wanted the regalia secured under council authority."

"Then be pleased."

"Put it down."

Rusk did.

That frightened Aven more than refusal would have.

Rusk was willing to obey when obedience cost nothing.

"Who authorized the assault?"

"The situation."

"That is not a person."

"It is today."

Aven ordered two Companions to take custody of the crown.

Rusk allowed it.

The crown moved to the old council chamber under Aven's seal.

By evening three different proclamations would claim that this meant three different things.

None would be entirely true.

---

Mera Doss heard the proclamation from under guard.

"Royal regalia secured. Emergency council restoring lawful administration. All household staff remain at assigned stations."

She laughed.

The guard looked at her.

"What?"

"Assigned stations."

Her station was a locked room.

Her daughter was somewhere below the mountain, possibly already holding money that could now be evidence of treason.

"Did Parel come through here?"

"No."

"Did anyone ask for me?"

"No."

That hurt more than she expected.

She had imagined herself compromised.

She had not imagined herself irrelevant.

"Ask Nera Vos to check the washhouse wage book."

"Why?"

"Parel knew my daughter's apprenticeship. He knew the vat. He knew when I stopped guard washing. Somebody didn't learn all that from one conversation."

The guard hesitated.

"Please."

This time, Mera did not ask to be forgiven.

She asked to be useful.

---

Calienne's chapel acquired a rule.

No arrest while a healer's hands were inside a wound.

She made the rule after two loyal guards arrived to take a wounded coup supporter and one of Aven's Companions arrived to take a wounded loyal messenger.

"Both of you can wait," she said.

The Companion protested first.

"He carried military information."

"So did the other one."

The loyal guard looked offended.

Calienne pointed to opposite walls.

"You. There. You. There. If either of you draws steel, I will have Brother Talen ring the death bell until everyone in this castle believes someone important has died."

Brother Talen, who had not been consulted, nodded gravely.

Both sides sat.

The wounded men lived through the hour.

The chapel remained neutral not because neutrality was sacred, but because enough armed people wanted somewhere their wounded could survive.

That was politics too.

---

Ysabet Fenn heard axes against the archive door at dusk.

Selise stood beside her with three public registers and a kitchen cleaver.

"You cannot possibly intend to use that."

"I don't."

"Good."

"I intend to look as though I might."

The barred door shook.

Ysabet had spent years thinking archives survived because scholars valued them.

Now she understood that archives survived because someone had paid for oak.

There was a second route out through the old catalog passage, but opening it would expose the family papers to anyone watching the retreat.

She made the same choice Bram had made upstairs.

People before objects.

"Take the accession ledger."

"The family papers?"

"Leave them."

Selise looked at her.

Ysabet hated the answer.

"Leave them."

They evacuated six people through the catalog passage, carrying only the accession ledger, the current seal register, and the public registers Selise already held.

When the archive door finally broke, the papers remained.

So did the record of what they were.

Possession was not provenance.

Ysabet intended to make that distinction hurt later.

---

The inner lower grille fell after dark.

Its defenders did not surrender to Rusk.

They surrendered to Sir Aven Marr.

That distinction mattered to them.

Aven approached without helmet, sword sheathed, two Companions behind him.

Bram's remaining guard at the grille had eleven people, four wounded, and no route to reinforcement.

Aven called through the bars.

"Yield the grille to me. I give my word the wounded go to the chapel, household staff are not harmed, and no prisoner is executed without constituted judgment."

"Constituted by whom?" Bram asked from the darkness behind the defenders.

"That is precisely the question we are trying to force."

"With hidden men in grain wagons."

Aven's face tightened.

"Yes."

Bram stepped into the torchlight, limping.

"You still think you're in charge."

Aven said nothing.

That was answer enough.

Bram looked at his people.

He could hold another quarter hour.

Perhaps half.

Then Rusk would come from behind.

"Terms in writing."

Aven produced them.

Bram made him add the names of every defender present.

He made two copies.

One went with Calienne.

One went inside Edrik's boot.

Then Bram surrendered the grille.

He did not surrender the family corridor.

Not yet.

---

The family corridor lasted another hour.

It ended because the people inside it needed somewhere to go.

Bram and Edrik had preserved a narrow route toward the old linen galleries. Anja reached them with three guards and Mikel carried between two kitchen workers.

Behind them came servants, a page, one terrified clerk, and a woman carrying a sleeping child who had somehow slept through an attempted coup.

Bram looked at the corridor.

Months of security work had taught him how to hold it.

Nothing in that work could manufacture soldiers.

"Break the lamp."

Edrik understood.

They smashed the corridor lamp, withdrew in darkness, and jammed two doors behind them.

Aven's men occupied the empty position minutes later.

Rusk's men occupied the room behind Aven's men.

By midnight, every mapped public route from the lower gate to the great hall and royal apartments was under coup control.

The loyalists no longer held a coherent defensive position inside Edselsto.

That was the moment the castle was taken.

Not when the crown changed hands.

Not when a proclamation was read.

When the defenders could no longer move openly from one loyal position to another.

---

They were not all captured.

That was the part the proclamations omitted.

Anja and two guards disappeared into unfinished works passages Olan's crews had opened during the first months of reconstruction.

Ysabet, Selise and the archive staff reached the chapel through the old catalog route.

Bram, Edrik, Mikel and nine others reached a sealed-off laundry gallery above the eastern kitchens, where a warped service door could be opened from inside but looked nailed shut from the corridor.

Ardel still held the village road.

Calienne still held the chapel because both sides still needed her.

Pera was outside the walls.

The accession ledger was outside the archive.

Mera's accusation about the wage book existed in a guard's written note.

Aven's surrender terms existed in two copies.

The coup had won the castle.

It had not won every person in it.

---

Luceran received Bram's boot leather at a roadside charcoal camp.

The Black Pine scout found them near midnight.

Rhyse read the message first.

Then Luceran.

Miren watched his face.

"How old?"

"Yesterday evening," the scout said. "Maybe older now."

"Who carried it?"

"A girl. Pera Crewe. She's safe with our ridge post."

Luceran read the four words again.

**EDSELSTO ATTACKED. RETURN. TRUST BLACK PINE.**

Below them:

**BOOT / TACK**

Rhyse said, "That challenge is compromised by now."

Luceran nodded.

The fact that he understood did not make the leather easier to hold.

"How far?"

"If we ride the horses dead, we arrive with dead horses."

Luceran looked south.

"Then we don't."

Rhyse waited.

"Hard pace. Rotate mounts where we can. No fires after this one."

Miren stepped closer.

"Do you know who holds it?"

"No."

"Do you know who is alive?"

"No."

"Do you know whether the crown is taken?"

"No."

Every answer was a wound.

None became knowledge merely because he wanted it.

He folded the leather and put it inside his coat.

"Move."

---

In the Hall of Veils, Brother Talen checked the Seven Ancestral Lights after midnight.

He had checked them six times.

Canon Merel would later call that devotion.

It was fear.

One flame bent when the door opened.

Talen held his breath.

It straightened.

All seven remained lit.

Outside, armed men crossed the hall carrying orders from an emergency council no lawful sovereign had appointed.

The castle did nothing.

The candles did nothing.

Stone remained stone.

The coup had succeeded without permission from anything ancient.

It required only people, money, access, conviction, fear, lies, courage, exhaustion, and enough force applied to the right doors.

Edselsto was taken.

Its king was still coming home.
