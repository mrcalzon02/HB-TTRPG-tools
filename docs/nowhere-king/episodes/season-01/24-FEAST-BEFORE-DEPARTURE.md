# Season One, Episode 24 — Feast Before Departure

The feast began with an argument about boots.

Luceran had expected maps.

He had expected Rhyse to object to the number of outriders, Mara to object to the baggage, Ardel to object to the road, and Miren to object whenever all three men in the room began pretending that discomfort was a form of military virtue.

He had not expected Bram Tolland to stand in the lower hall holding one of the king's riding boots as though presenting evidence at a murder trial.

"No."

Luceran looked at the boot.

"It is mine."

"That is not the objection."

"It fits."

"Also not the objection."

Bram turned it over. One iron heel plate had pulled loose enough to show daylight beneath the leather.

Edrik Saye, beside him, said, "Stable boy caught it when he was checking the travelling tack."

"I am not tack."

"Majesty," Edrik said, with the careful face of a man who had learned exactly how much insolence a king would tolerate before breakfast, "tomorrow you are approximately tack with opinions."

Miren laughed into her cup.

Rhyse did not.

That was worse.

Luceran pointed at Edrik. "You are staying here. I can still arrange for that to be permanent."

"Bram already did."

Bram put the boot on the table. "Family corridor, receiving gate, lower reserve. He knows the credential changes and the night reliefs. Taking him would make me replace him with somebody who knows less because His Majesty enjoys being insulted by familiar people."

"I do not enjoy it."

"Then the Crown receives this service free of charge."

Mara Kest came through the door with three folded tallies under one arm.

"Good. Since we are discussing things the Crown receives whether it likes them or not, the baggage is six carts too large."

"We only have two baggage wagons," Luceran said.

"We have two baggage wagons after I removed six carts' worth of people attempting to send things with you."

"What things?"

"A folding bath. Four cases of wine. Two devotional chests. A carpet. Three boxes of court clothes. A silver warming pan. Someone's lute."

"Whose lute?"

"Nobody admitted it."

Miren said, "Keep the wine."

Mara gave her a flat look.

"One case."

"Two."

"One, my lady. The king is going to investigate cattle, not conquer a vineyard."

Luceran leaned back.

Edselsto was awake around them. Hammers rang somewhere below the east wall. A saw rasped in the village. From the kitchens came the smell of onions, roast birds and bread being made in quantities too large for an ordinary supper. Boots crossed the passage. A porter shouted for somebody named Hesk to stop stacking flour where rain could reach it.

The castle sounded alive.

Tomorrow, Luceran would leave it.

He discovered he disliked that thought more than he had expected.

---

By noon the departure had acquired a shape.

Fifty-eight mounted guards and household troops would travel with the royal party. Twelve Black Pine scouts would move ahead in pairs, never more than a half-day beyond the main body unless Rhyse ordered otherwise. Miren would travel with Luceran. Mara would go as the household officer responsible for turning royal intention into food, beds, fodder and receipts. Sera Vale would join at Northbridge after finishing witness copies moving south from Red Ford.

Bram would remain.

So would Edrik.

So would Ardel.

That last decision had been hers.

"Kaelmont has people on the road already," she told Luceran in the west yard. "You do not require me to stand beside a repaired bridge and announce that stone is heavy."

"You could announce it very persuasively."

"I have practice."

Workers were loading dressed blocks onto a sled behind her. Ardel stepped aside before a teamster had to ask a king to move.

"The lower fork remains the route," she said. "If Tomas sends word that Northbridge is not ready, you stop. You do not decide a royal coach can cross because being king has altered timber."

"You have a very poor opinion of kings."

"I have an excellent opinion of timber."

He smiled.

She did too.

It was easy with Ardel in a way that was increasingly dangerous precisely because neither of them was stupid about it.

"When I return," Luceran said, "I want the west retaining wall costed again. The first figure was obscene."

"The first figure was honest."

"Those are not mutually exclusive."

"No. But if you want it cheaper, decide which part you want to fall down."

"You are invited to supper tonight."

"I assumed so. Half the castle assumes so."

"That is not why."

"I know."

She held his gaze one heartbeat too long.

Then a mason called her name and the moment ended because Ardel had work.

That, Luceran thought, was one of the things he liked about her.

It was also one of the reasons Miren liked her despite herself.

---

The farewell feast was not formally a farewell feast.

Luceran had forbidden the phrase after Pellin Joss observed that calling a meal a farewell before riding toward a possible border crisis was the sort of thing poets did immediately before killing someone.

So it became supper.

A very large supper.

The great hall was still too cold near the north wall and too warm near the temporary braziers. One section of plaster had been removed for repair, leaving bare stone behind the musicians. The trestles did not match. Half the cups were royal silver and the other half belonged to a village brewer who had become wealthy enough in six months to complain about the delay in returning them.

Luceran loved it.

At the high table Miren sat on his right, Rhyse on his left. Ardel was two places down beside Calienne Orsanne, who had somehow turned a conversation about bridge tolls into an argument about whether a Crown-supported infirmary could charge northern wounded for clean linen.

"No," Calienne said.

Ardel chewed, swallowed, and said, "I did not say it should."

"You said costs exist."

"They do."

"Compassion is not a toll road."

"Linen is still linen."

Mara called from farther down, "Charge the Crown. His Majesty already promised care without distinction of bank."

Luceran raised his cup.

"I am delighted to discover my promises can be shouted across dinner."

"Cheaper than copying them," Pellin said.

Ysabet Fenn threw a crust at him.

It struck his sleeve.

He looked at it with grave offense.

"Chief Archivist."

"You were becoming pleased with yourself."

"That is not an archival offense."

"It is now."

Selise Varo sat beside Ysabet but no longer close enough to reach the sensitive papers that Ysabet had once trusted her to carry without thought. They spoke. They even laughed once. The empty space between trust and forgiveness remained exactly where Episode 20 had left it.

Luceran noticed.

He did not try to repair it for them.

At the lower tables guards ate beside masons, copyists, grooms, village councilors and two Black Pine scouts who seemed to regard forks as an affectation of lowland decadence. Bram had allowed half his watch to eat early and half late so the feast did not hollow the posts. Edrik appeared only after the first relief and left before dessert.

Competence was rarely dramatic when it worked.

That was why people forgot how much of life depended on it.

---

Miren found Luceran in the passage behind the musicians after the second course.

"You are hiding."

"I am standing."

"Behind a tapestry."

"The tapestry is in front of me."

"Remarkable legal defense."

She took his cup from him, drank, and gave it back.

"One case of wine?"

"Mara won."

"Mara usually does when the opponent is arithmetic."

Through the doorway they could see Ardel laughing at something Calienne had said. Luceran felt Miren notice him noticing.

Neither pretended otherwise.

"She is beautiful," Miren said.

"Yes."

"I dislike how much easier it would be if she were stupid."

"She has said almost exactly that about you."

Miren turned.

"When?"

"She did not."

Miren punched him lightly in the ribs.

He caught her wrist and kissed her.

The kiss lasted long enough to become private despite the hall being ten paces away.

When they separated, Miren kept her hand against his chest.

"When we come back," she said, "we decide whether we are going to keep allowing half the court to plan our marriage for us."

"We already decided they do not get to decide it."

"That was the principle. I mean the question."

Luceran looked at her.

This was not a proposal.

That mattered.

It was a promise to have a conversation neither of them could safely postpone forever.

"When we come back," he said.

"Together."

"Together."

She kissed him again, shorter this time.

"Good. Now return before Pellin tells everyone you fled through a secret door."

"There is no secret door here."

"That has never stopped Pellin."

---

Later, Rhyse and Bram stood over the watch board while music came through two stone walls as a dull pulse.

Luceran was not present.

That made the conversation easier.

Bram tapped the family corridor marker.

"Anja Pell has second relief. Mikel Orr takes third. They still hate each other's method."

"Do they obey the same orders?"

"Yes."

"Then let them hate efficiently."

Bram grunted.

Rhyse checked the lower gate.

"Reserve?"

"Restored. Eight on first call, four more within a quarter hour unless west service is active. Edrik has receiving. Vale's deputy has west service until she returns. No relief credential changes while you are gone without my seal and Edrik's countersign."

"Good."

"Do not sound relieved."

Rhyse looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you are taking the king into a border dispute and leaving me a castle full of nobles who think a departing king creates unused authority."

"You wanted command."

"I wanted wages. Command happened to me."

Rhyse laughed.

Then Bram stopped smiling.

"If you send a route change, use the black-thread packet. If somebody sends me one without it, I assume the messenger is wrong until I can prove otherwise."

"Even if it carries Luceran's seal."

"Especially then. Seals can be carried by idiots."

Rhyse held out his hand.

Bram clasped it.

They had known each other long enough not to make speeches.

"Bring him back," Bram said.

"Keep the doors boring."

"That is the ambition."

Neither man knew how expensive that ambition was about to become.

---

Near midnight the feast had stopped pretending to be official.

A mason danced with a kitchen clerk. Calienne had removed her formal green mantle and was beating Pellin at a dice game with such regularity that he accused the temple of possessing doctrinal exemptions from probability. Mara confiscated a third cup from a young messenger scheduled to ride before dawn. Ysabet and Selise argued quietly about whether a copied burial register belonged in the family archive or the Hall of Veils collection.

Ardel found Miren by the fire.

"Your coach will hate the lower fork," she said.

"I have been told."

"Luceran will complain."

"Certainly."

"Do not let him bully the driver into speed on the descent."

Miren looked at her.

"You know I am not his keeper."

"Yes. That is why I asked you rather than ordered you."

Miren considered that.

"I will tell him he is being an ass if the opportunity arises."

"I knew the realm was safe."

They shared a smile.

There were things between them that neither woman had resolved.

There were also roads.

Adult life had the indecency to contain both.

Across the room Luceran was promising Olan Crewe that the south-roof carpenters would not lose their lime allotment merely because the king was absent. Nera Vos had cornered Mara with a receiving tally. Brother Talen Veyr had come down from the Hall of Veils only long enough to eat and complain that somebody had again tied ribbon too close to the candle stands.

Plans accumulated everywhere.

When Luceran returned, the west wall would be costed again.

Miren and Luceran would discuss marriage as a real question rather than a rumor.

Calienne would inspect the winter Hall of Veils work.

Ysabet would finish the disputed burial register.

Ardel would argue about retaining walls.

Bram intended to make the doors boring.

Edrik intended to sleep through an entire afternoon after three consecutive night reliefs.

Mara intended to discover who owned the anonymous lute.

None of them understood these plans as precious.

They were simply tomorrow.

---

Before dawn, Edselsto smelled of wet stone, horse sweat and bread.

The feast had ended only a few hours earlier. Servants were still clearing cups from the hall while stablehands tightened girths in the lower yard.

Luceran stood once more before the Seven Ancestral Lights.

All seven burned.

Brother Talen was asleep in a chair against the wall, chin on his chest.

Luceran did not wake him.

He did not ask the candles for protection.

He did not imagine they promised anything.

He only looked long enough to remember the room as it was.

Then he left.

In the yard, Rhyse was mounted. Mara was already furious with a baggage strap. Miren sat her horse rather than taking the coach for the first stretch, wrapped in a dark travelling cloak. The first pair of Black Pine scouts had departed before the bells. The next pair waited at the gate.

Bram stood beneath the arch with Edrik.

"West service?" Luceran asked.

Bram's mouth twitched.

"Covered."

"Family corridor?"

"Covered."

"Lower reserve?"

"Covered. Go away, Majesty."

Luceran laughed.

Then the gate opened.

Fifty-eight mounted guards and household troops moved out by files, not parade. The baggage wagons followed. The coach rolled empty for now. Miren fell in beside Luceran. Rhyse rode ahead far enough to command and close enough to turn if needed.

Behind them Edselsto did not become empty.

Smoke rose from the village cookfires. Hammers began again. Guards took posts. Clerks opened shutters. Ardel's road crews were already moving. Somewhere a kitchen servant was probably discovering another cup under a bench.

The six Crown stores wagons remained at Northbridge, where Tomas Reed expected them.

The repaired bridge waited to be tested.

Red Ford waited beyond it with cattle, old stones, contradictory memories and one burned shelter.

Luceran looked back once.

Miren saw him.

"You expect it to disappear?"

"No."

"Good."

He faced the road.

Behind him, people he trusted continued living lives he did not control.

Ahead of him, people he did not know were already deciding what his arrival meant.

The distinction felt important.

By noon, Edselsto was behind the mountain shoulder.

The king was gone.

The court was not.
