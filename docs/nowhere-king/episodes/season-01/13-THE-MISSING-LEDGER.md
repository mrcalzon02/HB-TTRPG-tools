# Season One, Episode 13 — The Missing Ledger

**Status:** Committed episode prose  
**Season:** One — The Return to Edselsto  
**Episode:** 13 of 32  
**Reveal ceiling:** Seven candles remain lit. The discrepancy is entirely human and institutional. No supernatural cause, sealed-stair exploration, containment truth, or castle intelligence is confirmed.  
**Primary dramatic purpose:** Turn Pellin Joss's grain mismatch into a material investigation of receipts, carriage, storage, signatures, and incentives. Promote low-status accounting competence, force scrutiny across friendly interests, and establish that corruption can grow from ordinary shortcuts before anyone thinks of themselves as a conspirator.

---

Pellin Joss found the clerk asleep on a sack of lentils.

This was not where the clerk was supposed to sleep.

It was, however, where Pellin had expected to find him.

"Wake up."

Tomas Reed opened one eye.

"No."

"The south grain tally."

The eye closed.

Pellin kicked the sack.

Tomas sat up.

"What about it?"

"It is wrong."

"Then fix it."

"I cannot."

That woke him.

Pellin held out two sheets.

One was the stores tally.

One was the receiving copy.

Tomas rubbed his face and read both.

Then read them again.

"That's not mine."

"The hand is yours."

"The number isn't."

Pellin stared.

Tomas pointed.

"Look at the seven."

Pellin did.

The tally showed seventy-four sacks received from the south-road consignment.

Tomas wrote sevens with a crossbar.

This seven had none.

"Who changed it?"

"I don't know."

"What did you write?"

"Sixty-four."

Pellin looked at the line.

Ten sacks.

Not a kingdom.

Not yet.

"Where is the receiving original?"

Tomas stood.

"If Venn filed it, lower records."

"Get dressed."

"I am dressed."

"You are wearing one boot."

Tomas looked down.

"So I am."

---

By dawn they had three numbers.

The stores tally said seventy-four sacks.

Tomas said he had written sixty-four.

The carrier's receipt said sixty-eight.

Pellin disliked all three.

Master Venn disliked being awakened.

Avarren disliked discovering that Master Venn had been awakened before him.

Luceran disliked being awakened by Avarren.

This hierarchy of suffering brought them to the stores office before breakfast.

Pellin had arranged the papers on an empty flour bin.

Luceran looked at them.

"Which one is true?"

Pellin said, "That is what we are finding out."

A month ago Luceran might have hated the answer.

This morning he nodded.

"Start."

Pellin pointed to the carrier's receipt.

"Household purchasing ordered seventy-four sacks from Maret & Sons."

"Friendly supplier?" Luceran asked.

Avarren said, "They have supplied Edevane households."

Miren, who had come because she was already awake and because Luceran had sent for her after hearing the name, folded her arms.

"That does not make them mine."

"I know."

She looked at him.

He meant it.

That mattered.

Pellin continued.

"Maret invoiced seventy-four. South-road carrier Dovren Haulage accepted sixty-eight."

"Why six fewer?"

"Invoice note says six held at river depot for mold inspection."

"Then why did we pay for seventy-four?"

Clavard Clifford entered behind them carrying his coat.

"Because the purchase contract transfers title at depot acceptance, not castle delivery."

Luceran looked at him.

"Good morning."

"It was."

Clavard read the papers.

"Six quarantined sacks remain Crown property pending inspection. That part may be correct."

Pellin tapped Tomas's receiving entry.

"Then sixty-four reached Edselsto."

"Four missing in carriage?"

"Maybe."

"And stores says seventy-four."

"Yes."

Luceran looked at the flour bins.

"Do we have seventy-four?"

Pellin's expression became offended.

"We have flour."

"That was not my question."

"No, Majesty. It was not."

---

Counting grain sounded easy until they tried it.

Some sacks had already been opened.

Some had gone to the kitchens.

Some to worker cookfires.

Two had been transferred to the guard bakehouse.

One had torn and been swept into a clean barrel.

Three different units were in use because old Edselsto measures had not yet been fully replaced.

Pellin refused to estimate.

He brought Havel from the kitchen.

Havel brought his flour book.

The guard bakehouse sent Corporal Senn with an issue slate.

Tessa Rane arrived because two sacks had been temporarily stored in the village dryhouse during rain and she wanted it recorded that village storage did not make village grain Crown grain.

Luceran looked around the stores office.

"This is becoming a council."

"No," Avarren said. "Councils have chairs."

Tessa sat on a barrel.

"Better."

They reconstructed movement.

Sixty-four sacks had entered Edselsto.

That number held.

Four sacks accepted by the carrier had never reached the receiving yard.

Six remained at the river depot.

The stores tally had been altered from sixty-four to seventy-four after receipt.

Then the payment book had treated all seventy-four as delivered.

Clavard said, "That is a problem."

Havel said, "That is theft."

Pellin said, "It is evidence."

Havel looked at him.

"Of theft."

"Of a false tally."

"Why falsify it if not to steal?"

Pellin shrugged.

"To hide loss. To hide spoilage. To make a carrier whole. To make a supplier whole. To make yourself look competent. To move debt between accounts. To cover a mistake. To steal."

Luceran said, "Good."

Havel looked at him.

"Majesty?"

"Keep all the possibilities until one survives."

Pellin nodded.

That was the first time Luceran saw him smile.

---

The four missing carriage sacks belonged to Dovren Haulage's driver, apparently.

At least on paper.

The driver was a woman named Eska Dovren, niece of the firm's owner and possessed of the calm hostility of someone who had spent twenty years explaining roads to people who owned maps.

She arrived at noon.

"You lost four sacks," Rhyse said.

"No."

"The receipt says sixty-eight."

"I loaded sixty-eight."

"Receiving says sixty-four."

"Then four vanished after I arrived."

Master Venn said, "No."

Eska looked at him.

Venn continued.

"I signed the wagon into the lower yard at sixty-four."

Eska stared.

"Show me."

He did.

Her face changed.

"That is not the depot receipt."

"No."

"Where is my road sheet?"

She had kept it.

Sixty-four.

Pellin put the sheets beside each other.

The carrier's depot receipt said sixty-eight.

Eska's road sheet said sixty-four.

"Who signed the depot receipt?" Luceran asked.

Eska pointed.

"Jalen Marr."

"Your clerk?"

"Depot weighman. Not mine. Contracted by the river consortium."

"Alive?"

"I assume."

"Friendly to anyone?"

Eska laughed.

"Majesty, he weighs grain. Everyone is friendly to him."

That answer went into Avarren's notes almost word for word.

---

By afternoon the six quarantined sacks became four.

Not because two had vanished.

Because they had never existed.

The river depot's mold inspector had marked four sacks unfit, not six.

The purchasing copy had been overwritten.

Pellin put the documents in a row.

Original order: seventy-four.

Actual depot release: sixty-eight.

Actual mold hold: four.

Actual wagon load: sixty-four.

Actual castle receipt: sixty-four.

The physical chain now reconciled.

The paperwork did not.

Someone had altered the mold hold from four to six.

Someone had altered the castle receipt from sixty-four to seventy-four.

The combined effect made the Crown pay Maret & Sons as though seventy-four sound sacks had completed the contracted chain while concealing that ten had not.

Luceran said, "How much money?"

Clavard named the amount.

It was not enormous.

That made Luceran angrier.

"All this for that?"

Pellin looked at him.

"Majesty."

"What?"

"People steal small amounts because small amounts are less likely to summon kings."

The room went quiet.

Luceran sat back.

"Fair."

---

Maret & Sons sent a factor before sunset.

Not one of the sons.

A factor named Iven Cale.

Miren knew him.

That complicated the room immediately.

"Iven," she said.

"My lady."

"You handled my aunt's winter stores."

"Three years."

Luceran watched them.

Iven saw him watching.

"Majesty, House Edevane has no interest in this consignment."

Miren said, "Do not use me as a certificate."

Iven flushed.

"I meant no offense."

"I know. Don't."

That was useful too.

Pellin showed him the altered copies.

Iven became pale.

"Our invoice is correct."

"Seventy-four ordered," Pellin said.

"Yes."

"Four rejected for mold."

"Then seventy should have remained payable subject to contract."

"Sixty-four loaded."

Iven stared.

"No."

Eska Dovren, still present because she refused to leave while anyone implied she had lost grain, said, "Yes."

Iven looked at her road sheet.

Then at the depot copy.

"Jalen."

"You know him?" Rhyse asked.

"Everyone knows Jalen."

Eska made a sound of agreement.

Iven rubbed his mouth.

"He sometimes advances clean certificates."

"What does that mean?" Luceran asked.

"If a supplier is waiting on inspection and the result is obvious, he marks the expected release so payment can move."

"Before inspection?"

"Sometimes."

"Illegal?"

Clavard said, "Improper. Contractually dangerous. Not necessarily criminal by itself."

Luceran looked at Iven.

"And if the result is not what he expected?"

"Then the next paper corrects it."

Pellin tapped the altered mold copy.

"Unless someone corrects the correction."

Iven looked sick.

---

The clerk who had altered the castle tally was easier to find.

That was Tomas Reed's fault.

He recognized the ink.

Not the handwriting.

The ink.

"West office."

Avarren stared at him.

"You can identify an office by ink?"

"No. By the sand."

"What?"

Tomas held the page sideways.

Fine pale grit clung in the strokes.

"The west office uses quarry sand because someone bought six jars of it before we moved. Lower records uses river sand. Archive uses black."

Ysabet Marris, summoned because the archive had become involved in comparing copies, said, "Archive uses black because pale sand makes a mess."

Tomas nodded.

"Yes."

"Who has access to west office?"

Avarren listed seven people.

One was Pellan Edevane.

Selise's uncle.

Miren's distant relation.

Another was a Moncler claims clerk.

Another worked for Crown purchasing.

Another had previously served Bellisar.

One was simply a copyist.

Luceran felt the old instinct arrive.

Connections.

Lines.

Patrons.

He let it pass.

"Who used the desk?"

Tomas said, "Ask the sand."

Montegrain, who had wandered in during the sixth hour and refused to leave, whispered, "I love him."

The west-office sand jar had a lending slate because the clerk responsible for supplies was miserly.

Three people had signed it out.

Only one during the relevant day.

A junior purchasing clerk named Olan Vey.

Olan Vey was twenty-four and cried before anyone accused him.

That was not evidence of innocence.

It was evidence that he was twenty-four and terrified.

"I changed it."

Rhyse said nothing.

Luceran said, "Why?"

Olan wiped his face.

"Because the account was already closed."

"What does that mean?"

"We had paid the invoice."

"Before delivery."

"On expected depot release."

"Why?"

"Because Maret needed settlement to release the next grain block."

Clavard closed his eyes.

Luceran said, "Did Maret ask you to falsify the tally?"

"No."

"Iven Cale?"

"No."

"House Edevane?"

"No."

"No one?"

Olan shook his head.

"Then why?"

"Because if I entered sixty-four, the payment account reopened. It would show overpayment. Purchasing would freeze the next order. Damaris carriage was already tightening. We would lose days."

Pellin said, "So you wrote seventy-four."

"I thought the depot would correct."

"That makes no sense."

"I know."

"Did you?"

Olan looked at him.

"At the time?"

That answer hurt because everyone in the room understood it.

---

The four-sack depot discrepancy belonged to Jalen Marr.

Not theft.

At least not proven theft.

He had signed sixty-eight before final mold inspection because Maret's factor expected only six sacks held and because the carrier wanted to leave before rain.

Then inspection rejected ten.

Four were later salvageable after rebagging.

Six were not.

The paperwork became a knot.

Jalen changed one number.

A depot copyist changed another.

Olan Vey, seeing a paid account that would reopen and delay future grain, changed the castle tally to make the chain appear complete.

Nobody had coordinated the whole thing.

Each person had made the next person's shortcut easier.

And somewhere inside those shortcuts, Maret & Sons had been overpaid.

"Corruption?" Luceran asked.

Clavard considered.

"Yes."

Olan flinched.

Clavard continued.

"Not necessarily conspiracy. Not necessarily theft by every participant. But the record was knowingly made false to preserve a commercial outcome."

Pellin said, "And the false record hid an overpayment."

"Yes."

Miren looked at Iven Cale.

"Will Maret repay?"

Iven said, "Yes."

Luceran said, "You answered quickly."

"Because if I answer slowly, you freeze us."

"Should I?"

Iven looked at him.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you need grain."

There it was.

Luceran hated him a little for saying it.

He respected him for saying it.

---

House Damaris entered the story without entering the room.

Their second message arrived while the inquiry was still open.

Guaranteed autumn volume was available.

At a higher price.

Carriage risk would be shared if the Crown accepted depot measurement as the commercial transfer point.

Clavard laughed when he read that clause.

"What?"

"They want us to trust the depot."

Everyone looked at the papers spread across the table.

Montegrain said, "Excellent timing."

Luceran did not laugh.

"Can we?"

Pellin answered before anyone else.

"Not as it works now."

Clavard looked at him.

Pellin continued.

"Depot measure can be the transfer point if the Crown has its own receiving copy there. Not a supplier copy. Not carrier copy. Crown copy."

"That means a clerk at the river."

"Yes."

"Cost."

"Less than paying for grain we don't receive."

Luceran looked at Avarren.

"Can we staff it?"

"Not without taking someone from somewhere else."

Selise, at the wall with Ysabet, said, "The archive has two applicants who passed hand tests and have not been assigned."

Ysabet turned.

"One passed."

"One nearly passed."

"Nearly is not passed."

"Then one."

Luceran looked at Ysabet.

She looked annoyed.

Not because Selise was wrong.

Because Selise had volunteered archive capacity in front of the king.

Luceran recognized the boundary.

"Would the archive lose something?"

"Yes."

"Then no."

Pellin said, "Stores has Tomas."

Tomas looked horrified.

"I am standing here."

"Yes."

"You are giving me away."

"For six weeks."

"To a river depot."

"You like numbers."

"I like dry numbers."

Eska Dovren said, "The depot has a roof."

"Does it?"

"Mostly."

Luceran watched them negotiate Tomas's life while Tomas objected to every part of it.

Eventually the arrangement became temporary.

Six weeks.

Travel allowance.

Dry lodging guaranteed by Damaris.

Authority to create a Crown receiving copy at depot measurement.

No authority to certify quality independently of inspectors.

Daily duplicate dispatch when Crown grain moved.

Tomas demanded two pairs of boots.

Clavard asked why.

"One will become river."

He got them.

---

Olan Vey was not imprisoned.

Rhyse objected.

Not strongly.

Enough.

"He falsified a Crown account."

"Yes," Luceran said.

"He admits it."

"Yes."

"Then consequence matters."

"I agree."

"What consequence?"

Luceran looked at Pellin.

Pellin looked surprised.

"Why me?"

"Because you found it."

"That does not make me judge."

"No. It makes you the person who knows what damage he caused."

Pellin considered.

"Remove him from payment authority. Make him reconstruct the entire grain chain under supervision. Every open order. Every advance. Every depot correction. Then audit his work."

Olan looked as if prison might have been kinder.

Clavard said, "Restitution?"

"Maret repays the overpayment. If Olan personally profited, we haven't found it."

Rhyse said, "And if he didn't?"

Pellin shrugged.

"Then don't invent profit."

Luceran smiled slightly.

"Do that."

Olan swallowed.

"Majesty."

"You lied in my accounts."

"Yes."

"You did it because you thought an accurate number would stop grain."

"Yes."

"You may even have been right."

Olan stared.

"That does not make the lie acceptable."

"No."

"But I need people to stop hiding problems because they think I will punish the problem instead of the lie."

Olan's face changed.

Luceran hoped he understood.

Luceran was not sure he did.

That uncertainty also stayed.

---

Miren found Luceran after the inquiry.

"You were looking at me."

"When?"

"When Maret came up."

"They supply your family."

"They supplied my aunt."

"Yes."

"You wondered."

"I did."

She nodded.

"Good."

Luceran frowned.

"Good?"

"You wondered and then investigated the transaction."

"I thought you might be angry."

"I would be angry if you decided I was guilty because I know Iven."

"Fair."

"I would also be angry if you refused to inspect Iven because I know him."

Luceran leaned against the corridor wall.

"This is exhausting."

"Yes."

"Do relationships ever make anything easier?"

Miren stepped closer.

"Sometimes."

She kissed him.

Not long.

Enough.

Then she said, "That was easier."

Luceran laughed.

"Politically?"

"No."

"Of course not."

She rested her hand against his chest.

"My aunt will be embarrassed."

"About Iven?"

"About being adjacent to sloppy grain accounting."

"She wasn't involved."

"That will not prevent embarrassment."

"What will she do?"

"Probably send you a very correct letter explaining exactly how uninvolved she was."

"Useful."

"And then she will quietly stop using Maret until they repair their books."

Luceran looked at her.

"Will you tell her to?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because she will think of it herself."

There it was again.

People acting without waiting for the king.

He was beginning to find it reassuring.

---

The political consequence arrived the next morning.

Not from Edevane.

From Moncler.

Their claims factor requested that all pending direct-loss compensation be reviewed under the same evidentiary standard used in the grain inquiry.

Avarren read the request twice.

"They are using this against us."

Clavard said, "They are using our rule."

"Same thing."

"No."

Luceran took the paper.

Moncler had been waiting for payment on verified losses from the worker requisition.

Now they wanted carrier sheets, wage records, tool inventories, interrupted-contract evidence, and Crown acknowledgment that discrepancies would be resolved by documents rather than by which house had better access.

Luceran looked at Pellin.

Pellin said, "Reasonable."

Avarren glared at him.

Pellin shrugged.

"You asked for numbers."

Luceran signed the review order.

That was the moment the missing ledger became political.

Not because it exposed a grand enemy.

Because a rule applied to a friendly supplier could also be demanded by a house Luceran had inconvenienced.

Moncler had learned something useful about the new king.

So had everyone else.

---

Pellin returned to stores.

He had missed breakfast.

Havel saved him bread anyway.

Tomas Reed packed for the river while complaining that nobody had asked whether he wanted to become important.

Selise told him importance was mostly being sent somewhere unpleasant with paper.

Ysabet agreed.

Eska Dovren left with corrected carriage copies and a demand that future depot receipts be attached to road sheets.

Iven Cale sent Maret & Sons' repayment undertaking before noon.

Olan Vey began reconstructing every open grain order under Pellin's supervision.

Rhyse kept a copy of his confession because false records could also conceal security movements.

Avarren opened a new procedural file titled COMMERCIAL RECEIPT — DEPOT / DELIVERY DISTINCTION.

Luceran hated the title.

It was exactly correct.

At the lower well, stone arrived late.

At the west road, Kaelmont surveyors began marking drainage lines under Tessa's eye.

At the Vess pasture, the paid observation watch saw nothing suspicious.

Jeron Bellisar remained unable to ride.

The false horn remained locked away.

The rope remained evidence.

The old family doors remained closed.

And in the nave, seven candles burned.

The missing ledger had contained no ghost.

Only people.

That was enough.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 13 resolves the immediate grain mismatch while opening a broader institutional corruption/audit lane.

### Durable changes

- Pellin Joss and Tomas Reed identify conflicting grain records and reconstruct the physical chain through order, depot inspection, carrier road sheet, castle receipt, stores, kitchens, guard bakehouse, and village temporary storage.
- The apparent ten-sack discrepancy is produced by layered human shortcuts: mold inspection changes, premature depot certification, and Olan Vey's deliberate falsification of the castle tally after payment had already closed.
- No coordinated grand conspiracy is established. Individual shortcuts compound into a corrupt record that conceals Crown overpayment.
- Maret & Sons agrees to repay the overpayment; no evidence establishes House Edevane involvement despite prior supplier relationships.
- Miren explicitly supports scrutinizing people connected to her while rejecting guilt by association.
- Olan Vey loses payment authority and must reconstruct all open grain orders under supervision. No personal profit has yet been proven.
- Pellin's competence is promoted: Luceran asks him to define the material damage and accepts his recommendation without converting him into judge or minister.
- Tomas Reed receives a temporary six-week Crown depot assignment with travel allowance, dry lodging, two pairs of boots, and authority to create an independent Crown receiving copy at river measurement.
- Damaris grain negotiation remains active; Crown may accept depot measurement only with independent Crown documentation and quality distinctions preserved.
- The inquiry establishes a reusable evidentiary standard separating physical receipt, commercial transfer, quality inspection, payment, and inferred loyalty.
- Moncler immediately invokes that standard for its unresolved direct-loss compensation claim, forcing Luceran to apply documentary fairness to a house he previously harmed.
- Kaelmont road concession, Bellisar hunt debt/injury, Vess observation and water rights, Royal Companion review, Henn property protections, village infrastructure, and security investigation remain active.
- Luceran and Miren's intimacy continues without exclusivity or political simplification.
- Seven of seven candles remain lit. No confirmed supernatural behavior occurs.
