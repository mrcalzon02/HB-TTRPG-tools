# Season One, Episode 29 — The Confidant's Last Stand

Edrik Saye had intended to sleep through an afternoon.

The thought returned to him while Bram Tolland burned beside the wall and Mikel Orr shivered beneath two stolen blankets.

It was an absurd thing to remember.

Three nights ago, at the farewell feast, Edrik had announced the plan with the seriousness of a military campaign. He would finish the king's departure watch, hand receiving to somebody else, eat until he could no longer stand upright, and sleep from noon until supper. Luceran had told him this was the first sensible ambition he had ever expressed.

Edrik had replied that serving the Crown had lowered his standards.

Now there was no noon in the old laundry gallery. There was only the thin grey light leaking through a high vent, the smell of starch and fever, and the question of water.

Anja Pell had returned from the unfinished works passages with one guard instead of two. Bram could no longer put weight on his wounded leg without shaking. Mikel's skin was too hot. Eleven other people occupied a room designed to store linen, and every jug they possessed was nearly empty.

Below them, men were searching.

Not randomly anymore.

That was worse.

Anja crouched beside the warped service panel and listened to footsteps move through the eastern kitchens.

"They're counting doors."

Edrik handed the last full cup to Mikel.

"Everyone counts doors after they take a castle."

"They're measuring walls too."

Bram opened his eyes.

"Works plans?"

"Maybe."

"Who?"

"Rusk's men. Not Aven's."

Edrik looked at the folded payment list beneath Bram's hand.

**P.R. — 18 — due. Settled.**

One scrap of paper. One missing washerman. One boot full of blood. Enough to ask questions. Not enough to answer them.

"Can you get back to Calienne?" Bram asked.

Anja shook her head.

"Not the way I came. They're posting the east run."

"Archive passage?"

"Don't know it."

"Village road?"

"Ardel owns the road, not the castle between us and it."

Bram closed his eyes again.

Edrik knew that expression. Bram was not resting. He was trying to turn bad choices into an order.

"We need a runner," Bram said.

"We need water," Edrik replied.

"We need both."

"We have one person who can still move quickly through receiving routes without needing a map."

Anja looked at him.

"No."

Edrik smiled.

"I hadn't proposed anything."

"You were about to."

"I might have proposed that you go."

"You know the receiving routes better."

"Exactly."

Bram said, "Nobody goes alone."

Edrik looked at the twelve people in the room.

"With whom?"

Nobody answered.

That was arithmetic.

---

Sir Aven Marr discovered by midmorning that a coup could have too many seals.

His own seal remained on the crown coffer.

The emergency council had acquired another for routine orders.

Cale Rusk's men carried warrants bearing an impression copied from Aven's surrender terms.

Two quartermasters had accepted requisitions because the handwriting looked official and the bearer had arrived armed.

A kitchen clerk had refused one because the date was written in the capital style rather than Edselsto's household form.

Aven promoted the kitchen clerk's objection into policy.

"No requisition without a named issuer, named bearer, purpose, and countersignature."

The clerk stared at him.

"Sir, there isn't a countersignature office."

"Then establish one."

"With whom?"

Aven looked around the great hall.

That was becoming the question beneath every question.

"Use three clerks. No two from the same office."

"Which offices?"

"Household, stores, and council."

"Household won't recognize the council."

"Then they may write that beside the signature."

The clerk's expression suggested that this was not administration but a punishment invented specifically for clerks.

Aven understood.

He was beginning to suspect government was mostly the art of discovering how many people one decision required to become real.

Cale Rusk entered carrying a sealed packet.

"We have found another loyalist route."

Aven did not look up.

"Where?"

"Eastern service works."

"Found how?"

"A guard saw movement."

"Name?"

Rusk set the packet down.

"You cannot govern by interrogating every noun."

"I can try."

"You are trying."

Aven finally looked at him.

"Pellon Rusk is still missing."

"So are dozens of people."

"His wage records were cut out."

"Yes."

"His blood was found."

"Some blood was found."

"A payment list exists."

Rusk's eyes changed by almost nothing.

Aven noticed.

"Does it?"

"You didn't know?"

"No."

"Good."

Rusk's mouth hardened.

"Where is it?"

Aven leaned back.

"I didn't say I had it."

For the first time since the castle fell, Cale Rusk looked honestly angry.

It pleased Aven less than he expected.

"If evidence is being concealed by armed loyalists—"

"Then I would like it preserved."

"From us?"

"From everyone."

Rusk laughed once.

"The crown. The washerman. The records. You keep seizing things from your own victory."

Aven stood.

"My victory was supposed to establish a lawful regency until the king answered charges before the estates."

"Your victory was supposed to take the castle."

"Those are not the same sentence."

"No," Rusk said. "Only one of them happened."

He left the packet on the table.

Inside was a rough sketch of the eastern kitchens and laundry wall.

Someone had marked three possible voids.

---

Calienne Orsanne had twenty-three patients and four beds.

The rest occupied pallets, chapel benches, folded rugs and one expensive devotional carpet that would never recover.

She had stopped asking which side anyone belonged to before treating them.

She did ask before allowing them to leave.

A young coup soldier with a stitched forearm tried to stand.

Calienne pushed him back onto the bench.

"I have orders."

"You have twelve stitches."

"I can walk."

"So can a table if four men carry it."

He stared at her.

"You cannot detain me."

"I am not detaining you. Your arm is detaining you. I merely speak for it."

At the far wall, Ysabet Fenn sat beside the medicine chest containing Aven's written surrender terms. Selise Varo had fallen asleep upright against a pillar, one hand still resting on the satchel containing the current seal register.

Nobody in the chapel trusted anyone enough to sleep properly.

That was why the knock at the vestry door woke six people.

Three short.

Pause.

One.

Pause.

Three.

Calienne frowned.

Ysabet stood.

"Do you know that?"

"No."

Selise woke instantly.

The knock came again.

Three. One. Three.

A voice whispered through the door.

"Receiving."

Calienne crossed the chapel and did not open it.

"Name."

"Hessa."

Selise looked at Ysabet.

"Hessa Dorn?"

The voice answered before they could ask.

"Yes."

Calienne opened the door one hand's width.

Hessa Dorn nearly fell through it.

The Black Pine scout had a bandage around her ribs and dried mud to her knees. She had not been in the chapel since the first fighting.

"How did you get inside?"

"Village culvert."

"Ardel let you through?"

"Ardel doesn't know."

"That will delight her."

Hessa held out a strip of cloth.

A receiving tally had been written on it in charcoal.

At the bottom were two words.

**KING RETURNING.**

The chapel changed.

Nobody cheered.

That would have been easier.

Calienne closed her eyes.

Ysabet sat down.

Selise whispered, "How long?"

"Black Pine estimate. Before tomorrow if they keep pace. Maybe tonight if they ruin the horses."

"They won't," Hessa said. "Rhyse is with him."

That mattered.

Then Hessa added, "The gates are being prepared to close."

That mattered more.

---

Edrik left the laundry gallery with an empty water skin, Anja's payment list inside his shirt, and Bram's objection following him through the wall.

"You come back."

Edrik paused with one shoulder through the service panel.

"That's the plan."

Bram's fever-bright eyes fixed on him.

"Don't be clever."

"I have never been accused of that."

"I am accusing you now."

Edrik looked back at him.

They had stood beside each other at Luceran's first departure from the old capital, when neither of them had expected to become part of the king's close escort. They had learned his habits because somebody had to. Which stair he took when angry. Which petitioners he remembered. Which doors he forgot to close. How far behind to stand when Miren was present. How much wine meant he would start arguing with furniture.

Bram had made the doors boring.

Edrik had made the king's movement boring.

It had been good work.

"I'll come back if the route stays open."

"That's not what I said."

"No."

Edrik pulled the panel closed.

It was the closest he came to promising.

---

The receiving corridors were not secret passages.

That saved him.

Secret passages attracted maps, legends and men who wanted to find secret passages.

Receiving corridors attracted barrels.

Edrik moved behind kitchens, storage rooms and service stairs built to keep deliveries from crossing formal halls. He carried the water skin openly. Twice he passed armed men and looked annoyed rather than afraid.

The first ignored him.

The second said, "You."

Edrik stopped.

"Me."

"Where are you going?"

"To get water."

"For whom?"

"People who drink it."

The man stared.

Edrik sighed.

"Lower kitchen."

"That's the other way."

"Lower kitchen cistern is fouled."

"Since when?"

"Since somebody put a dead man in it."

The soldier's face changed.

Edrik pointed back.

"If you want to check, I strongly recommend not tasting it."

The soldier waved him on.

Edrik turned the corner before allowing himself to breathe.

Luceran would have laughed.

That thought hurt more than it should have.

At the old bread room he found the first new lock.

At the flour stair he found two guards.

At receiving he found Cale Rusk.

Edrik stepped backward before Rusk saw his face.

Too late.

"Hold."

Edrik ran.

---

The castle did not help him.

No door opened by itself.

No corridor lengthened.

No ancestral voice warned him which stair was clear.

Edrik survived the first pursuit because he knew Edselsto better than the men behind him.

He cut through the cold pantry, vaulted a broken workbench, took the narrow stair beside the old charcoal store and emerged behind a line of servants carrying flour under guard.

Someone shouted.

A sack fell.

White dust exploded across the passage.

Edrik went through it blind.

He reached the lower cistern room with three men still behind him.

The door was open.

That was bad.

Hessa Dorn stood inside with a knife in one hand.

That was worse.

She pulled him through.

"Why are you here?"

"Water."

"You're being chased."

"Apparently it is valuable."

Hessa barred the door.

Edrik bent over, breathing hard.

"King?"

"Returning."

"How long?"

"Tonight. Tomorrow. Depends."

He laughed once.

"Of course."

Hessa saw the blood beginning to soak through his sleeve.

"You hit?"

"Wall."

"Walls don't stab."

"This one was ambitious."

She reached for him.

"No time."

"There is never time. Sit."

He sat because Black Pine scouts had a way of turning advice into geography.

While she bound the shallow cut, he pulled out the payment list.

"Can you get this to Ardel?"

Hessa read it.

"Maybe."

"Or Calienne. Or Ysabet. Anybody who doesn't answer to Rusk."

"Who is P.R.?"

"Maybe Pellon Rusk."

"Maybe isn't proof."

"Bram said the same."

"Then Bram is occasionally useful."

"Don't tell him. He'll become impossible."

Hessa folded the paper and tucked it beneath her bandage.

Edrik filled the water skin.

Then they heard the first bell.

Not alarm.

Gate bell.

Three strokes.

Pause.

Three.

Hessa stood.

"They're closing."

---

Cale Rusk did not need Aven's permission to close the gates.

That was precisely the problem.

Aven reached the lower hall while the inner portcullis was already descending.

"Stop it."

The winch crew looked at Rusk.

Aven saw that.

So did Rusk.

"The king is returning," Rusk said.

Aven's stomach tightened.

"Source?"

"Black Pine movement. Road reports. Your loyalists are trying to pass messages."

"Then we negotiate before he reaches the walls."

"From behind closed gates."

"We agreed the king would be called before the estates."

"We agreed many things."

Aven stepped between Rusk and the winch.

"Open the inner gate."

Rusk looked almost regretful.

"No."

Aven's four Companions moved.

So did Rusk's men.

For several seconds the coup nearly became a second coup in the lower hall.

Then Aven looked at the numbers.

He could order violence.

He could also count.

"Who commands these men?" he asked.

Rusk smiled without pleasure.

"Today?"

The portcullis struck stone.

Aven understood the answer.

Not him.

---

Edrik and Hessa reached the chapel through the vestry route just before the outer service closures began.

Calienne took one look at Edrik's arm.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to."

"I need water taken to Bram."

"No."

"That is becoming a popular answer."

Hessa handed the payment list to Ysabet.

Ysabet read it without touching the ink.

"Where did this come from?"

"Anja took it from a man searching the works passages," Edrik said.

"Chain?"

"Anja to Bram. Bram to me. Me to Hessa. Hessa to you."

Ysabet nodded.

She wrote the chain on the back.

Selise said, "The seal register can authenticate Aven's real impressions against the false transfer order."

Edrik looked at her.

"You have it?"

She touched the satchel.

"Yes."

"Then the chapel has everything everyone wants."

Calienne said, "Thank you for noticing."

The gate bell sounded again.

Hessa went pale.

Edrik looked toward the nave.

"How many exits?"

"That remain usable?" Calienne asked. "One I trust. Two I don't."

"How many wounded?"

"Twenty-three."

"How many can walk?"

"Fourteen. Maybe sixteen if being shot at improves medicine."

Ysabet understood before the others.

"No."

Edrik looked at her.

"No what?"

"Whatever you are deciding."

"I'm deciding we cannot leave twenty-three wounded people beside the seal register and the surrender terms when Rusk is closing the castle."

Calienne folded her arms.

"They are under my protection."

"Rusk forged Aven's order to move Mera."

That silenced the room.

Selise said, "If he gets the current register, he can improve the next forgery."

"Yes."

"And the surrender copy?"

"Proof Aven made terms he may no longer be able to enforce."

Ysabet closed her eyes.

"What do you propose?"

Edrik looked at Hessa.

"The culvert."

Hessa shook her head.

"Too narrow for stretchers."

"Not the wounded. The records."

Calienne said, "And then?"

"Then you remain a chapel full of wounded people instead of a chapel full of wounded people sitting on evidence."

"And Bram?"

Edrik lifted the water skin.

"I take this back."

Calienne swore.

It was surprisingly inventive for a physician.

---

They had one chance to move the papers.

Rusk's men were concentrating on gates and known service routes. The village culvert emerged below Ardel's roadblock through a drainage arch clogged with roots and old mortar. Hessa could crawl it. So could Selise.

Ysabet refused to send the original accession ledger through mud.

Then she looked at the wounded.

She wrapped it in altar linen.

"History can survive dirt."

Selise took the seal register, accession ledger, surrender copy, payment list and a written account of Pellon's missing wage pages.

She did not take the family papers.

She did not take the old genealogies.

She did not take anything merely because it was precious.

Calienne watched her pack.

"If you are caught?"

Selise looked at Ysabet.

Ysabet answered.

"She says nothing she does not know."

Selise almost smiled.

Their trust was not repaired.

It was functioning.

That was different.

Hessa led her into the vestry.

Edrik waited until the stone closed behind them.

Then he picked up the water.

Calienne blocked the door.

"You are bleeding through my bandage."

"It is a very judgmental bandage."

"Bram has eleven people."

"Twelve."

"You counted yourself."

"Yes."

"You may not get back."

"I know."

"Then stay. The king is coming."

Edrik looked toward the sound of the gate chains.

"That is why I can't."

---

Luceran saw Edselsto before sunset.

The mountain rose dark above the pines, its towers catching the last light.

For one heartbeat it looked exactly as it had when he left.

Then Rhyse pointed.

"No lower-road traffic."

Miren saw it too.

The usual carts were gone.

No workers moved through the lower approach. Ardel's stone obstruction was visible from the switchback, but beyond it the castle gate showed no ordinary receiving queue.

Luceran's thirty-four riders slowed without being ordered.

They had begun the return with more.

Horses, messages, detached scouts, the Red Ford settlement and simple exhaustion had reduced them.

Luceran touched the boot leather inside his coat.

**EDSELSTO ATTACKED. RETURN. TRUST BLACK PINE.**

He could see his home.

He still did not know who inside it was alive.

A rider appeared below the Kaelmont stones.

Ardel.

She did not ride toward him.

She stopped in the road and waited for him to enter the ground she controlled.

Luceran almost smiled.

Even now.

Especially now.

Rhyse raised a hand and the column halted.

Luceran rode forward with Miren and Rhyse.

Ardel's face told him before her mouth did.

"It's taken."

"By whom?"

"Depends which room you ask."

"Ardel."

"Sir Aven Marr claims an emergency council. Cale Rusk has men Aven does not command. They have the crown."

Luceran stopped breathing.

Miren's hand touched his wrist.

Not to comfort him.

To keep him present.

"Who is alive?"

"Bram was alive after the crown stair fell. Wounded. Edrik alive then. Anja alive then. Calienne has wounded. Ysabet and Selise made the chapel. I have not seen Tolland since."

Luceran absorbed each name separately.

"Rhyse?"

"I'm here."

Luceran had not realized he had said it aloud.

Rhyse looked at the walls.

"Gates?"

"Closing."

As if the castle wished to confirm her, the distant outer chains began to move.

Luceran watched his own portcullis descend.

No magic.

No curse.

Men on a winch.

He had never hated anything so ordinary.

---

Edrik found the eastern route blocked.

He tried receiving.

Blocked.

He tried the charcoal stair.

Two men.

He backed away before they saw him and returned to the flour passage.

The water skin dragged at his shoulder.

He could abandon it.

That was the sensible choice.

Bram needed water.

Mikel needed water.

Eleven other people needed water.

Edrik had spent a year learning that kings were not kingdoms. Castles were not kingdoms either. Kingdoms were people carrying stupidly heavy things because somebody on the other side of a wall needed them.

He took the old linen lift.

The lift had not worked since Luceran arrived.

The shaft did.

Edrik tied the water skin to his belt and climbed.

Halfway up, voices entered below.

"There."

He climbed faster.

The old rope burned his palms.

A bolt struck the timber beside his head.

He reached the upper opening and rolled through.

The warped laundry panel was twenty yards away.

Anja opened it.

"Edrik."

"Take this."

He shoved the water skin at her.

She took it.

Then saw the men entering the passage behind him.

"Inside."

Edrik looked at the panel.

Twelve people behind it.

Bram fevered.

Mikel fevered.

The passage had one turn before the hidden gallery.

If he entered, the pursuers would see exactly where.

"No."

Anja understood.

Her face changed.

"No."

"Shut it."

"Edrik."

"Shut the fucking panel."

Bram's voice came from inside.

"What's happening?"

Edrik smiled despite himself.

"Tell him I found water."

Anja grabbed his coat.

He pulled free.

There was another route at the end of the passage: a narrow stair rising toward the old family corridor.

It did not lead to safety.

It led away.

That was enough.

Edrik ran.

He shouted as he did.

"Here!"

The men followed.

Anja stood frozen for one second.

Then she closed the panel.

Inside, Bram heard the latch settle.

He knew.

He did not say Edrik's name.

Not yet.

---

Edrik reached the family corridor with six men behind him.

He could have surrendered.

That was the alternative.

Aven's terms existed. Some coup soldiers had honored them. Calienne's chapel had survived because enough people still recognized rules. Edrik could put down his sword, name himself, and gamble that he would become a prisoner rather than a corpse.

He stopped.

The men behind him stopped too.

One said, "Drop it."

Edrik looked at the sword in his hand.

He thought about sleeping through an afternoon.

He thought about Luceran complaining that Edrik insulted him because proximity had destroyed proper respect.

He thought about Bram saying bring him back.

He thought about the hidden panel twenty yards behind six armed men who now believed the fugitive they wanted was standing in front of them.

If he surrendered, they would search backward.

If he ran farther, they would follow.

The choice was real.

That was what made it difficult.

He ran.

Not toward the laundry.

Away.

Up.

The family corridor had three doors and one narrow turn where two men could not fight abreast.

Edrik made them pay for the turn.

The first man took a cut across the hand and dropped his weapon.

The second hit Edrik in the ribs.

The third drove him backward.

Edrik did not win.

He was not supposed to.

He only had to remain more interesting than the wall behind them.

He reached the old portrait landing.

There was nowhere else to go.

Below, more boots arrived.

The man with the wounded hand said, "Enough."

Edrik leaned against the stone.

"Probably."

"Drop it."

He could still surrender.

Even now.

He looked through the arrow slit.

Far below, on the lower road, riders stood beyond Ardel Kaelmont's stones.

One horse carried a dark-cloaked figure he knew by posture before he could know by face.

Luceran.

Home.

Edrik laughed.

The soldiers looked at one another.

"What's funny?"

"You're late."

He raised the sword.

They killed him.

---

The gates closed before Luceran reached them.

The sound traveled down the mountain as iron striking stone.

Nobody in his party spoke.

Ardel had received Selise and Hessa minutes earlier through the culvert. The records lay wrapped in muddy altar linen beneath a Kaelmont guard's cloak.

She did not give them to Luceran immediately.

First she told him Edrik was dead.

"Seen?"

"Yes."

"By whom?"

"A kitchen boy forced to carry bodies from the family corridor. He knew Edrik."

Luceran's face did not change.

Miren knew him well enough to be frightened by that.

"Body?"

"Inside."

"How?"

"Six men chased him from the laundry side. He ran toward the family corridor. He fought at the portrait landing."

Rhyse looked at Ardel.

"Why?"

"We don't know."

Luceran did.

Not the facts.

The shape.

Edrik knew those routes.

Edrik did not get lost.

If he ran away from somewhere, he had wanted pursuit to go with him.

"Who was behind him?"

Ardel's expression shifted.

"I don't know."

"Find out."

"Luceran."

"Find out."

Miren stepped closer.

"She will."

He looked at the gate.

For a moment he was no longer looking at architecture.

He was looking at every breakfast Edrik had interrupted, every corridor in which he had appeared exactly when Luceran wanted privacy, every joke delivered half a breath beyond propriety, every door opened before Luceran noticed it needed opening.

A king acquired attendants.

A man acquired habits.

Grief entered through the habits.

"Majesty," Rhyse said quietly.

Luceran turned.

Rhyse's eyes were wet.

That almost broke him.

Almost.

"What do we have?"

Ardel answered because practical questions were mercy.

"The crown is inside. Aven sealed it. Rusk's people are using forged authority. Pellon Rusk is missing and likely dead. Washhouse records were cut. We have a payment list that may connect him to whoever bought household information, but it proves less than we want. Aven's surrender terms survive. The current seal register survives. Ysabet saved the accession ledger."

"Where?"

"Here."

"Who else?"

"Calienne's chapel has wounded from both sides. Bram may be concealed somewhere near the eastern laundry works. Anja may be with him. We do not know."

Luceran looked again at the closed gates.

"Then we do not assault tonight."

Rhyse exhaled.

Miren closed her eyes briefly.

Ardel said, "Good."

Luceran turned on her.

"Do not mistake me."

"I didn't."

"There are people inside."

"I know."

"Edrik is inside."

"I know."

"And if I put ladders on those walls tonight, Rusk gets to choose where every defender stands while I choose which of my own people die below them."

"Yes."

The word hurt because it was true.

Luceran looked at Rhyse.

"Outer camps. No fires within bowshot. Count everyone. Find every Black Pine scout. Establish signals with the village. Nobody approaches a gate alone."

Rhyse nodded.

"Miren."

"I'm here."

"Get the records copied. Three copies. Separate hands. Ardel keeps originals until we have somewhere safer."

Ardel raised an eyebrow.

"You are learning."

"I am in a poor mood."

"I noticed."

Luceran looked toward the castle again.

Seven small lights were invisible from here.

He did not think of them.

He thought of a man who had wanted one afternoon of sleep.

---

In the laundry gallery, Anja gave Bram water.

He drank too quickly and coughed.

"Where is he?"

Anja did not answer.

Bram looked at the closed panel.

"Where is Edrik?"

Mikel began to cry before she spoke.

Not loudly.

That was worse.

Anja sat beside Bram.

"He drew them away."

"Did you see him die?"

"No."

"Then you don't know."

"I heard the fighting."

"You don't know."

"No."

Bram took another drink.

His hand shook so badly that water spilled down his shirt.

"He said he would come back."

Anja looked at him.

"No," she said. "He said that was the plan."

Bram closed his eyes.

He remembered the exact words.

That was worse too.

Outside the hidden wall, searchers passed twice.

They did not stop.

Edrik had bought them that.

Not victory.

Not rescue.

Time.

Water.

Ignorance.

A king at the road with evidence still alive inside his kingdom.

It was enough to matter.

It was not enough to make dying fair.

---

Night settled over Edselsto.

Below the walls, Luceran's returning column became a camp.

Inside, Sir Aven Marr learned that Edrik Saye had been killed after refusing surrender at the portrait landing.

He demanded the names of the men involved.

Cale Rusk asked why.

Aven answered, "Because names improve responsibility."

At the chapel, Calienne counted twenty-three patients and discovered she still had twenty-three.

At the village road, Ardel's clerks copied the payment list, the surrender terms, the seal register entries and the account of Pellon's missing pages.

In the hidden laundry gallery, Bram's fever climbed, but the water lasted.

Mera Doss remained locked in the same room because the guard who had refused the forged transfer refused three more attempts to move her without Aven standing in front of him.

Ysabet Fenn slept for eleven minutes with her head against the chapel wall.

Selise Varo did not sleep at all.

And outside his own castle, Luceran sat beneath a pine with Edrik's absence occupying the space beside him like another person.

Miren found him there.

She did not tell him Edrik had died bravely.

She did not tell him Edrik had died for him.

Neither of them knew enough to say either thing.

She sat.

After a long time Luceran said, "He was supposed to sleep."

Miren understood.

"I know."

"He complained about it for three days."

"I know."

"I told him he could have the whole afternoon."

"I know."

Luceran pressed both hands against his face.

The king disappeared for a while.

Miren let him.

Above them, the gates remained closed.

All seven ancestral lights remained lit.

Edselsto did nothing.

Its king had come home.

His people had not yet let him in.
