# Season One, Episode 11 — The Hunt

The stag had been seen three times in six days.

That was enough to make it royal.

Luceran learned this from Bellisar's master of horse, who delivered the information as though the animal had petitioned for recognition.

"Sixteen points," Master Oren Tal said.

"Antlers do not have points."

"These do."

"They have tines."

"Sixteen."

Luceran looked at Rhyse.

Rhyse was reading a route sheet.

"Do not involve me."

Lady Ardel Kaelmont said, "He is afraid of the stag."

Rhyse did not look up.

"I am afraid of everyone who thinks a royal hunt is recreation."

Montegrain, already dressed for riding, said, "It is recreation."

Rhyse folded the route sheet.

"It is thirty mounted people, twelve beaters, six hounds, three pack animals, servants, weapons, excited nobles, villagers who know shortcuts, courtiers who do not, and His Majesty moving through trees where half the kingdom can plausibly claim not to have known he would be."

Montegrain considered.

"So recreation with paperwork."

"Exactly."

Luceran had wanted the hunt because Edselsto had begun to feel like a construction site with a throne in it.

That was still true.

It was also true that Bellisar had supplied horses, Kaelmont wanted him to see the north road grade, Pell wanted several provincial guests included, Rhyse wanted to test the new escort pattern away from walls, and half the court wanted to know who rode beside the king when seating charts could no longer decide proximity.

By breakfast, the stag was the least political creature involved.

---

Rhyse refused to let them simply ride out.

There were three routes.

The main party would leave through the lower north gate, descend past the upper village turn, and enter the west pine track.

A smaller beater line would approach from the old charcoal cut.

The funeral grade through the east pines would remain unused except as a rally and withdrawal route if weather, injury, or lost horses forced them east.

"Why not use it?" Luceran asked.

"Because we just learned it is unguarded."

"Wouldn't using it teach us something?"

"It would teach everyone watching that the king uses it."

Mara Henn, standing beside a rough charcoal map pinned to a stable door, said, "They already know people use it."

Rhyse nodded.

"People. Not the king."

That distinction settled it.

The rally points were a stone sheepfold near the west ridge, the charcoal crossing, and the lower village pump.

Bram carried a horn.

Edrik carried another.

The beaters knew three calls: hold, turn, and return.

The mounted party knew two: rally and emergency.

Montegrain asked why nobles received fewer instructions than beaters.

"Experience," Rhyse said.

Ardel laughed.

Master Oren Tal did not.

He had spent the morning assigning horses and had discovered that several noble guests believed riding competence was hereditary.

It was not.

Lady Ardel received a rangy gray mare named Kite.

Miren chose a chestnut gelding called Morrow.

Montegrain was given a black horse with one white sock and an expression of personal disappointment.

Luceran's mount was a bay named Sable.

Bram said, "That's a confusing name."

Luceran looked at the bay.

"Why?"

"Because he's brown."

Master Tal looked offended.

"He is dark bay."

"He is brown."

"Bram."

"Captain."

"Stop discussing the king's horse."

"Yes, Captain."

Sable remained brown.

---

Tessa Rane did not attend.

She had work.

This surprised three courtiers and no villagers.

The lower well lining was being reset. The old mill spring inspection had been scheduled for the same morning. Tessa sent Mara with the hunt only because Rhyse had requested someone who knew the west and east tracks and because Mara's Crown mapping engagement covered route work.

Tessa herself went downhill with two well masons, Elder Sovan, and a boy carrying measuring cord.

Luceran heard about this while mounting.

"She chose the well?"

Mara tightened her saddle strap.

"The village would prefer drinking."

"I know."

"You asked."

"I was making conversation."

"Then yes. She chose the well."

Luceran looked toward the road.

The court was going hunting.

Government was going somewhere else.

He was beginning to understand that this was normal.

---

They entered the pines just after the morning mist lifted.

The forest swallowed ceremony quickly.

Branches did not care who held precedence.

Mud did not recognize heraldry.

A Bellisar cousin named Jeron attempted to ride beside Luceran and discovered that the track narrowed around an exposed root shelf.

Miren passed him without comment.

Ardel did comment.

"Excellent flank work."

Jeron said something impolite.

Ardel smiled.

Luceran heard it and pretended not to.

The first hour was easy.

The beaters moved somewhere downslope.

Dogs sounded intermittently through the trees.

Mara rode near Rhyse rather than the king.

That was her assigned function.

She pointed out a drainage hollow that became impassable after heavy rain, a charcoal burner’s abandoned shelter, and a fork where the old boundary track had been moved around a fallen stone twenty years earlier.

Rhyse asked questions.

Bram remembered answers.

Edrik marked two turns with narrow cloth ties that would be collected on the return.

Nobody found the stag.

They found six deer.

Apparently none had enough points to become royal.

---

At the west ridge the court stopped.

Below them, the country opened into a long descending valley.

The old north road could be seen through breaks in the trees, pale where carts had cut the surface.

Ardel dismounted.

"This is what my aunt wants you to understand."

Luceran joined her.

"The road?"

"The grade."

She pointed.

The current road crossed the lower slope twice to avoid a rocky shoulder.

"Kaelmont's proposal cuts there."

"Through whose land?"

Ardel looked at him.

"You have been spending too much time with Mara."

"Answer."

"Mostly Crown waste. One disputed grazing strip. We sent the papers."

"Mostly?"

"There is always a mostly."

Luceran looked at the line.

The shorter route was obvious from above.

So was the quarry face beyond it.

"You brought me hunting to sell a road."

"No."

Ardel leaned closer.

"I came hunting because I like riding, because I wanted to see whether you fall off a horse, and because if you happened to see the road my house wants built, I would not blindfold you."

Luceran laughed.

Miren, ten paces away, heard him.

Her expression did not sour.

It did change.

Ardel noticed that too.

Nobody said anything.

That was courtship at Edselsto now: three people accurately noticing the same thing and refusing to become idiots about it.

---

The horn sounded once.

Hold.

Rhyse raised a hand.

The party stopped.

A second call came from farther south.

Wrong direction.

Mara turned in the saddle.

"That's not our beater line."

Rhyse's hand closed.

"How sure?"

"The south hollow carries sound uphill. That came east."

Bram said, "Funeral grade?"

"Near it."

Rhyse looked at Edrik.

"Count."

Edrik counted mounted heads.

All present.

Rhyse signaled two guards.

"With me."

Luceran said, "I'm coming."

"No."

"I am the reason you're investigating."

"Which is why you're staying here."

Luceran hated that this was correct.

"Take Bram."

"Bram stays."

"Why?"

"Because if I disappear, he knows the return plan."

Bram's face became very serious.

Luceran nodded.

Rhyse left with Edrik, Mara, and two guards.

The court waited.

That was harder than riding.

---

They heard dogs again.

Then a shout.

Then nothing.

Montegrain moved closer to Luceran.

"Do not do the thing."

"What thing?"

"The thing where you decide waiting is cowardice."

"I wasn't."

"You were looking at the path."

"I can look at paths."

"You look at paths differently before making bad decisions."

Miren said, "He's right."

Ardel said, "He is."

Luceran looked between them.

"This is intolerable."

"Good," Montegrain said.

Ten minutes passed.

Then fifteen.

Bram kept checking the ridge behind them.

Master Tal checked the horses.

One of the Pell guests asked whether they should return.

Bram said, "Not yet."

The guest looked at Luceran.

Luceran said, "Bram has the return plan."

That mattered.

Bram heard it.

So did everyone else.

Five minutes later Rhyse returned.

He was carrying a horn.

Not his.

---

The horn was cheap.

Goat horn, brass rim, leather loop.

No badge.

No maker's mark visible.

Rhyse set it on a flat stone.

"Found where?"

Luceran asked.

"East slope. Twenty yards above the funeral grade."

"Person?"

"Gone."

"Tracks?"

Mara said, "One horse. Maybe two people at different times. Ground is bad."

Rhyse continued.

"Someone gave a hold call."

"Why?"

"To see what we did."

Luceran looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"I don't."

"Then don't say it like you do."

Rhyse nodded once.

"Fair."

Mara crouched beside the horn.

"They were above the route."

"Could be a charcoal burner?" Montegrain asked.

"With a hunting horn?"

"A hunter."

Mara looked toward the east trees.

"Could be."

Rhyse said, "Except the call matched ours."

Luceran felt the cold arrive slowly.

"Exactly?"

Bram answered.

"Close enough."

"Could they have heard it this morning?"

Rhyse said, "We used the calls at the stable yard."

Miren looked back toward the castle, invisible through the trees.

"So anyone nearby could learn them."

"Yes."

Ardel said, "Then this proves nothing about the court."

"No," Rhyse said.

"And nothing about a conspiracy."

"No."

Luceran appreciated both answers.

"What does it prove?"

Rhyse looked at the horn.

"Someone not in our party was close enough to imitate our signal and interested enough to do it while the king was in the woods."

That was enough.

---

The hunt could have ended there.

Rhyse wanted it to.

Luceran almost agreed.

Then Master Tal said, "If we return immediately, whoever sounded it learns one false call turns the king around."

Rhyse looked at him.

The master of horse shifted.

"I am not advising security."

"You just did."

"I advise horses. The horses are fine."

Mara said, "And if they wanted to pull you east?"

Everyone looked at her.

She pointed to the map.

"False hold from the funeral grade. You send men to look. If the whole party follows, you move toward the unguarded route."

Rhyse's jaw tightened.

Luceran said, "You think it was bait."

"I think if I wanted to know whether the king uses that road, I might make him curious about it."

Montegrain said, "I dislike competent villagers."

Mara ignored him.

Rhyse considered.

Then he changed the hunt.

They would continue west.

Nobody would pursue the false signal.

Two additional guards would shadow the eastern flank from inside the main formation.

The beaters would be recalled and restarted on a shorter line.

The funeral grade would remain unused.

Luceran said, "And if someone is watching?"

"Then they learn we don't chase noises."

That seemed worth teaching.

---

They found the stag forty minutes later.

Or the stag found them.

It burst from a stand of young pine below the ridge with the hounds screaming behind it.

For one magnificent second every political concern disappeared.

The animal was enormous.

Master Tal shouted something.

Jeron Bellisar spurred too early.

His horse slipped.

Miren swore.

Ardel laughed.

Luceran went after the stag.

Rhyse shouted his name.

Not title.

Name.

That should have warned him.

The stag cut north.

Sable followed.

The forest narrowed.

Luceran ducked a branch, felt it tear at his coat, and heard hooves behind him.

Ardel.

Then Bram.

Then someone else.

The stag crossed a shallow stream.

Sable jumped.

Luceran landed badly and nearly lost a stirrup.

He recovered.

Ahead, the animal climbed toward a broken stone wall.

Mara had mentioned that wall.

Boundary track.

Beyond it, the ground fell sharply.

Luceran remembered too late.

The stag cleared the wall.

Sable tried to follow.

Bram hit Luceran's horse from the side.

Not hard.

Enough.

Sable swerved.

Luceran nearly came out of the saddle.

The stag vanished over the wall.

Luceran shouted something unroyal.

Bram shouted back.

"DROP!"

Luceran did not understand.

Then he saw the cord.

A thin rope ran between two trees at chest height on the far side of the wall.

Not old.

Not accidental.

Ardel hauled Kite sideways.

The fourth rider behind them did not.

Jeron Bellisar hit the cord.

He left the saddle.

---

The sound he made when he landed ended the hunt.

Luceran was off Sable before Rhyse arrived.

Jeron lay on his side, breathing.

That was the first good thing.

The second was that his neck moved.

The third was that he could swear.

Master Tal knelt beside him.

"Don't move."

"My shoulder."

"I know."

"It is broken."

"Probably."

"Excellent."

"Be quiet."

Bram cut the rope.

He did not pull it down.

He cut both ends beyond the knots and laid the whole span on the ground.

Rhyse arrived.

He looked at Jeron.

Then at the rope.

Then at Luceran.

"Who crossed first?"

"The stag."

"Then?"

"Me."

"Bram?"

"Behind."

"Ardel?"

"Behind Bram."

"Jeron?"

"Fourth."

Rhyse looked at the height.

The cord would have struck a mounted rider between chest and throat depending on horse and posture.

Luceran felt sick.

Mara arrived last.

She looked at the rope.

Then at the trees.

"That's new."

"How new?"

"Bark isn't grown around it."

Rhyse stared at her.

She sighed.

"Hours. Days. I don't know. Not winter."

"Could it be for game?"

Master Tal said, "Not at that height."

"Could it be a boundary line?"

Mara said, "Not tied like that."

Luceran asked the question nobody wanted.

"Was it for me?"

Rhyse said, "We do not know."

That answer saved the episode from becoming a lie.

---

Jeron's shoulder was dislocated, not broken.

Master Tal and a guard medic reset it beside the stream.

Jeron screamed.

Then vomited.

Then apologized for vomiting.

Nobody knew why.

His horse had a cut fetlock and would be led home.

The stag escaped.

Montegrain declared this evidence of treason by wildlife.

Nobody laughed.

They examined the rope.

Common hemp.

One end had been tied with a hitch Bram recognized from baggage work.

The other had a different knot.

Edrik found a boot mark in soft ground beyond the tree.

Mara found where a horse had stood farther upslope.

Rhyse found nothing that named anyone.

That mattered.

No badge.

No dropped letter.

No convenient confession.

Only placement.

A false horn call from the east.

A rope across the line a running stag might naturally take.

A mounted observer's trace upslope.

Someone had either prepared a dangerous hunting trick before the royal party arrived or had known enough about the hunt to place it where it might matter.

Luceran said, "Could this kill someone?"

The medic answered.

"Yes."

"Was it likely to?"

"Depends how fast."

Jeron, pale and furious, said, "Fast."

Luceran looked at Rhyse.

"Then this is not just observation."

Rhyse's expression was hard.

"No."

"Assassination?"

"Not enough evidence."

Ardel said, "A test can still draw blood."

Rhyse nodded.

"Yes."

---

They returned by the west track.

Not the funeral grade.

Jeron rode in a litter rigged between two pack horses because Master Tal refused to let him remount.

The court entered the village dirty, quiet, and hours earlier than expected.

That was how Tessa learned something had happened.

She was standing knee-deep beside the lower well arguing about stone.

She looked at the litter.

Then at Luceran.

"What happened?"

"Rope."

"Where?"

"West ridge."

Mara said, "Old boundary wall."

Tessa climbed out of the work trench.

"Whose?"

Nobody answered.

She looked at Mara.

Mara's expression changed.

"Vess."

Rhyse said, "You're sure?"

"The wall is Vess east line."

"The proposed observation post is also likely Vess."

"Yes."

Luceran felt every eye move.

There it was.

A connection.

Not proof.

He had just spent an episode learning the difference.

"Find the Vess family," he said.

Tessa said, "They're not hiding."

"Good."

"Old Bren Vess is at the mill spring."

Luceran blinked.

"The spring we're inspecting?"

"His granddaughter says it's theirs."

Of course she did.

---

Bren Vess was seventy, broad as a barrel, and furious before anyone accused him of anything.

He arrived at the castle with his granddaughter Lysa, Tessa, two village council members, and the deed box.

Not metaphorically.

An actual iron-bound box.

Rhyse had asked for Bren.

Bren had brought evidence.

"I hear someone put a rope on my trees."

Luceran said, "Possibly."

"Then why are your guards asking whether I own the trees?"

"Because we need to know whose ground it was."

Bren looked offended.

"Mine."

Lysa put the deed box on the table.

Avarren almost smiled.

Inside were tax receipts, marriage settlements, a boundary sketch, two water-use agreements, and a judgment from forty-three years earlier concerning grazing rights.

The wall belonged to Vess.

The spring probably did too, though the old water agreement complicated Crown use.

The charcoal crossing observation-post site was less clear because the boundary shifted around a landslide.

None of that answered the rope.

Rhyse asked, "Who knew the hunt crossed the west boundary?"

Bren shrugged.

"Everyone."

Luceran said, "Everyone?"

"The beaters were hired yesterday."

Lysa said, "Bellisar stable boys asked which track stayed dry."

Tessa added, "Kitchen bought travel bread."

Mara said, "I marked the route with Rhyse at the stable."

Bren looked at Luceran.

"You rode thirty people into a forest and thought it was secret?"

Luceran rubbed his face.

"No."

"Good."

Rhyse asked, "Anyone unusual on your land?"

Bren thought.

Lysa answered first.

"Two riders yesterday."

"Who?"

"Don't know."

"Colors?"

"Plain."

"Horses?"

"One gray. One red-brown."

"Doing what?"

"Asked whether the west track reached the north road."

"What did you tell them?"

"Yes."

Rhyse leaned forward.

"Anything else?"

"They asked whether the king hunted often."

The room changed.

Luceran said, "What did you say?"

Lysa looked at him.

"That you hadn't been here long enough for anyone to know."

"Did that seem strange?"

"Now it does."

"Then?"

"They bought apples."

Montegrain, at the wall, murmured, "Villainy."

Luceran gave him a look.

He stopped.

Rhyse asked Lysa to describe them.

She could.

Not perfectly.

One older.

One younger.

Both men.

Travel coats.

No visible livery.

The older rider had a damaged left glove.

The younger had a narrow silver clasp shaped like a leaf or flame.

Maybe.

She was not sure.

That uncertainty stayed in the record.

---

The rope itself produced the best clue.

Not from Rhyse.

Not from Luceran.

From Jory Fell.

He saw it because Bram carried it through the lower yard while Jory was moving cut timber.

"That's scaffold line."

Bram stopped.

"Rope is rope."

"No."

Jory took one end.

"This is Crown issue."

Rhyse was summoned.

Jory showed him the twist.

Three-strand hemp with a black tracer fiber.

Royal Works used it to distinguish measured scaffold and lifting line from household cordage.

"Could someone buy it?"

"Probably."

"Steal it?"

"Definitely."

"How much is missing?"

Jory laughed.

"You think anyone knows?"

Rhyse did not.

Jory stopped laughing.

"Ah."

Royal Works did know.

Eventually.

Not because its storehouse was perfect.

Because Derrin Voss had started recording rope issue after two coils vanished during the first roof work and he had been blamed for wasting material.

Three lengths were unaccounted for.

One had been signed to a mason.

One to the west roof crew.

One to a cart repair team.

The cart repair issue was the right length.

The signature belonged to a worker named Pellan Dorr.

Pellan Dorr existed.

He was currently asleep in a tent with a fever.

He had not signed anything for two days.

Someone else had used his name.

Avarren looked at the issue sheet.

"Who knew his name?"

Derrin said, "Half the yard."

"Who knew he was sick?"

"The other half."

Luceran felt the pattern forming.

Public hunt.

Public route.

Public supply yard.

Ordinary knowledge.

No impossible access.

No master spy.

Someone was using the fact that Edselsto was becoming crowded.

---

By evening, Rhyse had enough to change procedures and not enough to arrest anyone.

He hated that combination.

Luceran recognized it.

"What do you want?"

"Rope issue countersigned for security-sensitive uses."

Derrin objected.

"That will slow work."

"Yes."

"How much?"

"I don't know."

"Then no."

Rhyse looked at him.

Derrin held.

Luceran watched.

There it was again.

Someone who worked for the Crown refusing a Crown officer because the practical cost mattered.

They compromised.

Ordinary rope issue remained with Royal Works.

Long lengths leaving the yard after dusk required a second mark.

Lost line had to be reported by morning count.

No guard countersign unless the rope was being issued into a secured area.

Rhyse disliked it.

Derrin disliked it.

Therefore it was probably government.

The funeral grade received no permanent observation post yet.

Instead, Rhyse assigned rotating mounted passes through existing public tracks while Avarren, Mara, Tessa, and the Vess family sorted the boundary.

Bren Vess agreed to temporary observation from his own upper pasture for seven days.

For payment.

"How much?" Luceran asked.

Bren named a number.

Tessa winced.

Luceran said, "That is robbery."

Bren shrugged.

"You need the hill."

Luceran looked at Mara.

She smiled.

He paid.

---

Jeron Bellisar spent the evening with his arm bound to his chest and three members of his family arguing over whether the incident proved the king's security was incompetent or that Bellisar should receive a larger role in it.

Jeron told them all to leave.

Ardel visited anyway.

Miren sent broth.

Master Tal checked the injured horse twice.

Bram sat outside the guard office with the false horn and rope on a table, writing down everyone who had touched either after recovery.

Edrik went back to the west ridge with two villagers before the light failed.

Selise Varo copied the first incident statements.

She did not send them to her aunt.

She did send her aunt a letter saying work was busy and she had eaten badly.

Both facts were true.

---

Luceran found Miren in the family gallery after dark.

She was looking at a portrait that had not yet been rehung properly.

"Jeron?"

"Painful. Alive."

"Good."

She looked at him.

"You chased the stag."

"I did."

"Past the escort."

"Briefly."

"Toward a wall you had been told about."

"Yes."

"That was stupid."

"Yes."

Miren blinked.

"You are supposed to defend yourself."

"I nearly put my throat into a rope."

She came closer.

"Do you think it was meant for you?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

"Good?"

"That you don't know."

Luceran looked at her.

Miren touched the tear in his coat where the branch had caught it.

"Old you would have known by now."

"What would old me have known?"

"Whoever you were already angry with did it."

That was unfair.

It was also accurate enough to hurt.

Luceran looked away.

"I thought moving here would make it easier."

"I know."

"Fewer people. Fewer networks."

"I know."

"Now someone can learn my route because a stable boy asks which track is dry."

"Yes."

"Steal rope because a sick worker's name is common knowledge."

"Yes."

"Imitate a horn because we practiced in a yard."

"Yes."

He looked at her.

"How do you protect against that?"

"You don't protect against all of it."

"Rhyse would disagree."

"Rhyse protects against as much as he can. That is different."

Luceran exhaled.

Miren rested her forehead against his.

No audience.

Probably.

At Edselsto he was learning never to promise that.

"Do you still love me when I chase deer into traps?"

"No."

He pulled back.

She smiled.

"I love you despite that."

"Important distinction."

"Very."

He kissed her.

Briefly.

Not because the moment was politically useful.

Not because it was politically empty.

Because he wanted to.

When they separated, Miren said, "Ardel will hear."

"Probably."

"Are you going to panic?"

"No."

"Good."

"Are you?"

"A little."

That was honest enough.

---

Rhyse came for him before midnight.

Not because they had caught anyone.

They had not.

Because Edrik had returned from the ridge.

He had found the place where the observer's horse had stood.

Under a flat stone nearby was a scrap of waxed cloth.

Inside it were three charcoal marks.

A circle.

A line.

Another circle.

Luceran stared.

"What does it mean?"

Rhyse said, "I don't know."

"Route marks?"

"Maybe."

Mara had not recognized them.

Neither had the Vess family.

Master Tal thought they might represent horses.

Avarren thought they might be nothing more than tally marks.

Edrik had a different idea.

"Positions."

Luceran looked at him.

Edrik put three cups on the table.

"First circle. Main party at the ridge."

He moved one.

"Line. Escort stretched on the track."

He moved the last.

"Second circle. Party after the false call."

Rhyse said, "That is speculation."

"Yes."

"Useful speculation."

"Maybe."

Luceran looked at the cloth.

No names.

No house.

No crest.

No prophecy.

No convenient answer.

Only evidence that someone had been there before them or while they moved.

Watching.

Learning.

Testing what happened when a signal came from the wrong direction.

Testing how quickly guards peeled away.

Testing whether the king chased.

Maybe testing whether a rope would be noticed before someone hit it.

Luceran said, "We keep this quiet."

Rhyse nodded.

"From whom?"

Luceran thought about Episode 10's table.

Reasons people might listen.

"Not everyone."

Rhyse waited.

"People who need it for their work get the facts they need. No invented story. No accusation."

"Good."

"Jeron's family?"

"They know enough to demand an explanation."

"Give them one."

"We don't have one."

"Then tell them that."

Rhyse looked almost pleased.

"Better."

Luceran hated when people said that.

---

The stag survived.

Jeron survived.

The court did too.

By morning, three versions of the story had already reached the village.

In one, assassins had attacked the king.

In another, Jeron Bellisar had fallen off his horse while drunk.

In the third, Luceran had personally strangled a conspirator with the rope.

Bram heard that one at breakfast.

He stared at his porridge.

"I was there."

The kitchen maid shrugged.

"People improve things."

"Why?"

"They get boring otherwise."

Bram considered the previous day.

"It was not boring."

"No," she agreed. "But you didn't strangle anyone."

He could not argue with that.

Above them, the castle remained stone.

Below them, the village opened its stalls.

At the lower well, Tessa discovered the north lining would require more stone than estimated.

At the old mill spring, Bren Vess's granddaughter arrived with another water agreement Avarren had not seen.

At the stables, Master Tal wrote a new rule about chasing quarry beyond marked boundaries.

At Royal Works, Derrin counted rope.

In the archive, Selise filed statements under SECURITY — HUNT — UNRESOLVED.

Rhyse put the false horn in a locked cabinet.

Luceran went to court.

Seven candles burned.

None went out.

Someone living had tested the king's security.

That was frightening enough.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 11 completes the hunt/security test while preserving uncertainty about sponsor and motive.

### Durable changes
