# Nowhere King — Season One, Episode Five
## Rooms Are Power

**Status:** Committed episode prose  
**Season:** One — The Return to Edselsto  
**Episode:** 05 of 32  
**Reveal ceiling:** Political and material court-building. Family wing remains closed except for already established approaches. Seven candles remain lit. No confirmed supernatural behavior.  
**Primary dramatic purpose:** Begin Luceran's real education in creating a court. Friendly people solve genuine problems, receive reasonable favors, and thereby begin becoming a political bloc. Simultaneously turn the neglected village below Edselsto into a crude royal works settlement whose tents, wells, smithy, storage ground, roads, ruins, and labor are inseparable from patronage.

---

Luceran discovered the first law of his new capital before breakfast.

The better rooms had already become hereditary.

He learned this because Lord Montegrain was standing in the corridor outside Luceran's chamber, barefoot, carrying a cup of something hot, while Mara Kest explained to a furious gentleman in a fur-lined traveling coat that no, he could not have Montegrain's room.

"It is the second chamber from His Majesty."

"Yes," Mara said.

"My house has served the Crown for three hundred years."

"Then it has survived worse."

"Madam—"

"Housekeeper."

"I beg your pardon?"

"If you intend to shout at me, use the correct office."

Montegrain drank.

Luceran stopped in his doorway.

Bram, already posted outside, said quietly, "Morning, Majesty."

The gentleman turned so quickly that his coat swung around him.

"Your Majesty."

Luceran recognized him after a moment: Baron Pellisar Pell, a distant relation through one of the Small Crown's innumerable branches. Not important enough to command policy, not unimportant enough to ignore, and possessed of the extraordinary Pell family talent for appearing anywhere a royal household might accidentally create an office.

"Baron."

Pell bowed.

"I arrived before dawn. I would not trouble Your Majesty over accommodations, but Lady Pell is expected tomorrow, my secretary has the dispatch chests, and I understood the near chambers were being reserved for senior household and close kin."

Luceran looked at Montegrain.

Montegrain looked down at his bare feet.

"I am senior at something."

"Annoyance," Luceran said.

"Distinguished service."

Pell smiled because everyone liked Montegrain when he was being funny.

That was part of the problem, though Luceran did not yet know it.

"Which room is the baron asking for?" Luceran asked.

Mara pointed at Montegrain's door.

"That one."

"Why?"

Pell answered before Mara could.

"It adjoins the king's corridor."

Luceran waited.

Pell waited.

"And?"

Pell's smile changed by less than a finger's width.

"It is convenient."

Montegrain coughed into his cup.

Luceran looked at Mara.

"Is there another dry room?"

"Six."

"Then give him one."

"Which?"

"Whichever is best."

Mara's eyes narrowed.

Avarren's voice came from behind Luceran.

"There."

Luceran turned.

Avarren stood inside his room holding three folded letters and looking offensively awake.

"What?"

"You just did it."

"Did what?"

"Created rank."

Luceran stared at him.

Avarren pointed down the corridor.

"Montegrain keeps the second chamber from your door. Pell receives the best remaining chamber because he asked in your hearing. Everyone who arrives today will learn both facts before dinner."

Pell looked politely at the floor.

Luceran looked at him.

"Is that true?"

Pell considered lying.

Luceran saw him consider it.

That was new.

"Yes, Majesty."

"You wanted the room because it is close to me."

"I wanted the room because people who need Your Majesty will pass it."

There was no shame in the answer.

Luceran almost respected that.

"And what would you do with that?"

Pell smiled.

"Be useful."

Montegrain lifted his cup toward him.

"I like him."

"Of course you do."

Luceran looked at Mara.

"Give him the third-best room."

Pell blinked.

"Majesty?"

"The best remaining one is now political. Apparently. I haven't decided what politics it is yet."

Mara's mouth twitched.

"Third-best."

"Dry?"

"Yes."

"Fireplace?"

"Mostly."

Pell said, "Mostly?"

"It contains one. Whether it agrees is between you and the chimney."

Pell bowed.

"I am honored."

Luceran watched him go.

"He is not honored."

Avarren handed him the letters.

"He is delighted."

"I gave him a worse room."

"Personally."

Luceran looked down the corridor after Pell.

Montegrain drank again.

"Home."

Luceran took his cup away.

---

By eight in the morning, three more people wanted rooms.

By nine, two wanted offices.

By ten, somebody wanted a staircase.

The staircase belonged to no one.

This did not prevent Lady Serise Bellisar's factor from asking that her household be permitted to use it exclusively between the lower court and the east gallery because Bellisar had sent twenty-four remounts, six pack horses, two farriers, and enough oats to keep the royal stables from becoming a famine with hooves.

Luceran knew Lady Serise.

She had taught him to ride a difficult mare when he was fourteen and had laughed when he fell off.

Her factor, Harl Ven, was a square man with red cheeks and the patient expression of someone accustomed to explaining horses to people who believed horses were furniture.

"Not exclusively," Luceran said.

"Naturally, Majesty."

"You just asked exclusively."

"I asked because if I asked for what I expected, I would have nowhere to retreat."

Luceran looked at Avarren.

Avarren said, "Do not look at me. I am enjoying this."

Harl continued.

"We need the east stair during morning feed and evening stable return. The lower passage is too narrow for tack trunks and the west route crosses the kitchen queue. Give us those hours and everyone else may use it the rest of the day."

Rhyse, who had been listening from beside the door, said, "No."

Harl turned.

"Captain?"

"East stair reaches the king's corridor."

"Through two doors."

"One of which doesn't latch."

Harl looked at Orven.

Orven nodded.

"Doesn't."

"Can it?"

"Eventually."

Luceran said, "How eventually?"

Orven looked at the ceiling as if asking the castle for patience.

"Iron strap. New catch. Carpenter. Smith."

"We have carpenters."

"We do not have a smith."

Harl Ven smiled.

"House Bellisar brought two farriers."

Everyone looked at him.

Harl spread his hands.

"They can make a latch."

Rhyse said, "If they make the latch and I approve the door, Bellisar gets the stair at feed and return. Not exclusive. Guards can close it whenever required."

Harl looked at Luceran.

Luceran said, "Done."

Harl bowed.

"Thank you, Majesty."

He left.

Luceran watched him go.

"That seemed reasonable."

Avarren nodded.

"It was."

"Why do you sound like that?"

"Because House Bellisar just turned two farriers into scheduled access to the corridor outside your rooms."

"For horses."

"Yes."

"Because the other route is bad."

"Yes."

"And Rhyse can close it."

"Yes."

Luceran waited.

Avarren smiled.

"Politics is irritating when everyone is behaving sensibly, isn't it?"

Luceran hated him a little.

---

The village looked worse in daylight.

Luceran had thought the rain had been unkind to it.

The rain had been charitable.

From the upper road he could see the three streets, the grass-grown third lane, the mill, the old shrine, perhaps thirty surviving roofs, and the remains of many more. Foundations lay beneath nettles and bramble. Roofless stone houses stood with trees growing through them. One building had only a chimney and half a wall. Another had become a goat pen because the goats were apparently less troubled by dynastic decline than anyone else.

The lower well had collapsed inward.

The upper well worked but had a cracked curb and muddy drainage.

The village green had already become a wagon yard.

Tents from the convoy stood in two crooked rows.

Men slept under carts.

Someone had tied three horses to the old memorial stone.

Orven saw that and shouted from halfway down the hill.

"Not there!"

A groom looked up.

"What?"

"That's a grave marker!"

The groom untied them immediately.

Luceran looked at Orven.

"Whose?"

"No idea."

"You just—"

"Still a grave marker."

Fair.

Mara had brought chalk boards from the castle. Olan Crewe had brought a hammer. Rhyse had brought two guards. Clavard had brought ledgers. Avarren had brought the expression of a man expecting entertainment.

Luceran had brought authority.

He was beginning to understand that authority was the lightest item among them.

The village council met them outside what had once been an alehouse.

There were four members because the fifth had died in winter and nobody had replaced him.

Orven introduced them without ceremony.

Tessa Rane, miller and council elder.

Old Marrec Voss, no relation to Derrin as far as either knew, who kept goats and remembered every boundary stone.

Lysa Dorn, who owned the surviving bakehouse.

And Perren Holt, whose family had once maintained the lower well.

Tessa Rane looked at the tents.

Then at the wagons.

Then at Luceran.

"How many more?"

Luceran looked at Clavard.

Clavard looked at Mara.

Mara looked at Rhyse.

Rhyse said, "People or horses?"

Tessa closed her eyes.

"Oh, gods."

Luceran almost laughed.

He did not.

"Several hundred eventually," he said.

Tessa opened her eyes.

"Eventually when?"

That was harder.

Avarren watched him.

Luceran said, "Not all at once. More workers first. Household after. Court as rooms become usable."

"Food?"

"Coming."

"From where?"

Clavard answered.

"Current stores from the convoy. Contracts are being arranged through Damaris and Weyn factors, and local purchasing where it doesn't strip the village."

Tessa looked at him.

"You know what stripping the village looks like?"

Clavard paused.

"No."

That answer pleased her.

"Good. Ask us before you decide you've avoided it."

Luceran looked at her.

"Done."

Avarren's eyes moved toward him.

Luceran saw it.

"What now?"

"Nothing."

"That was another thing, wasn't it?"

"Yes."

Tessa said, "What thing?"

Avarren answered.

"His Majesty just made the village council part of Crown purchasing practice at Edselsto."

Tessa looked at Luceran.

Luceran looked at Avarren.

"I said ask them."

"Yes."

"Because they know how much food exists."

"Yes."

Tessa folded her arms.

"I like this man."

"Everyone does when he is explaining my mistakes."

Avarren bowed to her.

"I have found my calling."

Perren Holt interrupted.

"If you're putting hundreds here, fix the lower well first."

Luceran turned.

"Before housing?"

"Before anything. Upper well can't carry that many. Lower one used to feed half the village. Wall slipped six years ago. We filled the mouth so children wouldn't fall in."

Olan asked, "Stone-lined?"

"Twenty feet. Then cut rock."

"Water?"

"Still there."

"How do you know?"

"You can hear it."

Olan looked at Derrin.

Derrin, who had come down behind the work crew, shrugged.

"I'll look."

Perren looked at him.

"You're a mason?"

"Yes."

"Then don't stand on the north edge."

Derrin stopped.

"Why?"

"Because you'll be in the well."

Derrin smiled.

"I like him too."

Luceran looked over the green.

"Where do we put people until we have houses?"

Mara pointed.

"Tents along the old south lane. Kitchens on the lee side. Latrines downhill and away from both wells."

Olan pointed somewhere else.

"Timber there."

Tessa said, "No."

Olan looked at her.

"Ground is flat."

"Floods."

"It isn't raining now."

"It will."

Orven nodded.

"She's right."

Olan looked offended by the weather personally.

"Where?"

Marrec Voss pointed toward a broad field beyond the ruined houses.

"Old common."

Tessa said, "Half."

Marrec looked at her.

"Need more than half."

"Half until we know whether the Crown intends to return it."

There it was.

Not hostility.

Not rebellion.

Memory.

Luceran looked at the field.

"What was it used for?"

"Grazing. Fair days. Timber sorting when the castle still bought timber."

"Who owns it?"

Tessa looked directly at him.

"Depends which dead king's charter you read."

Orven made a sound that might have been laughter.

Luceran rubbed his forehead.

"Half. For storage. Temporary. Mark the boundary. Nothing permanent without the council being heard."

Clavard immediately began writing.

Tessa watched him.

"He writes fast."

"That is why I keep him."

Clavard did not look up.

"I had assumed affection."

"Never."

"Cruel."

The village council smiled.

That mattered too.

---

The smithy began because a horse lost a shoe.

That was less dignified than the later histories would claim.

One of Bellisar's farriers, a woman named Sera Morcant, removed the damaged shoe beside the green, looked at the castle forge that Orven had declared unsafe, looked at the rain-dark sky, and asked whether anyone had an anvil.

Nobody did.

She swore.

Harl Ven said, "We brought the small one."

"Where?"

"Wagon nine."

"Wagon nine is still below the mill."

"Then apparently the horse waits."

Sera looked at him with deep contempt.

"You can explain that to the horse."

By noon, wagon nine had been unloaded on the green.

By one, Sera had commandeered the shell of an old stone shed whose roof had collapsed on one side.

By two, Jory Fell and Iven Marr had put a temporary lean-to over half of it.

By three, Corren Bale had declared the chimney salvageable.

By four, Sera had a fire.

Luceran arrived just as she struck the first hot iron.

The sound carried across the village.

People turned toward it.

Not because it was magical.

Because it was work.

Sera looked up.

"Majesty."

"You're Bellisar?"

"Morcant."

Luceran blinked.

Harl Ven, standing nearby, explained.

"Her mother is Morcant. Father served Bellisar stables. She apprenticed through the Copper Bell workshops, then took contract with us."

Luceran looked at Sera.

"So whose smith are you?"

Sera put the iron back in the coals.

"Mine."

Harl smiled.

"I told you she was good."

Luceran liked her immediately.

That would also matter.

"Can you keep this working?"

"If I get coal or charcoal, iron stock, another striker, proper tongs, dry cover, and people stop asking me to repair cooking pots while I'm shoeing horses."

Mara, behind Luceran, said, "You will repair cooking pots."

Sera looked at her.

"After horses."

"Before decorative ironwork."

"Obviously."

They regarded each other.

An alliance formed without Luceran's involvement.

Harl said, "House Bellisar can send charcoal."

Sera said, "House Falbrecht can send better charcoal."

Harl looked betrayed.

"We brought you here."

"You don't own trees."

Avarren, who had somehow appeared again, murmured, "And there is tomorrow's argument."

Luceran watched sparks rise into the gray air.

"Send for what she needs."

Harl said, "Bellisar can—"

Sera said, "Morcant can supply iron."

Clavard said, "The Crown can pay neither until I know the price."

Everyone stopped.

Clavard held up his ledger.

"I am delighted that the kingdom has discovered metallurgy. I still require numbers."

Luceran laughed.

Then he pointed at Sera.

"You run the smithy for now. Crown work first, village work second, private work if there is time. Harl gets his horse. Mara gets her pots. Rhyse gets his latches."

Sera nodded.

"Pay?"

Luceran looked at Clavard.

Clavard named a figure.

Sera named a larger one.

Clavard looked wounded.

They settled between them.

Luceran realized, only after walking away, that he had just appointed a Morcant-trained smith recommended by a Bellisar factor to control the first functioning forge in his new capital.

Nobody had plotted it.

That was the troubling part.

---

The rooms became worse after lunch.

House Edevane sent Luceran's cousin, Lady Miren Edevane, with four wagons of bedding, lamp oil, preserved meat, two clerks, six servants, and a letter that began with condolences and ended with an offer to advance money against future household appropriations.

Miren herself rode at the front.

Luceran had known her since childhood.

She was six years older, beautiful in the way royal portraits liked, and possessed of the unnerving ability to make a person feel clever while she was disagreeing with them.

She embraced him before anyone could decide whether protocol permitted it.

"You look terrible."

"Thank you."

"Your castle is worse."

"Thank you again."

"I brought blankets."

"I love you."

"I knew there was a price."

Montegrain kissed her cheek.

"Miren."

"You look worse than he does."

"I slept beautifully."

"Then it is your face."

Luceran laughed harder than the joke deserved.

He was happy to see her.

That was the beginning of it.

Miren's factor had already spoken to Mara before Miren finished removing her riding gloves.

Her clerks had found Clavard.

Her servants were helping unload bedding.

One of her men knew how to repair oil lamps.

Another had brought three coils of good rope because someone in the Edevane household had heard the castle wells were unreliable.

They were useful before they had been assigned anywhere.

So when Mara said, "East solar is dry, has two small bedchambers, and can take her household without putting servants in the hall," Luceran said, "Give it to Miren."

Avarren closed his eyes.

Luceran saw him.

"What?"

Miren looked between them.

"Oh, have I arrived during a lesson?"

"Apparently I am not allowed to put people in rooms."

"You are king. You may put anyone anywhere."

Avarren said, "That is the lesson."

Miren understood immediately.

Luceran hated that too.

"The east solar is good?"

Mara said, "Best suite currently habitable after His Majesty's corridor."

Miren looked at Luceran.

"Give me something worse."

He stared at her.

"Why?"

"Because I arrived with four wagons and hugged you in the courtyard. If I also take the best suite, by sunset I will be your favorite cousin and by tomorrow my father will have appointed himself your future regent in six people's imaginations."

"My father is dead. I am an adult. I don't need a regent."

"Imaginations are rarely constitutional."

Montegrain laughed.

Luceran looked at Mara.

"What else?"

"North guest pair. Smaller. Dry. Farther from you."

Miren said, "Perfect."

Luceran felt relieved.

Then Miren added:

"Put my clerks in the east solar."

Luceran stared at her.

She smiled.

"They brought the household credit books."

Avarren began laughing.

"I like her."

"Everyone likes everyone. This is becoming unbearable."

Miren touched Luceran's arm.

"That is because the people who dislike you haven't arrived yet."

For a moment, the humor left the corridor.

Then she squeezed his arm.

"Enjoy us while you can."

Luceran did.

---

By late afternoon, the old common had stakes around half its boundary.

The first tents stood in rows rather than wherever exhausted people had dropped them.

A ditch was being cut above the lower camp to divert runoff.

Perren Holt and Derrin Voss had opened the lower well far enough to confirm the lining had collapsed across one side but the water below remained clean.

Two village boys had been hired to carry messages between the castle and green because they could run the hill faster than clerks.

Lysa Dorn had doubled bread production and informed the Crown that this did not make flour appear by miracle.

Clavard sent a rider toward the nearest mill under Damaris influence.

Tessa Rane made him send a second message specifying that local grain purchases were not to exceed a price she considered survivable.

He argued.

She won.

Olan Crewe marked three roofless stone houses for salvage and two for repair.

Marrec Voss objected to one.

"Why?" Olan asked.

"Belongs to the Henn family."

"There is no Henn family."

"Not here."

"Where?"

"South."

"How far south?"

"Two days."

Olan looked at Luceran.

Luceran looked at Avarren.

Avarren raised both hands.

"Do not involve me."

Luceran turned back to Marrec.

"Can we use it?"

"Ask them."

"We need housing now."

"Then use the roofless cooper's house. Nobody owns that except weeds."

"Why?"

"Last owner died owing everyone."

Clavard said, "The Crown may own it."

Marrec said, "Then the Crown can finally get something useful out of him."

They used the cooper's house.

That decision cost an hour.

Luceran would once have considered the hour wasted.

Now he looked at the Henn ruin and understood that a wall was not merely a wall if somebody remembered whose grandfather had built it.

He was learning.

Slowly.

Painfully.

Mostly because people kept refusing to let him remain ignorant.

---

The first actual fight over repair priority happened at sunset.

It involved a roof, a secretary, and a latrine.

Baron Pell wanted the chamber above his newly assigned room repaired because water had begun dripping beside his secretary's document chest.

Olan wanted the same crew on the lower camp drainage before night rain.

Mara wanted them on a broken privy wall because sixty people had been using a temporary trench and she had reached the end of civilization.

Pell found Luceran in the courtyard.

"Majesty, I require only two men for perhaps an hour."

Olan, behind him, said, "Three hours."

Pell turned.

"You told my secretary one."

"Your secretary asked how long to patch the hole. You asked how long to stop the leak. Different question."

Pell looked at Luceran.

"My dispatches are getting wet."

Mara arrived.

"Move the chest."

Pell inhaled.

"Housekeeper—"

"Baron."

Luceran put up a hand.

"What happens if we don't fix the drainage?"

Olan said, "Tents flood if the rain is hard."

"Privy?"

Mara said, "You do not want me to describe it."

"Roof?"

Pell said, "Documents may be damaged."

Luceran looked at him.

Pell sighed.

"The chest can be moved."

"Move it. Drainage first. Privy second. Roof tomorrow."

Pell bowed.

"Of course."

He was disappointed.

Luceran could see it.

But he was not offended.

That mattered.

As Pell walked away, Olan said, "Good."

Luceran turned.

"You sound surprised."

"You gave him the room this morning."

"That doesn't mean his roof outranks sixty people sleeping in tents."

Olan shrugged.

"Now I know."

Luceran stared at him.

There it was again.

Not a decision about a roof.

A decision about what kind of king Olan thought he was.

And Olan would tell Jory.

Jory would tell Derrin.

Derrin would write home.

Mara would tell the kitchen.

Pell would write his family.

By morning, half the castle would know that Luceran had made a baron move a chest rather than flood the workers.

Luceran looked at Avarren, who was not there.

"Damn him."

Olan frowned.

"Who?"

"Nobody."

---

That night, the great hall had more tables.

Not better tables.

More.

Miren Edevane sat beside Montegrain because she had known him for years. Harl Ven sat near Rhyse because stable access had become security. Sera Morcant sat with Jory and Derrin because the smithy needed carpentry and masonry. Tessa Rane sat near Clavard because food purchasing had become village business whether the Crown liked it or not.

Nobody had designed the seating.

That was why Luceran noticed it.

Networks were forming faster than protocol.

Miren's clerk passed a note to Clavard.

Harl leaned over to ask Rhyse something.

Sera pointed at a bent hinge someone had brought her during dinner.

Mara took it away and told her to eat.

Montegrain was telling Tessa a story about Luceran falling into a fountain when he was twelve.

"I was pushed."

"You were showing off," Montegrain said.

"Those are not mutually exclusive," Miren added.

Tessa laughed.

Luceran looked around the table.

He liked this.

That was the dangerous truth.

Nobody was whispering about his parents.

Nobody was asking whether his grief proved instability.

Nobody was measuring how long he stayed in his rooms.

Nobody was sending servants to discover whether he had eaten.

These people were building something.

His something.

When Miren asked whether the Edevane clerks could remain in the east solar because it was close to the temporary archive, he said yes.

When Harl asked whether Bellisar's farriers could use the shed beside Sera's new forge, he said yes.

When Tessa asked whether the village council could keep one of the two runner boys on Crown pay because the council now needed messages carried too, he said yes.

When Olan asked whether the workers could take timber from a collapsed royal hunting lodge two miles down the old east track, Orven said it was Crown property and Luceran said yes.

Four reasonable decisions.

Four beneficiaries.

Four new expectations.

Avarren waited until the meal was nearly over.

Then he slid a scrap of paper toward Luceran.

Luceran opened it.

**Edevane — clerks / archive access**

**Bellisar — east stair / farrier yard**

**Morcant — forge / Crown iron work**

**Village council — purchasing voice / messenger wage**

**Crewe & recruited works — royal timber priority**

Luceran looked at him.

"What is this?"

"Your court."

"This is five lines."

"It is morning."

"It is night."

"Exactly."

Luceran looked down the table again.

Miren caught his eye and smiled.

He smiled back automatically.

Avarren said quietly:

"None of them have done anything wrong."

"I know."

"That is why you need to see it."

Luceran folded the paper.

"You think I should stop helping people who help me."

"No."

That surprised him.

Avarren leaned back.

"I think you should know when gratitude becomes architecture."

Luceran looked toward the hall doors.

Bram stood outside.

Beyond him, a corridor led to the east stair Bellisar would use tomorrow morning.

Beyond that, the courtyard.

The road.

The village.

The new forge.

The half-common already filling with timber and carts.

His capital was appearing one favor at a time.

"And what do I do about it?"

Avarren picked up his wine.

"Learn everyone's name."

"That is your advice?"

"For tonight."

"Tomorrow?"

"Learn what they want."

Luceran looked at the scrap again.

"And after that?"

Avarren drank.

"Eventually, learn what they think you want."

That was worse.

---

After dinner, Luceran walked down to the village again.

Edrik went with him this time.

No procession.

No Montegrain.

No Avarren.

Just a king, a guard, two lanterns, and the wet road.

The settlement below looked different already.

Not transformed.

That would have been absurd.

But occupied.

Cookfires burned between tents.

Someone had hung wet clothing on rope between two ruined walls.

The first storage stakes stood black against the field.

Three wagons had been unloaded into ordered piles rather than abandoned where they stopped.

The lower well had a timber barrier around it and a lantern marking the dangerous edge.

From the smithy came the orange pulse of Sera's forge.

Hammer on iron.

Pause.

Hammer.

Pause.

Luceran stopped.

"That sound is going to keep everyone awake."

Edrik said, "Better than a horse throwing a shoe tomorrow."

"You always ruin poetry."

"Was that poetry?"

"It was becoming poetry."

They continued.

Near the tents, Jory Fell sat on an overturned bucket writing by lantern light.

Luceran almost walked past.

Then he saw the page.

"Ellyn?"

Jory looked up sharply and started to rise.

"Stay."

Jory stayed.

"Yes, Majesty."

"Tell her you have a roof."

Jory looked toward the tent behind him.

"I don't."

Luceran followed his gaze.

"Right."

Jory smiled.

"I'll tell her I have work. She'll believe that."

Luceran looked at the letter.

"Children?"

"Fine when I left. Probably furious with me by now."

"Why?"

"I promised the little one I'd fix the wheel on her toy cart."

Luceran had no answer to that.

He had taken Jory from Moncler's construction site because he needed carpenters.

Reasonable.

Necessary, perhaps.

The toy cart remained broken.

Politics reached farther than rooms.

"When the next wagon goes south, send the letter. Royal post."

Jory hesitated.

"Majesty, that's—"

"A letter."

"Yes."

"Send it."

Jory nodded.

"Thank you."

Luceran walked on.

Edrik waited until they were out of earshot.

"That'll spread too."

Luceran looked at him.

"What will?"

"Workers getting royal post for home letters."

Luceran stopped.

Edrik stopped.

"You too?"

"Too what?"

"Everyone has become Avarren."

Edrik considered.

"Sounds serious."

Luceran laughed.

Then he looked back at Jory.

"Is it bad?"

"No."

"Then why mention it?"

Edrik shrugged.

"Because you'll have fifty letters tomorrow."

Luceran stared.

Edrik smiled.

"Rooms are power. Post is apparently power too."

"Go to hell."

"After shift, Majesty."

---

They climbed back after midnight.

At the family nave, light showed beneath the door.

Luceran stopped.

Edrik's hand moved toward his sword.

Then Orven came around the corner carrying a box of candles.

"You," Luceran said.

"Me."

"Why are you awake?"

"Because candles burn."

Luceran looked at the box.

"Replacing one?"

"Trimming."

"All seven?"

"All seven."

Luceran hesitated.

"Still lit?"

Orven gave him a strange look.

"Yes."

Nothing in the answer suggested mystery.

Luceran felt foolish for asking.

Good.

He preferred foolish to haunted.

Orven unlocked the nave.

Seven flames stood before the black altar.

All seven.

Luceran watched them for perhaps five seconds.

Then Orven said, "If you're going to stare, hold this."

He handed the king the candle box.

Luceran stared at him.

Orven had already gone to the first wick.

Edrik looked away because he was definitely laughing.

Luceran held the box.

Orven trimmed the candles.

The king of Embar-Zara assisted.

Nothing supernatural happened.

That, too, felt like progress.

---

In the morning there were fifty-three letters waiting for the royal post.

Edrik was unbearable about it.

And outside the lower gate, three new wagons had arrived.

One carried iron fittings under House Morcant seals.

One carried charcoal under House Falbrecht marks.

One carried a locksmith from the Guild of Keys who had heard, from someone who had heard from someone else, that the king's new castle had more doors than functioning locks.

Luceran stood above the gate and watched them climb.

Avarren joined him.

"Did you send for all of those?"

"No."

"Good."

Luceran looked at him.

"Why is that good?"

Avarren smiled.

"Because now we begin the next lesson."

Below them, the locksmith's wagon struck a rut and nearly lost a wheel.

Sera Morcant came out of the village forge, hammer in hand, to see what the noise was.

Harl Ven was already walking down from the stables.

Tessa Rane was crossing the green toward the newcomers.

Nobody waited for Luceran.

The court was beginning to move by itself.

He had wanted that.

He still wanted it.

He was only beginning to understand what it meant.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 05 begins Luceran's political education after the four-episode lead-in.

### Durable changes

- Emergency rooms become recognized political signals rather than mere shelter.
- Baron Pellisar Pell attempts to secure proximity to Luceran and receives a personally assigned but less prestigious chamber; even the refusal becomes favor because the king handled it personally.
- House Bellisar, through factor Harl Ven, receives scheduled practical use of the east stair in exchange for solving horse/farrier needs and helping repair its security latch; Captain Rhyse retains authority to close it.
- Lady Miren Edevane arrives as a genuinely affectionate royal cousin with bedding, oil, preserved food, clerks, servants, rope, and an offer of household credit. She deliberately avoids taking the best suite herself but places useful Edevane clerks in it near the archive, demonstrating sophisticated awareness of appearances and access.
- Luceran recognizes that friendly, reasonable assistance can produce factional advantage without conspiracy.
- Village Council of Edselsto becomes part of local Crown purchasing consultation after Tessa Rane challenges capital assumptions about stripping local food supply.
- Half of the old common is temporarily allocated as open Crown storage, with the boundary marked and no permanent use authorized without hearing the village council.
- Lower well repair begins under Perren Holt and Derrin Voss; village knowledge materially controls safe work.
- Worker tents are organized along the old south lane; runoff ditching, latrine placement, cookfires, material yards, and reuse of ruined structures begin.
- A Henn-family ruin is preserved pending contact rather than casually seized; a genuinely abandoned cooper's ruin is used instead.
- Sera Morcant, a Morcant-trained smith working under Bellisar contract, establishes the first crude functioning village forge and becomes its working master under negotiated Crown pay.
- Bellisar, Morcant, and Falbrecht interests now overlap around horses, iron, charcoal, and smithing rather than forming clean single-house monopolies.
- Luceran sides with worker-camp drainage and sanitation over Baron Pell's leaking-room repair, establishing a visible repair-priority precedent.
- Jory Fell is permitted to send a family letter by royal post, unintentionally creating the expectation that other displaced Crown workers may do the same; fifty-three letters appear the next morning.
- New suppliers begin arriving without direct royal summons, including Morcant iron, Falbrecht charcoal, and a Guild of Keys locksmith. Edselsto is becoming a market and patronage center under its own momentum.
- The court's emerging networks now move information and resources without waiting for Luceran personally.

### Theory-of-mind movement

- Luceran → Avarren: increasing irritation mixed with trust; he now recognizes that Avarren sees political consequences several steps earlier than he does.
- Luceran → Miren Edevane: genuine familial affection and trust reinforced; he also learns she is politically sophisticated enough to manage appearances while still advancing her household's useful position.
- Luceran → workers/villagers: begins understanding that apparently minor royal decisions reach families, property memory, food supply, sanitation, and private obligations.
- Olan → Luceran: updates from impulsive labor-seizing king toward a ruler who may accept practical priority over noble comfort.
- Village Council → Luceran: cautious willingness to work with him increases because he listens to local knowledge and limits immediate Crown seizure, without erasing memory of dynastic neglect.
- Edrik → Luceran: comfortable enough to point out secondary consequences and joke about them; professional trust continues growing.

### Political / institutional consequences

An early friendly bloc is forming through useful service rather than conspiracy: Edevane clerks and credit, Bellisar horses and access, Morcant smithing and iron, Falbrecht charcoal, Pell proximity-seeking, village purchasing knowledge, and recruited Crown workers. Luceran likes many of these people and has good reasons for each decision.

The first physical map of the new capital now has political meaning: rooms, stairs, forge, common, wells, ruined houses, storage yards, post, roads, and repair sequence all create access or obligation.

### Mystery / candle state

Family wing remains closed. No new supernatural evidence is established. Orven trims the nave candles as ordinary stewardship while Luceran assists. Seven of seven remain lit.

Episode 06 — *The Price of a Door* begins with suppliers and specialists arriving faster than the unfinished castle can absorb them. Locks, hinges, keys, timber, iron, smithing, security routes, contracts, and one intentionally sealed architectural route turn practical repair into the next political contest.
