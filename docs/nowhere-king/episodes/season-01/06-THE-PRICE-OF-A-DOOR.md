# Nowhere King — Season One, Episode Six
## The Price of a Door

**Status:** Committed episode prose  
**Season:** One — The Return to Edselsto  
**Episode:** 06 of 32  
**Reveal ceiling:** Suspicious architecture, inherited closures, missing records, and deliberate alterations are permitted. Seven candles remain lit. No containment truth, sentient-castle proof, candle failure, or authoritative explanation of why older routes were sealed.  
**Primary dramatic purpose:** Turn doors, keys, iron, charcoal, timber, locks, roads, and repair sequence into politics. Let useful friendly suppliers compete without becoming villains. Establish that access control creates durable obligations, and discover one deliberately sealed route whose existence creates a future question rather than answering one.

---

The locksmith arrived with eleven locks and nowhere to sleep.

This was, Luceran thought, a remarkably Edselsto way to begin the morning.

The woman standing in the lower courtyard was perhaps forty, broad through the shoulders, grey beginning at both temples, with a leather roll of tools under one arm and a brass badge shaped like a key pinned to her coat. Behind her stood two apprentices, a mule, three narrow crates, and a carter who had already decided the Crown owed him money.

Sera Morcant stood beside the crates with black on both hands.

"You sent for her?" Luceran asked.

Sera shook her head.

"No."

The locksmith bowed.

"Nella Veyr, Guild of Keys, Majesty. Master doorwright, third ward of the capital charter, licensed for treasury boxes, estate strongrooms, prison work, and noble household service."

"Who sent for you?"

"Nobody."

Luceran looked at Avarren.

Avarren looked delighted.

Nella continued.

"Your court bought every serviceable hinge in Barrow Cross yesterday. The cooper there said Edselsto was paying Crown silver for ironwork and had more doors than locks. I had locks."

"You rode here because of a cooper?"

"I rode here because of money. The cooper provided geography."

Sera snorted.

Luceran liked her immediately.

This was becoming dangerous.

"And the eleven locks?"

"What I could bring without stripping my shop. Four warded chamber locks. Three box locks. Two gate locks. One old-pattern mortise. One very good strongroom lock you cannot afford unless your treasurer is frightened."

"My treasurer is always frightened."

"Then I may sell it."

Avarren said, "Majesty, before you buy a door because you enjoy the woman selling it—"

"Lock."

"The distinction strengthens my point."

Luceran looked at Nella.

"Who else wants your work?"

"Everyone with a door."

"That is not helpful."

"It is accurate."

Behind them, a hammer rang from the village forge.

Nella turned toward the sound.

"That yours?"

Sera folded her arms.

"Mine until somebody with a title discovers it."

Nella looked at her hands.

"Morcant shops?"

"Trained there."

"Bellisar shoes?"

"Contracted there."

"Good. I need a striker."

Sera said, "I need iron."

Nella looked at the crates.

"I brought some."

Sera smiled.

Luceran watched an economy happen without him.

That was also new.

---

By breakfast, the eleven locks had become seventeen arguments.

Treasury wanted three.

The archive wanted two.

Rhyse wanted four doors repaired before any new lock was installed because, as he put it, "a magnificent lock on rotten oak is a decorative confession."

Mara wanted the pantry secured against rats and people, in that order.

Pell wanted his chimney repaired and had somehow entered the lock discussion anyway.

Miren Edevane's senior clerk wanted a lock on the east solar because the room now contained copied warrants, household credit tallies, and enough correspondence to make theft interesting.

Olan wanted nobody touching any door in the north range until he had checked the frames.

Orven wanted everyone to stop calling keys by numbers because the old keys had names.

Luceran stared at him.

"Names."

Orven placed six keys on the breakfast table.

"Long Chapel. Old Salt. West Guest. Lower Red. Hunting. Widow's."

Nella picked up Old Salt.

"Why?"

"Old salt store."

"Reasonable."

She picked up Widow's.

"And this?"

"Don't know."

"You have used it for forty-seven years."

"Not that one."

Nella examined its bit.

"Hand-filed. Old. Repaired twice."

Orven nodded.

"Was old when I came."

Luceran reached for it.

Orven put his hand over the key.

Not quickly.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

Luceran stopped.

"What?"

"Nothing you need before breakfast."

"That is becoming your answer to everything interesting."

"It has kept you alive through two breakfasts."

Montegrain buttered bread.

"An extraordinary record."

Luceran pointed at him.

"You have contributed nothing."

"I ate the dangerous edge piece."

Nella was still looking at Widow's.

"Where does it go?"

Orven took his hand away.

"Used to open a service door off the old family passage."

Luceran's attention sharpened.

"Used to?"

"Door isn't there."

Silence.

Avarren stopped reading.

Rhyse looked up.

Nella said, "Removed?"

"Covered."

"With what?"

"Stone."

Luceran looked at Orven.

"When?"

"Before my time."

"Why?"

"Before my time."

"Who ordered it?"

"Before my time."

Montegrain said, "I believe we have reached the limits of the witness."

Luceran ignored him.

"You never opened it?"

"There is no door to open."

"You know what I mean."

"Yes. No."

Nella set the key down.

"If the opening was properly infilled, the key proves only that there used to be a lock."

"Can you tell when it was sealed?"

"I'm a locksmith. Ask a mason."

Derrin, eating two places down because breakfast seating had not yet learned shame, raised one hand.

"You can ask a mason."

Luceran looked at him.

"Can you tell?"

"Maybe. Mortar changes. Tool marks change. Stone might match a quarry period. Depends how clever whoever closed it was."

Orven said, "After breakfast."

Luceran almost objected.

Then Mara put a bowl in front of him.

"Eat."

He ate.

This, he suspected, was how absolute monarchy actually worked.

---

The Morcant iron arrived before noon.

It came in two wagons bearing no noble banner, only chalk marks on the boards and a stamped copper bell on the accompanying tally.

The factor responsible was a narrow-faced man named Tomas Brinn who wore excellent boots and apologized for nothing.

Sera inspected the bars before anyone spoke about price.

She rejected four.

Tomas looked offended.

"Those are sound."

"Those are cold-short."

"They passed shop inspection."

"Then your shop inspector likes paperwork."

Tomas turned to Luceran as though appealing to the Crown against metallurgy.

Luceran raised both hands.

"I do not know iron."

Sera looked at him.

"Good."

Tomas looked less pleased.

"Majesty, House Morcant has supplied royal armories for—"

"Sera knows iron."

"She was trained in our shops."

"Then this should reassure you."

It did not.

Sera took one rejected bar to the forge, heated the end, and worked it under the hammer with one apprentice striking. The edge opened in a thin ugly split.

Tomas stared.

Sera quenched it.

"Four bars go back."

Tomas exhaled through his nose.

"Four bars go back."

Then, because he was competent rather than theatrical, he took out his tally and marked them rejected.

"I want the Crown order for the next three deliveries."

Avarren said, "At what price?"

"Today's price for the first. Two points less for the second if ordered now. Third indexed to charcoal cost."

Sera laughed.

"No."

Tomas turned.

"You are not the paymaster."

"No. I'm the woman who will tell him how much of your iron I can use."

"And I am the man who knows how much iron can reach this mountain before winter."

That quieted her.

Luceran watched them revise each other.

Not enemies.

Not friends.

Necessary.

Avarren asked, "How much?"

Tomas gave the number.

Avarren swore.

Tomas smiled.

"Now we understand one another."

The bargain took an hour.

Morcant received first right to quote on the next two ordinary iron deliveries, not an exclusive contract. The Crown could buy elsewhere if price or quality failed. Sera would inspect incoming forge iron regardless of supplier. Morcant received no automatic right to finished work merely because it supplied stock.

Tomas disliked the last clause.

Sera loved it.

Luceran approved it because it sounded fair.

Avarren wrote it down because fairness, once written beside quantities and dates, became something creditors could remember.

---

Falbrecht charcoal arrived wet.

Not all of it.

Enough.

The charcoal master, a young widow named Maelin Falbrecht who was a cousin rather than a principal of the house, stood in the storage field while Sera split sacks open with a knife.

"Road rain," Maelin said.

"Bad covering," Sera replied.

"Both."

"I don't burn both."

Maelin crouched and pushed her hand into a sack.

She came up black to the wrist.

"Top quarter is spoiled. Lower is dry. Separate it. You pay only dry weight."

Sera looked at her.

"That easy?"

"No. I want the dry sheds when they exist."

"Everyone wants the dry sheds when they exist."

"I brought the fuel that lets you build the hinges that let them close."

Sera grinned.

"There it is."

Luceran, standing nearby with Miren, said, "There what is?"

Sera pointed between the women.

"Politics."

Miren laughed.

Luceran did not.

Maelin bowed to him.

"Majesty, I want a storage covenant. Covered charcoal space, raised floor, no grain beside it, no lamp storage, and Falbrecht first refusal on supplying the forge through winter if our price remains within one twentieth of the lowest sound bid."

"Why first refusal?"

"Because I will build the sheds."

"With whose timber?"

"Mine."

Miren said quietly, "And then the shed becomes the argument."

Maelin heard her.

"Of course it does."

There was no embarrassment.

Luceran looked across the old common.

Two days earlier it had been grass, mud, a broken wall, and goats.

Now there were stacked timbers, marked stone, carts, lime under tarpaulin, barrels, a rope line, a feed pile, three tents, and two boys running messages uphill.

The Crown was not merely repairing Edselsto.

It was making property valuable by standing near it.

"Temporary shed," Luceran said. "On Crown storage ground. Falbrecht builds it. The Crown owns the structure after one year. You get first refusal through winter if price and quality hold."

Maelin considered.

"Two years."

"One."

"Eighteen months."

Luceran looked at Avarren.

Avarren shrugged.

"You are learning."

"I hate when you say that."

"Eighteen months," Luceran said.

Maelin bowed.

"Done."

Sera said, "And dry weight."

Maelin sighed.

"And dry weight."

The charcoal problem became a shed.

The shed became a supply privilege.

The supply privilege became a date in Avarren's ledger.

Luceran was beginning to understand that politics was often simply the name given to a practical decision after enough people depended on it.

---

The lower well had become six feet deeper and twice as argumentative.

Perren Holt stood in it.

Derrin Voss stood above it.

Neither was happy.

"Stop sending stone down," Perren called.

"I'm not sending stone down."

"Then the mountain is."

"Tell it to stop."

"You're the mason."

Luceran leaned over the edge.

"Progress?"

Both men looked up.

Derrin said, "Water's good. Wall isn't."

Perren said, "North side slipped years ago. Somebody packed rubble behind it instead of rebuilding."

Orven, behind Luceran, said, "That sounds familiar."

"Was everything here repaired badly?" Luceran asked.

"No. Some things were not repaired at all."

Derrin pointed toward a line of stone laid beside the well.

"Need dressed pieces. Not palace grade. Good enough to take load."

"Use the stone pile."

"Which stone pile?"

Luceran looked toward the storage field.

There were now three.

He had not authorized three.

A runner arrived breathless.

"Master Crewe says east pile is roof stone, west pile belongs to Kaelmont carters until weighed, middle pile is salvage and Mister Bale says don't touch the long pieces."

Luceran stared at him.

"How old are you?"

"Twelve."

"How do you know more about my kingdom than I do?"

The boy looked terrified.

Orven said, "Answer him."

"I carry the notes, Majesty."

Luceran straightened.

There it was again.

Information lived where work happened.

"What's your name?"

"Tavin Rane."

"Tessa's?"

"Grandson."

"Good. Tell Master Crewe the well gets enough salvage stone to make it safe unless he can show Derrin a reason it cannot."

Tavin nodded and ran.

Derrin watched him go.

"That'll annoy Olan."

"Will I be wrong?"

"No."

"Then he can be annoyed."

Perren shouted from the well.

"Majesty?"

"Yes?"

"If you want water before winter, stop talking and send stone."

Luceran looked at Orven.

"Do they all become like this?"

"Only the useful ones."

---

Nella Veyr began with the treasury.

She refused to install her best lock.

The treasury clerk nearly suffered a religious crisis.

"You brought a strongroom lock."

"Yes."

"This is the Royal Treasury."

"This is a linen room."

"Temporarily."

"Then temporarily use the lock you have."

The clerk appealed to Luceran.

Luceran had made the mistake of attending.

"Why?"

Nella tapped the door frame.

"Soft wood. Split here. Old worm here. Hinge screws are shallow. Your current lock is adequate against servants, curiosity, and a man without tools. My strongroom lock would be adequate against a patient thief, but the door would still be adequate against a boot."

Rhyse nodded.

"Replace door first."

"And frame," Nella said.

Olan, from the corridor, said, "Not today."

The treasury clerk made a sound.

Olan ignored him.

"Kitchen roof. East sleeping brace. Lower camp drainage. Then doors."

"Treasury—"

"Money doesn't rot when rained on. People do."

Luceran looked at the clerk.

"Current lock stays. Two guards."

Rhyse said, "One guard and a sealed tally at shift change. I don't have men to decorate linen."

The clerk looked wounded.

Luceran said, "One guard."

Another precedent.

He could almost hear Avarren writing it down from three rooms away.

Nella moved to the archive.

There she approved the door but condemned the lock.

At the east solar she approved the lock but condemned the hinges.

At the pantry she said the rats had already won.

At Pell's room she fixed the latch in six minutes and charged him personally.

Pell paid.

"Can I claim this from the household?"

Mara said, "No."

"I admire your consistency."

"I don't. It is exhausting."

By afternoon Nella had sold only four of the eleven locks.

Luceran trusted her more because of it.

He noticed that too.

---

Widow's key waited until late afternoon.

Luceran did not forget it.

Neither did Nella.

Orven tried.

They met at the old family passage with Derrin, Corren Bale, Rhyse, Bram, Montegrain, and one lamp too few.

Olan had refused to come because, in his words, "if the wall has stood a hundred years it can stand until I finish keeping people dry."

Luceran had permitted this insolence because Olan was correct.

The passage lay one turn short of the locked family wing. Its walls were dressed dark stone, older than the plastered guest corridors and colder despite the mild afternoon.

Orven stopped beside a stretch of wall between a narrow window and a shallow alcove.

"There."

Luceran looked.

"That is a wall."

"Excellent."

Derrin crouched.

Corren held the lamp lower.

For a while neither mason spoke.

Luceran began to ask.

Montegrain touched his sleeve.

Wait.

Luceran waited.

Derrin traced a vertical line with one dirty finger.

Once he showed it, Luceran could see it.

The stones inside a tall rectangle were fractionally different in size from those around them.

Corren pointed to the bottom.

"Threshold's still under the floor."

Nella knelt and examined the right side.

"Lock pocket was here."

"How can you tell?" Bram asked.

"Because whoever filled it used a separate piece instead of cutting the jamb out."

She held Widow's key against the air where the lock had once been.

It looked absurd.

A key for a wall.

Luceran said, "When?"

Derrin scraped a little mortar from a deep joint and rubbed it between thumb and finger.

Corren did the same.

"Not original," Derrin said.

"No," Corren agreed.

"Lime's finer than the wall bed."

"Stone came from inside the estate. Same family of basalt."

"Tooling is later."

Luceran waited.

"How much later?"

Corren grimaced.

"Could be a century. Could be three."

"That is useless."

"It's honest."

Nella said, "The lock was maintained before the door was sealed. Key's been repaired."

Orven said, "Or the key was used somewhere else before."

Nella nodded.

"Also possible."

Luceran stared at the rectangle.

"Can we open it?"

Rhyse answered first.

"Where does it go?"

Orven said, "Service stair, according to the old steward."

"According to him personally?"

"According to what he told me."

"Where does the stair emerge?"

"Lower family stores. Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"He never opened it either."

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

"Then no."

Luceran felt the old irritation rise.

"Captain—"

"A sealed route beside the family wing, destination uncertain, with no cleared lower exit and no knowledge of structural condition. If we open it today, I have to guard it today."

Derrin added, "And if the fill is carrying anything it wasn't meant to carry, we may open the ceiling too."

Corren nodded.

"Need shoring before removal."

Nella stood.

"And the key is irrelevant until there is a door again."

Montegrain leaned toward Luceran.

"You have assembled an impressive coalition against your curiosity."

Luceran looked at the wall.

He wanted it opened.

Because his father had known this place.

Because his mother had walked these corridors.

Because somebody had taken a functioning door and made it into stone.

Because he was king and the wall was in his house.

All terrible reasons to knock down masonry at dusk.

"Mark it," he said.

Derrin looked up.

"For opening?"

"For inspection. Shore it. Find the lower end if there is one. Search plans and inventories before anyone removes stone."

Rhyse relaxed by an amount only Bram seemed to notice.

"Guard?" Luceran asked.

"No permanent post," Rhyse said. "That would advertise it. Bram, add it to rounds."

Bram nodded.

Orven looked at Luceran.

"Good."

Luceran turned.

"Do not sound surprised."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"A little."

Luceran sighed.

Nella held out Widow's key.

"Who keeps this?"

Orven reached for it.

Rhyse reached at the same time.

They stopped.

There, Luceran thought.

Another door.

Except this one was a key.

Orven said, "Steward's ring."

Rhyse said, "Security evidence."

Nella said, "Guild would record the pattern."

Derrin said, "Doesn't open anything."

Montegrain said, "I would like it because everyone else does."

Luceran laughed despite himself.

Then he looked at Orven.

"You kept it forty-seven years. Keep it. Nella records it. Rhyse records that Orven has it."

Rhyse considered.

"Acceptable."

Orven put Widow's back on the ring.

The little piece of iron disappeared among the others.

Nothing about the wall changed.

That made it worse.

---

The village forge worked past sunset.

Luceran heard it from the castle.

Hammer.

Pause.

Hammer-hammer.

Pause.

A rhythm made by people who knew what they were doing.

He went down after dinner with Edrik rather than Bram.

The storage common smelled of wet timber, charcoal, horse piss, lime, cook smoke, and iron.

Falbrecht men were already raising the first posts for the charcoal shed under Maelin's supervision. Bellisar farriers had a separate line of horses waiting. A Morcant wagon was being unloaded under Sera's eye. Tavin Rane ran past with a folded note in one hand and half a heel of bread in the other.

Nobody stopped because the king had arrived.

Luceran liked that.

Then realized liking it was itself a privilege.

Sera had Nella's apprentice at the anvil.

"Again," she said.

The boy struck.

"Flat. You're making a hinge, not murdering a coin."

Nella sat on an upturned barrel filing a lock plate.

"He usually works brass."

"Brass forgives him."

"That's why I like brass."

Luceran leaned against the shed post.

"How many doors today?"

Nella did not look up.

"Four locks sold. Nine latches repaired. Three hinges replaced. Two doors condemned. One wall discovered."

"Productive."

"For me. You owe money."

"Avarren owes money."

Edrik coughed.

Luceran looked at him.

"What?"

"Nothing, Majesty."

"Say it."

"I don't think that's how owing works."

Nella laughed.

Luceran pointed at him.

"You are supposed to protect me."

"From knives. Not invoices."

Sera turned the hinge in the coals.

"Nobody can protect you from invoices."

Maelin Falbrecht entered carrying a tally board.

"Majesty. The shed line crosses the old goat path."

Luceran stared at her.

"Of course it does."

"Marrec Voss says move it four paces east."

"Can you?"

"Yes."

"Then move it."

"It costs another half-day."

"Move it."

She nodded.

No argument.

Edrik watched her go.

"That one will remember."

"The half-day?"

"That you made her move it."

Luceran looked at him.

"I also gave her eighteen months of first refusal."

"She'll remember that too."

"Which matters more?"

Edrik thought.

"Depends what happens next."

Luceran hated how often the answer was that.

---

Miren found him on the walk back up.

She had exchanged court silk for a heavy wool cloak and looked more like the cousin who had once smuggled him into a stable than the cadet princess whose clerks now occupied one of the best workrooms in Edselsto.

"You smell like smoke."

"I have a capital."

"You have a forge."

"Same thing today."

They walked beneath the pines.

Edrik kept far enough back to grant privacy and near enough to ruin any illusion of it.

Miren said, "I heard about the wall."

Luceran stopped.

"How?"

"My clerk heard Nella's apprentice asking for old plans."

Luceran stared at her.

Miren lifted both hands.

"There. That face. Keep it."

"What face?"

"The one where you realize information is not treason merely because it moved without your permission."

"It moved very quickly."

"Everything here moves quickly because everyone is sleeping on top of everyone else."

They resumed walking.

"Do you think my father sealed it?"

"No idea."

"Orven says before his time."

"Then probably not your father."

"He could have resealed it."

"He could have painted it pink. You have no evidence of either."

Luceran glanced at her.

"You are terrible at mystery."

"I am excellent at not helping you invent one."

He smiled.

For a little while they walked without talking.

Below them the new camp showed through the trees in pieces: firelight, lanterns, tent cloth, a wagon moving, sparks from the forge.

Miren said, "You like this."

"What?"

"Building."

Luceran looked down.

"Things happen when I decide them here."

"Yes."

"In the capital every decision became twelve objections and three dinners."

"Here it becomes a shed, a contract, a goat path, and somebody's cousin getting work."

"That sounds better."

"It is better."

He looked at her.

She meant it.

Then she added:

"It is also politics."

He groaned.

"You and Avarren should marry."

"I would kill him by Thursday."

"Wednesday."

"You do know me."

That warmth was real.

So was the fact that her clerks had the east solar.

Luceran was beginning to understand that two truths did not cancel each other merely because holding both was inconvenient.

---

Before bed, Rhyse brought him a sheet of paper.

Not a petition.

A door list.

Twenty-three entries.

Luceran stared at it.

"No."

"You need to see three."

"Then why are there twenty-three?"

"Because I need to see twenty-three."

Rhyse pointed.

"East stair latch is repaired. Bellisar feed access begins tomorrow under the agreed hours. Treasury door remains weak; guard procedure compensates. Family-passage infill is added to rounds, no fixed guard."

"Good."

"And this."

A door on the lower west service route had been found barred from the wrong side.

Luceran looked up.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning somebody once wanted it not merely locked but unusable from the castle side."

"Another mystery?"

"Another old door."

Luceran waited.

Rhyse did not embellish.

"Do we open it?"

"Not tonight."

Luceran smiled.

"You are learning."

Rhyse looked offended.

"Majesty."

"Sorry. Apparently that's irritating."

Rhyse left.

Luceran stood alone with the list.

Twenty-three doors.

Four locks bought.

A treasury room that could be kicked open.

A staircase Bellisar could use at certain hours.

A wall with a key.

A barred service door nobody had yet explained.

A charcoal shed that now represented eighteen months of commercial advantage.

Morcant iron on a conditional supply agreement.

A village well consuming stone that might otherwise repair his roof.

Everywhere he looked, Edselsto was becoming more functional.

Everywhere it became more functional, somebody acquired a reason to care how it was governed.

He had thought power meant being able to open doors.

It was beginning to occur to him that power might be deciding which doors remained closed, who held the key, who paid for the hinge, who stood watch outside, and who got to complain when the answer was no.

From somewhere down the mountain came the faint ring of Sera's hammer.

From the family nave came nothing at all.

Seven candles burned there in silence.

All seven.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 06 turns Edselsto's repair economy into explicit access and supply politics while preserving the Season One mystery ceiling.

### Durable changes

- Nella Veyr, Guild of Keys master doorwright, arrives independently because Edselsto's demand has become commercially visible. She becomes a recurring professional actor rather than a summoned plot solution.
- Nella sells only four of eleven brought locks after inspecting actual doors/frames; treasury retains its existing lock because the door/frame are the greater weakness.
- Royal security and repair priorities now explicitly distinguish locks, doors, frames, guards, latches, and routes rather than treating a lock as magical security.
- Sera Morcant demonstrates independent craft authority by rejecting four defective Morcant iron bars despite Morcant training; factor Tomas Brinn accepts the rejection after physical proof.
- Morcant gains first right to quote on the next two ordinary iron deliveries, not exclusivity. Crown may buy elsewhere for price/quality failure. Sera inspects forge iron regardless of supplier.
- Maelin Falbrecht establishes a charcoal supply bargain: Falbrecht builds a temporary covered charcoal shed on Crown storage ground; Crown owns the structure after eighteen months; Falbrecht receives first refusal through winter if price and quality remain acceptable; wet charcoal is paid only by dry weight.
- The Falbrecht shed is moved four paces to preserve Marrec Voss's remembered goat path, costing a half-day and reinforcing village local knowledge as a real constraint.
- Lower-well repair continues; Perren Holt and Derrin Voss confirm a historic rubble patch and need salvage stone. Luceran gives the well safety priority subject to Olan's structural objection if one exists.
- Tavin Rane, Tessa Rane's twelve-year-old grandson and one of the village runners, is named and demonstrates low-status information advantage because carrying work notes gives him a practical map of material movement.
- Widow's key is established as an inherited old key formerly associated, according to steward tradition, with a service door off the old family passage.
- Derrin Voss, Corren Bale, and Nella identify a deliberately infilled former doorway beside the family passage. The closure predates Orven's forty-seven years at Edselsto; exact date, authority, destination, and reason remain unknown.
- Luceran refuses to have the infill immediately broken open. He orders shoring, plan/inventory research, and an attempt to identify the lower end first.
- Orven retains Widow's key; Nella records its pattern; Rhyse records custody. The arrangement creates shared knowledge without stripping inherited stewardship by reflex.
- A second old lower-west service door is found barred from the opposite side, but remains unopened and unexplained.
- Bellisar east-stair access begins under previously agreed limited hours after latch repair, subject to Rhyse's security authority.
- Luceran increasingly understands that material choices create political constituencies even when choices are sensible and suppliers are friendly.
- Miren reinforces the distinction between information moving socially and actual disloyalty; Luceran accepts that genuine affection and institutional advantage can coexist.

### Political / institutional consequences

- Morcant, Falbrecht, Bellisar, Guild of Keys, Crown Works, village council, guards, and household now overlap around repair decisions without forming one coordinated faction.
