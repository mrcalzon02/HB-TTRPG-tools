# Nowhere King — Season One, Episode Seven
## The Family Wing

**Status:** Committed episode prose  
**Season:** One — The Return to Edselsto  
**Episode:** 07 of 32  
**Reveal ceiling:** Family history, deliberate record management, missing catalog material, inherited closures, and evidence that Luceran's father knew more about Edselsto than he told his son. Seven candles remain lit. No containment truth, sentient-castle proof, Nowhere King archaeology, candle failure, or authoritative explanation of sealed routes.  
**Primary dramatic purpose:** Turn Edselsto from ancestral refuge into family wound. Let records, rooms, objects, and other people's memories complicate Luceran's model of his parents without granting him the answer he wants. Promote archival competence through work rather than exposition, while construction and court-building continue outside the family wing.

---

The family wing opened at nine in the morning because Nella Veyr refused to open it at eight.

Luceran had been standing in the corridor since a quarter past seven.

"It is a lock," he said.

"It is a lock attached to a door," Nella replied.

"You have said this before."

"And yet people continue buying locks without noticing doors."

She was kneeling beside the north-suite entrance with the old escutcheon removed and three tools laid on a cloth. Jory Fell held a lamp. Orven Dast held the key ring. Captain Rhyse held everyone else outside the chalk line Nella had drawn across the corridor.

Montegrain leaned against the wall beside Luceran.

"I admire a woman who can make a king wait with chalk."

"I could cross it."

"You could also lick the hinge. Neither improves your reign."

Luceran ignored him.

Nella pushed a thin probe into the lock, frowned, withdrew it, and looked at Orven.

"This key has not turned here recently."

"No."

"How recently?"

"Before my time."

Luceran sighed.

Orven looked at him.

"You wanted accurate answers."

"I did."

"This is what they sound like."

Nella finally sat back.

"Lock is serviceable. Door is swollen. Upper hinge has dropped. If I turn the key now and you push, the bolt may clear and the door may still split at the top."

Jory nodded.

"I can lift it from below."

"Without levering against the stone?"

"Yes."

"Show me."

Luceran looked at Montegrain.

"Do they know I am here?"

"They appear to have mistaken you for the client."

Jory wedged a broad timber beneath the door with folded leather protecting the old oak. Nella turned the key. Jory lifted. The door gave a sound like a man waking with a bad back.

Then it opened.

Cold air moved into the corridor.

Nothing whispered.

Nothing glowed.

No dead ancestor appeared to explain the monarchy.

Luceran was disappointed again.

He was becoming accustomed to it.

---

The north suite had been abandoned carefully.

That was worse than ruin.

Dust lay over the furniture, but sheets covered the bed and chairs. The shutters had been latched. Two trunks stood against the inner wall. A writing table remained beneath the window with its drawers closed. The hearth had been swept before the room was left.

This was not a room people had fled.

It was a room someone had finished using.

Luceran stood just inside.

"My father slept here?"

Orven remained at the threshold.

"When he came."

"How often?"

"Less after he married. More before. Then twice after you were born."

Luceran turned.

"Twice?"

"That I remember."

"I was never brought."

"No."

Montegrain did not move closer.

Luceran walked to the writing table.

There was a pale rectangle in the dust where something had once sat.

"What was here?"

Orven looked.

"Writing case, perhaps."

"Perhaps?"

"I did not inventory his desk every time he left."

Luceran opened the first drawer.

Empty.

Second.

Empty.

Third contained sealing wax hardened into useless red sticks, two broken quills, a packet of old sand, and a brass token stamped with the royal stag used during his grandfather's reign.

No confession.

No secret history.

His father had apparently been capable of leaving rubbish like anyone else.

Luceran picked up the token.

He remembered his father's hands teaching him to seal a letter without drowning the paper in wax.

Not here.

At the capital.

He had been eleven.

His father had laughed because Luceran had stamped the seal upside down.

The memory arrived with such ordinary force that he sat down.

Montegrain looked toward the window.

Orven looked toward the floor.

Neither asked what was wrong.

Luceran put the token down.

"He hated bad pens."

Montegrain glanced over.

"I remember."

"He used to steal mine and then complain they were bad."

"That also sounds like him."

Luceran smiled once.

Then he noticed the wall.

Three portraits hung there.

His grandfather.

His grandmother.

His father, perhaps twenty years younger than Luceran remembered him, standing beside a black pine with one hand resting on the shoulder of a man Luceran did not know.

The stranger's face had been cut out.

Not damaged by damp.

Cut.

Cleanly.

Luceran stood.

"Orven."

The steward came in.

He looked at the portrait.

His face changed.

Luceran saw recognition.

"Who?"

Orven took longer than Luceran liked.

"Master Halven Sare."

"Who was he?"

"Your father's secretary here. For a time."

"Why is his face cut out?"

"I don't know."

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Did you know it was like this?"

"Yes."

Luceran stared at him.

"And you never thought to mention it?"

Orven looked back.

"To whom?"

That stopped him.

The castle had spent decades without anyone who cared about a damaged family portrait.

Orven continued.

"Sare left service before your father became king. I was told he went south. Never saw him again."

"Told by whom?"

"Your father."

That was the first thing in the room that felt like a door.

---

The archive problem arrived wearing spectacles and carrying three boxes.

Her name was Ysabet Marris, assistant keeper of royal household records, and she was furious that everyone had started searching before she arrived.

"You moved these?"

The question was directed at Miren Edevane's senior clerk.

He looked offended.

"We preserved them."

"That was not my question."

"They were in damp storage."

"Where?"

"South counting room."

"Which shelf?"

"I don't know."

Ysabet closed her eyes.

Luceran watched from the archive doorway.

Miren stood beside him.

"I like her," he whispered.

"You like anyone who scolds someone other than you."

"It is refreshing."

Ysabet opened her eyes.

"If you move a record without recording where it came from, you preserve the paper and damage the evidence."

The clerk stiffened.

"We were saving them from water."

"Good. Next time save the shelf mark too."

Miren said, "She is correct."

Her clerk looked betrayed.

Miren smiled at him.

"Affection is not immunity from being wrong."

Luceran looked at her.

"You practice that sentence?"

"Constantly."

Ysabet bowed when she finally noticed the king.

"Majesty."

"You came from the capital?"

"Yesterday."

"Who sent you?"

"Keeper Avrel."

"Why you?"

"Because I asked."

Luceran blinked.

"Why?"

She looked around the room as though the answer were physically stacked against the walls.

"Because half the household catalog says 'Edselsto copy' or 'Edselsto original' and nobody in the capital could tell me what that meant."

Miren murmured, "There it is."

Luceran had learned enough to recognize the sound of somebody developing an independent reason to remain near him.

"What do you want?"

Ysabet considered the question seriously.

"A dry room, two copyists, authority to stop people moving records without marking them, and access to Orven Dast while he is alive enough to be annoyed by me."

Orven, entering behind them, said, "No."

Ysabet turned.

"Which part?"

"The implication I am nearly dead."

"Then you have time to help."

Orven stared at her.

Luceran looked at Miren.

Miren whispered, "Keep her."

"I heard that," Ysabet said.

"Good," Miren replied.

---

By midday, the family wing had become less mysterious and more complicated.

Two rooms were structurally safe.

One was not.

A nursery had been stripped nearly bare except for a painted wooden horse missing its tail.

A dressing room contained six gowns wrapped in linen, none belonging to Luceran's mother.

A narrow withdrawing room had a cracked mirror, a card table, and seventeen years of dead flies behind the shutters.

The north library contained books.

This delighted Luceran until Ysabet explained that books were not the same thing as records.

"You are determined to ruin everything."

"I am an archivist."

"Assistant keeper."

"For now."

Montegrain laughed.

Ysabet had begun comparing three inventories: an Edselsto household list copied thirty-one years ago, a royal-family papers register from the capital, and a loose bundle of older shelf tallies Orven had produced from a locked cabinet in the steward's office.

She did not read them dramatically.

She counted.

That was somehow more ominous.

Luceran watched her finger move down a page.

"What?"

"Nothing yet."

"You stopped."

"Because the numbering changes."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning I need the next sheet."

She searched the bundle.

It was not there.

Luceran felt his pulse quicken.

Ysabet did not.

She checked again.

Then the other registers.

Then she asked Orven, "Were these bound?"

"Once."

"When did they come apart?"

"Before me."

"Of course."

Luceran said, "What's missing?"

Ysabet held up one sheet.

"This ends at Family Papers, North Cabinet, sequence forty-three. The next surviving sheet begins at fifty-one."

"Seven entries."

"Eight. Forty-four through fifty-one if fifty-one began on the missing leaf's reverse. I need the binding pattern before I promise a number."

Luceran almost laughed.

"You will not even let me have a missing seven?"

"Majesty, if you want mythology, Brother Calven is down the corridor. If you want to know what paper is missing, let me count holes."

Miren covered her mouth.

Luceran pointed at Ysabet.

"Dry room. Two copyists."

"And authority to stop people moving records."

"Within reason."

"Whose reason?"

"Mine."

"Then write it down."

Avarren would have loved her.

Luceran hated that thought because it meant he was beginning to sort people by institutional compatibility.

He was becoming a court.

---

Outside, the lower village rang with hammers.

Jory left the family wing before lunch because a wagon of black pine had arrived wet and Olan needed someone who could tell which pieces might still serve under cover.

Derrin returned to the well.

Nella went down to the forge because Sera had finished two lock plates and wanted them inspected before wasting more iron.

The king's family mystery did not stop construction.

This also annoyed Luceran.

He stood at a north window overlooking the village.

The old common was almost unrecognizable.

Falbrecht's shed had walls now.

Smoke rose from Sera's forge.

A line of workers carried salvaged stone toward the lower well.

Three new tents had appeared beside the cookfires.

Someone had built a rack for tools.

Two goats were eating something they had certainly been forbidden to eat.

Miren joined him.

"You thought everyone would stop because you found a cut portrait."

"No."

"You did."

"A little."

"Your father would have been offended by the goats."

Luceran smiled.

"He hated goats."

"He hated one goat."

"It ate his sash."

"Therefore all goats became enemies of the Crown."

For several moments they watched the workers below.

Luceran said, "Did he ever talk to you about this place?"

Miren's expression changed.

"Not much."

"That means yes."

"It means not much."

He looked at her.

She sighed.

"Once. At my mother's winter table. You were perhaps fourteen. Someone asked why Edselsto was never used for summer court. He said it was too expensive to maintain and too inconvenient to reach."

"That's true."

"Yes."

"Anything else?"

"He changed the subject."

Luceran waited.

"That's all."

He believed her.

That mattered.

Then she added, "Your mother asked him afterward whether he had written to Orven."

Luceran turned fully.

"You heard that?"

"I was beside her."

"What did he say?"

"Yes."

"About what?"

"I don't know."

"Did she?"

"Probably. They were married. That is not the same as saying she knew everything."

Luceran looked back toward the village.

His parents had become dead before he had learned how much of marriage was simply two people having conversations their children never heard.

He had mistaken being loved for being informed.

The realization hurt more than the cut portrait.

---

Ysabet found his father's handwriting at three in the afternoon.

Not a letter.

A correction.

The note appeared in the margin of an old household inventory beside a line reading: **North family cabinet — retained papers, private.**

The original clerk had written a shelf number.

Luceran's father had crossed it out.

Beside it, in the narrow hard hand Luceran knew immediately, he had written:

**Remove from general register. Keep under household seal. No copies. — L.**

Luceran stared.

Nobody spoke.

Ysabet finally said, "I can confirm the hand against three signed letters if you want formal attribution."

"It's his."

"I believe you. I still want comparison before I enter it as confirmed."

Luceran looked at her.

"Do it."

She nodded.

Montegrain stood at the other end of the table.

He did not come closer.

Luceran read the line again.

"When?"

Ysabet pointed to the inventory heading.

"Register compiled twenty-six years ago. The ink of the correction is later, but I cannot date ink by staring at it."

"Before I was born?"

"The register was. The correction could be later."

"Orven?"

The steward shook his head.

"Never saw that note."

"Household seal. What did that mean?"

"Could mean the king's private papers. Could mean the family secretary. Could mean a sealed chest. Depends when."

"Halven Sare?"

Orven looked at the cut portrait in the adjoining room.

"Could."

"Did he keep the seal?"

"For a time."

Luceran felt the story assembling itself too quickly.

His father.

A secretary whose face had been cut from a portrait.

Records deliberately removed.

A sealed doorway.

Widow's key.

Voices in walls.

He could feel his mind making a shape because minds hated loose pieces.

Ysabet interrupted it.

"Majesty."

"What?"

"The note says papers were removed from a general register. It does not say why."

Luceran looked at her.

"I know."

"Good."

"You think I don't."

"I think you are grieving and have seven missing entries, a damaged portrait, and an old door. That is enough material to build a very convincing wrong answer."

Montegrain looked at Ysabet with sudden approval.

Luceran did not enjoy this.

Which was probably why he needed it.

"Find the household-seal register."

Ysabet nodded.

"If it survives."

"Find whether it survives."

"That I can do."

---

The next discovery belonged to Orven.

He made it unwillingly.

They were in the old dressing room because Ysabet wanted to know whether a narrow cabinet built into the wall had ever held papers. Orven said it held gloves.

Ysabet asked how he knew.

"Because I put gloves in it."

"Whose?"

"The queen's."

Luceran stopped.

"My mother used this room?"

Orven looked annoyed with himself.

"Once."

"When?"

"Last time they came."

"They came together?"

"Yes."

"You said my father came twice after I was born."

"Yes."

"Was she here both times?"

"No."

"When was this?"

Orven thought.

"Nine years ago. Maybe ten."

Luceran would have been an adolescent in the capital.

He remembered his parents leaving for what he had been told was an estate circuit.

"How long?"

"Two nights."

"Why?"

"They did not tell me."

"Who came with them?"

"Four guards. Your father's secretary then. One maid for your mother. No court."

"Did they use the family wing?"

"Yes."

"Did they go anywhere else?"

Orven hesitated.

There.

Luceran saw it.

"Where?"

"Your father asked for old keys."

"Which?"

"Several."

"Widow's?"

"I don't remember."

Luceran nearly snapped at him.

Then stopped.

Forty-seven years of keys.

Nine or ten years ago.

A two-night visit that had meant nothing to Orven until the dead man's son arrived demanding chronology.

"What do you remember?"

Orven considered.

"He asked which lower family stores were still accessible. He asked whether the old north stair had shifted. He asked whether anyone had opened the wall beside the family passage."

Silence.

Luceran said, "The infilled doorway."

"Probably."

"And what did you tell him?"

"No."

"Did he try?"

"Not while I was with him."

"My mother?"

Orven looked toward the little cabinet.

"She was angry."

Luceran's throat tightened.

"At him?"

"I assumed so."

"About what?"

"I did not hear enough to know."

"What did you hear?"

Orven was quiet.

"She said, 'Not while he is a child.'"

Luceran did not move.

Montegrain did.

One step closer.

Not touching.

Close enough.

Luceran asked, "He?"

Orven shook his head.

"I assumed you. There were other boys in the world. I don't know."

"What did my father say?"

"Nothing I heard."

"And you never told me."

Orven's expression hardened.

"Your parents had an argument in their own room ten years ago. I was steward, not spy."

Luceran looked away.

That rebuke landed because it was deserved.

He had spent his first week at Edselsto complaining that the old capital knew everything about him.

Now he was angry that Orven had not listened harder at his parents' door.

"You're right," he said.

Orven's face softened by almost nothing.

"Usually."

Montegrain exhaled a laugh.

The room became survivable again.

---

By evening, Ysabet had found the hole.

Not the missing papers.

The hole where the missing papers had once been accounted for.

A household-seal register survived in the capital archive copy, but the Edselsto counterpart did not. The capital register contained a cross-reference written in a clerk's hand:

**Edselsto private packet — N.F. 44–51 — transferred under royal instruction. Receipt filed separately.**

The receipt was not present.

The transfer date was nine years ago.

Luceran sat very still.

Ysabet had already checked the arithmetic twice.

"N.F.?"

"Probably North Family. Possibly North Folio. I will not choose because one is more dramatic."

"Nine years."

"Yes."

"The visit."

"Likely the same year. I have not established the month."

"Transferred where?"

"Not stated here."

"Who signed?"

"The receipt would tell us."

"And the receipt is missing."

"Not necessarily missing. Not present in this sequence."

Luceran laughed without humor.

"You are relentless."

"Words are the work."

He looked at the marginal instruction in his father's hand.

Remove from general register. Keep under household seal. No copies.

Then at the later cross-reference.

Transferred under royal instruction.

Nine years ago.

His parents had come here quietly.

His mother had said, not while he is a child.

Maybe about him.

Maybe not.

His father had asked about old keys and a wall.

Maybe the same wall.

Maybe not.

For the first time, Luceran did not try to make the pieces agree.

"What can we actually say?"

Ysabet answered immediately.

"Your father ordered a set of private family papers removed from the general register. A later register says a packet numbered forty-four through fifty-one was transferred under royal instruction nine years ago. The receipt is not with the material we have. Orven remembers your parents visiting in roughly that period and your father asking about old keys, lower stores, the north stair, and whether the family-passage wall had been opened."

"And my mother."

Ysabet looked at Orven.

He said, "I remember one sentence from an argument."

Ysabet nodded.

"Then that is what we have."

Luceran leaned back.

Montegrain finally sat beside him.

"Enough?"

Luceran looked at him.

"No."

"I know."

"I thought he told me things."

Montegrain considered.

"He did."

"Not this."

"Apparently not."

Luceran stared at the table.

"I don't know whether to be angry."

"You can postpone it."

"Can kings do that?"

"No. Friends can."

Luceran looked at him.

There were a hundred political uses for a man allowed to sit beside a grieving king after dark.

For once, neither of them cared.

That did not make the uses disappear.

---

Before bed, Luceran walked to the family passage.

Bram went with him.

The infilled doorway remained stone.

Chalk marks from Derrin and Corren showed the old opening. A small notation in Nella's hand marked the probable lock position. Nothing had been broken.

Luceran touched the later masonry.

His father had stood somewhere near here nine years ago.

Perhaps.

That word had become the most important word in Edselsto.

Bram said, "Majesty?"

"Yes?"

"Captain says no breach until Master Crewe clears shoring."

Luceran smiled faintly.

"You thought I might?"

"I thought you might think about it."

"I am thinking about it."

"Then my work is excellent."

Luceran laughed.

He took his hand away.

"Not tonight."

"Good."

They walked back toward the inhabited wing.

At the junction, Brother Calven emerged from the nave carrying a snuffer and fresh wax.

Luceran stopped.

"All seven?"

Calven looked back through the open door.

"All seven."

Luceran could see them from where he stood.

Seven flames.

Steady.

Meaningless, as far as he knew.

Old family custom.

He found himself counting anyway.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Then he went to bed.

## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 07 advances the family-history investigation without crossing the Season One reveal ceiling.

### Durable changes

- The north family suite is opened through ordinary locksmith/carpentry work; no supernatural mechanism is involved.
- Luceran encounters his father's former room as an abandoned lived space rather than a mystery chamber.
- A portrait of Luceran's younger father includes former Edselsto secretary **Halven Sare**, whose face was deliberately cut from the portrait at an unknown time. Orven reports Sare left royal service before Luceran's father became king and was said to have gone south; this remains unverified.
- **Ysabet Marris**, assistant keeper of royal household records, arrives from the capital by her own request because Edselsto provenance problems matter professionally. She becomes a recurring archival specialist.
- Ysabet establishes an archival rule important to future continuity: preserving paper without preserving provenance can destroy evidence.
- The Edselsto family-paper catalog has a gap around entries 44–51.
- Luceran's father's handwriting is identified on an inventory instruction ordering private North Family papers removed from the general register, kept under household seal, and not copied. Ysabet requires comparison before formal attribution despite Luceran recognizing the hand.
- A later household-seal register cross-reference says **Edselsto private packet — N.F. 44–51 — transferred under royal instruction**. The associated receipt is not present with current material.
- The transfer is dated nine years earlier, approximately the period of a quiet two-night visit by Luceran's parents.
- Orven remembers that visit. Luceran's father asked about old keys, lower family stores, the old north stair, and whether the wall beside the family passage had been opened.
- Orven remembers Luceran's mother saying during a private argument, **"Not while he is a child."** Orven assumed the pronoun referred to Luceran but cannot confirm subject or context.
- Luceran recognizes that his parents' love did not imply complete disclosure and revises his model of his father toward deliberate withholding without deciding why.
- Luceran explicitly resists assembling the evidence into one confident theory.
- The infilled family-passage doorway remains unopened. No breach occurs.
- Construction continues independently outside the investigation: forge, lower well, charcoal shed, timber handling, worker camp, and material movement do not stop for royal grief.
- Seven candles remain lit.

### Relationship / theory-of-mind movement

- Luceran → father: from broadly trusted parent with unexplained omissions toward a man who deliberately managed private Edselsto information; motive remains unknown.
- Luceran → mother: gains evidence she participated in at least one private Edselsto dispute and may have argued for delaying something while a child remained young; exact meaning unresolved.
- Luceran → Orven: learns that respecting privacy can itself create frustrating information gaps; accepts that Orven was steward rather than spy.
- Luceran → Ysabet: quickly values her because she refuses dramatic conclusions and protects evidentiary distinctions even against royal impatience.
- Ysabet → Luceran: sees a grieving king prone to pattern-making but capable of accepting correction.
- Luceran ↔ Montegrain: friendship deepens through quiet presence rather than explanation; Montegrain does not solve grief or exploit it.
- Miren → Luceran: continues affectionate correction and supplies one bounded memory about his parents without pretending certainty.

### Institutional / material consequences

- Ysabet receives a dry room, two copyists, and practical authority to preserve record provenance subject to royal authority.
- Archive practice at Edselsto becomes more rigorous because records are now evidence as well as property.
- The missing receipt for packet N.F. 44–51 becomes a concrete documentary obligation.
- Halven Sare becomes a concrete historical person requiring provenance research rather than a supernatural clue.
- Construction and supply systems continue operating without waiting on the king, reinforcing side-character and institutional agency.

### Mystery state

Observed: deliberate record restriction by Luceran's father; later transfer of private packet; damaged portrait; quiet parental visit; questions about old keys/stairs/wall; one overheard sentence from Luceran's mother.

Not established: why papers were restricted, what N.F. definitively means, where packet 44–51 went, who received it, why Sare's face was cut out, why the family-passage route was infilled, what Luceran's mother meant, or whether any of these facts share one cause.

No prison truth, castle-interface truth, Nowhere King archaeology, sentient-castle proof, or confirmed supernatural behavior is revealed.

### Candle state

Seven of seven remain lit.

Friendly houses, guilds, officers, temple representatives, factors, creditors, and later arrivals will discover that temporary rooms, supply bargains, introductions, guard routes, archive access, village arrangements, and personal friendships have already hardened into recognizable advantage.
