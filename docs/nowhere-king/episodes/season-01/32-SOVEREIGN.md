# Season One, Episode 32 — Sovereign

The next room was the kitchens.

That sounded ridiculous until men began dying for them.

The lower kitchen doors were oak, iron-banded and broad enough for two laden carts. They had never been intended as fortifications. Someone had overturned flour bins behind them, wedged a butcher's table beneath the handles and poured lamp oil across the flagstones beyond.

Fenn smelled it before Rhyse saw it.

"Not there."

Rhyse held up a fist.

The men behind him stopped badly. Shields knocked shields. Somebody cursed.

Fenn, pale beneath a streak of soot, pointed at the dark line under the door.

"They moved the oil."

"You said that before."

"I know where."

Rhyse looked at him.

The boy swallowed.

"The scullery crossing. If they burn the kitchens, the smoke goes up the serving stair. If they burn the crossing, nobody gets from the north service to the great hall without going through the gallery."

Luceran said, "Rusk."

Miren said, "Or someone who knows how kitchens work."

Luceran looked at her.

She did not apologize.

Rhyse crouched, touched the wet stone with two fingers, smelled them, and wiped them on the dead man's white sash lying beside the wall.

"Water."

"We don't have enough," Mara said.

"Then sand. Ash. Flour if we have to."

Fenn made a face.

"Not flour."

Everyone looked at him.

"It goes everywhere."

Mara almost laughed.

It came out as a cough.

"Boy says no flour."

The kitchen doors shook.

Someone on the other side had heard them.

Rhyse pointed. "Mara, hold this. Ardel takes the scullery route. I want the crossing before they can light it. Nobody chases beyond the serving stair. We take the room, then the next room."

Luceran stepped forward.

Rhyse put an arm across his chest.

"Sixth."

"Fifth."

"Your Majesty, I am tired."

"So am I."

"Sixth."

Miren said, "Take the bargain."

Luceran took the bargain.

---

Ardel Kaelmont had eight people left with her who could still fight.

She had entered Edselsto with twelve.

One had a bolt through the thigh and was already with Calienne. One had lost two fingers in the receiving gallery and refused to admit that this made holding a sword difficult. One had been hit in the head hard enough that he kept asking whether they had taken the gate yet.

The fourth was dead.

Ardel had not learned his name until after he died.

That fact sat badly in her.

Fenn led her to the scullery turn and stopped.

"Past that arch."

"You are done."

"I can show—"

"You have shown."

"My mother—"

"Would prefer you alive."

He glared at her.

Ardel pointed back the way they had come.

"Go tell Rhyse I am moving."

"I can carry."

"Then carry that."

She handed him a folded strip of white cloth taken from a surrendered guard.

Fenn looked at it.

"What is it?"

"Permission to leave."

He understood.

His face changed.

Ardel regretted making him understand.

"Go."

He went.

Ardel drew her sword.

The scullery crossing had once been a place where three kinds of servant collided and blamed one another for being in the way. The cooks came through with trays. Laundry workers crossed with baskets. Coal and wood came up from below. A little saint's niche had been cut into one wall generations ago and converted into a shelf for broken cups.

Now two tables had been tipped on their sides.

A crossbow rested between them.

Ardel saw the quarrel head.

"Down."

It fired.

The man behind her did not get down.

The bolt entered his cheek and came out beneath his ear.

He dropped without a sound.

Ardel went over the first table before the crossbowman could span again.

The defender abandoned the weapon and drew a knife.

She hit his wrist against the wall.

Once.

Twice.

The knife fell.

He punched her in the mouth.

She tasted blood.

Then one of her retainers drove a spear through him from behind.

The point came out under his breastbone.

For an instant the three of them stood together, Ardel pressed against the dying man, his breath hot on her face.

He said, "Wait."

Nobody knew what he meant.

Then he sagged.

"Oil," someone shouted.

A clay lamp broke against the far arch.

Fire ran.

Not toward them.

Across the crossing.

Ardel saw the purpose immediately.

"Blankets!"

There were no blankets.

There were aprons.

There were sacks.

There was a tub of gray dishwater that smelled of onions and fat.

They threw everything.

The fire climbed one table, found grease, and flashed.

A servant screamed from behind a pantry door.

Ardel kicked it open.

Two women and a boy were inside.

The older woman had a carving knife.

She nearly stabbed Ardel.

"King's people!"

The woman stared at her.

Ardel pointed to the smoke.

"Can you kill fire?"

The woman looked offended.

"Better than you."

That was how Edselsto's scullery was taken.

Not by the king.

By Lady Ardel Kaelmont, six exhausted retainers, three kitchen workers, two buckets of filthy water, a wet grain sack, and a woman named Bessa who spent the next twenty minutes insulting every soldier who stepped where she had just extinguished oil.

---

Rusk's clerk burned the wrong ledger first.

His name was Harl Ven.

He had spent most of his adult life making copies of other men's decisions.

He knew how to imitate a seal impression in wax well enough to fool someone in a hurry. He knew which ink browned first. He knew that a folded warrant looked more official if the fold passed through the signature rather than around it.

He also knew that Rusk had never learned his name.

"Those," Rusk said.

Harl looked at the stack.

"Those are accession tallies."

"Burn them."

"The payment book is underneath."

Rusk looked at him.

Harl corrected himself.

"Yes, my lord."

There were six men in the administrative room. Two had swords. One had a crossbow. Three were clerks who had discovered during the coup that handwriting did not protect a person from war.

Harl put the accession tallies into the brazier.

The top page curled.

He reached beneath the stack and slid the payment book into his coat.

Not because he loved Luceran.

Not because he had changed sides.

Because Rusk had told him yesterday that men who knew too much were expensive.

Harl had spent his life understanding euphemism.

The smoke thickened.

From the corridor came shouting.

One of Rusk's soldiers said, "They're in the kitchens."

Rusk said, "Then we go up."

"What about the rest?"

"Burn it."

Harl looked at the shelves.

Years of petitions. Gate rosters. Supplier tallies. Receipts. Temporary credentials. Copy warrants. Things that were boring until somebody needed to prove who had been where.

Rusk left.

Harl waited until the last soldier followed.

Then he pulled three bundles from the fire.

The cord burned his palm.

He screamed, dropped one, kicked it away from the brazier and stamped until the flame died.

The other clerks stared.

"Help me."

"Why?"

"Because if he wins, we say the king's men did it."

"And if the king wins?"

Harl held up his burned hand.

"We say we saved the records."

The oldest clerk nodded.

"That is disgusting."

"Yes."

He began pulling papers from the fire.

The old clerk joined him.

---

The first man Luceran killed in the kitchens had cooked for him.

Not often.

Not well.

Luceran knew him as a broad man who salted stew too early and once blamed a ruined venison pie on mountain air.

Now he came around the butcher's block with a cleaver.

There was no recognition until after Luceran's sword entered him.

The man's eyes widened.

"Majesty?"

Luceran pulled the blade free.

The cook fell against the block.

His hand remained closed around the cleaver.

Luceran stood still.

Miren struck his shoulder.

"Move."

He moved.

A spear passed through the space his throat had occupied.

Rhyse caught the spearman.

The room became fragments.

A pot on the floor.

A man screaming because his hand was under someone's boot.

Mara on top of a table, shield gone, sword red to the guard.

A surrendered defender crawling beneath a bench because both sides kept stepping over him.

A kitchen girl beating at a burning curtain with her skirt.

Luceran saw a coup soldier throw down his sword.

Then another loyalist, too frightened or too angry to stop, raised an axe.

Luceran shouted.

The axe fell anyway.

It entered the surrendered man's shoulder.

Rhyse hit the loyalist with the flat of his sword so hard that he dropped.

"What did I say?"

"He—"

"What did I say?"

The loyalist looked at the man he had struck.

Blood pumped between the man's fingers.

Rhyse pointed toward the rear.

"Carry him to Calienne."

"But—"

"Carry him."

The loyalist obeyed.

That would not settle it.

Nothing was settling.

Every room created another future hearing.

---

Calienne had run out of clean linen.

She cut altar cloth.

One of her assistants stared.

"Canon Merel will murder you."

"Then he may wait until I finish."

The chapel floor had disappeared beneath bodies.

Not corpses.

Not yet.

That distinction required work.

Jeron Bellisar lay near the wall with his old injury reopened. Pellan Vey sat under guard beside a man whose colors he had fought under an hour before. Iven Marrick had been carried in from the gatehouse breathing shallowly enough that Calienne knew before she touched him.

Mara arrived as Calienne was cutting the cuirass away.

"How bad?"

Calienne did not answer.

Mara crouched.

Iven opened his eyes.

"Corner held."

"I know."

"Did we—"

"Gatehouse is ours."

He nodded.

Mara took his hand.

Calienne kept working because sometimes work mattered and sometimes it only gave everyone something to do while a man died.

Iven looked at Mara.

"Tell my sister I wasn't stupid."

Mara's face twisted.

"You were extremely stupid."

He smiled.

"Good."

Then he stopped.

Mara remained crouched.

Calienne closed Iven's eyes.

Somewhere beyond the chapel, a cheer rose and broke into shouting again.

Mara stood.

"Write his name."

"It is already written."

"Write it twice."

Calienne looked at her.

Mara wiped her face with the heel of her hand.

"So nobody loses it."

Then she went back to the battle.

Calienne looked at her assistant.

"Write it twice."

The assistant did.

---

Aven surrendered the east guard room at the second bell after the gate opened.

He did not surrender himself.

Not yet.

That distinction mattered to him.

He stood behind a barricade of benches with twenty-three men and three women who had joined the coup for reasons ranging from constitutional principle to personal hatred of Luceran's household appointments.

The king's soldiers filled the corridor.

Aven raised both hands.

"Terms."

The lieutenant opposite him said, "Weapons down."

"Terms first."

"You are surrounded."

"That does not make my people legally dead."

The lieutenant looked as though he might solve the problem by becoming unreasonable.

Miren arrived before he could.

Her hair was stuck to one side of her face with blood that was not hers.

"Aven."

He looked relieved.

That offended her.

"Lady Miren."

"Do not lady me today."

"Fair."

"What do you want?"

"Protection under my posted surrender order. Names taken. Wounded treated. No stripping beyond weapons. No summary punishment."

"You issued that order while occupying the king's castle."

"Yes."

"You understand how little authority you had."

"I understand exactly how much authority I believed I had."

That was more difficult.

Miren looked at the people behind him.

Some were barely adults.

One was old enough to be her father.

One had a bandage around his head.

"Rusk?"

"Gone upward."

"Montegrain?"

"With his own."

"Will they surrender?"

Aven's mouth tightened.

"Montegrain might."

"Rusk?"

"No."

Miren looked at the lieutenant.

"Take their names. Separate officers. Weapons in the center. Anyone who strikes a surrendered prisoner answers to Rhyse."

The lieutenant hesitated.

"Do you have authority?"

"No."

Aven almost smiled.

Miren pointed at him.

"Do not."

He stopped.

She sent a runner.

Five minutes later Rhyse's answer came back.

HONOR TERMS GIVEN. HOLD AVEN SEPARATE.

Aven read it upside down.

"There."

Miren said, "Congratulations. You have been successfully arrested."

He sat down on the bench.

For the first time since the coup began, he looked tired.

---

The administrative wing was burning badly enough to make heroism stupid.

Ardel arrived first and became angry at the architecture.

"Why is there only one useful door?"

Bessa, who had followed the soldiers because soldiers were apparently incapable of handling fire without supervision, shouted, "There are three."

Ardel turned.

Bessa pointed to a panel that looked like wall.

"Copyists' hatch."

"That is not a door."

"It opens."

"Then it is a door."

They opened it.

Smoke punched through.

A clerk fell out coughing.

Then another.

Harl Ven emerged last with his burned hand wrapped in a strip torn from his shirt and a ledger under the other arm.

Ardel caught him.

"Who are you?"

"Useful."

"Bad answer."

"Clerk."

"Whose?"

Harl looked back at the burning room.

"Currently undecided."

Ardel almost hit him.

Instead she took the ledger.

Harl held on.

"This buys me a hearing."

"You are in a burning corridor."

"It buys me a hearing after the burning corridor."

"What is it?"

"Payments."

"Whose?"

Harl looked toward the upper stairs.

"Lord Rusk's."

That changed the room.

Ardel handed the ledger to one of her people.

"Under your shirt. If I die, you give it to Rhyse. If he dies, Miren. If she dies—"

"That is becoming discouraging."

"Then keep them alive."

Harl said, "There are more."

They went back into the smoke.

---

By the time Luceran reached the great-hall approach, he had stopped knowing whose blood was on him.

His left sleeve was torn.

His right glove had been cut through across the palm.

His sword felt heavier than it had at the gate.

Rhyse had lost his helmet.

Mara had lost two more people.

Miren had found Aven and then lost Luceran and then found him again, which produced a thirty-second argument conducted while both crouched behind a stone bench under crossbow fire.

"You cannot keep doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Being elsewhere."

"I am the king."

"That is not a location."

A bolt shattered against the wall.

Luceran laughed.

He did not know why.

Miren stared at him.

"That is a terrible sign."

"Probably."

Across the hall, Montegrain shouted, "Luceran!"

Everything stopped for less than a second.

Then fighting resumed around the name.

Montegrain stood behind the far barricade.

He had removed his helmet.

There was blood down one side of his face.

Not much.

Enough.

Luceran rose.

Rhyse dragged him down again.

"Stay."

"Montegrain!"

His old friend looked at him across thirty yards of furniture, bodies and people trying to kill one another.

"This does not need another hundred dead!"

Luceran shouted back, "Then yield!"

"Rusk has the upper record rooms."

"I know."

Montegrain looked surprised.

"Of course you do."

"Yield."

Montegrain looked behind him.

His people were watching.

That was the trap.

Luceran understood it too late.

Montegrain could not simply become Luceran's friend again because Rusk had become worse.

He had brought people here.

He had asked them to risk treason for a political judgment.

He could not erase their act by discovering that another conspirator was less honorable.

Montegrain shouted, "My people withdraw to the throne approach. We will not burn records. We will not kill prisoners. We will not fire on healers."

"That is not surrender."

"No."

"Then get out of my hall."

Montegrain almost smiled.

"You always hated losing arguments."

"I am going to arrest you."

"That is not an argument."

Rhyse said, "Enough."

He looked at the hall.

Two stair mouths. Three barricades. Gallery above. Crossbows behind the long table Luceran had commissioned in Episode Twelve's happier world, though nobody in the room thought of stories or episodes; they thought of oak thick enough to stop bolts.

Rhyse pointed to Mara.

"West."

Ardel appeared through smoke at the north entry.

Rhyse looked at her.

She held up a ledger.

"Found something."

"Later."

"Very much later if we do this badly."

He nodded.

Then the great hall became a battlefield.

---

The first barricade took nine minutes.

The second took four.

The gallery took twenty-three.

Time did not correspond to suffering.

At the first barricade, shields met spearpoints in a space too narrow to swing.

A man named Tovan lost three teeth when a shield rim struck his mouth.

He swallowed one.

Another lodged in his lip.

He kept fighting until somebody noticed that the blood down his chest was not all from his mouth.

At the second barricade, a coup defender tried to surrender and was stabbed from behind by one of Rusk's men.

Mara saw it.

She killed the man who did it.

Then she knelt beside the surrendering defender.

He looked at her uniform.

"Wrong side."

"Apparently."

He died before Calienne's runners reached him.

At the gallery, Ardel's people fought men they had eaten beside during the feast before departure.

One called her by her childhood nickname.

She froze.

He did not.

His sword cut her upper arm.

Her retainer killed him.

Ardel sat against the wall afterward with blood running to her elbow and said his name three times so she would not later pretend she had not known him.

Below, Luceran saw a young royal guard drive a spear through a former doorwarden and vomit immediately afterward.

Rhyse stepped over both.

"Forward."

Not because he did not care.

Because if they stopped in the killing ground, more people died.

That was command.

Luceran hated him for it for almost a minute.

Then understood.

Then hated that he understood.

---

Rusk reached the upper archive before the king's people.

He had twelve men left.

He had also acquired three prisoners.

Ysabet was one.

Selise was another.

The third was a copyist whose name Rusk did not know.

Ysabet looked at the torch in his hand.

"If you burn this room, half the west roof may go."

Rusk said, "Then perhaps His Majesty should have built it better."

"He did not build it."

"Everything is his when it flatters him."

Selise said, "And yours when it can be destroyed?"

Rusk turned.

Selise was afraid.

She was also furious.

Rusk had met many people who confused those conditions with courage.

Sometimes they were correct.

"You think this is destruction."

"I think you have a torch."

"I am removing instruments of illegitimate rule."

Ysabet said, "Those include grain assessments."

Rusk looked at her.

"History is untidy."

"No. You are."

One of his men laughed.

Rusk struck him.

The laugh stopped.

Then a bell rang below.

Not a castle bell.

A handbell.

Three strokes.

Pause.

Three.

Calienne's casualty signal.

Rusk looked toward the door.

His men did too.

That was when Selise moved.

She did not attack him.

She kicked the torch.

It rolled beneath a cabinet.

Everyone moved at once.

Ysabet stamped on the flame.

The copyist threw himself at the nearest soldier.

Selise ran for the door.

Rusk caught her hair.

She screamed.

Then the door opened inward and Bram Tolland hit Rusk in the knee with a crutch.

It was not heroic.

It was extremely effective.

Rusk fell.

Bram fell too.

Anja came through behind him with a kitchen poker.

Mikel Orr had a wood axe.

The household workers who had spent two days hiding, carrying messages and surviving had not transformed into soldiers.

They fought like people who knew the room.

Mikel threw a stool.

Anja pulled Selise free.

Bram, on the floor, hooked his crutch around Rusk's ankle and held on while Rusk kicked him in the chest.

One of Rusk's soldiers raised a sword over Bram.

Ysabet shoved an archive ladder into him.

The ladder, the soldier and two boxes of tax copies went down together.

Then royal troops arrived from the other door.

The fight lasted perhaps forty seconds.

Rusk escaped through the west family passage.

Four of his men did not.

Two died.

Two surrendered.

One of the dead fell across a cabinet and bled through thirty years of marriage petitions.

Ysabet looked at the papers.

Then at the body.

Then at Luceran's soldiers entering.

"Do not move him yet."

A soldier stared.

"The records underneath him are wet."

"He's dead."

"Yes."

She swallowed.

"That does not make the records dry."

---

Bram could not breathe properly.

Calienne's assistant found him sitting against the archive wall.

Anja was beside him.

"You were told not to fight," she said.

Bram wheezed.

"I didn't."

"You struck Lord Rusk with a crutch."

"Walking accident."

She started laughing.

Then crying.

Then both.

Luceran arrived while she was still doing it.

He stopped.

Bram looked up.

For a moment the king and the worker regarded one another across all the impossible distance that had existed between them before Edselsto became a place where both could bleed.

"Majesty."

Luceran crouched.

"I heard you were alive."

"Mostly."

Anja said, "He is an idiot."

"I've heard that too."

Bram smiled.

Then Luceran saw the bruise rising across his chest.

His smile vanished.

"Calienne."

"Already sent for."

Bram caught Luceran's wrist.

"Rusk went west."

"I know."

"No. West family passage."

Luceran looked at Ysabet.

She nodded.

"Old route toward the council rooms. Not the sealed stair."

That distinction mattered.

Luceran stood.

"Thank you."

Bram let go.

Anja said, "Bring the castle back."

Luceran looked around the archive.

The wounded. The dead. The scorched cabinet. The scattered petitions. Ysabet already directing people to lift papers away from blood.

"I am trying."

---

Rusk's last stand happened in a room nobody would later call important.

It had been a waiting room outside the old council chamber.

Three benches.

A cracked mural.

One window too narrow for a man.

Rusk had seven people with him.

Montegrain had none.

That was because Montegrain had gone the other way.

Rusk understood what that meant.

"Traitor," he said.

One of his soldiers looked at him.

Rusk laughed.

"Not you."

The royal force came from both ends.

Mara from the west.

Rhyse from the east.

Ardel, wounded arm bound, behind Mara.

Luceran arrived with Rhyse.

Rusk looked at the king.

"You opened the gates with ghosts."

Luceran said, "Yes."

It was the first time he had said it aloud.

Rusk smiled.

"And they call us traitors."

"You are traitors."

"To what? A crown that commands the dead?"

"I did not know they were there."

"That makes it better?"

"No."

Rusk blinked.

Luceran stepped forward.

Rhyse did not stop him this time.

"Put down your sword."

Rusk looked at the seven people around him.

Two did.

One of Rusk's officers shouted at them.

Mara said, "Don't."

The officer turned toward her.

That was his mistake.

Ardel's spearwoman put a point through his thigh.

He fell screaming.

Three more surrendered.

Rusk had two left.

Then one.

The last man looked at Rusk.

"My lord."

Rusk said nothing.

The man dropped his sword.

Rusk stood alone.

Luceran said, "It is over."

"No."

Rusk reached inside his coat.

Six people moved.

He pulled out paper.

Not a weapon.

A folded packet.

He held it over the candle on the wall.

Harl Ven, arriving behind Ardel with his burned hand, shouted, "That's the authorization!"

Rusk touched the paper to flame.

Mara threw her sword.

It was a terrible throw.

The hilt struck Rusk's wrist.

The packet fell.

The candle caught the edge.

Luceran lunged.

So did Rusk.

They collided on the floor.

Rusk punched him in the cut hand.

Luceran screamed.

Rusk reached for the burning packet.

Miren stamped on it.

Hard.

Once.

Twice.

Then she kicked Rusk in the face.

He went still.

Everyone stared at her.

Miren looked down.

"What?"

Rhyse said, "Take him."

They did.

The packet survived with one corner burned.

Not enough to answer everything.

Enough to ensure that somebody would have to.

---

Montegrain waited in the throne room.

He had twenty-one people when he entered it.

He had thirteen when Luceran arrived.

The missing eight had surrendered in the antechamber.

Montegrain had allowed them.

That fact did not absolve him.

It did mean eight people were alive.

The throne room doors stood open.

No barricade.

No archers.

No oil.

Montegrain stood at the foot of the dais.

The crown was on the throne.

Luceran saw it.

For the first time since returning to Edselsto, he saw the physical thing that had been taken from him.

He felt almost nothing.

That frightened him.

Montegrain's remaining people had their weapons lowered.

Rhyse stopped at the threshold.

"Terms?"

Montegrain looked at Luceran.

"No."

Rhyse's face hardened.

Montegrain raised one hand.

"No terms for me."

He looked at his people.

"They were under my command. I ask the protections Aven asked."

Luceran said, "You don't get to ask me for anything."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

"You stood on my wall."

"Yes."

"You offered me surrender."

"Yes."

"You let them kill—"

Luceran stopped.

Miren moved beside him.

Montegrain's face changed.

"I did not order Lio's death."

"You joined the people who made it possible."

"Yes."

"You thought you could correct me."

"Yes."

"You thought taking my home, my crown and my people would teach me government."

Montegrain looked at the blood on Luceran.

"No."

Luceran laughed once.

"Now you learn humility?"

"I thought removing you temporarily would force a settlement."

"A settlement."

"I thought Aven could hold Rusk."

"Aven thought Aven could hold Rusk."

"Yes."

"And you?"

Montegrain looked at the throne.

"I thought I could hold you."

That landed harder than any excuse.

Luceran walked toward him.

Rhyse said, "Majesty."

Luceran kept walking.

Montegrain did not reach for his sword.

Luceran stopped close enough to see the burst vessels in his friend's eyes.

"You were my friend."

"I am."

Luceran hit him.

Not with the sword.

With his fist.

Montegrain fell.

Nobody moved.

Luceran stood over him.

"You do not get that word."

Montegrain touched his mouth.

Blood came away on his fingers.

"Fair."

Luceran kicked his sword away.

"Bind him."

Two guards approached.

Montegrain held out his hands.

One guard looked at Luceran.

Luceran understood the question.

Kill him?

Not in words.

Worse.

In permission.

Luceran looked at Montegrain.

At the man who had known him before the crown sat properly. Who had eaten with him, mocked him, advised him, disagreed with him, loved him in whatever complicated form friendship between powerful men became.

Luceran wanted him dead.

The wanting was clean.

Simple.

Almost restful.

Miren said nothing.

Rhyse said nothing.

That was why the choice remained his.

"Bind him."

The guard did.

Montegrain closed his eyes.

Luceran turned away.

"All of them. Weapons down. Names taken. Wounded first."

One of Montegrain's people began to cry.

Quietly.

Like someone who had expected death and found paperwork instead.

---

Victory did not sound like cheering.

It sounded like inventory.

"Twenty-six prisoners in the lower hall."

"Thirty-one."

"That count includes wounded."

"It should."

"Not if Calienne has them."

"Calienne says bodies are not jurisdictions."

"She said that?"

"More or less."

"Rusk?"

"Bound."

"Aven?"

"Bound."

"Montegrain?"

"Bound."

"Upper west?"

"Being searched."

"Fire?"

"Contained."

"Archive?"

"Damaged, not lost."

"Kitchen?"

"Usable if nobody minds blood."

"Nobody is eating there tonight."

"Then unusable."

The throne room filled.

Not ceremonially.

It filled because it was large.

Wounded were laid along the walls.

Prisoners sat in groups under guard.

Officers argued over lists.

Harl Ven refused to surrender the payment ledger until somebody wrote his name beside the fact that he had surrendered it.

Ysabet wrote it.

Then wrote that he had probably helped create some of the records he was now preserving.

Harl read that too.

"Is that necessary?"

"Yes."

Pellan Vey was brought in under guard because nobody knew where else to classify him.

A loyal soldier pointed at him.

"He wore their mark."

Another said, "He saved the king."

Pellan said, "I surrendered."

Mara said, "You also fought after surrender."

"I fought the man trying to kill the king."

"That is still fighting."

"I know."

Rhyse rubbed his face.

"Separate holding."

Pellan nodded.

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"Not solving me quickly."

Rhyse stared at him.

"Do not make me regret it."

Calienne entered with blood to both elbows.

Everyone moved out of her way.

She hated that.

"Stop doing that."

Nobody knew what she meant.

She pointed at Rhyse.

"I need the west gallery cleared for wounded."

"It is evidence."

"It is floor."

"There are bodies."

"Yes. That is why I need it."

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

Luceran said, "Give it to her."

Ysabet said, "After the bodies are marked."

Calienne turned.

"Five minutes."

"Ten."

"Seven."

"Done."

Luceran looked between them.

The kingdom had returned.

It was bargaining over corpses.

---

Aven and Rusk saw one another again in chains.

Aven looked at Rusk's bruised face.

"Miren?"

Rusk said nothing.

Miren, across the room, said, "Yes."

Aven nodded.

"Good."

Rusk looked at him.

"You sanctimonious coward."

"You burned records."

"You surrendered."

"I preserved people."

"You preserved yourself."

"Also true."

Luceran heard them.

He wanted to order silence.

Instead he listened.

Aven looked at him.

"Majesty."

"Don't."

Aven stopped.

Rusk laughed.

Luceran turned on him.

"You do not get to enjoy this."

"I enjoy hypocrisy wherever I find it."

Harl Ven said from the records table, "My lord Rusk."

Rusk looked.

Harl held up the payment ledger.

For the first time, Rusk looked afraid.

Only for a moment.

It was enough.

Luceran saw.

So did Miren.

So did Ysabet.

So did three guards who would later remember seeing it.

Evidence was not only paper.

---

They brought the dead in by names where they could.

Jalen Orris.

Iven Marrick.

Edrik Saye.

Lio Venn.

Others.

Too many others.

Some belonged to Luceran's force.

Some had fought against him.

Some had changed sides halfway through.

Some had probably never understood which side they were on.

Calienne refused to let anyone create two rows.

"No loyal dead and rebel dead?"

"No."

"Canon—"

"They are dead."

That ended it.

Mara found Jalen's shield.

The bolt hole was still in it.

She put it beside him.

Then she sat on the floor.

Ardel sat beside her.

Neither spoke.

Across the room, Anja arrived with Bram on a litter and Mikel walking behind.

Luceran went to them.

Bram looked worse.

Calienne had wrapped his chest.

"You hit Rusk."

Bram closed his eyes.

"I am never going to be allowed to forget that."

"No."

"Good."

Luceran looked at Anja.

She had soot in her hair.

"Your people saved the archive."

"They saved themselves."

"And the archive."

"That too."

Luceran nodded.

He wanted to say something kingly.

Nothing came.

Anja looked at him.

"Sit down."

"What?"

"You look terrible."

Miren, passing behind him, said, "I have been saying this."

Luceran almost smiled.

Then someone called for him from the prisoner table.

Then someone from the records table.

Then Rhyse.

Then Calienne.

Then Montegrain.

Luceran closed his eyes.

The castle was his again.

Everyone needed something.

---

Montegrain sat beneath the east wall with his hands bound.

Luceran stood in front of him.

"What?"

Montegrain looked toward the crown.

"You should take it."

"No."

That surprised him.

Luceran followed his gaze.

The crown remained on the throne exactly where the coup had put it.

An object.

Gold.

Weight.

"Not from there."

Montegrain understood.

"Luceran."

"Do not."

"I need you to know one thing."

"I need you to stop deciding what I need."

Montegrain looked down.

"All right."

Luceran waited.

That was the cruelty of it.

Montegrain had something to say.

Luceran wanted to hear it.

He hated himself for wanting it.

"What?"

Montegrain looked up.

"I was wrong about Rusk."

"That is your revelation?"

"No."

"What, then?"

"I was wrong about you."

Luceran went very still.

Montegrain continued.

"I thought you would choose the castle over the people outside it."

Luceran looked around the room.

At the wounded.

At the prisoners.

At the dead.

"I nearly did."

"But you didn't."

"Do not turn this into praise."

"I am not."

"Good."

"I am telling you why I did it."

"You did it because you thought you knew me."

"Yes."

"And you didn't."

"No."

Luceran looked at him for a long time.

"Neither did I."

He walked away before Montegrain could answer.

---

Night reached Edselsto without anyone noticing.

The seven ancestral lights burned in the family nave.

Canon Merel counted them himself.

Once.

Twice.

Seven.

He did not tell Luceran.

Not yet.

He told Calienne.

She said, "Good."

Merel said, "You do not sound relieved."

"I am relieved that one thing did not happen."

"That is not the same as being relieved."

"No."

They stood together among wounded people who had been moved beneath sacred images because sacred images occupied dry walls.

Merel looked toward the nave door.

"What opened the gate?"

Calienne tied off a bandage.

"The gate opened."

"That is not what I asked."

"It is what I know."

Merel looked at her.

She looked back.

He nodded.

"Seven."

"Seven."

For now, that was enough.

---

Near midnight, Rhyse found Luceran standing in the throne room with no one immediately speaking to him.

This was sufficiently rare that Rhyse almost did not interrupt.

"The upper house is secure."

Luceran nodded.

"All of it?"

"All searched rooms. Some sealed rooms remain sealed."

"Good."

"Rusk's people?"

"Accounted for as far as we can account for anyone tonight."

"Aven?"

"Still in custody."

"Montegrain?"

"Still in custody."

Luceran looked toward him.

Montegrain had fallen asleep sitting against the wall.

Bound hands in his lap.

Rhyse said, "You should sleep."

"So should you."

"I outrank sleep."

"I am the king."

"Then perhaps command it."

Luceran laughed.

It hurt.

Rhyse looked at the throne.

"Sit."

Luceran's face changed.

Rhyse saw it.

"Not ceremony."

"No."

"Then don't make it one."

Luceran looked at the chair.

His father's throne.

His grandfather's.

The thing Montegrain's people had taken.

The thing Rusk had used as a prop.

The thing Luceran had spent the entire day bleeding toward.

He walked up the steps.

The crown was still there.

He picked it up.

Everyone in the room noticed.

Nobody spoke.

Luceran looked at it.

Then set it on the table beside the throne.

Not on his head.

Miren watched.

Ardel watched.

Mara watched.

Aven, awake again, watched.

Rusk watched.

Montegrain slept through it.

Luceran turned and sat.

Not straight.

Not beautifully.

He collapsed.

His back hit the carved wood.

His head fell against one side.

His cut hand hung over the armrest.

For several seconds he did nothing.

The throne room continued without him.

Lists.

Bandages.

Whispers.

Boots.

Someone crying.

Someone laughing too loudly because they had survived.

Someone asking where the clean water had gone.

Then the room became cold.

Luceran opened his eyes.

Black vapor moved across the floor.

Not much.

A thread.

It came from beneath the throne.

Miren saw it first.

Her hand went to her sword.

Rhyse turned.

The talking stopped by degrees.

Something pale appeared in the air before Luceran.

Not a face.

A mask.

Silver-white.

Theatrical.

Smooth cheeks.

A narrow mouth.

Empty eyes that somehow seemed to look directly at him.

It floated at the height of a standing person's face.

Nobody moved.

Luceran stared.

The mask inclined itself.

A bow.

Small.

Perfect.

Then Edselsto spoke.

"Your Majesty."
