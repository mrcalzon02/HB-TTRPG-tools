# Season One, Episode 31 — Open the Gates

"OPEN THE GATES!"

The words struck Edselsto hard enough to come back.

Luceran heard them once from the gatehouse stone, once from the wall above, and once again from somewhere deeper in the castle where no echo should have reached.

For a heartbeat nothing happened.

Montegrain stared down at him.

Rusk smiled.

Aven closed his eyes.

Then the mountain answered.

The first sound was not thunder.

It was a latch.

A single iron latch, very large and very old, withdrawing inside the gatehouse.

Then another.

Then six at once.

Men on the wall turned toward the sound.

Someone shouted, "Winch!"

The outer doors shuddered.

Black vapor began to seep from the seams around them.

Not smoke. There was no fire. It poured downward against the wind and spread ankle-low across the stones beneath the arch.

Rhyse seized Luceran by the back of his cuirass and dragged him three steps sideways.

"What did you do?"

"I don't know."

Above them, Montegrain had gone white.

The portcullis lifted one handspan.

A defender threw himself onto the brake lever.

It moved under him.

He screamed for help.

Three more men caught the lever. Their boots skidded over the gatehouse floor as something on the other end of the mechanism pulled with patient, impossible strength.

The portcullis rose another handspan.

Another.

The chains began moving.

They had no hands on them.

Then they did.

Pale shapes appeared around the capstan.

Not men.

Not fully.

The suggestion of shoulders. Sleeves. Hands where hands were needed. Faces that never became faces.

They did not look at the living.

They worked.

One spectral figure leaned into a spoke.

Another took a chain.

A third reached through a defender's arm as though the man were fog and released the second brake.

The defender recoiled so violently that he fell backward.

The apparitions never struck him.

They never even seemed to notice him.

The gate continued upward.

Black vapor rolled out beneath it.

Rhyse looked once at Luceran.

Luceran had no explanation to give.

Rhyse did not ask for one.

He turned.

"SHIELDS!"

The road exploded into motion.

---

The first man through the opening died before the portcullis reached his shoulders.

Jalen Orris of Mara's Red Ford twelve had been standing nearest the ram crew when Rhyse called. He dropped the timber, snatched up his shield and ran.

A crossbow bolt entered above the rim and drove through his throat.

He fell on his back under the gate.

The second man tripped over him.

The third dragged the second upright.

Then the defenders fired together.

Bolts struck shields like hammers.

One punched through a village buckler and the forearm behind it.

Another ricocheted from a helmet and tore a man's ear away.

Rhyse was already running.

"Do not bunch in the throat! Left wall! Left wall!"

Luceran ran after him.

Miren caught his belt.

"No."

He tore free.

She caught him again.

"Not first."

"I am not standing in the road."

"Then be fifth."

It was absurd enough that he obeyed.

Rhyse entered first among the command group.

Mara went behind him.

Two Doorwardens went next.

Luceran counted four and went.

Miren swore and followed.

The black vapor reached his knees.

It was cold.

Not winter cold.

Cellar cold. Stone cold. The cold of a room shut for generations.

He expected hands.

Voices.

Something.

Nothing touched him.

The spectral workers remained at the mechanisms above, hauling the gate open as living defenders fought to stop them.

A man with a hammer struck at one pale wrist.

The hammer passed through.

The wrist continued turning the capstan.

Then Luceran was inside.

The gatehouse smelled of oil, sweat, old stone and fresh blood.

A defender lunged from the right.

Rhyse caught the spearshaft against his shield.

Mara stepped inside it and drove her sword under the man's ribs.

The man made a small astonished sound.

Mara shoved him off the blade.

"Left!"

Luceran turned.

Someone he knew was there.

Guardsman Pellan Vey.

Pellan had stood west stair watches for four months. He had once complained to Luceran that the kitchen sent cold pies after midnight and the king had told him to complain to the kitchen rather than the throne.

Now Pellan wore a strip of white cloth around one arm.

Coup mark.

His sword was up.

Luceran's was up.

They recognized one another.

Neither moved.

Behind Pellan, another defender shouted, "Kill him!"

Pellan looked over his shoulder.

That saved Luceran's life.

The other defender fired.

Pellan struck the crossbow aside.

The bolt hit stone.

"Down!" Pellan shouted.

At Luceran.

At the crossbowman.

At everyone.

Nobody knew.

Rhyse did.

He kicked Pellan's sword away, slammed him against the wall with a shield and shouted, "Yield?"

"Yes!"

"Stay down!"

Pellan dropped.

Rhyse moved on.

That was all the trial he received.

For now.

---

Above the gate, Montegrain grabbed Aven's arm.

"We lose the gatehouse."

Aven was watching the impossible capstan.

"We already lost it."

"Then pull back."

Rusk said, "Hold."

Montegrain turned.

"With what?"

"The inner receiving doors."

"Those are not a defensive line."

"They are doors."

"They open into a hall with three service entries and a gallery above it."

Rusk's eyes narrowed.

"You know the castle well."

"So does the king."

Below them, men screamed.

Aven looked toward the inner stairs.

"Withdraw the wall companies to the lower hall. Any man who yields is not to be cut down. Chapel protection remains."

Rusk laughed.

"You are issuing surrender law while the gate is falling."

"I am issuing orders."

"To whose men?"

Aven looked at him.

That question had become dangerous.

Montegrain said, "Mine will withdraw."

Rusk said, "Yours?"

"Did you imagine I came alone?"

For the first time Rusk looked uncertain.

Montegrain did not enjoy it.

That surprised him.

He had spent two days telling himself that when Luceran returned, the sight of him would make the decision easier.

It had not.

Then Luceran had shouted at a castle.

And the castle had obeyed.

Montegrain looked down through the murder slit.

Black vapor crawled across the gatehouse floor.

Pale hands worked the chains.

His oldest friend was somewhere beneath him, entering through a gate no living person had opened.

There would be time to understand that later.

If there was a later.

"Fall back," Montegrain said.

Aven nodded.

Rusk did not.

---

The gatehouse did not fall.

It broke apart.

That was different.

Some defenders fought because they believed Luceran had become exactly what the coup claimed: a king commanding forces no institution could answer.

Some fought because Rusk's men were behind them.

Some fought because their friends had already died.

Some dropped weapons.

Some tried to run.

A man in Aven's white sash stood on the inner stair and shouted, "Surrender protections! Down weapons and kneel!"

One of Rusk's soldiers struck him in the mouth.

The Aven man hit him back.

For several seconds the coup fought itself while Luceran's people fought both.

Rhyse saw it.

"Leave the kneeling! Push the stairs!"

That order saved lives.

It also cost one.

Sergeant Iven Marrick turned his shield to step around three surrendering defenders.

A spear came between them from a man behind.

It entered Marrick below the arm.

He killed the spearman before he understood he had been hit.

Then he sat down.

Mara found him there.

"Can you walk?"

"No."

"Can you hold this corner?"

Marrick looked at the blood filling his glove.

"For a while."

Mara took his shoulder.

"You do not need to die here."

"I know."

That answer made her leave him two men instead of one.

Later, that would matter.

At the time it was simply the arithmetic of a room.

---

Miren had no business being in the gatehouse.

Everyone told her so.

She ignored them.

She was not a soldier and did not pretend to become one because swords were out. She carried a short blade she had been taught to use badly and a shield she hated.

Her usefulness was elsewhere.

"Pellan!"

The surrendered guardsman looked up.

"Which stair reaches the receiving gallery?"

He stared at her.

"Which one?"

"The north service."

"Is it held?"

"I don't know."

"Who uses it?"

"Kitchen. Lamp boys. Night servants."

Miren pointed at two Doorwardens.

"Find Anja Pell if she's alive. Find anyone from household service. Do not force the stair blind."

Rhyse heard.

"Good."

Luceran heard too.

He was breathing hard.

There was blood on his cheek that was not his.

Miren looked at him.

"You are fifth."

He almost laughed.

Then someone screamed from the arch.

The laugh died.

---

Anja Pell heard the gate open from two floors away.

Not the shout.

The castle.

Old mechanisms had voices if you worked around them long enough. Hinges complained. Counterweights knocked. Bad chains skipped teeth.

This was every gate mechanism in the lower house speaking at once.

The concealed household group stopped in the sorting room.

Bram Tolland lifted his head.

"What was that?"

Anja said, "Main gate."

"Opening?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I don't know."

Mikel Orr whispered, "Does it matter?"

Bram tried to stand.

His wounded leg folded.

Anja caught him.

"No."

"The king is here."

"You don't know that."

"Who else is opening the gate?"

Anja thought of Rusk.

Aven.

Montegrain.

Then she thought of the sound.

"No one," she said.

Bram stared at her.

The kitchen boy who had remembered the pulley began to cry.

Not loudly.

Relief did that sometimes.

Anja crouched in front of him.

"Oren?"

"That's not Oren," Bram said.

The boy sniffed.

"Fenn."

Anja closed her eyes.

"Fenn. Sorry."

He had been hiding with them for a day and she had called him the wrong name.

The castle was full of people becoming functions.

Kitchen boy.

Wounded guard.

Laundress.

Prisoner.

Traitor.

Loyalist.

She put both hands around Fenn's.

"Fenn. Which stair from here reaches the north service landing?"

His eyes widened.

"My mother's."

"Yes."

"Past the flour store."

"Can Rusk's men see it from the lower hall?"

"Not if the screen's still there."

Bram said, "We can get behind them."

Anja looked at his leg.

"You can get back on the floor."

"I can tell someone else."

That was better.

Anja nodded.

She chose four people who could move.

Not Bram.

Not Mikel.

Not herself.

Fenn led them.

A kitchen boy went to find the king's army inside the king's own castle.

---

Calienne Orsanne heard fighting before she heard the gate.

The chapel had become a hospital, morgue, sanctuary and argument.

Aven's protection order was nailed to the outer door.

Rusk's men had ignored it twice and been turned away once by their own officer.

Calienne had stopped believing paper was protection.

She believed in witnesses.

So she had them.

Every patient who could speak knew the names of the guards at the door.

Every assistant knew who had entered.

Every body was tagged.

Every surrendered weapon was stacked in sight.

When the first wounded man staggered into the chapel yard, Calienne recognized Rusk's colors.

He saw her and raised his sword.

Then he dropped it.

"Help."

She did.

One of her assistants stared.

"He was searching rooms yesterday."

"Yes."

"He dragged Meret out by her hair."

"I know."

The man fell to his knees.

Blood pumped through his fingers.

Calienne pointed.

"Table."

They lifted him.

Outside, someone shouted that the king was through the gate.

A patient began cheering.

Calienne slapped the table hard enough to silence half the room.

"This is still a hospital."

The wounded Rusk man looked at her.

"You'll save me?"

"If I can."

"Why?"

"Because if I start deciding who is human while angry, I will eventually become someone you would have liked."

He did not understand.

That was fine.

She cut his sleeve away.

---

The receiving hall had never been intended to stop an army.

It became a fortress because frightened people make fortresses from furniture.

Benches were overturned between pillars.

Two long tables had been tipped on their sides.

A cabinet Luceran had argued about moving in Episode Five—because it belonged to his grandmother and because three different people wanted the wall behind it—lay broken across the central approach.

Miren recognized it.

"So much for the cabinet."

Luceran looked at her.

Now he laughed.

Once.

A bolt struck the pillar beside his head.

The laugh stopped.

Rhyse crouched behind the broken cabinet.

"Two ranks. Maybe twenty. Gallery has crossbows."

Ardel arrived through the gatehouse with six Kaelmont retainers.

Rhyse looked at her.

"Your road."

"My people."

"I need the gallery."

"You need someone to reach it."

"Yes."

"I have climbers."

"Inside?"

"We own mountains."

"This is a staircase."

"Then it should be easy."

Rhyse stared at her.

Ardel grinned without humor.

"North service?"

Miren said, "Household stair. Pellan says it reaches the gallery."

"Held?"

"Unknown."

Ardel looked at her people.

"With me."

Luceran said, "Ardel."

She turned.

He almost ordered her not to go.

He saw her recognize the impulse.

"Do you need anything?" he asked instead.

"Two Doorwardens who know which doors lock from which side."

Rhyse pointed.

"Take them."

Ardel left.

Her force remained hers.

Her choice remained hers.

The king benefited anyway.

That mattered.

---

Rusk reached the lower hall before Montegrain.

He had thirty-two men there and more arriving.

The corridor behind them led toward the great hall approaches.

The left route led toward kitchens and service passages.

The right led toward the old administrative rooms.

Too many ways.

He hated castles built for living.

Fortresses were honest.

Montegrain came down the stair.

"Receiving hall?"

"Hold it."

"For how long?"

"As long as required."

"Required for what?"

Rusk did not answer.

Montegrain understood.

"For you to move what?"

Rusk looked at him.

There it was.

The question beneath the coup.

Not whether Luceran should be corrected.

Not whether the estates had rights.

Not whether Edselsto should remain the seat.

What had Rusk been doing while everyone argued law?

Montegrain said, "You are withdrawing something."

Aven arrived behind him.

Rusk's hand went to his sword.

Aven's did not.

"Sir Cale," Aven said, "where are the accession papers?"

"Secure."

"Where?"

"Secure."

"The payment records?"

"Secure."

Montegrain looked between them.

Aven understood at the same moment.

Rusk smiled.

"Win the hall."

Then he walked away with eight men.

Aven said, "Stop him."

Montegrain said, "With whose men?"

Again the question.

This time no one had an answer.

---

Ardel found Fenn before she found the gallery.

The kitchen boy came around the flour-store corner too fast and ran directly into a Kaelmont shield.

He bounced.

A retainer caught him before he hit the floor.

Fenn looked at six armed strangers and inhaled to scream.

Ardel put one finger to her lips.

"King?"

Fenn pointed behind her.

"Gate."

"No. I mean, are you for him?"

Fenn nodded violently.

"Good. Gallery?"

He pointed upward.

"Men there."

"How many?"

"Six. Maybe seven."

"Crossbows?"

"Yes."

"Other way?"

He nodded.

"Serving hatch. But small."

"How small?"

Fenn looked at her armor.

"For you?"

He considered.

"Bad."

One of Ardel's retainers snorted.

Ardel looked at the serving hatch.

Then at the narrow service stair.

Then at the flour sacks.

"What is above the gallery?"

"Old linen passage."

"Can we get there?"

Fenn nodded.

"Show me."

He did.

Nobody ordered him.

Nobody told him he was brave.

He was terrified.

He knew the route.

That was enough.

---

Rhyse did not wait for the gallery to be cleared.

He could not.

Every minute gave the defenders time to reinforce.

He split the receiving hall problem into smaller problems.

Shield line to the first pillar.

Crossbow return fire at the gallery.

Two men to drag wounded back.

No pursuit beyond the far doors until the gallery was suppressed.

Luceran heard the orders and understood perhaps half.

He stayed where Rhyse put him until a wounded Doorwarden fell in front of him.

Then he moved.

The man had a bolt in his thigh.

Luceran grabbed his harness and dragged.

The Doorwarden screamed.

"Sorry."

"Pull!"

Luceran pulled.

A second bolt hit the floor where the man's head had been.

Miren took his other shoulder.

Together they dragged him behind the cabinet.

"Your Majesty," the Doorwarden gasped.

"Not now."

"You are bleeding."

Luceran touched his cheek.

"Not mine."

"Arm."

He looked.

A shallow cut ran along his left forearm.

He had not noticed.

Miren had.

Her face went blank in the way it did when fear was trying to become anger.

"It is nothing."

"Do not say that."

"It is very little."

"Better."

Rhyse shouted, "Forward!"

They moved.

---

Above them, a flour sack fell out of the ceiling.

The gallery defenders looked up.

A second sack burst against the rail.

White dust exploded through the air.

One crossbowman fired blindly.

Another coughed.

Then Ardel Kaelmont came through the linen opening with a knife in her teeth because the space had been too narrow for a drawn sword.

She hit the floor on both knees.

A defender swung at her.

She caught his wrist with both hands and drove her forehead into his nose.

The Kaelmont retainer behind her came through headfirst.

The gallery became seven people trying to kill one another in flour.

Fenn stayed in the linen passage.

He did not join.

He did something more useful.

He ran back.

"Now!" he screamed down the service stair.

The Doorwardens below heard him.

Rhyse heard them.

"Gallery!"

The loyal line surged.

One of the coup crossbowmen looked down, saw Luceran's people coming, and threw his weapon away.

Another kept firing.

Ardel kicked his legs from under him.

The third jumped from the gallery rather than surrender.

He landed badly.

The sound his ankle made was louder than his scream.

Then the receiving hall was no longer defensible.

Men began to run.

Aven's white-sashed officer shouted after them.

"Lower hall! Lower hall! Keep formation!"

Rusk's men shouted something else.

The retreat split.

Rhyse saw it.

"Do not chase the right corridor!"

Luceran pointed.

"They're going deeper."

"Yes."

"We need the hall."

"They'll regroup."

"Yes."

"Then—"

Rhyse turned on him.

"Do you want the castle or do you want every back you can see?"

Luceran stopped.

The answer was obvious.

That did not make it easy.

"The castle."

"Then take rooms."

---

By late afternoon, the dead had begun to interfere with movement.

That was how Luceran knew the battle was larger than anger.

Bodies were no longer dramatic.

They were obstacles.

Jalen Orris still lay near the gate because the first aid crews had taken the living first.

Sergeant Marrick had died at the gatehouse corner after holding it long enough for surrendered defenders to be searched and moved.

Pellan Vey sat against a wall with his hands bound, repeatedly telling anyone who passed that he had surrendered before the second push.

No one had time to decide what that meant.

Three coup defenders lay in the receiving hall.

One had no visible wound until someone rolled him over.

Another was still breathing and had been marked with chalk for removal.

The man who had jumped from the gallery was alive and swearing at everyone.

Calienne's assistants had begun moving through secured spaces with strips of blue cloth.

Rhyse ordered both sides to let them pass.

Most did.

One did not.

Mara broke his wrist.

Then she sent him to Calienne.

---

The lower hall became the first place the assault stopped.

Not ended.

Stopped.

The defenders had prepared it.

Heavy tables.

Spear points through gaps.

Crossbows behind.

Oil on the floor, but no flame yet.

Rhyse smelled it.

"So they considered fire."

Luceran looked at the polished stone shining wetly.

"Can we go around?"

"Probably."

"How many ways?"

Miren said, "Four that I know."

Rhyse looked at her.

"That you know."

"Yes."

The castle had spent months teaching them how little anyone knew about it.

Now ignorance was terrain.

A messenger arrived from Mara.

"West service secured to the old accounts room. Lady Mara requests men to hold prisoners."

Another arrived from Ardel.

"Lady Kaelmont has the receiving gallery and north service. She says she is not advancing until someone tells her who controls the kitchen junction."

A third came from the gate.

"More village volunteers."

Rhyse swore.

"Keep them outside."

Luceran said, "Why?"

"Because enthusiasm is not armor."

The messenger left.

Miren looked down the oil-slick hall.

"Kitchen junction."

"What?"

"If Anja is alive, she knows it."

Luceran looked at her.

"You think she can hear us?"

"No."

"Then how—"

A voice behind them said, "She sent us."

Fenn stood there covered in flour.

Beside him were three household workers and a limping guard Luceran did not recognize.

Fenn looked at the king.

Luceran looked at Fenn.

"You opened the gallery."

Fenn's eyes widened.

"No."

"Ardel says—"

"I showed her."

Luceran stopped.

That was different.

"Good."

Fenn pointed toward the left service door.

"Anja says don't use the kitchen junction."

Rhyse crouched to his height.

"Why?"

"Rusk's men took it."

"How many?"

"She doesn't know."

"Then why warn us?"

"Because they moved lamp oil."

Rhyse looked at the wet floor.

Then toward the service door.

"Where from?"

"Kitchen stores."

"To here?"

Fenn shook his head.

"Other way."

Rhyse stood.

"This hall is bait."

Luceran understood.

Not enough oil to burn the hall.

Enough to make them fear it.

The real fire was somewhere else.

"Archives?" Luceran said.

Miren's face changed.

"Records."

Accession papers.

Payment records.

Seal registers.

Evidence.

Rusk was not merely retreating.

He was cleaning.

Luceran pointed left.

"Find him."

Rhyse said, "We hold this hall first."

"Rhyse—"

"If we open three routes with no secure rear, we lose wounded and prisoners when they come behind us."

Luceran hated him.

Again.

"Fine."

Rhyse looked at Fenn.

"Can you get a message to Anja?"

Fenn nodded.

"Tell her no heroics. Watch the kitchen route. Report smoke."

Fenn nodded again.

Luceran said, "And tell her Bram?"

Fenn understood.

"Alive."

Luceran closed his eyes for half a second.

"Tell her I know."

Fenn ran.

---

Montegrain heard the same reports from the other side.

Gatehouse lost.

Receiving hall lost.

North service compromised.

Kaelmont inside.

Mara Kest holding prisoners.

Rhyse advancing methodically.

Luceran alive.

That last report came twice.

He hated himself for the relief.

Aven stood over a table in the old petitions room, marking positions with coins because the proper military map had gone missing during the seizure.

"We cannot hold the lower hall if the kitchens turn."

Montegrain said, "Then don't."

Aven looked up.

"Withdraw?"

"To the great hall approaches."

"That abandons half the lower house."

"Half the lower house is already gone."

Aven rubbed both hands over his face.

"What did he do?"

Montegrain knew who he meant.

"I don't know."

"The gate."

"I don't know."

"Did he know?"

"No."

"You are certain?"

Montegrain thought of Luceran's face.

The rage.

The surprise when the chains moved.

"Yes."

Aven looked almost relieved.

Montegrain understood that too.

If Luceran had knowingly commanded the castle, then the coup's worst arguments acquired teeth.

If he had not—

That was worse in another way.

Aven said, "Rusk is burning records."

"I think so."

"Then he planned for failure."

"Or for victory."

Aven looked at him.

Montegrain continued.

"Records burn in either."

"Not mine."

"No."

Aven's mouth hardened.

"My men hold the lower hall. I can order withdrawal."

"Do it."

"And Rusk?"

Montegrain looked toward the corridor where Rusk had vanished.

"Find out what he is saving."

---

The lower hall did not fall with a charge.

It fell with an argument.

Rhyse sent a herald forward under shield.

"By command of Luceran, lawful King of Embar-Zara: surrendering defenders will be disarmed and held. Aven Marr's medical protections will be honored pending judgment. Men who lay down weapons now will not be killed for surrendering."

A voice behind the barricade shouted, "Liar!"

Another shouted, "Aven said the same!"

A third shouted, "Aven ordered withdrawal!"

Then someone struck someone.

The spear line wavered.

Rhyse waited.

Luceran whispered, "Now."

"No."

A shield dropped.

A man climbed over the barricade with both hands up.

One of Rusk's men shot him in the back.

That decided the room.

Not for everyone.

Enough.

Three defenders turned on the crossbowman.

Two opened the side gap.

Someone kicked a table aside.

Rhyse shouted, "Forward!"

The loyal line hit the barricade.

The fight lasted less than a minute.

It felt longer.

A spear slid along Luceran's breastplate and caught under his arm.

He trapped it against his body.

The defender on the other end was young.

Too young.

Luceran punched him in the face with the pommel.

The boy fell.

Luceran raised his sword.

The boy covered his head.

Luceran stopped.

"Yield."

The boy nodded.

Luceran moved past him.

Two steps later a man came from the side with an axe.

Miren screamed.

Luceran turned too slowly.

Pellan Vey, somehow loose from the prisoner line, hit the attacker from behind with his bound hands.

The axe missed.

Rhyse's soldier killed the attacker.

Then everyone pointed weapons at Pellan.

Pellan dropped to his knees.

"I was still surrendering!"

Luceran stared at him.

Blood ran from Pellan's mouth.

Miren began laughing.

Not because anything was funny.

Because sometimes terror had nowhere else to go.

Luceran started laughing too.

Rhyse did not.

"Bind him again."

Pellan groaned.

"Properly this time."

"Yes, Captain."

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

"Castle."

Luceran nodded.

"Castle."

---

At sunset, Edselsto belonged to no one.

The outer gate stood open.

The gatehouse belonged to Luceran.

The receiving hall belonged to Luceran.

The north service belonged to Ardel.

Mara held the west rooms and an expanding prisoner collection.

Calienne's chapel remained Calienne's because everyone had finally learned not to argue with her while bleeding.

The lower hall belonged to Luceran in the sense that his soldiers stood in it and men were still dying on its floor.

The kitchens were contested.

The administrative wing had smoke in it.

The great hall approaches remained in coup hands.

The upper house remained closed.

The throne room remained beyond them all.

Rusk had disappeared deeper with records, men and purpose.

Aven was withdrawing his people in something like order.

Montegrain was withdrawing his in something like shame.

Bram Tolland was alive somewhere inside.

Anja Pell was alive somewhere inside.

Mikel Orr was alive unless the next hour changed that.

All seven ancestral lights still burned.

Luceran stood in the lower hall and looked back through three open doors toward the road outside.

He could see evening sky.

For two days the gate had been the entire world.

Now it was open.

He should have felt victorious.

Instead he saw Jalen Orris carried past under a cloak.

Sergeant Marrick followed.

A boy in coup colors sat against a pillar holding his own teeth in his palm.

Calienne's assistants shouted for water.

Mara argued with a Doorwarden about prisoner space.

Ardel came down from the north service with flour in her hair and someone else's blood on her sleeve.

Miren leaned against a wall, shaking now that nobody needed her not to.

Rhyse had stopped asking what Luceran had done.

That frightened Luceran more than the question.

Montegrain was somewhere ahead.

Luceran looked at the open gate.

The black vapor had thinned.

The pale workers were gone.

The chains hung still.

No voice spoke.

No explanation came.

He had shouted.

Edselsto had obeyed.

Miren came to stand beside him.

"Do not make it mean anything yet."

Luceran looked at her.

She was watching the gate too.

"I wasn't."

"You were."

He almost denied it.

Then he didn't.

"What does it mean?"

"I don't know."

That answer should have been frightening.

Instead, after two days of arguments and bargains and refusals and friends on walls, it felt almost gentle.

Rhyse approached.

"Majesty."

Luceran turned.

"How bad?"

"Bad."

"Can we continue?"

"Yes."

"Should we?"

Rhyse looked toward the smoke in the administrative wing.

"Yes."

No hesitation.

That mattered.

"How far?"

"Secure kitchens. Stop the fire if there is one. Hold the service crossings. Establish casualty routes. Then we decide whether we push the great hall tonight."

Luceran looked deeper into his castle.

"Montegrain?"

"Still ahead."

"Rusk?"

"Still ahead."

"Aven?"

"Still ahead."

Everything was still ahead.

Good.

That meant nobody had escaped consequence yet.

Luceran drew a breath.

"Then take the next room."

Rhyse nodded.

Not the castle.

Not the throne.

The next room.

Behind them, Edselsto's gates remained open.
