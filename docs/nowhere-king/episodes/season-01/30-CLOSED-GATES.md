# Season One, Episode 30 — Closed Gates

By morning, Luceran had gathered everything Edselsto had left him outside its walls.

Thirty-four riders had returned with him. Ardel Kaelmont held the lower road with her own retainers. Black Pine scouts moved through the woods in pairs because nobody trusted a single message anymore. Village men arrived carrying hunting bows, wood axes, old spears, and one rusted billhook that had probably last been sharpened for a grandfather's war. Tomas Reed came from Northbridge without the six stores wagons because three contradictory orders had demanded them and he had decided that three mutually hostile keyholders were safer than any seal.

It was not an army.

It was enough people to make the road below Edselsto look occupied.

Above them, the gates remained shut.

Rhyse had spent the night making certain Luceran understood what that meant.

They could build ladders.

They could cut timber for a ram.

They could try the north postern.

They could feed one person at a time through the drainage culvert Hessa Dorn had used.

They could attempt the unfinished works passages if anyone outside could find an entrance the people inside had not already lost.

They could burn the lower doors.

They could wait.

Every option had a cost.

Most of the costs had names.

Luceran listened until Rhyse finished.

Then he said, "Again."

Rhyse repeated it.

This time Luceran asked how many men would die carrying a ram uphill.

Rhyse told him he did not know.

"How many before it reaches the gate?"

"If they defend properly? Enough."

"That's not a number."

"No. It's the honest answer."

Luceran looked up at his own arrow slits.

Men moved behind them.

Some wore colors he recognized.

Some did not.

"And ladders?"

"Worse."

"Postern?"

"Possible. Narrow. Watched."

"Culvert?"

"One man at a time without armor. If the inner mouth is held, we deliver prisoners."

"Fire?"

Miren answered before Rhyse could.

"You spent half a year repairing the lower roofs."

Luceran looked at her.

"I know."

"And Calienne has wounded inside."

"I know."

"And Bram may still be alive."

"I know."

Miren let the words sit.

Edrik was dead.

Nobody needed to say that one.

Luceran turned back toward the gate.

"Then we talk."

---

Sir Aven Marr came to the wall beneath a white cloth.

He still called Luceran Majesty.

That surprised almost everyone except Aven.

"Majesty."

Luceran stood in the road with Rhyse at his right and Miren at his left.

Ardel remained ten paces back because she refused to perform loyalty for anyone.

Luceran called up, "Open the gates."

Aven's face tightened.

"I cannot."

"Cannot or will not?"

"Both."

"Then why am I here?"

"To prevent more deaths."

Luceran almost laughed.

It came out as breath.

Aven held up a paper.

"I will recognize the surrender protections already issued. Calienne's medical neutrality remains in force. Named noncombatants can be released. We can exchange prisoners and convene representatives of the estates."

"Return the crown."

"No."

"Open the gates."

"No."

A second figure stepped onto the wall.

Cale Rusk.

Unlike Aven, Rusk did not bow.

"The terms are simpler than Sir Aven makes them," he said. "Dismiss Rhyse. Order Kaelmont off the lower road. Return the records stolen from royal custody. Submit yourself to an estates hearing under guard."

Rhyse did not react to hearing his own dismissal demanded.

Ardel laughed.

Rusk looked down at her.

"You find something amusing?"

"You want me to abandon my own road because you stole someone else's castle."

"It is a Crown road."

"Then perhaps the Crown should ask."

Luceran raised one hand.

Ardel stopped.

Not because she had to.

Because she chose to.

That distinction mattered.

Luceran looked at Aven.

"Are those your terms?"

"No."

Rusk turned.

Aven continued before he could speak.

"My terms do not require Captain Rhyse's dismissal or Lady Kaelmont's withdrawal."

"Your terms no longer secure the castle," Rusk said.

The words were meant as a rebuke.

They became information.

Luceran saw Rhyse notice.

Miren noticed too.

Aven knew what he had exposed.

For a moment the four of them stood on opposite sides of a wall and understood the same thing.

The coup had taken Edselsto.

It had not become one government.

Luceran said, "Release the wounded."

Aven answered, "Those fit to travel can be discussed."

Rusk said, "No one leaves while hostile forces occupy the village."

"They are patients," Aven said.

"They are leverage."

Silence crossed the road.

Rusk realized what he had said in front of witnesses.

Too late.

Luceran looked at him.

"Thank you."

Rusk's expression hardened.

Luceran turned away.

Aven called after him.

"Majesty."

Luceran stopped.

"I am trying to keep this from becoming a massacre."

Luceran looked back.

"So am I."

---

They tried the north postern before noon.

Not by assault.

By signal.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Two.

Pause.

Three.

An old household pattern.

Nothing answered.

A Black Pine scout crawled close enough to see fresh timber bracing the inner door and came back with an arrow through his cloak.

They tried the culvert.

Hessa Dorn stopped them.

"You can send me."

"No," Rhyse said.

"I've done it."

"Which is why they may know it."

"I can still fit."

"That is not a military argument."

"It is if nobody else can."

Luceran listened to them argue and finally said, "No."

Hessa looked at him.

"We have people inside."

"I know."

"Bram could be dying."

"I know."

"Then what are we waiting for?"

Luceran had no answer that did not sound like cowardice.

Miren gave one anyway.

"For a way to save him that does not require feeding you into a hole."

Hessa hated it.

She obeyed.

Not Luceran.

The argument.

That mattered too.

---

Inside the castle, Bram's fever broke.

He woke drenched in sweat and immediately tried to stand.

Anja Pell pushed him back down.

"No."

"How long?"

"Since what?"

"Edrik."

Anja's face changed.

Bram closed his eyes.

"Right."

Mikel Orr was worse.

The water Edrik had carried back had lasted through the night. Barely.

Outside the hidden laundry gallery, searchers had begun measuring walls.

Edrik had bought them ignorance.

Ignorance was expiring.

"We move," Bram said.

Anja looked at his wounded leg.

"Where?"

"The old linen chute."

"With Mikel?"

"We lower him."

"With what rope?"

A kitchen boy beside the wall said, "Sorting pulley."

Everyone looked at him.

"My mother worked here."

Bram nodded.

"Show us."

No ghost moved them.

No secret door opened because Luceran needed one.

A kitchen boy remembered his mother's work.

A laundress knew which pulley had not rotted.

Anja organized the order.

Two wounded guards bit leather while they were lowered.

Bram went last because somebody had to stay until everyone else was through.

By the time Rusk's searchers opened the false panel, the gallery contained dirty linen, an empty water skin, and nobody worth capturing.

---

Calienne received Edrik's body in the chapel yard.

Aven had ordered the dead from both sides identified before heat and haste made that impossible.

Rusk had objected.

Calienne had asked whether he feared the corpses would organize.

Aven permitted the transfer.

A kitchen boy named Oren Pell walked beside the stretcher.

He was fourteen.

Calienne asked why.

"I saw."

"What did you see?"

"The guard. Edrik."

"Tell me only what you saw."

Oren swallowed.

"Six men chased him. They told him to drop his sword. He stopped."

"Did he surrender?"

"No."

"Did they offer?"

"Yes."

"Then?"

"He looked out the arrow slit."

"At what?"

"I don't know."

"Then?"

"He laughed."

Calienne waited.

"He said they were late."

That was all.

Calienne wrote exactly that.

She did not write that Edrik died bravely.

She did not write that he died for the king.

She wrote what a frightened boy had seen.

Then she washed Edrik's face.

By evening the village knew.

Not because Luceran proclaimed it.

A chapel assistant told a cousin. The cousin told a baker. The baker had known Edrik because he stole burned loaf ends and claimed royal security required their disposal.

Small lamps began appearing on the turn below the castle.

Luceran saw them from camp.

"Who ordered that?"

Ardel said, "No one."

A woman he did not know placed another.

"He paid for my grandson's boot repair," she told Luceran.

He had not known.

Of course he had not known.

Edrik had possessed a life outside the radius of his king.

Luceran stood among the lamps until the woman left.

Then he said, "I keep thinking I should know what he would tell me to do."

Rhyse stood behind him.

"You knew him well."

"That's not an answer."

"No."

Luceran turned.

Rhyse's face was exhausted.

"I knew him too."

The words corrected something Luceran had not realized he was doing.

He had been making Edrik's death his.

It was not.

Bram's.

Mikel's.

Anja's.

Rhyse's.

The baker's.

The woman's grandson's.

A kingdom was larger than its king even in grief.

"Sorry," Luceran said.

Rhyse nodded.

It was enough for now.

---

Tomas Reed arrived after dark with no wagons.

Luceran met him below the lamps.

"Where are the stores?"

"Northbridge."

"Why?"

"Three orders told me to bring them here."

Luceran stared.

"That usually causes movement."

"First bore your seal. Second bore Aven's. Third bore no seal and threatened hanging."

"And?"

"They contradicted one another."

"So you did nothing."

"I moved the wagons into the toll enclosure, doubled civilian witnesses, and gave the keys to three people who dislike each other."

Rhyse laughed for the first time since returning.

Tomas looked offended.

"It seemed prudent."

Luceran almost smiled.

"Who?"

"Bridge reeve. Carters' steward. Canon's factor."

"They dislike each other?"

"Professionally."

"Excellent."

The coup had reached Northbridge with paper and failed because an ordinary quartermaster had learned what contradictory authority looked like.

Luceran felt something loosen inside him.

Not hope.

Something sturdier.

People.

---

The second day made everything worse.

More of Luceran's people arrived.

Mara returned with twelve of the eighteen she had kept at Red Ford. The other six remained because the survey, cattle dispute and stand-down did not cease to exist merely because the king wanted his castle back.

Two local houses sent men.

One sent apologies instead.

Black Pine found three possible approaches and rejected all three.

Ardel's road became a camp.

The camp became an army by accumulation rather than design.

Not a great army.

Enough.

Enough men to assault.

Enough men to die.

Rhyse showed Luceran the numbers.

"If you order it, we can take the lower gate."

"Can?"

"Probably."

"How many?"

"I don't know."

"Guess."

"No."

"Rhyse."

"I will not make dead men into arithmetic because you want permission."

Luceran stared at him.

Rhyse stared back.

Episode Sixteen's agreement still existed.

The king could override him.

The override would be recorded.

Luceran could order the assault.

Rhyse would lead it.

That was the horror of authority.

Luceran looked away first.

"No assault."

"Recorded."

"You're insufferable."

"Recorded."

For one second Luceran heard Edrik laughing.

It nearly destroyed him.

---

That afternoon Aven requested parley again.

This time he stood directly above the gate.

Rusk remained behind him with armed men.

Aven looked tired enough to be older.

Luceran looked worse.

"Majesty," Aven said.

"Open the gates."

"No."

"Then stop calling me Majesty."

Aven flinched.

Only slightly.

"I will not."

"Why?"

"Because taking your residence did not unmake your coronation."

"Then what did you think you were doing?"

Aven's answer took time.

"Stopping you."

"From what?"

"Becoming a king no institution could correct."

Luceran laughed once.

"By taking my crown."

"By forcing an estates settlement."

"With Cale Rusk?"

Aven's jaw tightened.

"That was not the arrangement I believed I was making."

"There it is."

"I was wrong about him."

"You were wrong about me."

"Perhaps."

Luceran stared up at the man who had once ridden beside him.

A Royal Companion.

A friend.

Someone who had eaten at his table and argued politics after midnight and known exactly how young the king really was beneath the title.

Aven said, "Surrender now."

The road went quiet.

Aven continued.

"I have the castle. I have the throne. My defenders control the castle you call home. Surrender and you will be treated with dignity."

Luceran did not answer.

Aven leaned over the stone.

"I mean it."

That made it worse.

"You can keep your household. Your personal property will be respected. There will be an estates hearing. No humiliation. No cage. No public chains."

Rusk watched from behind him.

Miren watched Luceran.

Rhyse watched the walls.

Ardel watched everyone.

Aven said, "This is over."

Something in Luceran broke.

Not strategically.

Not intelligently.

Not majestically.

He had spent two days listening to reasons.

He had counted arrows.

He had counted doors.

He had counted wounded people.

He had let Rhyse tell him no.

He had let Miren tell him he did not know what Edrik would have wanted.

He had watched strangers light lamps for a man he loved.

He had stood outside his own home while another man offered to let him keep his dignity.

Luceran stepped into the middle of the road.

Rhyse said, "Majesty."

Luceran ignored him.

He looked at Aven.

At Rusk.

At the soldiers on the wall.

At the narrow windows behind them where some servant or guard or wounded friend might still be listening.

And he screamed.

"OPEN THE GATES!"

It was not a spell.

It was not a prayer.

It was a tantrum with a crown behind it.

An order to Aven.

An order to the soldiers.

An order to every wavering soul inside.

An order to stone because there was nobody else left to order.

The words struck the wall.

For one heartbeat, nothing happened.

Aven closed his eyes.

Rusk smiled.

Then every chain in the lower gatehouse moved.

Not dropped.

Moved.

The great winch turned.

No hands touched it.

Men on the wall shouted.

The inner portcullis began to rise.

A soldier grabbed the brake lever with both hands.

It tore itself out of his grip.

The outer leaves shuddered.

Iron bolts as thick as a man's wrist withdrew into stone.

Black vapor poured through the seams.

Not smoke.

Something colder.

It crawled over the gatehouse floor and around the boots of men who had taken the mechanisms apart to prevent exactly this.

Someone screamed.

Above the arch, pale shapes moved where no bodies stood.

Not people.

The suggestion of attendants.

Hands without arms.

Mechanisms without operators.

The outer doors opened.

Slowly.

Impossibly.

On both sides of the gate, everyone stopped.

Luceran stared.

He had not known Edselsto could hear him.

He had not known he had been speaking to it.

Aven's face emptied.

Rusk shouted, "CLOSE IT!"

Men threw themselves onto the winch.

The winch continued turning beneath their hands.

Rhyse recovered first.

He drew his sword.

"FORWARD!"

That was human.

Everything after was human.

Luceran drew his own sword and ran.

Miren shouted his name.

Ardel shouted something much less printable.

The amassed loyalists surged uphill.

The gate stood open.

Edselsto did nothing else.

---

The first men through died.

A crossbowman above the gate put a bolt through a Black Pine scout's throat.

A spear took one of Mara's riders beneath the ribs.

Rhyse reached the inner arch and killed a man Luceran had seen serving wine at the farewell feast.

There was no clean side to look at.

Aven's Companions split.

Two obeyed Rusk and formed at the lower stair.

One threw down his sword.

Another shouted that Aven's surrender protections still stood and was cut across the face for it.

Ardel's people entered from the road and immediately peeled toward receiving rather than following Luceran.

She had her own people to find.

Mara took the great-hall stair.

Rhyse took the guard route.

Luceran went straight ahead until Miren caught his cloak.

"Don't be an idiot!"

"They have the throne."

"They have archers!"

A bolt struck the wall beside them.

Luceran reconsidered.

Briefly.

They went through the side passage.

The castle did not guide them.

They got lost once because a pantry door had been barricaded.

A kitchen maid showed them another route.

A wounded coup soldier surrendered to Miren and told them Rusk had moved men toward the throne room.

Anja Pell emerged from a service stair with blood on her sleeve and stared at Luceran as if he were a ghost.

"Bram?"

"Alive."

"Mikel?"

"Alive."

"Edrik?"

Luceran could not answer.

Anja understood.

"Later," she said.

Then she pointed.

"Rusk took the upper hall."

People.

Always people.

The castle had opened the door.

People did the rest.

---

Sir Aven Marr reached the throne room before Luceran.

Not to escape.

To stop Rusk.

"You knew."

Rusk turned from the dais.

"Knew what?"

"That you could not hold this once he returned."

"We are holding it."

"The gate opened itself."

Rusk's expression twisted.

"Mechanism."

"You watched the brake tear out of Harlon's hands."

"Mechanism."

"Then explain it after we survive."

Rusk looked at the crown coffer.

Its seals remained intact.

Aven saw what he intended.

"No."

"We crown an alternative."

"Whom?"

"Someone acceptable."

"To whom?"

"Enough people."

Aven laughed.

It was the first honest laugh he had managed in days.

"This was never about correcting him."

"It was about making government possible."

"By replacing the king with whoever your patrons find acceptable."

Rusk drew his sword.

Aven drew his.

They never fought.

The throne-room doors burst inward.

Mara's people entered first.

Rusk's line met them.

The room became noise.

Aven backed toward the dais, shouting for surrender.

Some listened.

Some did not.

Luceran entered through the side gallery with Rhyse, Miren and Anja.

He saw Aven.

Aven saw him.

For one impossible instant they were friends again because both recognized the other before remembering why they were armed.

Then Rusk lunged for the crown coffer.

Aven stopped him.

That choice cost him.

Rusk struck Aven across the shoulder and drove him to the floor.

Luceran reached them seconds later.

Rhyse took Rusk's sword arm.

Anja hit him behind the knee.

Mara's people collapsed the remaining line.

Rusk went down screaming.

Aven remained on the floor with one hand pressed to his shoulder.

Luceran stood over him.

Aven's sword lay three feet away.

He could reach for it.

He did not.

"Majesty," he said.

Luceran kicked the sword away.

"Don't."

Aven looked up at him.

"I am sorry."

"Don't."

"I thought—"

"Don't."

Luceran's voice broke.

Aven stopped.

Around them, fighting continued in pockets.

Someone died behind the council doors.

Someone shouted for a surgeon.

Calienne's bell began ringing from the chapel.

The crown coffer sat unopened beside the throne.

Aven looked at Luceran's bloody sword.

"Do you mean to kill me?"

Luceran did not know.

That frightened both of them.

Rhyse said, "Majesty."

Miren said nothing.

Luceran looked at the man who had betrayed him.

His friend.

His enemy.

Both true.

"Bind him."

Rhyse exhaled.

Aven closed his eyes.

Luceran added, "Alive."

---

By midnight the castle belonged to no one cleanly enough to celebrate.

Rusk was under guard.

Aven was bound in the throne room because Luceran had not yet decided where else to put him.

The surviving coup soldiers were being separated by unit, surrender circumstances and command.

Calienne had transformed the chapel and two adjoining rooms into a field hospital.

Bram had been found alive.

Mikel too.

Ardel had found three of her missing retainers and one body.

Mera remained alive.

Ysabet had already begun shouting at people not to move documents merely because they had won.

Tomas Reed still had not brought the wagons.

This was apparently correct.

The throne room smelled of blood, lamp oil and wet wool.

Aven sat against the wall with his hands bound.

Rhyse wanted him moved.

Mara wanted Rusk moved first.

Ardel wanted every prisoner recorded before anyone disappeared into a cellar.

Calienne wanted everyone who could still stand to stop bleeding on her floors.

Miren wanted Luceran to sit down before he fell down.

Luceran wanted everyone to stop talking.

Nobody did.

"Majesty, we need a decision on the Companions."

"Majesty, the lower barracks surrendered under Aven's terms."

"Majesty, Rusk's men claim they were acting under emergency warrants."

"Majesty, the crown coffer—"

"Majesty—"

"Majesty—"

Luceran walked to the throne.

He did not ascend ceremonially.

He stumbled up the dais, turned, and dropped into it because his legs had begun shaking.

The room continued around him.

Aven looked up.

Miren saw Luceran's face and started toward him.

Then the air in front of the throne changed.

Not darkened.

Changed.

Silver gathered from nothing.

A shape formed at eye level.

Oval.

Pale.

Smooth.

A theatrical mask with no body behind it.

Every voice in the throne room stopped.

The mask hovered before Luceran.

He stared at it.

It inclined itself by the smallest degree.

And spoke.

"Your Majesty."

All seven ancestral lights burned.

Luceran did not answer.
