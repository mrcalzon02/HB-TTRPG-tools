# Season One, Episode 30 — Closed Gates

By morning, the road below Edselsto had become an army by accident.

Luceran had returned with thirty-four riders.

Ardel Kaelmont still held the lower road with people who answered to her before they answered to anyone wearing a crown. Black Pine scouts moved through the trees in pairs because no one trusted a lone messenger anymore. Village men came uphill with hunting bows, axes, old spears and whatever armor could be borrowed from walls or trunks. Tomas Reed arrived from Northbridge without the six Crown stores wagons because three contradictory orders had demanded them and he had decided that three mutually suspicious civilian keyholders were safer than any seal.

Later, Mara Kest returned with twelve of the eighteen troops she had kept at Red Ford.

Luceran stared at the missing six.

Mara answered the question before he asked it.

"The survey still exists. The cattle still exist. The people who nearly killed each other over both still exist."

"My castle is occupied."

"Yes."

"And you left six."

"Yes."

He looked at her.

Mara looked back.

She was not ashamed.

That mattered.

Luceran nodded once.

"Good."

It did not feel good.

It felt like learning that kingship continued even while someone else sat behind your gates.

Above them, Edselsto remained shut.

Rhyse had spent the night making certain Luceran understood what that meant.

They could build ladders.

They could cut a ram.

They could try the north postern.

They could send people through the drainage culvert Hessa Dorn had used.

They could search for an external mouth to the unfinished works passages.

They could burn the lower doors.

They could wait.

They could attack.

Every option had a cost.

Most costs had names.

Luceran listened until Rhyse finished.

Then he said, "Again."

Rhyse repeated all of it.

This time Luceran asked questions.

"Ram?"

"Possible."

"How many to carry it?"

"At least twelve if the timber is heavy enough."

"How many reach the gate?"

Rhyse looked at the arrow slits.

"Not twelve."

"Ladders?"

"Worse."

"Postern?"

"Narrow approach. Freshly watched."

"Culvert?"

"One person at a time without armor. If they hold the inside mouth, we deliver prisoners."

"Fire?"

Miren answered.

"Calienne has wounded inside."

"I know."

"Your people live under those roofs."

"I know."

"You spent half a year repairing them."

"I know."

The third time he said it, his voice cracked.

Nobody pretended not to hear.

Edrik Saye was dead.

That was the name beneath every other name.

Luceran looked at the gate.

"Then we talk."

---

Sir Aven Marr came to the wall beneath a white cloth.

He still bowed.

"Majesty."

Luceran stood in the road with Rhyse to his right and Miren to his left.

Ardel remained ten paces behind them because she refused to turn independence into theater merely because everyone else had chosen banners.

Luceran called up.

"Open the gates."

Aven's face tightened.

"I cannot."

"Cannot or will not?"

"Both."

"Then why are we talking?"

"Because people are still alive."

Luceran almost laughed.

It came out as breath.

Aven held up a folded paper.

"My surrender protections remain in force. Calienne's chapel remains neutral. Named noncombatants can be released. We can exchange prisoners and convene representatives of the estates."

"Return the crown."

"No."

"Open the gates."

"No."

A second figure stepped onto the wall.

Cale Rusk did not bow.

"The terms are simpler than Sir Aven makes them," he said. "Dismiss Rhyse. Order Kaelmont off the lower road. Return the seal register, accession ledger, surrender copy and payment records stolen from royal custody. Submit yourself to an estates hearing under guard."

Rhyse did not react to hearing his own dismissal demanded.

Ardel laughed.

Rusk looked down at her.

"You find something amusing?"

"You want me off my road because you stole someone else's castle."

"It is a Crown road."

"Then perhaps the Crown should ask."

Luceran raised one hand.

Ardel stopped.

Not because she had to.

Because she chose to.

Luceran looked at Aven.

"Are those your terms?"

"No."

Rusk turned.

Aven continued.

"My terms do not require Captain Rhyse's dismissal or Lady Kaelmont's withdrawal."

"Your terms no longer secure the castle," Rusk said.

The words were meant to humiliate Aven.

They became information.

Luceran saw Rhyse notice.

Miren noticed too.

Aven knew what he had exposed.

The coup had taken Edselsto.

It had not become one government.

Luceran said, "Release the wounded."

Aven answered, "Those fit to travel can be discussed."

Rusk said, "No one leaves while hostile forces occupy the village."

"They are patients," Aven said.

"They are leverage."

Silence crossed the road.

Rusk realized what he had said in front of witnesses.

Too late.

Luceran looked at him.

"Thank you."

Rusk's expression hardened.

Luceran turned away.

Aven called after him.

"Majesty."

Luceran stopped.

"I am trying to keep this from becoming a massacre."

Luceran looked back.

"So am I."

---

They tried the north postern before noon.

Not with an assault.

With a signal.

Three knocks.

Pause.

Two.

Pause.

Three.

An old household pattern.

Nothing answered.

A Black Pine scout crawled close enough to see fresh timber bracing the inner door and returned with an arrow through his cloak.

They tried the drainage culvert.

Hessa Dorn volunteered before anyone asked.

"You can send me."

Rhyse said, "No."

"I've done it."

"Which is why they may know it."

"I can still fit."

"That is not a military argument."

"It is if nobody else can."

Luceran listened until Hessa looked at him.

"We have people inside."

"I know."

"Bram could be dying."

"I know."

"Then what are we waiting for?"

Luceran had no answer that did not sound like cowardice.

Miren gave one anyway.

"For a way to save him that does not require feeding you into a hole."

Hessa hated the answer.

She obeyed it.

Not because Luceran was king.

Because the answer was right.

That mattered too.

---

Inside Edselsto, Bram Tolland's fever broke.

He woke drenched in sweat and immediately tried to stand.

Anja Pell pushed him back down.

"No."

"How long?"

"Since what?"

"Edrik."

Anja's face changed.

Bram closed his eyes.

"Right."

Mikel Orr was worse.

The water Edrik had carried back had lasted through the night.

Barely.

Outside the hidden laundry gallery, searchers had begun measuring walls.

Edrik had bought them ignorance.

Ignorance was expiring.

"We move," Bram said.

Anja looked at his wounded leg.

"Where?"

"The old linen chute."

"With Mikel?"

"We lower him."

"With what rope?"

A kitchen boy beside the wall said, "Sorting pulley."

Everyone looked at him.

"My mother worked here."

Bram nodded.

"Show us."

No ghost moved them.

No wall changed shape.

A kitchen boy remembered his mother's work.

A laundress knew which pulley had not rotted.

Anja organized the order.

Two wounded guards bit leather while they were lowered.

Bram went last because somebody had to remain until everyone else was through.

By the time Rusk's searchers opened the false panel, the gallery contained dirty linen, an empty water skin, and nobody worth capturing.

---

Calienne Orsanne received Edrik's body in the chapel yard.

Aven had ordered the dead from both sides identified before heat and haste made that impossible.

Rusk had objected.

Calienne had asked whether he feared the corpses would organize.

Aven permitted the transfer.

A kitchen boy named Oren Pell walked beside the stretcher.

He was fourteen.

Calienne asked why.

"I saw."

"What did you see?"

"The guard. Edrik."

"Tell me only what you saw."

Oren swallowed.

"Six men chased him. They told him to drop his sword. He stopped."

"Did he surrender?"

"No."

"Did they offer?"

"Yes."

"Then?"

"He looked out the arrow slit."

"At what?"

"I don't know."

"Then?"

"He laughed."

Calienne waited.

"He said they were late."

That was all.

Calienne wrote exactly that.

She did not write that Edrik died bravely.

She did not write that he died for the king.

She wrote what a frightened boy had seen.

Then she washed Edrik's face.

By evening the village knew.

Not because Luceran proclaimed it.

A chapel assistant told a cousin. The cousin told a baker. The baker had known Edrik because he stole burned loaf ends and claimed royal security required their disposal.

Small lamps began appearing on the stone turn below the castle.

Luceran saw them from camp.

"Who ordered that?"

Ardel said, "No one."

A woman he did not know placed another.

"He paid for my grandson's boot repair," she told Luceran.

He had not known.

Of course he had not known.

Edrik had possessed a life outside the radius of his king.

Luceran stood among the lamps until the woman left.

Then he said, "I keep thinking I should know what he would tell me to do."

Rhyse stood behind him.

"You knew him well."

"That's not an answer."

"No."

Luceran turned.

Rhyse's face was exhausted.

"I knew him too."

The words corrected something Luceran had not realized he was doing.

He had been making Edrik's death his.

It was not.

Bram's.

Mikel's.

Anja's.

Rhyse's.

The baker's.

The woman's grandson's.

The kingdom was larger than the crown even in grief.

"Sorry," Luceran said.

Rhyse nodded.

It was enough for now.

---

Tomas Reed had brought no wagons, but he had brought paperwork.

Luceran looked at the three orders.

One bore a version of his seal.

One bore Aven's.

One bore no seal and threatened hanging.

"They all told you to bring the stores here?"

"Yes."

"And you did not."

"No."

"Why?"

"They contradicted one another."

"So you ignored all three."

"I moved the wagons into the Northbridge toll enclosure, doubled civilian witnesses and gave the keys to three people who dislike each other."

Rhyse laughed for the first time since returning.

Tomas looked offended.

"It seemed prudent."

"Who has the keys?" Luceran asked.

"Bridge reeve. Carters' steward. Canon's factor."

"They dislike each other?"

"Professionally."

Luceran almost smiled.

"Excellent."

The coup had reached Northbridge with paper and failed because an ordinary quartermaster had learned what contradictory authority looked like.

Luceran felt something loosen inside him.

Not hope.

Something sturdier.

People.

---

The second day made everything worse.

More people arrived.

Two local houses sent men.

One sent apologies.

Mara's twelve joined the outer camp.

Black Pine found three approaches and rejected all three.

Ardel's road became a supply lane, picket line and argument.

The accidental army became large enough to do something terrible.

Rhyse showed Luceran the numbers.

"If you order it, we can assault the lower gate."

"Can?"

"Probably."

"How many die?"

"I don't know."

"Guess."

"No."

"Rhyse."

"I will not make dead men into arithmetic because you want permission."

Luceran stared at him.

Rhyse stared back.

The agreement from the security scare still existed.

Rhyse controlled the route while the threat remained active.

Luceran could override him.

The override would be recorded.

Luceran could order the assault.

Rhyse would lead it.

That was the horror of authority.

"Ram?"

"Feasible."

"Ladders?"

"Feasible."

"Fire?"

"Feasible."

"Mining?"

"Too slow."

"Postern?"

"Possible."

"Works route?"

"Unconfirmed."

"Culvert?"

"Possible for a few people."

"So we are not trapped."

"No."

"We can get in."

"Yes."

Luceran looked up at Edselsto.

Rhyse continued.

"We can also kill half the people we are trying to save while doing it."

Luceran looked away first.

"No assault."

"Recorded."

"You're insufferable."

"Recorded."

For one second Luceran heard Edrik laughing.

It nearly destroyed him.

---

That afternoon, Aven requested parley again.

This time Rusk stood behind him with enough armed men to make the balance visible.

Aven looked older.

Luceran looked worse.

"Majesty," Aven said.

"Open the gates."

"No."

"Then stop calling me Majesty."

Aven flinched.

"I will not."

"Why?"

"Because taking your residence did not unmake your coronation."

"Then what did you think you were doing?"

Aven's answer took time.

"Stopping you."

"From what?"

"Becoming a king no institution could correct."

Luceran laughed once.

"By taking my crown."

"By forcing an estates settlement."

"With Cale Rusk?"

Aven's jaw tightened.

"That was not the arrangement I believed I was making."

"There it is."

"I was wrong about him."

"You were wrong about me."

"Perhaps."

Rusk stepped forward.

"This conversation has become sentimental."

Luceran looked at him.

"You called wounded people leverage yesterday."

"They are facts on a board."

"No. They are people."

Rusk shrugged.

Aven's disgust was visible.

The fracture was no longer a seam.

It was a crack.

But the gate remained closed.

That was the problem.

Aven said, "I can still arrange protected surrender."

Luceran answered, "I am not surrendering."

"Then people will die."

"People already died."

"I know."

"Edrik Saye."

Aven looked down.

"I know."

"Did you kill him?"

"No."

"Did your coup?"

Aven did not answer.

Luceran turned away.

He had learned enough.

Not enough to get inside.

Enough to know no negotiation would hand him the gate.

---

That night Miren found him standing beyond the lamps.

He had not removed his sword.

"You are going to fall asleep upright."

"I might."

"You will hit the ground."

"I have guards."

"That is not what guards are for."

"Edrik would have caught me."

Miren's face changed.

Luceran immediately regretted the sentence.

"Sorry."

"You don't have to apologize for remembering him."

"I keep using him."

"Yes."

He looked at her.

Miren folded her arms.

"You ask what he would have wanted every time you want permission to do the thing you already want to do."

Luceran's anger arrived before thought.

"And what do you want me to do?"

"I want you to stop making dead people vote."

The words landed.

He looked away.

Miren's voice softened.

"You loved him."

"Yes."

"So did other people."

"I know."

"Then let his choice remain his choice."

Luceran stood silent for a long time.

Finally he said, "I can take the gate."

"Probably."

"I can."

"Probably."

"You sound like Rhyse."

"Good."

"I hate both of you."

"I know."

He looked at her then.

She was exhausted too.

Still there.

Still willing to tell him no.

That was what love looked like when it was useful.

Not obedience.

Friction.

He took her hand.

They stood that way until a messenger came for the king.

---

The message was from the wall.

Final parley.

No paper terms.

No clerk.

No neutral delegation.

The lower road filled anyway.

Luceran walked forward with Rhyse, Miren, Mara and Ardel behind him.

His people spread across the road and the terraces below.

More than thirty-four now.

More than the force that had returned with him.

Not enough to make the walls irrelevant.

Enough that every defender above could see what waited outside.

Aven appeared first.

Rusk appeared beside him.

Then someone stepped between them.

Luceran stopped.

For a moment he did not understand what he was seeing.

The coat was wrong.

Too clean for the wall.

Elbren blue beneath a borrowed cuirass.

One cuff repaired with thread that did not match.

Montegrain rested both hands on the parapet.

The man who had walked into Luceran's painting room without knocking.

The boy he had known since he was twelve.

The friend who had stolen food from his plate, mocked his court, listened when grief made everyone else political, and been one of the very few people before whom Luceran had never needed to wonder who would be told.

Luceran forgot the army.

"Montegrain."

His friend looked down at him.

"Luceran."

No title.

That hurt more than treason should have been able to.

Rhyse shifted beside the king.

Luceran did not move.

"You are with them."

Montegrain's mouth tightened.

"Yes."

"Since when?"

"Long enough."

"That is not an answer."

"No."

Luceran laughed.

It sounded terrible.

"Of course."

Montegrain looked past him at the gathered force.

"You came back faster than we expected."

"We?"

"Do not make me pretend I stood apart from this."

Aven looked toward him but said nothing.

Rusk smiled faintly.

Luceran saw all three and understood almost nothing.

That was important.

Montegrain was here.

That did not make him the author of every forged message, every paid servant, every rumor, every dead guard or every choice Rusk had made.

Luceran wanted one villain badly enough to feel the temptation.

He did not take it.

"Why?" he asked.

Montegrain looked tired.

"Because I knew you before the crown."

"That is not a reason."

"It is mine."

"You thought I had become a tyrant."

"I thought you were becoming impossible to correct."

"So you took my home."

"We took the seat of government."

"You took my home."

"Both are true."

Luceran looked up at him.

There had been a time when that answer would have made him laugh.

Now he wanted to drag Montegrain from the wall with his hands.

Montegrain said, "Look around you."

Luceran did not.

"I am."

"No. You are looking at me."

"Yes."

"Then look at what this has become."

Luceran finally turned his head.

Mara's soldiers.

Black Pine.

Kaelmont retainers.

Village volunteers.

Local house banners.

People carrying weapons because the king's own gate was closed against him.

He looked back.

Montegrain said, "You can assault."

"I know."

"You may even win."

"I know."

"Then half the people inside become the price of proving that."

Luceran said nothing.

Montegrain leaned forward.

"I have the castle."

The words carried down the road.

"I have the throne."

Luceran's hands curled.

"My defenders control the castle you call home."

Someone behind Luceran swore.

Montegrain continued.

"Surrender, and you will be treated with dignity."

The world narrowed.

Montegrain meant it.

That was the worst part.

"No cage," Montegrain said. "No public chains. Your household can remain with you. Your property will be respected. There will be an estates hearing. You can answer the charges as king."

Luceran stared.

"As king."

"Yes."

"Without a castle."

"Until the estates decide."

"Without a throne."

"Until the estates decide."

"Without my crown."

Montegrain looked toward the stone behind him.

"Until the estates decide."

Luceran's face went hot.

Miren said his name softly.

He did not hear her.

Montegrain said, "This is over."

Behind him stood the gate.

Above him stood the friend who had known him before any of this.

Behind Luceran stood everyone who had come because he was not done.

He had spent two days listening to sensible people.

He had counted arrows.

He had counted doors.

He had counted wounded.

He had accepted delay.

He had refused ladders.

He had refused fire.

He had refused to feed Hessa into a culvert.

He had let Rhyse tell him no.

He had let Miren tell him the dead did not vote.

He had watched people light lamps for Edrik without being ordered.

He had learned that even grief belonged to more than the king.

And now his oldest friend stood over his own gate and offered him dignity.

Luceran stepped into the center of the road.

Rhyse said, "Majesty."

Montegrain's face changed.

He knew that look.

He had seen it when they were boys and Luceran had reached the point beyond argument.

"Luceran."

The king looked at him.

At Aven.

At Rusk.

At the soldiers above the gate.

At the narrow windows where servants, prisoners, wounded loyalists and wavering guards might be listening.

At Edselsto itself.

He did not know he was looking at anything that could answer.

Not yet.

His mouth opened.

All seven ancestral lights burned behind the walls.

The gates remained closed.
