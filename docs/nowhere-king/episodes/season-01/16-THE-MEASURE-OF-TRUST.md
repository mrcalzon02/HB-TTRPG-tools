# Season One, Episode 16 — The Measure of Trust

The alarm began with a door that was supposed to stay shut.

Not the sealed stair.

That distinction saved three people from doing something stupid before breakfast.

Sergeant Bram Tolland was crossing the west service yard when Guardsman Edrik Saye came down the steps at a run.

"West post says the surveyors are missing."

Bram stopped.

"Which surveyors?"

"Kaelmont's."

"They are Crown-paid for twenty days."

"Fine. Crown's Kaelmont surveyors are missing."

"How long?"

"They missed first tally."

"That is not missing."

"Their room is empty."

"That is more interesting."

Edrik held out a folded scrap of paper.

"And this was under Quill's door."

Bram did not take it.

"Who touched it?"

"Housemaid found it. Night corporal picked it up. I took it from him."

"So three people."

"Four if you count whoever wrote it."

"I generally do."

The note contained six words.

WEST CUT. BEFORE LIGHT. COME QUIETLY.

No signature.

No seal.

Bram looked toward the mountain road.

"Where is Quill?"

"Lower works. She says both surveyors were in their room when she went to bed."

"Names."

"Dena Mar and Corvin Hale."

"Did either of them have leave?"

"Quill says no."

"Does Quill command them after dark?"

Edrik paused.

"I don't know."

"Good. Keep not knowing until we ask."

Bram folded the note without putting it in his pocket.

"Wake Captain Rhyse. Quietly. Nobody closes a gate unless he orders it. Nobody arrests a Kaelmont man because his bed is empty. Find out whether Mar and Hale own horses, borrowed horses, walked, drank, gambled, visited anyone, or were seen after supper. And find the night porter who logged the west door."

Edrik looked toward the upper castle.

"The king?"

"Rhyse decides."

"If this is the hunt people—"

"Then they already taught us what happens when we decide what evidence means before it finishes being evidence."

Edrik nodded and ran.

Bram looked again at the note.

The charcoal marks from the hunt had never identified anyone.

The false horn had never identified anyone.

The rope had come from Royal Works and still had not identified anyone.

Six words were not going to become a conspiracy because dawn made them frightening.

He went to find the porter.

---

Luceran learned of the scare while Miren was still asleep in his bed.

That was how politics worked now.

A knock came at the outer door. Not the bedroom door. Rhyse had become particular about distinctions.

Luceran pulled on a robe and stepped into the sitting room.

Tovan Rhyse stood there with his hair badly tied and his boots properly fastened.

"Two surveyors are unaccounted for."

Luceran was awake immediately.

"Kaelmont?"

"Dena Mar and Corvin Hale. Both attached to Lysa Quill."

"Where?"

"Unknown. Their room was empty at first tally. An unsigned note directed someone to the west cut before light and to come quietly."

Luceran thought of the rope across the hunt line.

"Close the west gate."

"No."

The word arrived so quickly that Luceran stared at him.

Rhyse continued.

"Not yet."

"Two people are missing."

"Two people are not where their supervisor expected them."

"There is a note."

"There is a note."

"After the hunt."

"Yes."

"After somebody used Crown rope under a false issue."

"Yes."

"And you do not want the gate closed."

"I do not want to tell whoever is watching us that an unsigned scrap can stop the west road."

Luceran looked at him.

Rhyse's expression did not change.

"If I close it," Luceran said, "I keep people in."

"And keep people out. Food carts. Workers. Village carriers. Quill's own people if they are simply early. You also concentrate everyone who needs the road at one gate and teach every guard that 'something happened' before we know what."

"Then restrict it."

"Already done. One guard checks names against morning work lists. No announcement. Bram is tracing the note. Edrik is checking the night log."

Luceran hated how reasonable that sounded.

"Why wake me?"

"Because if this becomes an abduction, infiltration, or attack, you should not learn after breakfast. And because I need authority to delay your morning inspection of the west works."

Luceran's anger found somewhere easier to go.

"I am not hiding in my rooms because two surveyors missed tally."

"Good. You are not going to the place named in the note either."

"I was not invited."

"You were scheduled to inspect the west retaining cut at second bell. That schedule has been public among works staff since yesterday afternoon."

Luceran went still.

Rhyse let him understand it.

The note named the same place.

"Cancel it," Luceran said.

"Delay it. Quietly."

"Why not cancel?"

"Because if the note is bait, changing your routine tells the writer it reached us. If it is ordinary work, cancellation creates a rumor. If Mar and Hale are doing something foolish, we do not reward foolishness with a court panic."

The bedroom door opened behind Luceran.

Miren had wrapped a blanket around herself and looked annoyed at the existence of morning.

"Who is missing?"

Rhyse glanced at Luceran.

Luceran said, "You can tell her."

Rhyse did.

Miren listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she said, "Who was the note addressed to?"

Rhyse looked at her.

"It does not say."

"Then why are we assuming it was for Quill?"

Luceran opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

Rhyse said, "We are not."

"You called it being under her door."

"Yes."

"That is location, not addressee."

Rhyse nodded once.

Miren looked at Luceran.

"Do not go to the west cut."

"Tovan already said that."

"Good."

"You are both enjoying this."

"No," Miren said. "I am frightened. That is why I want someone less frightened than you deciding where you walk for the next hour."

That struck harder than Rhyse's refusal.

Luceran felt himself become defensive before he had decided to.

"I am not incapable of judgment because someone threatened me once."

"I did not say you were."

"You just said—"

"I said you are frightened. So am I. Tovan is probably frightened too."

Rhyse said, "I am standing here."

Miren ignored him.

"You nearly rode into a rope. Jeron did. Every uncertain thing near a road now looks like the beginning of the same story to you. That does not make you stupid. It makes you exactly the person who should not be the only person deciding what this note means."

Luceran looked at her.

She held his gaze.

He could order Rhyse to close the gates.

He could order the west cut searched by fifty men.

He could order every Kaelmont retainer confined.

He could make fear look like command and nobody in the room could legally stop him.

That was suddenly the ugliest part.

"What do you need from me?" he asked Rhyse.

"Stay off the west route until I clear it. Keep your ordinary breakfast. Let me use the people who actually know who moved through the doors last night."

Luceran nodded.

"Do it."

Miren exhaled.

Luceran looked at her.

"Do not look relieved."

"I am relieved."

"Disloyal."

"Terribly."

Rhyse left before either of them could become less dressed.

---

The night porter was named Alwin Marr, and he was furious that everyone kept asking whether he had seen the surveyors.

"I saw one."

Bram leaned against the porter desk.

"Which one?"

"Hale."

"When?"

"After midnight bell."

"Going where?"

"Out."

"Through which door?"

Alwin stared at him.

"The west service door, Sergeant. The one I keep."

"Was he alone?"

"When he passed me."

"Did he sign?"

Alwin shoved the book across the desk.

Corvin Hale's name was there, written badly but recognizably, beside a time and a destination.

LOWER WORKS — LEVEL CHECK.

"Did he say why?"

"Said Quill wanted a dawn level before carts started shaking the road."

"Did you believe him?"

Alwin frowned.

"I believed he was going to the lower works."

"That is not what I asked."

"I am a porter. I record who goes through my door. I do not inspect engineering excuses."

Bram smiled.

"Good answer."

"I know."

"Did Mar leave?"

"Not through here."

"Did Hale return?"

"Not through here."

"Anyone else out after him?"

Alwin tapped the book.

Three workers. One baker's boy. A dung cart. Two guards changing the pasture observation. Then, near first light, Lysa Quill herself.

"Quill went out before the alarm?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because she is an engineer and the mountain offends her personally. Ask her."

Bram copied the names.

At the bottom of the page he noticed something else.

"Who corrected this?"

One entry had been crossed through. Not erased. A single line, initials beside it.

Alwin looked.

"Night corporal."

"Why?"

"Guard Rell wrote east gate when he meant west."

"How do you know?"

"Because Rell was standing in front of me."

Bram looked at the correction.

The system was ugly.

It was also legible.

That was better than memory.

"Do not let anyone remove this book."

Alwin looked offended.

"I was not planning to feed it to the goats."

---

Dena Mar was found in the laundry yard.

Not hiding.

Sleeping.

She had spent the night with a laundress named Pera Sol and had failed to return to the room she shared with Quill's other surveyor.

Edrik discovered this because Pera came looking for breakfast and found two guards outside the surveyors' room.

"Dena is with me."

Both guards looked at her.

Pera looked back.

"What?"

Edrik said, "Since when?"

"That is a rude question."

"Since what time?"

"That is the same rude question wearing a uniform."

Edrik rubbed his face.

"There is a security inquiry."

Pera's expression changed.

"Is she in trouble?"

"I don't know. I need to know when she came to you."

Pera thought.

"After the kitchens banked the second fire. Before the rain started."

That was useful because kitchens and rain had witnesses.

"Did she leave?"

"Not unless she learned to climb through a shutter narrower than her shoulders."

"Can I speak to her?"

"When she has trousers."

"Reasonable."

Dena appeared five minutes later wearing yesterday's shirt, Pera's skirt, and the expression of someone discovering that private pleasure had become administrative evidence.

"What happened?"

Edrik showed her the note without handing it over.

She read it.

"Not mine."

"Do you recognize the hand?"

"No."

"Did Hale say anything about a dawn level?"

Dena frowned.

"He wanted one. Quill said we could take it after first light."

"Why?"

"Because you can see the rod after first light."

"Did Hale disagree?"

"Hale disagrees with daylight on principle. He thinks early work proves seriousness."

Pera, behind her, said, "That sounds exhausting."

Dena almost smiled.

"It is."

"Would he go alone?"

"Yes."

"Would he leave a note?"

Dena looked again.

"Not like that. He writes numbers as if the page owes him money. Words he writes normally."

"Who knew he wanted the level?"

Dena's expression tightened.

"Quill. Me. Two road hands. Mara Henn was there. A guard told us to move our line yesterday. Anyone close enough to hear us arguing."

"What were you arguing about?"

"Whether cart vibration was moving the marker or the ground."

"Which was it?"

"That is why we needed another reading."

Edrik nodded.

Dena glanced at the two guards.

"Are you going to tell Lady Ardel where I slept?"

"Only if where you slept matters to finding Hale."

"It doesn't."

Pera said, "It establishes she wasn't abducted."

Edrik looked at her.

Pera folded her arms.

"Laundry is not a vow of stupidity."

"No," Edrik said. "Apparently not."

He wrote down the time window and nothing else.

That choice would matter later too.

---

Corvin Hale was not at the west cut.

His measuring rod was.

So was blood.

Not much.

Enough.

Bram arrived with Rhyse, two guards, Mara Henn, and Lysa Quill. Nobody had summoned Luceran.

Quill saw the rod first.

"That is his."

Rhyse held up a hand before she stepped closer.

The rod lay below the retaining cut near a drainage channel, one end wedged beneath loose stone. A dark smear marked the rock beside it.

Mara crouched several paces away.

"Fresh fall."

Rhyse looked at her.

"How fresh?"

"Since yesterday. Maybe last night. See the pale faces?"

Quill said, "That cut should not have shed there."

Mara looked at her.

"Should not is not did not."

Quill's jaw tightened.

Rhyse pointed to the ground.

"Tracks?"

Bram said, "Too many. Road crew yesterday. Surveyors. Guard relief. Rain after midnight."

Quill stared uphill.

"Hale would have climbed for the upper marker."

"Why?"

"Because he is an idiot."

Rhyse waited.

Quill closed her eyes briefly.

"Because he thinks a reading taken before traffic is cleaner. He asked yesterday. I told him to wait until light."

"Would he disobey you?"

"For a measurement? Yes. For a contract? No. For danger? He would tell you he had assessed it."

Mara said, "Meaning yes."

"Meaning he is twenty-six and thinks surviving cliffs is evidence of understanding cliffs."

Bram looked toward the drainage run.

"Blood continues."

Three small marks led away from the rock, then vanished where water crossed the track.

Rhyse divided the search.

Not widely.

Not dramatically.

Two guards down drainage. Bram and Mara along the lower contour. Quill stayed where she could identify equipment and shout without trampling what remained.

They found Hale thirty minutes later under a thorn bank fifty yards downslope.

Alive.

His left ankle was badly swollen. His scalp was split above the ear. One sleeve had been torn into a bandage around his own forearm.

He was furious.

"Took you long enough."

Bram crouched beside him.

"Good morning."

"Is it?"

"Depends. Did someone push you?"

Hale stared at him.

"What?"

"Answer before Quill gets here."

"No. The edge went."

"Anyone with you?"

"No."

"Anyone call you out here?"

"No."

"Did you write a note?"

"No."

Bram looked at Rhyse.

The note had just become stranger, not clearer.

Hale tried to sit up and swore.

"My level."

"Your level can wait."

"It cannot. If the marker moved—"

"You moved."

"Yes, I noticed."

Mara arrived and looked at his ankle.

"You walked on that?"

"Crawled."

"Why didn't you shout?"

"I did."

Bram listened to the drainage water, carts beginning on the lower road, hammers from the works yard, and a mountain full of stone.

A man could disappear fifty yards from a royal project simply because noise was ordinary.

That was useful knowledge and an unpleasant one.

---

By the time Luceran heard that Hale was alive, he had already convinced himself the man had been murdered.

He hated Miren for noticing.

Not truly.

Enough to be irritated.

"You stopped eating," she said.

"I was waiting."

"You were imagining."

"There was blood."

"Yes."

"His rod was abandoned."

"Yes."

"And a note told someone to go there quietly."

"Yes."

Luceran pushed back from the breakfast table.

"You are doing that thing where agreeing sounds like disagreement."

"Because the facts are frightening enough without adding a body we do not have."

A knock interrupted them.

Rhyse entered.

"Hale is alive. Fall injury. He says nobody called him out and nobody was with him. Physician is bringing him up."

Luceran closed his eyes.

Relief came first.

Embarrassment followed.

"And Mar?"

Rhyse paused.

"Located. Safe. Her absence was unrelated."

Miren looked at him.

Rhyse looked back.

Neither said more.

Luceran noticed that too.

"Where was she?"

Rhyse said, "Somewhere that establishes she was not at the west cut and does not presently bear on the inquiry."

Luceran frowned.

"You are withholding it from me?"

"Yes."

The room went quiet.

Luceran felt the old reflex: I am the king.

Rhyse knew he felt it.

"If her location becomes material," Rhyse said, "you will have it. If not, the Crown does not need to turn every private bed into a security record because somebody missed tally."

Miren looked down at her cup.

Luceran knew that was deliberate mercy.

He also knew Rhyse was right.

"Fine."

Rhyse nodded.

"There is still the note."

"So this was not the note?"

"We do not know. Hale says he did not receive it. Mar says it is not hers. Quill did not write it. It was found under Quill's door."

"Could someone have meant Quill to find it?"

"Yes."

"Could someone have put it under the wrong door?"

"Yes."

"Could it be bait for the west cut?"

"Yes."

"Could it be a joke?"

"Yes."

Luceran glared at him.

"You are very helpful."

"Those are the possibilities."

Miren said, "Who knew you were going there this morning?"

Rhyse answered.

"Works staff, household scheduling, guards assigned to the route, Kaelmont's survey party, several village representatives, anyone who heard yesterday's arrangements. It was not secret."

"Then the note could be aimed at him."

"Yes."

Luceran said, "Or it could have nothing to do with me."

Rhyse looked at him.

"Yes."

It was somehow worse when the uncertainty belonged to him voluntarily.

---

The note's paper came from the lower works office.

Nera Vos recognized it because one corner carried a faint blue tally line used for tool-return slips.

That did not identify the writer.

Half the construction staff could reach the stack.

The charcoal was ordinary hearth charcoal.

That identified nearly everyone.

The handwriting produced three confident accusations within an hour and none survived comparison.

One clerk thought it resembled Olan Vey.

It did not.

A guard thought it resembled a dismissed Royal Companion.

He had never seen the man's handwriting.

A mason thought the letter M looked like a Kaelmont surveyor's.

There was no M in the note.

Nera listened to this for as long as she could tolerate it.

Then she took the note away from them.

"You are all making it worse."

Edrik said, "We need possibilities."

"You have possibilities. You need facts."

"That sounds like Pellin."

"Then Pellin is occasionally right."

She held the paper against a blank tool slip.

Same stock.

Same cut width.

Not proof of writer.

She wrote that down.

Then she looked at the issue shelf.

"Who took slips yesterday?"

The temporary desk keeper—her brother Joss Vos—looked horrified.

"Everyone."

"Excellent."

"You told me to let foremen take blanks."

"I told you to let them take the forms they need. I am not accusing you."

"Feels like it."

Nera stopped.

Her brother's face was red.

She lowered her voice.

"You're right. Sorry. Who took them?"

Joss thought.

"Derrin. Two carpenters. Quill's tall surveyor. A guard because he wanted scrap. I told him no. Olan. Kitchen runner took one by mistake and brought it back."

"Which surveyor?"

"Hale."

Edrik looked at Nera.

Nera immediately said, "That proves he touched paper. It does not prove he wrote the note."

Joss added, "He used it to copy measurements. I saw numbers."

Edrik exhaled.

Another attractive theory died before lunch.

Good.

---

Hale remembered something after the physician stitched his scalp.

Not a face.

Not a threat.

A question.

The previous afternoon, while he and Dena argued over the upper marker, a man carrying two coils of rope had asked whether the king truly inspected works himself.

Hale had said yes.

He could not describe the man well.

Brown work coat. Mud. Beard perhaps. Or shadow. He had assumed Royal Works because of the rope.

"Black tracer?" Rhyse asked.

Hale frowned.

"I don't know."

"Think."

"I am thinking. Rope is rope when you are trying to hold a level in wind."

"Did he ask when the king was coming?"

"I don't remember."

"Did you tell him?"

Hale looked miserable.

"Maybe."

Rhyse said nothing.

"I didn't think it was secret."

"It wasn't."

That surprised him.

"Then—"

"Public information can still be useful to someone dangerous. You are not guilty because someone asked an ordinary question. I need to know what was said."

Hale closed his eyes.

"He asked whether the king came down for measurements or only openings. I said he was coming tomorrow because Quill had complained about the road load. I think."

"Anything else?"

"He joked that kings like holes in mountains more than drains."

Rhyse looked at Bram.

Bram remembered the sealed stair rumors.

So would half the settlement.

"Would you know him again?"

"Maybe."

That was not yes.

They recorded maybe.

---

By afternoon the scare had produced no attacker, no abduction, no proven trap, and one injured surveyor who had disobeyed his supervisor for the sake of a cleaner measurement.

It had also produced a fact Rhyse disliked more than a dramatic conspiracy.

Too many people could know where the king would be without anyone having done anything improper.

Works schedules passed through clerks, guards, household staff, foremen, specialists, village representatives, carters, and messengers because work required coordination.

The answer could not be secrecy.

A construction site where nobody knew when inspections occurred would become a different kind of dangerous.

Rhyse brought the problem to Luceran in the west audience room.

Miren was there because Luceran asked her to stay.

Bram was there because he had handled the first response.

Nera was there because the note paper came from her office.

Mara was there because the west cut was hers to worry about whether kings existed or not.

Quill came because one of her people was injured and because she was furious.

"Hale is an ass," she began.

Luceran blinked.

"Good afternoon."

"He went onto an unstable cut alone before light after I told him not to. If your guards turn this into an assassination story, he will learn nothing."

Rhyse said, "We have not."

"Your guards asked whether someone pushed him."

"They should have."

Quill stopped.

"Yes. Fine. They should have."

Mara almost smiled.

Rhyse laid out the problem.

Not the private details.

Not theories as facts.

The note. The public schedule. Hale's report of the rope-carrying questioner. The absence of a reliable description. The ordinary accessibility of the paper. The fact that Luceran's planned inspection had become common knowledge through legitimate work channels.

Luceran listened.

"What changes?"

Rhyse said, "Your movements stop being copied into general works schedules."

Mara immediately said, "No."

Everyone looked at her.

"If he is inspecting a cut, I need warning. So does Quill. So do the crews. We clear loose work, stop lifting, make a safe path. You cannot arrive as a surprise and call it security."

Quill nodded.

"Agreed."

Rhyse said, "Then the works schedule gets an inspection window, not the king's route or exact arrival."

Bram added, "And the escort receives the exact route late enough that it cannot become yesterday's gossip."

Luceran frowned.

"How late?"

"Depends on the route."

"That is not a rule."

"No. It is judgment."

Luceran looked unhappy.

Miren noticed.

"You want a rule because rules feel fair."

"Rules are useful."

"So is judgment. That is why you have Tovan."

Rhyse said, "Still standing here."

Nera raised a hand slightly.

Luceran looked at her.

"Yes?"

"Stop using blank tool slips as scrap."

There was a pause.

"That is your security reform?"

"One of them. Numbered forms for issue records. Scrap goes in a separate box with a cut corner. If somebody writes another note on works paper, at least we know whether they stole a controlled form or used scrap."

Bram said, "That is useful."

Nera looked pleased despite trying not to.

Quill said, "My people sign out before-light work."

"They already sign out," Rhyse said.

"Hale wrote 'level check.' That told the porter nothing about whether he was expected. I want supervisor mark for work outside normal hours."

Mara said, "For Crown specialists. Not village people walking their own land."

"Fine."

Tension moved around the table and became procedure.

Luceran watched it happen.

Nobody had solved the note.

They were making the next note less powerful anyway.

"And me?" he asked.

Rhyse answered.

"You accept route authority when there is an active security question."

Luceran leaned back.

"Define active."

"I do."

"Convenient."

"Necessary."

"What if I disagree?"

"You are king. You can overrule me."

Rhyse's voice remained level.

"But you do it explicitly, in front of whoever is present, and I record that I advised against it."

Luceran stared at him.

That was not refusal.

It was worse.

It made obedience visible.

If Luceran chose badly, he would own the choice instead of quietly absorbing professional caution and later pretending everyone had agreed.

Miren said, "Yes."

Luceran looked at her.

"You do not get a vote."

"No. I get an opinion."

"Which is?"

"Yes."

Quill looked between them with open interest.

Mara looked at the table.

Bram became fascinated by the window.

Luceran felt heat rise in his face.

Then he laughed.

Not because it was funny.

Because the alternative was behaving like a child in front of six adults who had all spent the day keeping his fear from becoming policy.

"Fine," he said. "Write it."

Rhyse nodded.

"I already did."

"Of course you did."

---

The king went to the west cut before sunset.

Not by the route originally planned.

Not at the time originally planned.

And not because he had defeated Rhyse in an argument.

Bram chose the approach after checking it.

Mara halted lifting work ten minutes before arrival without being told the exact route.

Quill showed Luceran where the edge had failed.

Hale's blood had been washed from the rock, but the pale break remained.

"He was lucky," Luceran said.

"He was stupid," Quill replied.

"Both can be true."

She looked at him.

"Good."

Below them, Dena Mar held the measuring rod while Pera Sol watched from the laundry cart road with absolutely no professional reason to be there.

Dena noticed her and smiled.

Luceran noticed the smile.

He did not ask.

That was also a kind of government.

Mara pointed to the upper drainage seam.

"The cut failed because water got behind yesterday's face. Not because somebody loosened it."

"Certain?"

"No."

Luceran looked at her.

She continued.

"I can show you water. I can show you fresh stone. I can show you where the face broke. I cannot show you every hand that touched this hill."

"So what do you believe?"

"Ordinary failure until evidence says otherwise."

Quill said, "Same."

Luceran looked toward the road where the unknown rope-carrying man had supposedly stood.

He wanted a face.

He wanted the false horn, the rope, the riders, the charcoal marks, the note, and the questioner to become one person with one motive.

He wanted uncertainty to stop costing him sleep.

Nothing on the mountain owed him that.

"Repair it," he said.

Mara nodded.

"Already started."

Of course it had.

---

That evening Miren found Luceran in the family nave.

He was not praying.

He was looking at the candles.

Seven flames.

All ordinary from where he stood.

"You vanished," she said.

"I told Bram."

"Growth."

He looked at her.

"Do not start."

She came beside him.

For a while neither spoke.

Then Luceran said, "I was angry with you this morning."

"I know."

"I thought you were treating me like I was frightened and therefore useless."

"I was treating you like you were frightened and therefore frightened."

"That distinction was not obvious at the time."

"Most useful distinctions aren't."

He looked at the candles again.

"If I had ordered the gates closed, Tovan would have done it."

"Yes."

"He would have argued."

"Yes."

"Then done it."

"Probably."

Luceran rubbed his thumb against the edge of his ring.

"That is a terrible amount of power to have while afraid."

Miren did not reassure him.

That was why he had wanted her there.

"Yes," she said.

He laughed once, quietly.

"You could occasionally tell me I am doing wonderfully."

"You are doing wonderfully."

"That sounded false."

"It was."

He bumped his shoulder against hers.

She leaned back into him.

"Tovan said something today," Luceran said. "About recording when I overrule him."

"I heard."

"I hated it."

"I know."

"I agreed."

"I know."

"You are impossible to impress."

Miren turned her head.

"No. I am impressed that you agreed while hating it. Agreeing with things you already like is not difficult."

Luceran considered that.

"Is that what trust is?"

"Partly."

"Letting people stop you?"

"Sometimes. Sometimes letting them fail without deciding they betrayed you. Sometimes believing they will tell you something ugly before your enemy does. Sometimes knowing they want things from you and listening anyway."

"That sounds inconvenient."

"It is."

He looked at her.

"And loyalty?"

Miren smiled faintly.

"More inconvenient."

He kissed her.

No revelation followed.

No candle flickered.

The castle did not answer.

Outside, a porter copied names into a cleaner book. Nera Vos cut the corners from a box of scrap paper. Quill wrote a reprimand for Corvin Hale that he would have to sign before returning to field work. ## Episode-end state

Season One, Episode 16 turns an ambiguous security scare into a test of whether Luceran can tolerate loyal contradiction without converting uncertainty into accusation.

### Durable changes

- Dena Mar's apparent disappearance is resolved as private adult conduct unrelated to the security scare; Rhyse deliberately protects irrelevant intimate detail from becoming Crown security data.
- Corvin Hale leaves before light for an unauthorized solo level check, suffers a mundane cut failure, and survives with a scalp wound and injured ankle. Lysa Quill formally reprimands him; the event is not converted into supernatural or conspiratorial evidence.
- An unsigned note reading `WEST CUT. BEFORE LIGHT. COME QUIETLY.` remains unexplained. Its paper is ordinary lower-works tool-slip stock accessible to many people; handwriting comparisons produce no reliable identification.
- Hale reports that an indistinct rope-carrying worker asked the previous afternoon whether Luceran personally inspected works and may have learned the planned west-cut visit. This is a new lead, not identification of the hunt tester or proof that the note, hunt rope, and questioner share a sponsor.

- Nera Vos converts the note-paper weakness into a mundane control: numbered issue forms remain controlled; scrap paper is separated and corner-cut so later provenance questions are less ambiguous.

- No one identifies the living hunt sponsor. No evidence links Kaelmont, the sealed stair, or supernatural activity to the scare.
