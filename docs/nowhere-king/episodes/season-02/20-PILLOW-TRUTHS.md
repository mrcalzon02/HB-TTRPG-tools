# Season Two, Episode 20 — Pillow Truths

Miren ate breakfast in the physically deaf room. The silver bridge had been removed from the wall months ago. The plaster around the socket remained rough.

"His Majesty sent the pears," the chambermaid said.

"Did he ask whether I ate them?"

"He asked whether you had breakfast."

"Then tell him I have breakfast."

The maid hesitated. "Will he think I displeased you?"

Miren looked up. The girl had carried a tray, not the king's crime. She was afraid of being made responsible for an answer that was not hers.

"You have done your work. If anyone asks what we spoke of, say you brought breakfast and left. That is all you know."

Later Miren found Ysabet in the copy room.

"I want a rule for my chamber. No one serving me is to be questioned about my visitors or meals on the king's private authority. If an inquiry is made, let it be entered openly."

"A royal order could supersede it."

"I know. I want the next breach to have a name and an hour."

Ysabet wrote the request as dictated. Miren signed. The clerk filed it apart from the king's lethal instruction and the grain tally. Miren had not forgiven the earlier theft of her conversation; she was making its repetition harder to conceal.

---

Yara paid the cooper's widow for the room Corven had never occupied. The wounded attendant's fever had broken, but he still could not travel. His companion wanted Corven's broken walking stick returned.

At the village watch house the clerk unwrapped the pieces. He could establish where they had been found, not whose blood marked the split wood.

"Keep it with the entry," Yara said.

"The Crown may ask for it."

"Then enter the order and the name of whoever takes it."

"You think the envoy is dead."

"I think he was attacked and disappeared. I have heard a man with his voice who does not know things Corven knew. I cannot tell you how that happened."

"Should I change the missing entry?"

"No."

Yara had already sent Leora the physician's limited observations and a watch copy. Their arrival was unconfirmed. Now she wrote another letter, for Leora's hand alone, describing the man's unreliable recall of private matters. She claimed neither a death she had not witnessed nor a rite she had not seen.

She wrote one warning twice, then kept the second version: *Do not come alone on the strength of a voice that sounds like his.*

The first had said *Do not come*. Yara could warn a wife without deciding for her.

---

Beren Kalt's wife came to Rhyse with clean stockings and the names of two children who wanted their father home.

"Your men let him walk in the yard. They count how long he walks."

"Yes."

"They let him write. They watch who takes the letter."

"Yes."

"Then call him what he is."

Rhyse looked at his own order: *Protected witness.* The words were less shameful than *man held at the castle after refusing the king's private errand*.

"He is not accused."

"Can he leave?"

"Not yet."

"Then I have my answer."

She had brought a claim for the horse Beren lost during the coup, but did not raise it. Rhyse's control over her husband's movement had made an ordinary debt seem like a bargain.

"I will have the paymaster hear the horse claim," he said. "Whether or not you agree with my watch order."

He summoned Ysabet and recorded Beren's refusal, confinement, visitors and responsible officer before her clerk. A witnessed copy would go to the village priest in Beren's wife's presence. The account would exist outside the household roll.

He would not release Beren while the men who accepted the murderous errand could threaten him. He would not call the restriction a favor.

"Who protects us from the men who protect him?" she asked.

"I cannot give you a good answer."

"Then keep looking."

---

Jalen found his name in the casualty book before he found his sister's.

The clerk brought the roll to him because Corven's borrowed knee could not manage the archive stairs. Beside *Jalen Orris* stood the north-gate muster, his death and a sum marked for settlement. The payment line was blank. Another hand had written *family inquiry outstanding*.

"Does that mean she asked?"

"It means someone was meant to establish the proper claimant."

"Did they?"

"The next page is missing from this copy. The original is with the paymaster."

"Then ask him."

The clerk glanced toward the door, waiting for a royal preference.

"I am asking," Jalen said. "Not Corven Sareth."

A page brought a note addressed to the envoy. Jalen did not open it.

"Take it to Yara Fen. If it concerns the benches, she will know."

"You are expected at council."

"I will go. I will not read his wife's letters for them."

He asked for the other north-gate casualty entries. The dead soldier was discovering the Crown's obligations to people who had remained alive.

---

At Dols the watch captain declined a second grain cart. Six sacks remained in the far-bank store without a hired northern carrier. Water had risen against the repaired timber lip. One successful crossing was not permanent permission.

Savelin received Rhyse's request to extend the forty-eight-hour forage reprieve. A local patrol officer could spare one more day for the hamlet whose people had gathered fodder under watch. The other had no secure alternative. He would not promise that hungry mounts could go without feed indefinitely.

Savelin sent the two answers separately. No soldier crossed Dols; no military countersign appeared.

The miller demanded payment for mud left against his garden wall by the drainage cut. The ferryman wanted his promised fee for watching the water. Savelin entered both claims. He was tired of sending the king bad news. That did not make the river drier.

---

Luceran came to Miren's door after the evening bell, carrying no wine and no letter.

"Ysabet says you have asked for a new household rule."

"I have."

"You could have asked me."

"I did. Before. You heard what I said in a room where I believed you could not hear."

He looked away. She did not repeat the injury so that he could apologize to it again.

"The grain has crossed."

"Six sacks."

"One hamlet keeps its animals another day," he said.

"And the other may not. You have people who tell you hard things. Why did you need a dead man who would not refuse?"

"I thought if Corven were gone, the refusals would stop."

"And did they?"

"No."

"Do you wish they had?"

Luceran remembered his relief when Jalen accepted a simple instruction without argument.

"Sometimes. That is what frightens me."

Miren had wanted him to deny it. She had also wanted him to be capable of saying it.

"Find someone who can tell you no when I am absent. And let them survive telling you."

"I have not harmed Beren."

"You have not let him go. Your order made a danger the guard must now manage. You do not get credit for the guard's decency."

"Will you speak with me tomorrow?"

"About the king's work, if I choose. Not as a reward for confessing tonight."

She closed the door. Only after his footsteps faded did she turn the bolt.

The village priest would receive Beren's account. Yara's warning was on the road. Jalen's request had reached the paymaster. None of those papers told the whole truth; each made it harder for one person to decide what the truth would be called.

In the family nave all seven candles burned.
