# Season Two, Episode Two — Terms of Service

The rules began with a chair.

Rhyse put it in the middle of the old council chamber before breakfast and told Luceran to sit in it.

Luceran looked at the chair.

Then at Rhyse.

"I have a throne."

"That is why I brought the chair."

Miren, already at the table with bread in one hand and yesterday's casualty revisions under the other, laughed into her sleeve.

Luceran sat.

The silver mask waited beside the cold hearth.

It had followed him from the throne room without being summoned. Nobody had asked whether that counted as obedience.

Ysabet sharpened a quill.

Canon Merel stood by the window. Damaris had three ledgers open and the expression of a woman who regarded metaphysics as an attempt to evade arithmetic. Calienne had agreed to attend for half an hour and no longer.

Vess had refused until someone told him the subject included pipes.

That had brought him immediately.

Rhyse remained standing.

"First rule," he said. "Nobody calls it when they are alone."

Luceran frowned. "Why?"

"Because yesterday you asked it what Montegrain deserved."

Luceran's head came up.

Miren stopped chewing.

He had told her. He had not told Rhyse.

Rhyse saw the question.

"The throne room guard heard your side of it."

Luceran looked toward the mask.

"Did you hear the other side?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Rhyse pointed at it.

"That."

"What?"

"Half the castle can overhear you, and the other half can ask the castle what it heard."

"Can they?"

The mask turned toward Luceran.

He felt six people notice.

"Can anyone ask you what you heard me say?"

"No."

"Who can?"

"You."

"Only me?"

"Those you permit."

Rhyse said, "Do not permit me."

Luceran looked at him.

"I mean it."

"Why?"

"Because you will forget when you are tired. Or angry. Or frightened. You will say, answer Rhyse, and six months later I will ask it something I should have had to ask you."

Miren put down her bread.

"Write that."

Ysabet did.

Luceran rubbed his forehead.

"We are not making laws from whatever sentence Rhyse says before breakfast."

"No," Ysabet said. "We are making a list of things that can become laws after someone sensible rewrites them."

Rhyse nodded at her.

"That is why she has the quill."

Luceran looked at the mask.

"What do you call yourself?"

"I am Edselsto."

"That is the castle."

"Yes."

"Are you the castle?"

"Yes."

Merel crossed his arms.

"Ask whether the stones are Edselsto."

Luceran did.

"Yes."

"The village?"

"No."

"The people inside?"

"No."

"The crown?"

"No."

"The throne?"

"Part of Edselsto."

Vess looked interested despite himself.

"The pipes?"

"Some."

"Which?"

"Those that remain mine."

Vess leaned forward.

Rhyse caught his sleeve.

"We are writing rules."

"I am learning which pipes belong to a ghost."

"You can do that after."

"I have men digging under a lime room because yesterday the ghost knew more about the water than I did."

"Exactly."

Vess looked at him.

Then slowly sat back.

Damaris tapped her ledger.

"Second rule. If the answer can cost money, it gets checked by someone alive."

Luceran sighed.

"You have been waiting to say that."

"Since midnight."

The mask turned toward her.

Damaris looked back at it without enthusiasm.

"I do not care if your ancestors buried ten thousand silver crowns under the privy. Until someone digs them up and counts them, I have no silver crowns."

"None are buried beneath the privy," Edselsto said.

Damaris stared at Luceran.

"Did you permit it to answer me?"

"No."

The room went still.

Edselsto remained motionless.

Luceran said, "Why did you answer?"

"You permitted questions in this room yesterday."

Rhyse closed his eyes.

Ysabet wrote very quickly.

Miren said, "And there is our morning."

---

They spent the next hour discovering that yesterday's permissions had edges nobody had defined.

Edselsto would answer Calienne about wounded people because Luceran had told it to answer her during the first night.

It would answer Rhyse about rooms and passages because Luceran had done the same in the music room.

It would answer Ysabet about what had been written in the room while she was present.

It would not answer Vess unless Luceran permitted it.

It answered Miren almost whenever she spoke.

Nobody could identify when that permission had begun.

Luceran disliked that most.

"Stop answering everyone."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Except me."

"Yes."

Miren raised a finger.

"What color is his coat?"

Silence.

"Good."

Rhyse said, "How many people are in the passage?"

Silence.

Calienne said, "How many wounded are in the upper house?"

Silence.

She nodded.

"Better."

Luceran said, "Answer Calienne on matters of immediate injury."

"Yes."

Calienne's eyes narrowed.

"No."

Luceran looked at her.

"No?"

"No permanent permission."

"You need to know if someone is dying."

"I need people to shout for me when someone is dying. I need runners. I need assistants. I need patients who know that if they tell me something in confidence, the walls are not part of my staff."

Merel said, "Quite."

Calienne glanced at him.

"Do not sound pleased. Your confessional is next."

Merel stopped looking pleased.

Luceran looked at Edselsto.

"If Calienne asks whether someone needs immediate aid, answer only if delay is likely to kill them."

"Yes."

Calienne said, "How do you know what is likely to kill them?"

"I have observed deaths."

That quieted the room.

Luceran felt the temptation arrive again.

Not horror.

Usefulness.

"How many?"

Miren said, "Luceran."

He looked at her.

She shook her head once.

He swallowed the question.

Calienne saw him do it.

"Good."

He hated that he wanted praise for restraint.

Rhyse moved to the table.

"Permissions end at sunset unless renewed."

"Why sunset?"

"Because we can remember sunset."

Damaris said, "And because clerks can mark a day."

Ysabet wrote it.

"Emergency permission can be spoken," she said. "Anything continuing should be written."

"Sealed," Rhyse added.

Merel said, "Witnessed."

Vess said, "Useful."

Everyone looked at him.

"What?"

"You have made a permission that requires a king, a clerk, a seal and witnesses. If a wall bursts at midnight, I will be ankle-deep before you finish deciding who may ask where the water went."

Damaris said, "He is right."

Rhyse looked wounded.

Vess smiled.

"Write that too."

They did.

---

The first emergency arrived before the ink dried.

A bell rang from the service court.

Then again.

Then shouting.

Calienne was on her feet first.

Smoke pushed beneath the council chamber door.

Rhyse drew his sword.

Luceran stood.

"Edselsto. Where is the fire?"

"Kitchen."

Vess swore and ran.

They followed.

The lower passage was already crowded with servants carrying buckets. Smoke rolled from the bake room, thick enough to sting the eyes.

"How bad?" Luceran shouted.

"Smoke in the kitchen. Heat at the east oven. Twelve people moving quickly."

"Is anyone trapped?"

"Two people are still."

Calienne shoved past him.

"Where?"

"One beside the flour bins. One beneath the west table."

Rhyse caught Luceran by the shoulder.

"The clerk."

Luceran understood.

Still did not mean trapped.

They reached the kitchen.

Bessa stood beside the flour bins with both hands on her hips, perfectly motionless because she was screaming at three scullions who had thrown water where she had explicitly told them not to.

Under the west table, Ardel lay flat on her back.

Calienne dropped beside her.

Ardel opened one eye.

"If you step on me, I will kill you."

Calienne sat back.

"Why are you under the table?"

"Because the flue plate fell, and I am trying to hook the bastard back into its slot."

Bessa saw Luceran.

"Out!"

He stopped.

"This is my kitchen."

"Then command it from the hall."

A scullion ran past with a bucket.

Bessa pointed.

"Sand, not water! Do you want the fat to carry it?"

Luceran retreated.

Rhyse followed, coughing.

The mask waited in the passage.

"Is the kitchen burning?" Luceran asked.

"Yes."

Bessa shouted from inside, "No, it bloody isn't!"

Luceran stared at the mask.

Edselsto said, "There is flame."

"Contained flame," Bessa shouted.

A moment later Ardel emerged blackened with soot and carrying an iron plate wrapped in wet cloth.

"Draft caught flour dust at the oven mouth. It is done."

Calienne came behind her.

"Nobody injured."

Rhyse leaned against the wall.

Luceran looked at Edselsto.

"You called that a fire."

"You asked where the fire was."

"Was the kitchen in danger?"

"There was heat and flame."

"Was Bessa controlling it?"

"I do not know."

Bessa appeared in the doorway.

"Then perhaps the wall should stop telling kings how to run kitchens."

She went back inside.

Miren, coughing into her sleeve, looked at Luceran.

"Write that."

Ysabet had brought the paper with her.

She did.

Observation is not judgment.

Luceran looked at the words.

Then added beneath them himself:

The house may report what it senses. It may not claim what those signs mean unless asked, and any such answer is advice, not fact.

Rhyse read it.

"Better."

Edselsto said nothing.

That was also better.

---

Vess found the silver before noon.

Not treasure.

Wire.

Or what had once been wire.

A green-black length of corroded metal emerged from the broken masonry below the lime room, thin as a quill shaft and laid inside a groove cut through stone.

Vess carried a piece to the council chamber between two scraps of leather.

"Not iron."

Damaris looked at it.

"Please say copper."

"Mostly."

"Mostly is an expensive word."

Vess scraped the corrosion with a knife.

A pale line shone beneath.

Damaris closed her eyes.

"Silver."

"Some silver."

"That is worse."

Luceran looked toward Edselsto.

The mask had been silent since the kitchen.

He had not ordered it silent.

He realized he missed its answers.

"Edselsto. What is this?"

"A listening line."

Merel made a quiet sound.

Vess did not.

"Where does it go?"

"Broken."

"That was not the question."

"Many ways are broken."

Vess put the metal on the table.

"Can it be repaired?"

"Yes."

Damaris said, "No."

Luceran looked at her.

"I did not ask whether we should."

"You were going to."

"How much silver?"

Edselsto answered, "Less than before."

Damaris laughed once.

"There. It has learned finance."

Vess bent over the fragment.

"If this carries whatever lets it hear, then yesterday's west feed problem may have opened something else when we disturbed the junction."

Rhyse said, "Meaning?"

"Meaning the gate moved. The stone collar shifted. The old water channel opened. We dug into it. Now we have found this. I would prefer not to repair one ancient thing and wake three more by accident."

Luceran said, "Can you map the lines?"

Vess looked at Edselsto.

"No."

"Can it?"

The room waited.

Luceran knew exactly what everyone was thinking.

One question.

Useful.

Bounded.

Reasonable.

He said, "Edselsto, can you tell Vess where the listening lines run?"

"Some."

Vess exhaled.

Damaris said, "And there goes my silver."

"No repairs," Luceran said.

Vess nodded.

"Survey only."

"No opening sealed walls without approval."

"Agreed."

"No replacing metal."

Vess looked pained.

"Agreed."

Rhyse said, "And the house does not direct workers."

Luceran glanced at him.

"Why?"

"Because a mason should know whether an order came from Vess, from you, or from a voice in the wall."

Vess nodded immediately.

"That one."

Ysabet wrote it.

The supernatural had required less than a day to create a question of chain of command.

Luceran wondered whether this was impressive or humiliating.

Probably both.

---

After midday, they stopped writing rules and began trying to break them.

Rhyse insisted.

"If a rule only works while everyone is calm and pleased with it, it is decoration."

They moved to the lesser audience room.

Ysabet read the provisional articles aloud.

Edselsto could answer Luceran freely.

Others required specific authorization.

Continuing authorization expired unless written, witnessed and renewed.

Emergency questions were permitted where delay threatened life, fire, structural collapse, water supply, or immediate defense.

Answers about people were information, not judgment.

No accusation, punishment, medical priority, property claim, payment, appointment, military objective or sentence could rest solely on Edselsto's word.

The castle could advise on its own structure but could not command living workers.

Private chambers, medical care, confession and sealed council business were to be treated as restricted unless immediate danger overrode privacy.

Luceran listened.

Then said, "Rhyse may ask Edselsto any question concerning the security of the castle."

Rhyse stared at him.

"No."

"I am testing it."

"Test someone else."

"Ask something."

Rhyse's jaw tightened.

Then he turned to the mask.

"How many armed men are in the east barracks?"

"Twenty-seven."

"How many are loyal to the king?"

Silence.

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

Luceran said, "Answer him."

"Unknown."

Rhyse's expression did not change.

"Which of them fought for Montegrain?"

"Nine."

"Names?"

"Some."

"Give them."

Miren said, "Rhyse."

He kept looking at the mask.

"Give me the names."

It began.

"Jorren Vale. Pet—"

"Stop."

The mask stopped.

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

"That is how easy it is."

Luceran felt heat rise in his face.

"You asked."

"Because you told me to."

"Yes."

"And if tomorrow I decide everyone who fought for Montegrain is a security risk, I can turn your permission into a list."

"They are already being classified."

"By testimony, surrender terms, conduct, evidence and individual judgment."

Ysabet said quietly, "Not by walls."

Rhyse nodded.

Luceran looked at the page.

"Security questions cannot identify guilt."

"Better," Rhyse said.

Miren leaned back.

"My turn."

Luceran eyed her.

"I dislike that tone."

"You should."

She faced the mask.

"Where did Luceran sleep last night?"

Silence.

Luceran said nothing.

Miren smiled.

"Good."

Then she looked at him.

"Now authorize me."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because I know where this is going."

"Then the rule works."

Damaris said, "Until he wants you to know something."

Miren's smile faded.

That was the harder part.

Luceran could grant access as affection.

He could withhold it as punishment.

He could turn privacy into favor.

Everyone in the room understood it at once.

Nobody needed to explain.

Ysabet wrote for a while.

Then slid the page toward Luceran.

Authorization may define a duty. It may not make private access a personal reward.

Luceran read it twice.

"That sounds aimed at me."

"It is," Miren said.

He signed beneath it.

---

Merel waited until the others had gone for food.

He and Luceran remained with the mask.

The priest shut the door.

"Ask it whether it hears confession."

Luceran did not want to.

That was reason enough.

"Do you hear confession?"

"Sometimes."

Merel went pale.

Luceran stood.

"Which?"

"Words spoken where I hear."

"Do you understand they are forbidden to you?"

"I understand you forbid them."

Merel flinched.

"That is not the same thing."

"No," Luceran said.

He looked at the mask.

"Can you forget them?"

"No."

The answer was gentle.

That made it worse.

Merel walked to the window.

For several breaths he said nothing.

Then: "There are things a king is not entitled to know."

Luceran felt the old royal instinct rise before he could stop it.

"Even if they concern danger to the realm?"

Merel turned.

"Especially then."

"That makes no sense."

"It makes excellent sense. If a man believes confession is merely another road to the Crown, he will stop confessing. You will lose the warning you think you gain."

Luceran stared at him.

Merel's hands were trembling.

Not from fear of Edselsto.

From anger.

Luceran had rarely seen him angry.

"What do you want me to do?"

"Do not ask."

"That is all?"

"What else can you do? It says it cannot forget."

Luceran looked at the mask.

"Can you be made unable to hear a room?"

"Yes."

Merel turned back.

"How?"

"Break the listening ways."

Vess was going to hate this.

Luceran almost smiled.

Then did not.

"Can they be closed without breaking them?"

"Some."

"Show Vess which."

Merel said, "No."

Luceran looked at him.

The priest's anger had settled into something harder.

"Show Vess how. Do not show him which rooms until the people responsible for those rooms choose."

Luceran nodded slowly.

"Yes."

He looked at Edselsto.

"Do that."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Merel exhaled.

It was the first rule they wrote because someone had something to lose besides control.

---

They returned to the council chamber at dusk.

The page had become six pages.

The six pages had become nine because Ysabet refused to squeeze additions into margins.

Damaris wanted a copy kept with the household accounts because permissions could create expense.

Rhyse wanted one with the guard orders.

Merel wanted one in the chapel archive.

Miren wanted a copy somewhere Luceran could not quietly lose it.

"I am sitting here," he said.

"I know."

Calienne returned long enough to cross out three sentences and replace them with one.

Vess arrived with dust in his hair and reported that at least four listening lines could be isolated by removing small silver bridge pieces from junction boxes.

Damaris asked how many bridge pieces.

Vess said he did not know.

She asked how much silver.

He said not enough to matter.

She told him never to say that again.

For a few minutes the room felt ordinary.

Luceran loved them for it.

That frightened him too.

Ysabet sanded the final page.

"Read them?"

"No," Luceran said.

Everyone looked at him.

He took the pages.

"I will."

He read each article aloud.

Not as proclamation.

As promise.

When he finished, the room was dark except for lamps.

The mask floated near the hearth.

Luceran faced it.

"These rules govern how we use you."

"Yes."

"You will obey them."

"Yes."

"Even if I tell you not to."

The mask did not hesitate.

"If you command me to ignore them, I will obey."

Nobody moved.

Luceran felt the floor beneath his boots.

The paper in his hand.

The weight of eight living people watching him.

He had expected a lock.

He had written a mirror.

Rhyse spoke first.

"Then they are not rules for it."

"No," Miren said.

Luceran looked at her.

She was tired.

There was soot still caught near one ear from the kitchen.

"They are rules for us."

Damaris closed her ledger.

"For you, mostly."

Luceran almost objected.

Then stopped.

Edselsto waited.

Perfectly patient.

Perfectly willing.

Luceran looked down at the signatures.

Rhyse.

Miren.

Ysabet.

Merel.

Damaris.

Calienne.

Vess.

His own.

He understood then why a king's promise needed witnesses.

Not because witnesses made the promise true.

Because someday the king might want it not to be.

Luceran folded the pages.

"Three copies," he said.

Ysabet nodded.

"One with the guard. One with Merel. One with Damaris."

Miren raised an eyebrow.

"None with you?"

"I know what they say."

"That has never stopped anyone."

He smiled despite himself.

"Four copies."

"Better."

The mask inclined its pale face.

"Your Majesty."

Luceran looked at it.

"No questions tonight."

"Yes."

He left the chamber with people around him.

The mask did not follow.

Halfway down the passage, Luceran stopped and looked back.

It remained in the doorway.

Waiting for tomorrow.

Miren touched his shoulder.

"Come on."

He went with her.
