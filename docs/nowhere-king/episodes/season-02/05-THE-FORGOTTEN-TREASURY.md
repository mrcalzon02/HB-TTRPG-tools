# Season Two, Episode 5 — The Forgotten Treasury

Damaris refused to call it a treasury until somebody opened it.

"Edselsto remembers silver," Luceran said.

"So does every thief who has ever seen a coin."

The pale mask waited at the end of the east-foundation passage while Vess crouched beside a seam in the masonry. There was no door. There was a wall, two different ages of mortar, and an offended master of works.

"One hundred and eighty-three years?" Vess asked.

"Since this threshold last opened," Edselsto said.

"Threshold."

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Before you."

Vess stood.

"That is a wall."

"It became one."

Luceran smiled.

Vess did not.

Behind them Ysabet had three clerks, two archive boxes and instructions to record every person who crossed the work cordon. Rhyse had four living guards. Damaris had brought an assayer before anyone had found anything to assay.

That, Luceran thought, was what governing with miracles apparently looked like.

"Can you open it?" he asked Edselsto.

"No."

The answer pleased Vess so much that Luceran almost laughed.

"Good," Vess said. "Then we use tools."

They did.

It took most of the morning.

The wall had not been designed as a secret mechanism waiting for royal blood. Someone had deliberately filled an older doorway with fitted stone and lime. The outer courses came away cleanly. The inner ones did not. Pell Falbrecht arrived with wedges, declared the first approach idiotic, was told nobody had invited him, and improved it anyway.

By noon there was a hole large enough for a lamp.

Cold air came through.

Not tomb air. Dry air.

Vess held the lamp through first.

"Floor. Shelves. Chests."

Damaris moved closer.

"How many?"

"I can see six."

"Do not touch them."

"I was not planning to lick them."

"That was not the danger I had in mind."

Rhyse sent a guard through after Vess had shored the opening. Then Vess. Then Ysabet. Luceran went fourth and disliked being fourth until he remembered why he was fourth.

The chamber was longer than the room above it and lower than expected. Thirty-one chests stood on stone runners. Shelves held sealed jars, wrapped bars, three rotten document cases and stacks of blackened plate.

Silver showed where cloth had failed.

Luceran stopped breathing for half a second.

Damaris entered behind him.

"No."

He turned.

"I didn't say anything."

"You had a face."

"What face?"

"The face kings make immediately before spending money."

Ysabet laughed.

Damaris pointed at the nearest chest.

"Nobody opens anything until it has a number."

Luceran looked at thirty-one chests.

"All of them?"

"Especially all of them."

The mask remained outside the broken threshold.

Luceran noticed.

"Edselsto."

"Your Majesty."

"What is this room?"

"The Eastern Household Reserve."

Damaris looked up sharply.

"Reserve for what?"

"I remember silver."

"That was not the question."

"I remember payments."

"What payments?"

A pause.

"Many."

Damaris stared at Luceran.

"Your miracle has discovered accounting."

---

By evening the treasure had become a problem.

Chest One contained clipped royal plate and old mint bars.

Chest Two contained temple-marked cups wrapped beside a ledger fragment naming three funerary foundations.

Chest Three held coin.

Chest Four held coin and six packets of sealed merchant sureties.

Chest Five held nothing but corroded hinges.

Chest Six contained widow-payment rolls from a border levy no living clerk remembered.

By Chest Nine, Luceran stopped asking how rich he was.

By Chest Twelve, Damaris stopped permitting the word rich in the room.

"This is custody," she said. "Not ownership."

"It is under my castle."

"So is Miren's jewelry."

Miren, who had arrived after supper, said, "A persuasive legal doctrine. I oppose it."

Luceran rubbed his face.

Ysabet laid out a surviving strip of parchment.

"'Held against eastern road obligation.'"

"What does that mean?"

"It means someone expected the money to answer for something."

"What?"

"The rest is mold."

Damaris pointed at the chests.

"Temple deposits. Compensation. Sureties. Foreign gifts. Forfeitures. Maintenance stock. Crown reserve. Perhaps private deposits. We do not convert uncertainty into royal title because the king has bills."

Luceran looked at the silver.

He had bills.

Jalen Orris's household had bills. Iven Marrick's mother had compensation due regardless of what remained at the gate. Northbridge needed timber. Red Ford wanted surveyors. The cistern lip needed rebuilding. Guards needed wages. Vess wanted silver.

The money sat three feet away.

"How long to know?"

Ysabet answered before Damaris.

"Some? Days. All? Months. Perhaps never."

"Then what can we use?"

Damaris smiled without pleasure.

"The part we can prove is yours."

---

The next morning Hesta Vale arrived with a dead man's receipt.

She was sixty if Luceran had to guess, broad-backed, grey-haired, and furious at being searched twice.

"My grandfather's grandfather," she said, "hauled stone for the eastern road."

Damaris took the folded scrap without touching the seal.

"Where did you get this?"

"Family box."

"Why bring it now?"

"Because half the village knows you found old money."

Rhyse closed his eyes.

Luceran said, "Half?"

Hesta looked at him.

"Majesty, Pell Falbrecht helped open the wall."

Fair.

Ysabet compared the receipt against a recovered compensation roll.

Then again.

Then fetched another clerk.

Hesta waited without asking permission to sit.

Finally Ysabet said, "The name is here."

Hesta did not smile.

"Paid?"

Ysabet looked at Damaris.

"Marked assessed. No payment mark."

Hesta's jaw tightened.

Luceran asked, "How much?"

"Twenty-eight silver crowns."

The room went quiet.

It was not a fortune to a king.

It had been one to a carter.

Hesta said, "With one hundred and eighty-three years' interest?"

Damaris actually laughed.

Hesta did too.

That saved the negotiation.

"No," Damaris said.

"Thought I'd ask."

"What do you want?"

"Not twenty-eight old crowns."

Luceran blinked.

Hesta leaned forward.

"My family has a quarry cut east of Vale Brook. Kaelmont carriers take the good road. We take mud. Give us six seasons' right to haul Crown stone through the lower royal track without toll, and settle the old account."

Damaris's eyes sharpened.

"Three seasons."

"Five."

"Three, and the right ends if the track is closed for military use."

"Four, same condition."

Luceran looked between them.

Damaris said, "Four."

Hesta held out her hand.

Damaris did not take it.

"Written."

Hesta grinned.

"Better."

Luceran watched a two-century debt become a road privilege.

Edselsto had found silver.

Living people turned it into politics.

---

The first theft happened before any coin left the chamber.

A junior archive copyist named Senn Daro offered a Cendreval retainer the preliminary chest list for two crowns.

The retainer reported him.

Not from virtue.

Alys Cendreval arrived personally to explain.

"If I buy stolen inventories from your clerks today, Majesty, tomorrow my competitors buy mine."

Senn stood between two guards looking ill.

Luceran asked, "Did you offer it?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Senn swallowed.

"Two crowns is six weeks."

"For what?"

"My sister's apprenticeship bond."

There it was.

Not a conspiracy.

Money.

Ysabet looked angrier than Luceran had ever seen her.

"You had access because I trusted you."

"I know."

"Did you copy anything?"

"No."

"Did anyone else ask?"

"No."

Alys said, "My man asked what was in the east room. Daro said he could provide a list for two crowns. My man brought him to me."

Senn stared at the floor.

Luceran wanted a clean answer.

Dismiss him and prove the archive had standards. Keep him and prove desperation mattered. Punish him and perhaps destroy the apprenticeship he had been stupid enough to risk his position to fund.

Ysabet said, "He cannot remain on sealed inventory."

Senn flinched.

"Agreed," Luceran said.

Damaris said, "Dock wages?"

Ysabet looked at Senn.

"One week. Transfer him to public copy work. No sealed material for a year. If he sells information again, he is gone."

Rhyse asked, "The sister?"

Senn looked up.

Ysabet's anger changed shape.

"Her apprenticeship is not his office."

Luceran nodded.

"Good."

Senn began to cry only after he had been dismissed.

Alys watched him go.

"Your castle finds treasure quickly."

Luceran looked at her.

"People find ways to make it complicated quickly."

"That is why treasure is valuable."

---

Three days of inventory produced a number.

Damaris made Luceran wait until everyone responsible for the number was in the room.

Ysabet for provenance.

Master Ors for assay.

Two clerks for count.

A temple representative because some deposits bore sacred marks.

Hesta Vale because her claim had made everyone suddenly aware that old obligations could still possess descendants.

Miren because Luceran had asked her to come.

Vess because he wanted money.

Rhyse because money attracted knives.

Damaris placed one sheet on the table.

"Immediately defensible Crown property."

Luceran read the number.

Then read it again.

"That's all?"

Damaris smiled.

"There is the king I know."

It was enough.

Not enough to transform the realm. Not enough to build armies from air. Not enough to restore every silver channel.

Enough to pay the already-promised battle compensation without delaying Northbridge timber.

Enough to fund the Red Ford survey.

Enough to repair the cistern and two roofs.

Enough to maintain guard recruitment.

Enough to authorize a second bounded phase of conduit restoration.

Months of breathing room.

Not years.

Luceran sat back.

"And the rest?"

"Restricted, disputed, unproven, or somebody else's until shown otherwise."

"How much somebody else's?"

"We do not know."

"How much might become ours?"

"We do not know."

He looked at Edselsto.

Damaris slapped the table.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You had the face again."

Miren laughed.

Luceran did too.

Then he looked at the allocation sheet.

"Compensation first."

Damaris nodded once.

"Northbridge."

"Already due."

"Red Ford."

"Survey only. No settlement invented by coin."

"Vess."

Vess sat straighter.

Damaris said, "Six additional marks of silver restoration material after physical survey and ordinary procurement."

Luceran frowned.

"Six?"

"You wanted twelve."

"I did."

"Then I have saved you six."

Alys Cendreval would call that an opening bid.

Luceran signed.

---

That night he and Miren walked the east passage without attendants close enough to hear them.

The broken treasury wall had become a guarded temporary door. A black-ear tile had not been installed.

The chamber remained deaf.

Miren touched the lintel.

"You could have taken all of it."

"I could have ordered Damaris to call it mine."

"Yes."

"She would have refused."

"Would she?"

Luceran considered.

"Probably."

"And then?"

He looked at her.

That was the question Edselsto never made him answer.

"And then I would have had to decide whether I wanted a treasurer or obedience."

Miren slipped her hand into his.

"What did you decide?"

"Today?"

She smiled.

"Today."

"A treasurer."

"Good."

They stood together in the cold passage.

After a while Luceran said, "It solved the problem."

"No."

He looked at her.

"It found a room."

"The room contained the money."

"Some money."

"Enough."

"Enough because Damaris decided what could be spent, Ysabet found ownership, Ors assayed it, Hesta made a claim, guards kept it from walking away, and someone two centuries dead put papers beside the chests."

Luceran leaned against the wall.

"You are ruining miracles."

"I am trying to keep them small enough to survive."

The mask appeared at the far end of the passage.

It stopped outside conversational distance.

Luceran had not summoned it.

He could have told it to leave.

Instead he said, "Edselsto."

"Your Majesty."

"Are there other rooms like this?"

Miren's fingers tightened around his.

The mask answered.

"I remember other rooms."

"With silver?"

"Some."

"Coin?"

"I remember weight. Metal. Paper. Doors."

Miren said nothing.

Luceran knew what she wanted him to notice.

He noticed it.

Then he asked anyway.

"Show Vess the next threshold tomorrow."

The mask bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Under the existing survey rules," Luceran added. "No opening without inventory staff, guard custody and provenance review."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The safeguards were real.

That was what made the decision comfortable.

Luceran watched the mask disappear.

Miren did not release his hand.

"You know what worries me?"

"That I asked."

"No."

He waited.

"That you remembered the rules while asking."

He frowned.

"Isn't that what you wanted?"

"It is."

"Then why—"

"Because now this can become ordinary."

The east-foundation reserve had not paid for magic.

It had paid compensation, timber, stone, guards, surveyors, clerks, repairs and a little more silver in the walls.

That was better.

It was also worse.

A miracle that only dazzled could be distrusted.

A miracle that balanced accounts could acquire a department.
