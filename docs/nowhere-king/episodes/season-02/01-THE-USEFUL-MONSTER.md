# Season Two, Episode 1 — The Useful Monster

The mask waited.

Nobody in the throne room seemed willing to discover what happened if it became impatient.

Luceran had imagined, at various times in his life, what it would feel like to be acknowledged by Edselsto.

As a child he had imagined trumpets.

At fourteen he had imagined his father finally looking impressed.

At twenty he had imagined nothing at all, because by then ancestral approval had become one more thing old men used to explain why young men should obey them.

Now he sat half-collapsed in the throne, blood dried beneath one cuff, soot on his face, the crown abandoned on a table beside him, while a silver-white mask floated three paces away and called him king.

Miren drew her sword.

The mask turned toward her.

Rhyse said, "Don't."

"I haven't done anything."

"You were considering it loudly."

Miren did not lower the blade.

The mask looked back to Luceran.

"Your Majesty."

Luceran swallowed.

"Yes."

Nothing happened.

Rhyse looked at him.

Luceran looked at Rhyse.

"What?"

"You answered it."

"It spoke to me."

"So did Montegrain."

From beneath the east wall, Montegrain woke.

He blinked at the room, at the drawn weapons, at the black vapor lying thin as spilled ink around the foot of the throne.

Then he saw the mask.

His bound hands tightened.

"What is that?"

Nobody answered.

The mask did.

"I serve the sovereign of Edselsto."

Miren's sword came up another inch.

Luceran said, "What are you?"

There was a pause.

Not hesitation.

Something stranger.

The mask remained perfectly still while the answer seemed to arrive from the stone around them.

"Edselsto."

Aven made a sound that might have been prayer or disbelief.

Rusk, seated under guard near the records table, laughed once.

Everyone looked at him.

He had blood on his mouth.

"Of course," he said. "Of course the damned house picks tonight."

Rhyse crossed the room and struck him with the back of one hand.

Not hard.

Hard enough.

"Quiet."

Rusk smiled through the new blood.

The mask did not react.

Luceran stared at it.

"Did you open the gates?"

"Yes."

"Because I ordered you?"

"Yes."

"Could you hear me before that?"

"Yes."

That changed the room.

Miren lowered her sword, but only because she needed her other hand free to point.

"How long?"

The mask did not answer.

Miren looked at Luceran.

"It only answers you?"

Luceran said, "Answer her."

The mask turned.

"How long?" Miren repeated.

"Within the places where I can hear."

Rhyse said, "That is not a measure."

"No."

The answer was so immediate that Mara laughed from exhaustion.

Nobody joined her.

Rhyse stepped closer.

"Where can you hear?"

The mask looked at Luceran.

Luceran felt something ugly and warm move through his chest.

Not fear.

Possession.

He hated that he recognized it.

"Answer him."

"Some rooms. Some passages. Some walls. Less than once. More than when you arrived."

"Which rooms?"

"Not all."

Rhyse stared at the mask.

Then at Luceran.

"That thing is going to make me violent."

"It opened the gates."

"So did men, eventually."

"It opened them when men couldn't."

"Yes."

Luceran heard the sharpness in his own voice.

Rhyse heard it too.

Neither apologized.

Calienne entered carrying a basin pink with somebody else's blood.

She stopped.

Looked at the mask.

Looked at Luceran.

Looked at Miren's sword.

Then said, "No."

Luceran blinked.

"I haven't asked you anything."

"You were about to."

"I was not."

"You have the face."

"What face?"

"The one you make when something impossible has become your responsibility and you intend to give it to me."

The mask turned toward her.

Calienne set the basin down.

"If it bleeds, bring it to me. If it doesn't, I have forty-three people who do."

She picked the basin up again.

The mask said, "There are fifty-one living wounded within the upper house."

Calienne stopped.

The room stopped with her.

She turned very slowly.

"Say that again."

"Fifty-one living wounded within the upper house."

Calienne's face emptied.

"How do you know?"

"Sound. Heat. Movement. Memory of rooms."

Rhyse said, "You said some rooms."

"Yes."

"Are the wounded in rooms you hear?"

"Some."

"Then how do you count the others?"

"Passage."

Calienne put the basin down a second time.

"Where are the eight I have not counted?"

The mask looked at Luceran.

He almost said answer her.

Miren beat him to it.

"No."

Everyone looked at her.

Calienne's expression sharpened.

Miren pointed toward the mask.

"We do not ask it to find people until we know what finding means."

"There are wounded."

"I know."

"There are eight wounded."

"It says there are."

Calienne's jaw tightened.

Miren stepped closer.

"You just told Merel you know what you know."

Calienne looked at her.

The anger lasted three breaths.

Then went away.

"Fair."

Rhyse said, "We search normally."

Luceran stared at him.

"If there are eight—"

"We search normally."

"People could die."

"Yes."

The word landed badly.

Rhyse did not soften it.

"If it can tell us where every wounded man is, we learn something. If it cannot, we learn something. If it lies, we learn something. If we make it the only way we know anything before sunrise, we learn nothing except how quickly we can become dependent."

The mask remained motionless.

Luceran hated him for being right.

That, too, felt familiar.

"Search," Luceran said.

Calienne picked up her basin.

"Already happening."

She left.

The mask watched the doorway after her.

Luceran said, "Do you know where they are?"

"Yes."

Rhyse closed his eyes.

Miren said, "You bastard."

It was not clear whether she meant Luceran or the castle.

---

The first of the eight was behind a collapsed linen press in the north guest passage.

They found him because Bessa heard him kicking.

The second had crawled into a cupboard after taking a cut beneath the ribs.

The third was a coup guard who had hidden his wound because he expected surrender to end with a rope.

The fourth was dead by the time Calienne reached him.

That produced an argument over whether the mask had lied.

"It said living," Mara said.

"He may have been living when it counted," Calienne answered.

"Or it may not know the difference."

"That too."

The fifth and sixth were found together.

The seventh was not wounded at all. He was drunk, concussed, and asleep beneath a stair.

Rhyse personally wrote that down.

The eighth was a kitchen girl named Nella with a broken ankle who had refused to call for help because three armed men had been fighting in the passage outside.

She was furious at everyone.

This reassured Calienne more than anything supernatural had.

By dawn, forty-nine living wounded were under some kind of care.

Two had died.

The number had changed three times.

The mask had not corrected itself.

Rhyse wrote that down too.

---

The dead were harder.

Not because Edselsto could not count them.

Because Calienne would not let it.

"They have names," she said.

They were in the family nave, because it was dry.

They were in the lesser chapel, because it was empty.

They were beneath canvas in the old receiving room, because there was nowhere else.

Loyalists lay beside men who had fought for Montegrain.

Household workers lay beside soldiers.

A woman from the village lay beside a man whose name nobody knew because he had arrived with Rusk's hired company two days before the coup.

Canon Merel objected to none of it.

He had stopped objecting to many things during the night.

Mara stood with a slate.

"Jalen Orris."

A clerk marked the name.

"Household?"

"Royal guard. Sister in Moncler."

"Name?"

"I don't know."

"Then leave room."

"Iven Marrick."

Mara's voice changed.

The clerk looked up.

"Marrick?"

"Yes."

"Household?"

"Mara," Calienne said quietly.

Mara swallowed.

"Mother living. Two brothers. South market district in the capital. He sent money."

The clerk wrote.

At the far end of the room, Luceran listened.

He had not intended to.

He had come to ask how many.

That question now seemed obscene.

Calienne saw him.

"Come here."

He obeyed.

She handed him a strip of linen.

"What is this?"

"Hold his jaw."

Luceran looked down.

The dead man beneath her hands was one of Aven's.

He recognized the sash.

"I don't know him."

"No."

"Why am I—"

"Because I need two hands."

Luceran held the dead man's jaw while Calienne bound it closed.

The man's beard was wet.

His skin was already losing the quality that made skin belong to someone.

Luceran looked away.

Calienne said, "Don't."

He looked back.

When they finished, she moved to the next body.

"Name?"

A surrendered guard standing nearby said, "Petran Sol."

The clerk wrote.

Luceran said, "Family?"

The guard looked surprised.

"His wife is in Deren Holt."

"Children?"

"One. Maybe two."

"Maybe?"

"I didn't know him well."

Luceran nodded.

"Write one confirmed, second reported."

The clerk looked at him.

Then wrote it exactly that way.

Nobody mentioned the mask.

That was the first useful thing they did without it.

---

By midmorning the throne room had become unusable.

Not symbolically.

Practically.

Too many people needed to pass through it, and everyone slowed down when they saw the mask.

It had not moved far from the throne.

It did not breathe.

It did not fidget.

It did not become bored.

People did.

That difference made Luceran uneasy.

He moved the working tables to the old council chamber.

The mask followed him.

Miren noticed first.

"Did you call it?"

"No."

Rhyse turned.

The mask floated through the doorway behind Luceran with the quiet inevitability of moonlight.

Rhyse put down his cup.

"Go back."

The mask stopped.

Luceran looked at him.

Rhyse said, "I am testing something."

The mask remained where it was.

"Go back to the throne room."

Nothing.

Rhyse looked at Luceran.

Luceran sighed.

"Go back to the throne room."

The mask turned and left.

Miren watched it disappear.

"I hate that."

Luceran said, "Why?"

"Because it was funny for half a second."

Rhyse nodded.

"That is also why I hate it."

Luceran sat.

The council chamber smelled of wet wool, smoke, ink and the medicinal vinegar Calienne had begun throwing at everything.

On the table lay four lists.

Dead.

Wounded.

Prisoners.

Missing.

The fifth list was money.

It was already the longest.

Damaris had arrived before breakfast and taken possession of it with the grim happiness of a woman whose problems had become so terrible that nobody could accuse her of exaggeration.

"Compensation," she said. "Food. Timber. Linen. Medicine. Replacement locks. Glass. Lamp oil. Horses. Three carts damaged, one burned, one missing. Kitchen stores spoiled. Archive damage. Guard pay. Extra village watches. Burial cloth. Messages to families."

Luceran rubbed his eyes.

"How much?"

"I don't know."

"You always know."

"I know when the numbers exist."

"Estimate."

"No."

"Damaris."

"No."

Miren smiled into her cup.

Damaris pointed at the king.

"You have spent a season learning that asking me for a number does not create one."

"I thought sovereignty might help."

"It has not."

Rhyse said, "Good."

Luceran looked around the table.

"This is becoming hostile."

"Your Majesty," Damaris said, "the hostile people are in the cells."

A messenger entered.

"Speaking of."

He carried a folded note.

Rhyse read it first.

"Aven requests that the surrendered east guard be kept under the protections agreed last night."

Luceran said, "Yes."

Miren said, "Write it."

"He already has my word."

"Write it."

Luceran looked at her.

She did not blink.

He took the paper.

"Bring Ysabet."

The messenger left.

Damaris returned to her lists.

Luceran said, "Montegrain?"

Rhyse's face changed.

"Still asking to speak to you."

"No."

"Rusk?"

"Asked for wine."

Miren said, "Give him water."

"I did."

"Pellan?"

"Still separate."

Luceran nodded.

Then there was silence.

The mask was not in the room.

He could feel its absence.

That frightened him more than its presence had.

---

They tested it after noon.

Not because Luceran wanted to.

Because everyone agreed that pretending it did not exist would be stupider than asking careful questions.

Canon Merel insisted on witnessing.

Calienne arrived late and irritated.

Ysabet brought paper.

Rhyse brought three guards.

Miren brought herself.

Luceran brought the mask.

He hated that this was already how he thought of it.

The test took place in the old music room because it had two doors, one window, bare walls, and no wounded people.

Ysabet wrote the first question before anyone spoke.

Can you hear us here?

Luceran asked it aloud.

"Yes."

"Could you hear us here yesterday?"

"Faintly."

"Could you hear us here when I first arrived at Edselsto?"

"No."

Ysabet wrote.

Rhyse said, "Ask why."

Luceran did.

"Opened ways. Repaired metal. Lit rooms. Returned authority."

Merel looked sharply at the mask.

"What authority?"

It did not answer him.

Luceran repeated the question.

"Yours."

That did not improve Merel's expression.

Miren said, "Ask whether it knows what is happening in the village."

"No."

"Ask whether it can see the gate."

"Not as you see."

Rhyse said, "That answer I believe."

Ysabet looked up.

"Why?"

"Because it is irritating."

Luceran almost laughed.

The mask turned toward him.

He stopped.

Calienne noticed.

Of course she did.

She said, "Ask it how many people are in this room."

Luceran counted first.

Seven living people.

The mask.

"Seven."

"Ask it how many are in the passage."

"Four."

Rhyse opened the door.

Three guards and a clerk looked back.

The clerk held a stack of paper.

Rhyse closed the door.

"Wrong."

The mask said, "One is still."

Rhyse opened the door again.

The clerk stared at him.

"Move."

"What?"

"Walk."

The clerk took two confused steps.

Rhyse closed the door.

"How many now?"

"Four."

Ysabet wrote.

Luceran stared at the mask.

"So you did not know he was there."

"I did not know he was a person."

The room became very quiet.

Merel crossed himself.

Miren said, "Good."

Everyone looked at her.

"Good?"

"Yes. It can be wrong."

Luceran felt offended.

The emotion arrived before reason.

He recognized that too.

Miren saw it.

Her face softened, which somehow made it worse.

"Luceran."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

Rhyse said, "Then say it."

Luceran looked at him.

Rhyse did not look away.

Luceran turned toward Ysabet.

"Write: it can mistake a motionless person for part of the room."

Ysabet wrote.

The mask said nothing.

Calienne asked, "Does that trouble you?"

It turned toward her.

Then toward Luceran.

Luceran said, "Answer."

"No."

"Why?"

"I obey with what I have."

Calienne's expression changed.

Not fear.

Recognition.

"That," she said, "is dangerous."

Luceran looked at her.

"Because it can be wrong?"

"Because it can be wrong without doubt."

The mask inclined slightly.

"Would doubt improve obedience?"

Nobody spoke.

Luceran felt the question enter him like a blade.

Miren answered before he could.

"Often."

The mask did not reply.

---

That evening they found the first problem Edselsto could solve better than any of them.

It was not a murderer.

It was not a conspiracy.

It was water.

The lower cistern had begun draining faster than it filled.

Vess arrived filthy, furious, and carrying a broken wooden gauge.

"The old feed is losing pressure."

Damaris closed her eyes.

"How bad?"

"If we keep using at today's rate? Tomorrow night."

"Can the village wells cover?"

"Not the castle and camp."

"Repair?"

"If I knew where the break was, perhaps."

Luceran looked toward the wall.

Everyone saw him do it.

That was the moment.

Not when the gates opened.

Not when the mask bowed.

When six tired people with a real problem all thought of the same impossible servant.

Miren looked at Luceran.

Luceran looked at Rhyse.

Rhyse looked at Vess.

Vess said, "What?"

Nobody answered.

Then the mask emerged from the darkened doorway.

Vess stepped backward so quickly he struck the table.

"What in hell is that?"

"Complicated," Luceran said.

"That is not an answer."

"No."

The mask faced Luceran.

"Your Majesty."

Luceran felt relief.

Immediate.

Physical.

Disgusting.

"Do you know where the lower cistern is losing water?"

"Yes."

Vess stared at him.

Then at the mask.

Then back.

"You had a talking wall and waited until now?"

"It is not a wall."

"What is it?"

"Complicated."

"Still not an answer."

The mask said, "The west feed has opened beneath the lime room."

Vess went still.

"There is no west feed."

"There was."

"Where?"

"Below the lime room."

Vess looked at Luceran.

"I need picks."

Rhyse said, "You need two guards and someone who knows whether the floor is going to collapse."

"I need picks first."

They went.

Luceran did not.

That mattered.

Vess took masons, two guards, a clerk, and Canon Merel because Merel insisted that if they were about to dig where a ghost told them, someone ought to be professionally offended.

They found the old feed.

They also found why it had opened.

One of the gate mechanisms, moving for the first time in generations, had shifted a cracked stone collar in a buried junction. Water was spilling through an abandoned channel toward the old lower works.

Edselsto had not caused the damage deliberately.

Edselsto had not prevented it.

Edselsto knew where the water was going.

By midnight, Vess had the break choked with timber, wool, clay and profanity.

The cistern began rising.

That was worse than if the mask had been useless.

---

Luceran found Miren on the western gallery.

Below them, lanterns moved through the village.

The dead were being named.

The wounded were being counted again.

Somewhere a hammer struck metal with the slow rhythm of repair.

Miren leaned against the stone.

"You were right," Luceran said.

"I usually am."

"Do not ruin this."

She smiled.

He stood beside her.

For a while neither spoke.

Then Luceran said, "It saved the water."

"Vess saved the water."

"It told him where."

"Yes."

"Without it—"

"We might have found the break tomorrow."

"After losing the cistern."

"Possibly."

"People need water."

"I know."

"You keep saying everything as if I am trying to seduce you into something."

Miren looked at him.

"No."

"What, then?"

"I think it is seducing you."

Luceran laughed once.

There was no humor in it.

"It is a mask."

"It does not need to want anything."

He looked at her.

She turned toward him.

"That is the problem."

He said nothing.

Miren touched his cut hand.

Gently.

"Montegrain loved you and betrayed you."

Luceran's jaw tightened.

"Aven thought he was saving the realm from you."

"Do not."

"Rusk wanted to own the result. Pellan betrayed you and then saved you. Ardel argues. Rhyse tells you no in front of soldiers. Calienne ignores your rank when someone is bleeding. Damaris refuses to invent numbers because you are tired of waiting."

Luceran pulled his hand away.

Miren let him.

"And that thing?"

He looked toward the dark doorway.

"It obeys."

"Exactly."

The word hurt more than accusation would have.

Luceran said, "Maybe I have had enough of being protected by people who decide what I should be allowed to have."

Miren's face changed.

There it was.

Not anger.

Pain.

He wanted to take the sentence back.

He did not.

Because part of him meant it.

Miren looked out over the village.

"Then use it."

Luceran frowned.

"What?"

"Use it. Ask where pipes run. Ask which doors still work. Ask what rooms it can hear. Ask what stones are cracked. Ask what it remembers about the old drains."

She looked at him again.

"But when it tells you what a person deserves, remember that it cannot tell a clerk from a table if he stands still."

Luceran almost smiled.

Almost.

"That is unfair."

"Good."

She stepped away.

He caught her wrist.

She looked down at his hand.

Then up.

He released her.

"Miren."

"I am here."

That was not forgiveness.

It was better.

She left him on the gallery.

---

The mask was waiting in the throne room.

Of course it was.

Luceran entered alone.

The crown remained on the side table.

Nobody had moved it.

He sat on the lowest step instead of the throne.

The mask floated before him.

"Your Majesty."

Luceran looked at it.

"What should I do with Montegrain?"

The mask answered at once.

"Whatever you command."

Luceran stared.

"That is not an answer."

"It is the answer."

"What does he deserve?"

"Your judgment."

"What would you do?"

"I would obey."

Luceran leaned back against the throne step.

For the first time since the mask appeared, he understood why Miren was frightened.

Not because it was powerful.

Because it offered him nothing to push against.

No ambition.

No wounded pride.

No competing family.

No exhausted captain telling him sixth instead of fifth.

No lover saying that a useful answer was still dangerous.

No physician refusing the question.

No steward refusing the number.

No friend believing he knew Luceran better than Luceran knew himself.

Nothing.

Perfect service.

Luceran looked at the pale silver face.

"You opened the gates."

"Yes."

"You found the water."

"Yes."

"You counted wounded."

"Yes."

"You were wrong."

"Yes."

No defensiveness.

No embarrassment.

No fear.

Luceran rubbed both hands over his face.

"What do you want?"

"To serve."

"Why?"

"I am Edselsto."

"That is not a reason."

"It is sufficient."

Luceran looked toward the crown.

Then toward the door through which Miren had left.

Then back to the mask.

"No," he said.

The mask waited.

Luceran stood.

"Tomorrow we write rules."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

He hated how good that sounded.

Outside, the castle remained full of people who would tell him no.

For the first time in his life, Luceran understood that this might be a form of wealth.
