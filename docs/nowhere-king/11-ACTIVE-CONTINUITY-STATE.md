**Role:** Single authoritative current temporal continuity ledger.

## Current state

Season One is complete and Edselsto is back under Luceran's living government. Season Two has moved the castle from public apparition into bounded institutional use, voluntary dead service, restored listening infrastructure, and now material fiscal usefulness without resolving what Edselsto actually is.

Episodes 1–4 remain as previously established: Edselsto's perception is incomplete and conduit-dependent; the court's rules constrain humans rather than the castle; Iven Marrick voluntarily remains as a finite dead keeper under living authority; restored silver ways are physically verified, visibly marked, selectively isolated, and dependent on living workers, suppliers and custodians.

In Episode 5, *The Forgotten Treasury*, Edselsto identifies a chamber beneath the east foundation sealed for 183 years and remembers silver there. It cannot open the masonry. Vess, Pell Falbrecht and living workers physically expose the former doorway while Rhyse, Ysabet, Damaris and guards establish custody and inventory procedure.

The chamber is the old Eastern Household Reserve and contains thirty-one chests plus shelves of mixed assets. The contents are not a single Crown treasure: verified royal plate and mint bars coexist with temple deposits, merchant sureties, compensation rolls, road obligations, foreign or private material, maintenance stock and items whose provenance is damaged or unresolved. Damaris establishes that physical possession beneath Edselsto does not create Crown title. Only the immediately defensible Crown portion may be spent.

That usable portion creates months of fiscal breathing room rather than permanent wealth. Luceran authorizes already-promised battle compensation first, Northbridge timber, the Red Ford survey, cistern and roof repairs, guard obligations, and a second bounded phase of conduit restoration. Damaris permits six additional marks of restoration silver after physical survey and ordinary procurement rather than the twelve Luceran wants.

Hesta Vale produces a family receipt matching an unpaid eastern-road compensation roll. Rather than demand ancient coin plus impossible interest, she negotiates four seasons of toll-free Crown-track access for her family's quarry hauling, subject to military closure. The old debt therefore becomes a living infrastructure bargain and precedent for other claimants.

Junior archive copyist Senn Daro attempts to sell a preliminary treasury inventory to a Cendreval retainer for two crowns to fund his sister's apprenticeship bond. The retainer reports him because House Cendreval has its own institutional interest in discouraging stolen commercial intelligence. Ysabet removes Senn from sealed material for one year, docks one week's wages, and transfers him to public copy work; his sister's apprenticeship is explicitly not punished for his misconduct. The incident establishes that supernatural discovery immediately creates ordinary corruption pressure.

Luceran and Miren remain intimate and mutually corrective. Miren emphasizes that Edselsto found a room; living institutions converted the discovery into lawful money. Luceran nevertheless asks whether Edselsto remembers other rooms and orders Vess shown the next threshold under existing survey, custody and provenance rules. Miren identifies the deeper danger: safeguards make repeated supernatural use governable enough to become ordinary.

In Episode 7, *The Knife That Missed*, a hired laborer recorded as Marek Venn penetrates the lower archive approach during a royal inspection. The breach is materially ordinary rather than magical: active works, a service recess not treated as an access route, visible listening-zone markings, incomplete worker familiarity, and a momentary watch distraction let him cross a cordon that living guards had otherwise established.

The attack demonstrates a narrow but consequential capability: Edselsto can provide directional auditory warning inside a connected listening path quickly enough to alter an immediate outcome. It does not establish intent-reading, visual omniscience, predictive threat classification, or perception inside unconnected recesses. The living guard had already challenged Marek and Rhyse was already moving before the castle's warning.

Hesta Vale remembers seeing Marek with clean insteps and folded cloth near an improperly covered lime cart; her observation is useful for route reconstruction but is not upgraded into foreknowledge of the attack.
