# The Drawer Left Closed

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 first-night counting-room companion scene*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL CREATOR-REVIEW CANDIDATE — 18 November 2026.** Not inserted into the finished Episode 04 or the Google Docs Chapter One working draft; creator continuity review is required before any canonical use.

The counting room was dry. At Edselsto, that was enough to make a room valuable.

Its ceiling did not drip. Its old drawers, however, had swollen in their frames. The copying clerk tried the lowest pull and stopped when the wood creaked.

"Leave it," said Orven Dast. "That belongs to the old house. Your chests came on wagons."

The watch officer lifted the lamp. Only a finger of oil remained below the glass.

"How long?"

"Enough to read what is before us," Orven said. "Not enough to hunt for what isn't."

Two guards held their places: one at the counting-room door, the other on the landing above the inner kitchen. The officer had requested another and been told the gate needed him more.

Four archive chests waited on the table. Two bore intact royal seals; one had a broken hinge strapped shut with cord; the fourth wore three travel labels, none agreeing with the others.

"His Majesty ordered an inventory," the senior clerk said. "If we work through the night—"

"We won't," said Ilwen, the copying clerk. Ink stained her thumb. "You cannot inventory a drawer by copying its label."

"Labels are a beginning."

"Then call them labels."

Outside, a cartwheel struck stone. The landing guard glanced toward the noise but stayed where he stood.

The officer set the lamp between the clerks. "Two clerks and countersigned inventories before a chest is opened. What can you attest before this burns out?"

Ilwen wrote a heading: *Seals and cases seen upon the table.* She read each mark aloud while the senior clerk checked it. They recorded two unbroken seals, the cord on the damaged chest, and the conflicting labels on the fourth. Neither guessed at a page inside.

"We could untie that one," the senior clerk offered.

"With what light to count its contents?" the officer asked.

Orven tapped the old lower drawer. "And those are not yours to force merely because you've found the room."

Ilwen wrote one more line: *Other drawers present; contents unexamined.*

The senior clerk frowned. "It makes us look slow."

"It makes us able to swear to what we've seen."

They read the tally back, then both signed. The officer countersigned only the seals and bindings he had personally inspected. The door guard watched the room closed and its key turned; the landing guard remained at his station.

Orven took back the key. The officer carried the remaining oil to the landing, where a lamp would be needed when the watch changed.

The drawers remained shut. In the morning anyone asking why would find an answer in ink, rather than a lie made tidy by exhaustion.
