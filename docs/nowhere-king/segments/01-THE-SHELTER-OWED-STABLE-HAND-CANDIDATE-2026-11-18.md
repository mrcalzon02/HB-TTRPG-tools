# The Shelter Owed

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 first-night stable-yard companion scene*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL REVIEW CANDIDATE — 18 November 2026.** Not part of the finished Episode 04 or native Google Docs Chapter One; creator approval and continuity reconciliation required before insertion.

The Bellisar groom had spent an hour arguing that a royal horse could not stand in mud.

The village stable-hand agreed. It did not make another stall appear.

She led him around the lower stable, past runoff from the broken upper roof. One bay was dry beneath sound timbers. The rest were occupied by pack animals or harness.

"One," she said.

"I have two remounts shivering through their rugs."

"Then choose the colder."

"They're worth more than your house."

"Then why leave them in rain to tell me?"

He went to the picket line. The nearer mare stood with her head low, water dripping from her mane. He felt her shoulder tremble.

"She goes inside."

"Clear the broken harness first. And those wet rugs come off."

He hauled tackle from the bay while she rubbed the mare's neck with coarse cloth. They took turns: too much horse, too little dry cloth.

When he reached for bedding in the corner, she stopped him.

"Damp against the wall."

"Where's the dry straw?"

"In our shed below. Three bundles for my mother's goats."

"Bring all three."

"No."

He straightened. "I serve House Bellisar."

"I serve the goats. They must sleep tomorrow."

For the first time, he seemed to hear the answer.

"One bundle, then."

"At my price, paid now. I cannot take a lord's promise to the miller."

She named it. He counted the money from his own purse while she held the lantern. She fetched one tight-bound bundle under her cloak. It crackled as they opened it.

The mare stepped into the cleared bay, nosed the straw, and stood quietly. The groom rubbed her legs again before looking toward the second remount.

"She stays outside?"

"In the lee of this wall, not beneath the bad roof. Dry her and change cloth when it soaks through."

Together they shifted the line. He surrendered his spare saddlecloth without argument.

A guard on watch witnessed the bundle delivered and the price paid. On a torn packing slip the groom wrote his name, the coin spent, and that the straw was his purchase for the mare, not Crown stores. He kept the note to show his master.

"Will he repay you?" she asked.

"I shall ask."

"Good. Don't tell him my goats have agreed to feed the king's horses."

He nearly smiled.

Below, village lamps went out one by one. The second remount still stood in rain, but out of the wind. The groom stayed to tend her. The stable-hand took the last dry cloth home, where her own animals waited for morning.
