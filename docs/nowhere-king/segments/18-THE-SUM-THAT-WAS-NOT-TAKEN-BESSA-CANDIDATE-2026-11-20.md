# The Sum That Was Not Taken

*The Nowhere King — optional Season Three, Episode 18 east-gate scene-study.*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL — creator-review candidate — 20 November 2026.** Not an additional episode, not inserted into S3E18, and not approved for publication or canonical continuity.

The page arrived at the east gate without a purse or a warrant. He carried a narrow scrap, folded twice and softened at the corners by anxious hands.

“Someone at the castle says the gate has brought in six sacks,” he told Bessa. “They asked whether that agrees with today's reckoning.”

Bessa set her pen beside the open book. “Six sacks of what?”

“Grain, I suppose.”

“Then the castle may have grain. I have a toll book.”

The page looked at the arch, where a cartman was arguing with his mule. “I was told to bring back an answer before supper.”

“Who told you?”

“A clerk who heard the number from another clerk. He said the king had asked.”

“Did he tell you what the king asked?”

The boy shook his head. His ears reddened. He had clearly hoped to return with a number rather than a complication.

Bessa drew the original toward her, taking care not to catch the page's sleeve beneath its boards. The marks for the morning's traffic were written beside the charges and exemptions they belonged to. The signed extract had already gone uphill by its own runner. The original had not moved.

The house-paid Jorvan observer was still at the side bench, checking a copy of his witnessing notes. “If six sacks were sold,” he offered, “there might be a sum to enter.”

“If,” Bessa said. “And sold by whom? At what price? Under whose receipt? You witnessed a count of tolls. You did not see a grain sale.”

He lowered his hand from the margin. “I did not.”

The page examined the scrap again. It held no signature, no day, no named witness. Only the words *six sacks, east gate?* in a hurried hand.

“Could you put your name under it?” he asked. “Then they would know I came.”

“I'll tell them you came,” Bessa said. “I won't sign a thing I cannot attest.”

She turned a clean scrap over and dictated slowly enough for him to follow: *A report was brought to the east gate concerning six sacks. Its source and purpose have not been established. The current toll figures stand in Bessa's witnessed original and the extract already sent.*

The observer watched the page copy it. He did not add his witnessing mark to an exchange he had not himself witnessed from its beginning.

“Will they be angry?” the boy asked.

“Perhaps. A clerk hates being sent back for the question.”

“And a gatekeeper?”

Bessa closed the book before the next cart could splash dust across its wet ink. “A gatekeeper hates a wrong answer that looks like a right one.”

The page carried the two scraps separately. He had an answer now, though not the number he had been sent to fetch.
