# The Two Pails

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 first-night water-carriers and kitchen companion scene*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL CREATOR-REVIEW CANDIDATE — 19 November 2026.** Not inserted into the finished Episode 04, the native Chapter One Google Doc, or the published story bible. Creator approval and continuity reconciliation required before any canonical use.

The first two pails came into the inner kitchen with rain shining on their rims.

Hedd, who carried water for three households in the village, set his down carefully. His daughter Lysa put hers beside it and eased the wooden handle from fingers that would not straighten.

The kitchen lad reached for both.

“Not yet,” Lysa said. “Where will you put them?”

“Into that great pot.”

“Which pot?”

He pointed to the broth on the hearth.

The cook, sleeves blackened with soot, turned. “One pail for supper. One for washing. Then another six, if the king expects clean bowls.”

Hedd looked through the open door at the slippery stair. “We have brought two. Six more means three journeys apiece if we carry only what we can lift safely. There is scarcely light for one.”

“The main well is working.”

“Aye. The well is not the thing climbing these steps.”

The cook bit back an answer. Beyond her, someone was trying to draw smoke through the damaged second kitchen hood. No fire could be lit beneath it yet. On her own hearth a kettle was already boiling for the physicians.

“We cannot serve food in filthy bowls,” she said.

“Nor wash them in water that has run off that yard,” Lysa said. “Orven warned the carters away from the old trough. Nobody has looked beneath those stones.”

She was not claiming the well was fouled. Its bucket had risen clear; the trouble lay between well and table, where mud and old drains could turn a careless shortcut into another ruined room.

Mara Kest came in carrying a stack of dry cloths. The cook asked her to order more water.

“Order it from whom?” Mara asked.

Hedd held out his hands. Red grooves marked both palms. “My household has two covered pails set aside for tonight. Those are my wife's, for the children. I will not bring them here.”

Mara looked at the cook. “What can you actually use before the meal?”

“One pail for broth. Some of the second for the physicians. I need the rest for hands and utensils, once it has boiled.”

“And the floor?”

“Can wait.”

They agreed that much. The lad brought two clean vessels. Lysa watched her pails emptied into them, one at a time, and saw which was set to boil. Mara cut two marks on a wooden tally; Hedd held the matching piece while the cook witnessed the receipt. It recorded two pails delivered, not six owed, and no promise that the next drawing would be safe.

“Wages?” Hedd asked.

“Not fixed,” Mara said. “I will not put a sum in your mouth.”

“Then we have helped tonight,” Lysa said, “not hired ourselves forever.”

She picked up the empty pails. Her father took the heavier one, though it weighed almost nothing now. Outside, the path still shone with water. At the hearth the cook moved the wash basin aside until the kettle could be spared.
