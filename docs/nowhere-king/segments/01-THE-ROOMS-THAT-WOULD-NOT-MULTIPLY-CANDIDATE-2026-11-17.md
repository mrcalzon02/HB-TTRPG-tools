# The Rooms That Would Not Multiply
*The Nowhere King — optional Chapter One / first-morning gatehouse companion scene*
*UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL REVIEW CANDIDATE — 17 November 2026. Do not insert into any completed episode, native Google Doc, or public canon without creator approval.*

The first petitioner came to the gatehouse carrying a chamber key with a blue ribbon knotted through its bow.

“That color marks my office,” said the chamberlain.

Mara Kest set the key beside two others. “It marks someone who found ribbon before breakfast.”

Luceran entered to find three claims upon the same two chambers. The physicians had been given them beside the great hall on the first night. They were dry, close to boiled water, and therefore suddenly desirable to officials who had spent the night complaining about their own quarters.

“One chamber would suffice,” said the chamberlain.

“For whom?” asked the royal physician. “The man with a fever, the man who cannot stand, or the person trying to keep them apart?”

Mara had not slept. Her chalk marks still showed on the sleeves of her dress.

“Seventeen dry rooms,” she said, indicating Orven's ledger. “All assigned. Nine more might be made habitable with fires, if we find fuel and hands to tend them. Those four against the outer wall are counted, not safe. No one is gaining a room by demanding one.”

Orven Dast placed a splinter of damp timber on the table.

“West stair,” he said.

The king picked it up. The underside was soft enough to yield beneath his thumb.

“The roof?”

“Over the upper landing. One hard gust and it may bring slate down the stairs. We can keep people out today. We cannot mend it today while the boards are wet.”

The chamberlain opened his mouth. Luceran raised a hand.

“How many men to close the passage?”

“Two for the barriers,” Orven said. “A guard at each end until the ropes hold and the doors are marked. Olan will need to inspect from sound footing before anyone climbs.”

“And blankets?”

Mara glanced at the waiting officials. “Already spread in the great hall. We can move them for your guests, Majesty, but then the porters sleep cold.”

Luceran took the blue-ribboned key and returned it to Mara.

“The physicians keep both chambers. No officer moves a patient to improve his own address. The west stair closes at both ends; post the warning where a tired porter can read it. Pay the village men their agreed wage for the barriers. Have Olan examine the roof when he judges the footing safe, not when I grow impatient.”

“And the visiting lord?” the chamberlain asked.

“His riders have not arrived. His dignity may wait on the road.”

Mara wrote the orders in chalk before anyone could reinterpret them. The physician took his key. Orven took back the rotten wood.

By breakfast, Luceran had made the castle smaller. At least, for one morning, he knew exactly which parts were safe to use.
