# The Charcoal Tally

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 first-dawn charcoal-supplies companion scene*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL CREATOR-REVIEW CANDIDATE — 19 November 2026.** Not inserted into the finished Episode 04, native Google Docs, public series bible, or current Season Three continuity. Requires creator approval and continuity reconciliation before any canonical adoption.

The first light above the black pines found two charcoal burners arguing with a castle servant beside a mule that had stopped pretending to be patient.

Nerin lifted a sack from the pannier and set it beneath the gatehouse overhang. His sister Dessa kept a hand over its mouth.

“Five,” said Sella, counting the sacks. She had been sent down from the inner kitchen with a scrap of writing folded inside her sleeve. “Five for twelve pennies.”

“Five bags,” Dessa said. “Not five full sacks.”

Sella opened the paper. A clerk's cramped figures were beginning to blur in the mist. The advance rider had written FIVE SACKS, TWELVE PENCE. The burners had heard three pennies for each properly filled sack, with short sacks measured separately. Neither side had heard the other promise.

Nerin untied the nearest mouth. The charcoal pieces lay just below the cord, clean enough to leave a black print on Sella's thumb. He opened the next: half that depth, the remainder padded with a rolled piece of cloth so the brittle pieces would not break against the mule.

“Two like that,” Dessa said. “Three full. Count what you can see.”

Up the slope, smoke trailed from the one kitchen whose hearth could safely be used. Its cook wanted embers that would last until the breakfast pot was hot. Across the yard, men at the workshops had asked for fuel for a little iron brazier: a seized fastening would not yield merely because a king had arrived.

Sella had been told to bring back the fuel, not adjudicate a bargain.

“Take the kitchen's two and the workshop's one,” Nerin offered. “Let the others stand here until your mistress comes. But write down whose they are.”

“If the cook burns two, they cannot be handed back,” Sella said.

“Then write that too.”

A boy from the kitchen appeared, shivering, with a carrying pole. Sella stopped him before he reached for a sack. She asked Dessa to watch while she scratched the actual count on the reverse of the rider's paper: three full sacks offered for immediate use, two short sacks held with their owners. Beside it she copied the rider's twelve pennies and Dessa's three-penny full-sack price without crossing either out.

“That's not a settled price,” Dessa said.

“No,” said Sella. “And I haven't paid it.”

Nerin carried two full sacks uphill with the boy. The third waited where a workshop hand could collect it, under Sella's eye. Dessa stayed with the mule and the two short sacks, and Sella stayed beside her until another servant came to witness the writing.

The kitchen chimney breathed again above them. The workshop's brazier was still cold. On the paper, the two prices remained different, however carefully Sella formed the figures.
