# The Weight of Rain

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 / first-week kitchen-supply companion scene*
*UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL REVIEW CANDIDATE — 17 November 2026. Not part of finished Episode 04 or the Google Docs Chapter One draft. Creator review required before any canonical insertion.*

Meret had brought flour uphill in weather that was determined to turn every sack into paste. By the time she reached the cart turn, rain had worked beneath her collar and the borrowed handbarrow had begun to complain at every stone.

The castle's kitchen steward met her beside the gate with an empty tally board.

"Three sacks each morning," he said. "That is what the kitchens will need."

"Three is a wish," Meret said. "I've brought two. Ask me about the third when the miller knows whether his wheel will turn tomorrow."

He looked past her toward the bridge. A cart stood sideways at the narrow approach while soldiers argued over whose wheels should pass first.

"Bring the barrow across."

"The barrow won't make the corner with its load. Your carts proved that yesterday." She set down the handles. "I'll bring each sack by hand if there are two more backs to carry them, a board to keep them from the mud, and someone who says what those backs will be paid before they lift."

"The king has men."

"Then the king can tell them their wage. I won't promise it for him."

The steward considered this, then sent a boy for Mara Kest. Meret waited under the dripping arch instead of volunteering her shoulders. Eventually two porters came with a plank and a length of sailcloth. Mara named the task, agreed the porters' wage with them, and had the amount entered on a separate household slate. No one called it payment for flour.

The first sack crossed on the plank between the men. At the kitchen door Meret broke the string herself. The steward pinched flour from the middle, smelled it, and rubbed it between finger and thumb. Dry. He placed the sack on the hanging balance. Meret watched the weights settle and corrected him when he read the swing before it stopped.

The second sack was heavier on the scale, but water had crept through one corner. He reached for the chalk.

"Do not write both," she said.

"I cannot feed them a tally."

"And I cannot take home a receipt that says you took flour you may yet refuse."

He wrote one sack received, its weighed amount, and one sack held outside the stores for inspection. Meret watched him make two matching cuts in a small wooden tally, one half for the kitchen and one for her. Beside them he wrote the same marks on paper, then signed only for what had passed his hands.

"That isn't a promise of payment," he warned.

"I know the difference," Meret said. "I also know what happens when there's no proof anything arrived."

She tied her half under her coat. Tomorrow depended on the mill, the rain, the crossing, and whether this household remembered its own words. She had not sold the castle a week. She had delivered one dry sack, and made sure someone could say so.
