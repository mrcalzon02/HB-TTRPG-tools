# The Third Hour

*The Nowhere King — optional Season One, Episode 04 first-night guard-relief companion scene*  
**UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL CREATOR-REVIEW CANDIDATE — 19 November 2026.** Not inserted into the finished Episode 04, native Google Docs, or public story canon. Requires creator approval and continuity reconciliation.

Edrik knew he had missed the end of dinner when his spoon struck the empty bowl and he reached for his sword.

Across the trestle, Rhyse looked at the hand on the hilt.

“You are going to sleep.”

“I can finish the watch.”

“You just challenged a spoon.”

Edrik let go. The guard beside him pretended to be interested in the heel of his bread.

Outside the great hall, men were carrying blankets past a cracked basin. Somewhere above, a carpenter was still hammering. The noise made the castle feel less deserted than it looked, though every open door showed another stretch of unlit stone.

“Three hours,” Rhyse said.

“His Majesty is walking the corridors.”

“With Bram. And a file of men who have not been awake since the capital.”

“I was there when he left the palace without warning.”

“So was Bram. You cannot both remain awake forever because nine other men once failed to look up.”

Edrik had no answer to that. He remembered the confusion outside Moncler's works, the strange privilege of being one of the guards who happened to be present when the king decided to do something impossible to schedule. He had mistaken the accident for a claim upon all the hours that followed.

Rhyse led him to a chamber off the occupied hall. Four bedrolls lay on boards raised above the floor. A lantern burned outside, where someone still needed to find the steps. Inside, the dark smelled of wet wool.

“Which one?”

“The one that isn't occupied.”

Edrik chose the nearest. He began to unbuckle his sword.

“Keep it where you can reach it,” Rhyse said, “not where you'll roll onto it.”

The instruction was so ordinary that Edrik felt foolish for wanting something grander.

He pulled off his boots. One sock was soaked through at the heel. He folded his cloak beneath it so the damp would not spread into the blanket, then lay down without finding a comfortable place for his shoulders.

Rhyse remained at the door.

“Sir?”

“What?”

“If he asks for me?”

“Someone will tell him where you are.”

“And if he needs me?”

“Then I shall wake you. That is why we know where you've gone.”

The captain took the lantern farther down the passage. Edrik watched the diminishing light until it became a yellow edge beneath the door.

In the capital he had slept behind a barracks wall while polished Companions stood outside a king's chambers. Their helmets had looked magnificent. He had once envied how near they stood to power.

Now Bram was in a draughty corridor doing the less magnificent thing: listening for footsteps. Edrik could picture him without fear that the picture was wrong.

The next hammer blow came late enough that Edrik waited for another. No other followed.

He stopped trying to count the people between himself and Luceran. It was a count Rhyse had made while Edrik was still pretending that tiredness could be overcome by stubbornness.

At last he slept.
