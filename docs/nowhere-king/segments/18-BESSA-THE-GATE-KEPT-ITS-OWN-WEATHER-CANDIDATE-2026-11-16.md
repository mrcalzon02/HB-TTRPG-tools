# The Gate Kept Its Own Weather
*The Nowhere King — optional Season Three, Episode 18 side-scene*
*UNPUBLISHED / NONCANONICAL. Separate review candidate; not incorporated into “Default Intelligence.” Author and continuity approval required.*

Rain came sideways beneath the east arch. Bessa put a flat stone on the open gate book and asked the cartman to repeat where his load had come from.

He answered impatiently. The mare behind him had begun to worry at her traces, and a queue was forming. Bessa entered what he could attest, checked the fee against the road privilege written beside his name, and waved him through. His wet boots had left a mark on the ledge. That was all the mark proved.

The Jorvan observer bent over her shoulder. “Could your clerk make a cleaner copy for the castle? This one will be difficult to read.”

“He is making the copy,” Bessa said. “You may watch him. Hold that corner if you wish.”

He held it. A gust nearly took the paper from both of them.

Bessa checked the day's entries against the other gate keeper's duplicate. The ordinary gate charges stayed above the line; only what remained after them belonged in the pledged-net reckoning. The observer followed every figure but did not touch the toll box. When the signed extract was ready, he placed his witnessing mark beside the count, where it belonged.

The runner tucked it under his cloak and set off uphill. Bessa returned the original book to its chest. She could have kept the queue waiting until the rain eased, but the carts would still be wet tomorrow, and the men who drove them would still need to earn their hire.

Late in the day the runner came back for a dry wrapping for another packet. He mentioned, with the pleased astonishment of someone repeating a court marvel, that the king had asked the castle about the tolls.

“And did the castle count my gate?” Bessa asked.

“It remembered six sacks of grain.”

She stared at him. Then she laughed once, not unkindly.

“The grain may be exactly where they say it is. Did anyone ask whether it came through my arch?”

“The lady Ysabet said it was the wrong account.”

“Good. Take that answer uphill too, should somebody forget.”

The observer had heard enough to approach again. “Would you put the castle's answer in today's reckoning, so the two accounts may be compared?”

“No. Grain is not a toll receipt.” Bessa opened a fresh leaf in the ordinary inquiry book and entered the runner's report as **reported speech**, naming who had brought it. She did not change a single figure in the gate original.

The observer watched her close the book. Beyond him the last cart creaked under the arch, close enough for Bessa to hear its wheels and far enough from the castle that no stone voice could tell her who had paid.

She went out to see for herself.
