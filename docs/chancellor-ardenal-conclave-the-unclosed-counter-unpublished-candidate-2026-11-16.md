At the receiving office's outer counter, the duty clerk had kept the counterfoil of the old pouch token beneath a square of glass. It proved the courier had surrendered a sealed pouch there. She could not say what the pouch contained; the clerk who counted inside was assigned another seal, another door, and another set of records.

A new messenger brought a neutral packet from the tribunal. The wrapping held copies of the outgoing inquiry and the disbursing officer's return, each identified separately. The return said that the prescribed hour had passed without an authenticated acknowledgment from the receiving vault. It did not say the pouch had never passed inward.

“Received?” the messenger asked, offering the usual book. Its single box was marked COMPLETE.

“At this counter, yes,” she said. “Complete, no.”

He looked over her shoulder toward the inner hatch. No voice came through it; a sealed door was not itself testimony. He had instructions to bring back proof. She understood the temptation to give him a stamp that looked like proof.

Instead she opened a spare sheet and wrote three headings: *submitted to outer counter*, *accepted by receiving authority*, *delivered to intended recipient*. Under the first she copied the hour, the packet's identifying seal, and her own witness mark. She left the other two blank. The inward transfer slip for the packet had not been returned to her station. She could not testify that it never would be; she could testify that she did not possess it.

“They will ask why you've left it unfinished,” the messenger said.

“Because I have.”

He pointed at the old pouch token in the margin as grounds for filling the second column. She shook her head. “That token belongs to a different article and an earlier hour. It records a pouch reaching this counter. It neither counts the contents nor credits the vault. Your inquiry asks for records that have not been furnished with this return.”

She wrote the missing records below the line: *authenticated vault count; credit reference; available-balance certificate; wage-disbursement acknowledgments*. There was no declaration that these records did not exist, only that none had been supplied in answer to the inquiry. Nor did a messenger's signature prove that any claimant had read the tribunal's correction.

The messenger asked whether the clerk would at least seal his copy. She sealed the narrow witness statement, not the empty boxes. One copy stayed in a tied custody sleeve beside the intake book; the other went back through the counter with the messenger.

At the bottom she wrote: *Awaiting the receiving authority's authenticated reply.*

She put down the pen before the phrase could become *refused*.
