# The Unrelieved Post

The western passage smelled of cooling stone and the sharp oil from a lantern wick. The sentry on the residence side of the arch had been told that relief would come when the stair lamp turned low. It had turned low once, been trimmed, and was turning low again.

On the household duty roll, beside her post, someone had written *replacement assigned*. There was a name beneath the line, blurred where a sleeve had brushed fresh ink. No arrival time followed it.

The porter with the evening key ring asked whether he might mark the shift complete. He held the book open as though the paper itself could keep the passage.

"Complete for whom?" the sentry asked.

"For the office."

"The office isn't standing here."

He looked toward the inner door. This passage had once been the Chancellor's to command. Now the magistrates kept their own seals, and neither of these two servants had authority to examine the tribunal's papers. Their duty was the doorway, not the evidence.

"Perhaps your replacement was sent to the other stair," the porter said.

"Perhaps. Did you see him?"

"No."

"Then write that."

The porter set the book on a dry window ledge. In one column he copied the intended assignment. In another he entered the time at which he himself had brought the keys. Under *relief arrived*, he left a ruled space. It made the page look broken.

The sentry eased her heel inside a boot gone stiff with standing. She had enough oil left for one careful trim. A fresh vessel might be in stores, but neither of them could claim that one had been issued. She kept the flame low enough to spare the wick and high enough to see any face approaching the arch.

"Will they call this an extra shift?" the porter asked.

"They can call it what they please. I am still here."

He offered to stand nearby while a runner went for the duty officer. She accepted the company but not the fiction that a man holding keys had become an armed relief.

At the bottom of the page he wrote: *Post occupied by original sentry through this hour. Replacement not witnessed. Duty officer requested.* She put her mark beside it only after reading it aloud.

The runner took the message away. The sentry heard his boots fade down the stair and stayed at her place. The absent guard might be late, redirected, ill, or lost. For now he was missing only from this doorway, and that was danger enough to record without turning it into an accusation.
