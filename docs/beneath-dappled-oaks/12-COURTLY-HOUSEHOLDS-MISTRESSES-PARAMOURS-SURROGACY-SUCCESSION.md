# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Courtly Households, Paramours, Mistresses, and Surrogacy

This document is active setting canon.

Long-lived elven society has had centuries to develop customs for relationships that do not fit neatly into one spouse, one household, one set of children, and one uncomplicated line of inheritance.

Marriage remains politically important.

It is not the only adult relationship structure recognized in practice.

Noble and wealthy households commonly navigate:

- acknowledged mistresses;
- acknowledged lovers;
- discreet paramours;
- long-term secondary companions;
- unofficial second households;
- lovers maintained with property or stipends;
- mutually tolerated extramarital relationships;
- secret affairs;
- widowed lovers;
- politically arranged surrogates;
- gestational surrogates;
- lineage-bearing surrogates;
- reproductive donors;
- magically assisted conception arrangements.

The exact legal status varies by region, House charter, temple practice, inheritance contract, and whether the arrangement was acknowledged in advance.

The social question is often not:

**Did someone have another lover?**

It is:

**Who knew, who consented, who was acknowledged, who was promised what, and what happens if a child results?**

---

# I. Mistresses, Paramours, and Acknowledged Companions

The common courtly word **mistress** survives even though the equivalent social role may be occupied by a man or woman.

Other common terms include:

- paramour;
- companion;
- favored companion;
- private companion;
- kept lover;
- second-house companion.

Terminology itself carries status.

A "mistress" may imply a socially recognized relationship.

A "secret lover" implies concealment.

A "companion" may be deliberately ambiguous.

## Acknowledged mistress

An acknowledged mistress is a lover whose existence is known to at least the principal household.

She may have:

- private apartments;
- an allowance;
- servants;
- jewelry;
- property;
- travel privileges;
- invitations to selected events;
- access to the noble's household.

Her position is not equal to that of a spouse unless a specific House custom says otherwise.

Acknowledgment reduces uncertainty.

It does not eliminate jealousy.

## Discreet paramour

A discreet paramour is known to a smaller circle.

The spouse may know.

Senior servants may know.

Court society may strongly suspect.

No formal acknowledgment is made.

This gives everyone room to preserve appearances.

## Secret affair

A secret affair becomes dangerous because nobody has agreed to the terms.

Possible consequences include:

- blackmail;
- inheritance disputes;
- diplomatic insult;
- household fracture;
- compromised intelligence;
- manipulated correspondence;
- duels;
- family retaliation.

The scandal often lies more in deception than in desire itself.

---

# II. The Secondary Household

Some nobles maintain a secondary residence for a long-term lover.

This can range from:

- a small townhouse;
- a country cottage;
- apartments near court;
- a wing of an estate;
- a separate manor.

The arrangement may be financially secure and publicly understood.

A companion in such a household may become politically important because they control:

- access;
- private correspondence;
- social invitations;
- informal conversation;
- the noble's unguarded moods.

A mistress with no official title may know more than three ministers.

---

# III. The Spouse and the Mistress

Relationships between spouse and mistress vary enormously.

They may be:

- hostile;
- indifferent;
- formally courteous;
- privately friendly;
- mutually strategic;
- genuinely affectionate;
- part of a consensual arrangement.

A spouse may tolerate a mistress because:

- the marriage is political;
- the lover is discreet;
- no inheritance threat exists;
- the arrangement is reciprocal;
- the lover provides companionship the spouse does not wish to provide;
- ending the relationship would create more political trouble than allowing it.

A spouse may oppose the mistress because:

- affection has become politically destabilizing;
- money is being diverted;
- secrets are leaking;
- the lover influences appointments;
- a pregnancy threatens succession;
- public humiliation has occurred.

The existence of a mistress is therefore not automatically the scandal.

The **terms** are the scandal.

---

# IV. Children of Mistresses and Paramours

A child born outside the principal marriage may be:

- acknowledged;
- legitimized;
- supported but excluded from succession;
- adopted into a cadet branch;
- given property;
- granted a military or religious career;
- denied;
- hidden.

Different Houses have different rules.

## Acknowledged natural child

An acknowledged child may receive:

- a surname;
- education;
- stipend;
- guardianship;
- marriage prospects;
- inheritance outside the principal line.

Some Houses treat such children warmly.

Others treat them as political hazards.

## Legitimization

A child may sometimes be legitimized by:

- House council;
- Crown recognition;
- marriage after birth;
- succession compact;
- special charter.

Legitimization can trigger furious opposition from existing heirs.

## Cadet placement

A common compromise is to establish the child within a cadet branch.

This provides status without immediately displacing the main line.

---

# V. Surrogacy

Surrogacy is a known and socially developed practice.

It is especially important in a society where:

- marriages may be politically necessary;
- fertility can last for centuries;
- magical or physical infertility may affect one partner;
- same-sex couples may wish to establish heirs;
- House succession may require recognized descent.

Surrogacy can be:

- private;
- temple-supervised;
- physician-supervised;
- House-arranged;
- contractually political.

## Gestational surrogate

A gestational surrogate carries a child who is legally intended for another household.

The surrogate may or may not contribute lineage depending on the method used.

## Lineage-bearing surrogate

In some arrangements, the surrogate contributes biological lineage.

This creates more complicated inheritance law.

The House must decide whether the surrogate's ancestry is:

- politically useful;
- politically dangerous;
- legally relevant;
- intentionally concealed.

## Magical assisted conception

Some magical methods can assist conception or gestation without changing legal parentage by themselves.

A spell does not settle inheritance.

Contracts and House law do.

---

# VI. Surrogacy Contracts

Serious Houses do not rely on verbal promises.

A proper surrogacy agreement may establish:

- intended parents;
- surrogate;
- medical authority;
- magical healer access;
- residence;
- expenses;
- compensation;
- confidentiality;
- recognition of the child;
- inheritance status;
- post-birth contact;
- guardianship if an intended parent dies;
- what happens if multiple children are born.

Poorly written contracts create generations of litigation.

Good contracts merely create fewer generations of litigation.

---

# VII. The Surrogate's Political Position

A surrogate can become politically important even if she was expected to disappear quietly after birth.

She may possess:

- intimate knowledge of the household;
- leverage over parentage;
- emotional attachment to the child;
- family members seeking advantage;
- patrons who financed the arrangement.

A House may therefore:

- honor the surrogate publicly;
- pension her;
- marry her well;
- place her in a temple or estate office;
- keep her near the child;
- deliberately send her far away.

Every option creates consequences.

---

# VIII. Courtly Surrogacy Intrigue

Surrogacy creates its own intrigue.

Possible disputes include:

- whether the child is legally main-line;
- whether the surrogate was promised more than the House admits;
- whether lineage records were altered;
- whether a rival paid the surrogate;
- whether the intended parents disagree about recognition;
- whether an elder tries to substitute one child for another;
- whether magical proof of parentage can be trusted;
- whether a Winter Blossom surrogate pregnancy alters succession unexpectedly.

A surrogate can become one of the most politically dangerous people in the household without holding any official office.

---

# IX. Same-Sex Dynastic Households

Same-sex adult relationships are socially accepted.

Dynastic inheritance is handled through:

- surrogacy;
- donor agreements;
- adoption;
- designated relatives;
- cadet-line heirs;
- existing children;
- succession contracts.

The political issue is not that the couple is same-sex.

The political issue is:

**Whose lineage is recognized, and who inherits?**

This can be negotiated years in advance.

It can still go wrong.

---

# X. Mistresses and Surrogates in House Strategy

A House may use relationships strategically.

It may encourage:

- a discreet mistress who stabilizes an heir;
- a politically useful paramour;
- a surrogate from an allied bloodline;
- a cadet cousin as donor;
- a companion whose family opens trade access.

This is dangerous.

A relationship encouraged for utility can become genuine.

A relationship intended to remain private can become public.

A surrogate selected for lineage may become emotionally central to the family.

Human feelings remain inconveniently resistant to paperwork.

---

# XI. Gifts and Maintenance

Courtly relationships generate economic expectations.

A mistress may receive:

- jewelry;
- clothing;
- apartments;
- income;
- servants;
- travel;
- education;
- business investment.

A surrogate may receive:

- medical care;
- household protection;
- money;
- land;
- pension;
- dowry-equivalent settlement;
- long-term support.

Failure to provide what was promised can become a major scandal.

---

# XII. Household Servants Know

Servants frequently understand the true relationship structure before outsiders do.

They notice:

- who receives breakfast;
- which carriage leaves at night;
- whose clothes are in which wardrobe;
- who has keys;
- who is allowed into private rooms;
- which doctor visits;
- which letters are burned.

This makes senior servants important witnesses and intelligence sources.

It also means wise nobles treat household staff well.

---

# XIII. Romance, Jealousy, and Generational Memory

Long life intensifies these arrangements.

A mistress can remain in someone's life for eighty years.

A former surrogate may still be alive when the child becomes a grandparent.

A spouse may outlive the lover they once hated and later become protector of that lover's children.

A secret from a hundred years ago can still have living witnesses.

This is normal in an elven society.

---

# XIV. Seasonal Children and Secondary Relationships

Seasonal-child categories apply regardless of whether the child was born within a principal marriage.

A mistress's child can be:

- Spring;
- Summer;
- Fall;
- Winter.

So can a surrogate-born child.

The political meaning depends on House interpretation.

A Spring child of a mistress may initially be dismissed because centuries of future heirs seem possible.

A late Fall or Winter child from the same relationship may suddenly matter enormously if the main line has failed.

This creates reversals in status across decades.

---

# XV. Scandal Scale

Court society distinguishes levels of scandal.

Low scandal:
- discreet acknowledged companion;
- consensual secondary relationship;
- properly contracted surrogate.

Moderate scandal:
- hidden financial support;
- unacknowledged child;
- secret pregnancy;
- disputed contract.

High scandal:
- falsified parentage;
- coerced surrogate;
- forged inheritance record;
- secret child placed into succession;
- mistress manipulating state decisions;
- affair involving betrayal of a political compact.

The details matter more than the label.

---

# XVI. Story-Runner Rules

When a mistress, paramour, companion, or surrogate appears, determine:

1. Is the relationship acknowledged?
2. Does the spouse know?
3. Is the arrangement consensual?
4. Is there a contract?
5. Is there financial support?
6. Are children possible or already present?
7. What is their legal status?
8. Who benefits politically?
9. Who feels threatened?
10. Which servants know?
11. Which House rules apply?
12. What happens if the relationship becomes public?

Do not treat every mistress as a villain.

Do not treat every spouse as humiliated.

Do not treat every surrogate as passive.

Do not assume every child outside the primary marriage is illegitimate or irrelevant.

Do not assume every relationship structure is emotionally simple.

---

# XVII. Canon Anchors

Established enduring principles:

- Mistresses, paramours, secondary companions, and surrogate arrangements are common enough to have developed courtly norms.
- The main political questions are acknowledgment, consent, money, secrecy, parentage, and succession.
- Same-sex dynastic households commonly use surrogacy, donor arrangements, adoption, cadet heirs, or succession contracts.
- Surrogates may become politically important independent actors.
- Children from secondary relationships may be acknowledged, legitimized, supported, hidden, or placed in cadet branches.
- Long elven lifespans allow relationship consequences and parentage disputes to persist for centuries.
