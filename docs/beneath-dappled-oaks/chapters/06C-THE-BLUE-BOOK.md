# Chapter Six C — The Blue Book

The blue book arrived before Examiner Serrat did.

It was not blue anymore.

Eighteen years of dust, handling and the slow indignities of archival storage had faded the cloth cover to something between storm-grey and old lavender. One corner had been repaired with darker linen. A brass label frame held a handwritten card:

**ANNEX C — INTAKE EXCEPTIONS — DISSOLUTION TRANSFERS**

Ilyan stood on the other side of the inspection table and tried not to resent an object for looking ordinary.

Thessa noticed.

"You expected lightning?"

"No."

"A skull worked into the clasp?"

"No."

"Blood?"

"Thessa."

She folded her hands behind her back, entirely unrepentant.

Judge Vaun was speaking with the Hall registrar at the door. Examiner Sael had already checked the order twice, the register seal once, and Ilyan's patience more often than either of them strictly required.

Rain silvered the high windows. Bloomtide had spent three days threatening warmth and then changing its mind.

Sael put a narrow paper marker beside the book.

"Entry seventeen-four only."

Ilyan nodded.

"I heard you the first time."

"Then today is full of pleasant surprises."

Thessa looked away very quickly.

Ilyan gave Sael the expression he deserved.

Sael opened the register.

The entries were short.

Broken wax. Wrong crate number. A property bundle delivered to Annex B and walked back across the courtyard under two signatures. A locked box whose key had arrived in another cart. Somebody had packed a clerk's lunch tin inside a records crate and, judging by the annotation, had never recovered it.

Then entry 17-4.

Ilyan stopped breathing for a moment.

Not because the entry was dramatic.

Because it was specific.

**Crate 17 — comparative property intake. Weight excess on porter verification. One paper packet found between property bundles 17-11 and 17-12. Packet exterior: Credential Review, defect-inheritance comparisons, case CR-88. Three sheets under old review cord. Removed from property intake by Receiving Officer A. Saren. Re-receipted to Protected Review Continuation 16-2 pending successor disposition. Receiving clerk: N. Elsen.**

Below that, in another hand:

**Accepted to 16-2. Cord intact. No contents inspection at Annex C intake.**

A receiving mark followed.

No name Ilyan knew.

Thessa leaned closer without touching the page.

"Three sheets."

Vaun had returned to the table.

"Yes."

"Selwyn remembered three comparison impressions."

"He did."

"And his comparison sheet was missing when the old case came back."

"Yes."

Thessa looked at the entry again.

"Same sheet?"

Vaun's answer came immediately.

"We don't know."

Thessa nodded.

No argument.

Ilyan read the words **defect-inheritance comparisons** again.

Selwyn had used that phrase.

Not exactly. Close enough to make his skin tighten and not close enough to permit the conclusion he wanted.

"CR-88," he said.

Sael had already written it down.

"Credential Review case number."

"Can we retrieve the old index?"

"Requested."

Ilyan looked at him.

Sael looked back.

Thessa murmured, "He keeps doing that to you."

"I know."

Vaun pointed to the second line.

"More important first. Protected Review Continuation sixteen-two."

The three of them looked at it.

Crates Fourteen through Sixteen had contained active protected-review continuations when the Joint Office dissolved.

Crate Seventeen had contained comparative property.

The packet had crossed that boundary during intake.

Not secretly.

Not necessarily improperly.

Under a receipt.

Ilyan had begun to hate receipts in ways he had not known were available to him.

"If the master successor register maps sixteen-two," he said, "we know where the packet went."

"If the mapping survives," Vaun said.

"If Serrat permits it."

Sael's eyes moved toward him.

Ilyan heard his own phrasing after it was too late.

He had made Serrat the obstacle before Serrat had done anything.

He exhaled.

"If the restriction permits inspection under the judge's order."

Sael said nothing.

That was worse than correction.

Thessa's shoulder touched Ilyan's.

Not comfort exactly.

A reminder that she was there.

He let it remain.

The registrar entered carrying a smaller folder.

"Credential Review index."

Sael took it.

The index was not restricted.

CR-88 appeared halfway down a page of old investigations.

**Comparative study — cancelled sponsor dies / inherited defect replication. Lead examiner: M. Venn. Supporting examiner: S. Arven. Status: superseded by joint review.**

Nobody spoke.

Ilyan felt the room narrow around the table.

Mara.

Selwyn.

The old counterfeit investigation.

The packet in Crate Seventeen was not merely something with similar language.

It belonged to their case.

Vaun rested one finger beside the index entry.

"Now we know which case the packet came from."

Thessa said, "Still not what was on the three sheets."

Vaun looked at her.

"Correct."

Thessa smiled faintly.

"I'm learning."

"Dangerous habit."

Ilyan almost laughed.

Then the far door opened.

Examiner Halwen Serrat entered alone.

---

Ilyan remembered Serrat as a man who made silence feel graded.

He had not changed much in three months.

Tall, narrow-shouldered, dark hair going silver at the temples, robes cut without ornament except for the black-and-pearl clasp of Restricted Testimony. His face was neither severe nor kind. It was attentive in a way that made careless speech feel expensive.

During Ilyan's induction examination Serrat had asked him whether a witness from a disgraced House should be presumed compromised.

Ilyan had answered no.

Serrat had asked whether a witness from an honored House should be presumed reliable.

Ilyan had answered no again.

Serrat had nodded and moved to the next question.

At the time, Ilyan had thought that exchange reassuring.

Now Serrat held a register that might contain a path through his family's crime.

Reassurance had become more complicated.

Serrat greeted Vaun first.

"Judge."

"Examiner."

Then Sael.

Then Thessa.

Then Ilyan.

"Valeric."

"Examiner."

No recognition beyond what was professionally required.

Ilyan was absurdly grateful for it.

Serrat placed a sealed folio on the table.

"I have reviewed the order and the inherited restriction."

Vaun waited.

"The restriction does not bar judicial inspection. It bars unrestricted duplication and disclosure of protected successor identities. I can permit the docket mapping relevant to the transfer you identified. Names outside the requested chain remain masked unless the judge establishes necessity."

No obstruction.

No favor either.

Procedure.

Ilyan felt something in his chest loosen and immediately distrusted the relief.

Serrat noticed the blue register.

"You found the exception."

Vaun turned it toward him.

He read entry 17-4 once.

"That narrows the request."

"It does."

Serrat broke the folio seal.

Inside lay a certified successor extract rather than the master register itself.

Ilyan understood why before anyone explained it.

The extract showed only the requested Joint Office continuations and their successor destinations.

Crate Fourteen had been divided among two judicial archives.

Crate Fifteen had gone partly to Crown emergency records and partly to Hall restricted testimony.

Crate Sixteen remained almost entirely under Truth Beyond the Veil successor custody.

The docket list ran down the page.

16-1.

16-2.

16-3.

Ilyan found the second line.

**16-2 — Credential Integrity Support / Emergency Substitution Review. Successor docket: RSV-17-S. Supporting materials retained under restricted testimony custody.**

His mouth went dry.

"RSV-17-S."

Serrat nodded.

"Revision Seventeen support docket."

Thessa went utterly still beside him.

Vaun did not.

"What does 'Credential Integrity Support' mean in this docket?"

"The successor register does not define it."

"Does the support docket survive?"

"Yes."

"Can we inspect it?"

Serrat looked at the order.

"Not under the present scope."

Ilyan's relief vanished so quickly it almost embarrassed him.

Vaun merely asked, "Why?"

"Because the present order concerns successor mapping and transferred property. RSV-17-S contains restricted testimony and protected identities not yet shown relevant to your property chain."

"The exception register establishes that CR-88 material was transferred into 16-2."

"It establishes that a sealed packet bearing that exterior description was accepted to 16-2."

Vaun's eyes sharpened slightly.

Serrat continued.

"That is likely sufficient for a narrower supplemental order concerning the packet's receipt and disposition. It is not sufficient for me to open every testimony record in the docket."

Ilyan hated the answer.

He also knew it was the answer he would have admired if the records belonged to someone else.

That realization sat badly.

Thessa glanced at him.

She knew him well enough not to ask whether he was all right in front of three examiners and a judge.

Instead she said, "Does the successor extract show whether the packet remained in the docket?"

Serrat looked at her.

"No."

"Would the docket have an internal property or document receipt?"

"Yes."

"Then that's the next request."

Vaun said, "Exactly."

Thessa leaned back.

Ilyan looked at her.

She raised one eyebrow.

He had no idea when she had become good at this.

That was not true.

He knew exactly when.

The last week had been teaching all of them things they had never expected to need.

Serrat closed the successor extract.

Ilyan said, "Examiner."

Serrat waited.

"Did you know my family was connected to Revision Seventeen when you examined me?"

Vaun did not interrupt.

Neither did Sael.

Serrat's expression remained unreadable.

"I knew your surname."

"That isn't what I asked."

"No."

A pause.

"I knew Revision Seventeen had Valeric administrative contacts. I did not know what you knew about your father or uncle. I did not know whether those contacts were culpable. I still do not know that from the records before me."

Ilyan held his gaze.

"Why tell me family reputation was neither evidence for nor against me?"

"Because it isn't."

Nothing else.

No revelation that Serrat had secretly been protecting him.

No confession that the examination had been designed around his family.

Just the same answer he had given with his conduct months ago.

Ilyan looked down at the successor extract.

Professional respect remained.

Personal trust did not magically appear beside it.

But suspicion had changed shape.

That mattered.

Serrat said, "If Judge Vaun obtains the supplemental order, I will execute it."

Vaun was already writing.

"You will have it."

"Then I expect we will speak again."

He resealed the extract into its folio.

At the door he stopped.

"Valeric."

Ilyan looked up.

"Yes?"

"Do not confuse discovering that your institution possesses an ugly record with discovering that your institution created the ugliness."

Ilyan felt irritation flare.

Serrat's face did not change.

"And do not confuse custody with innocence either."

Then he left.

Thessa waited until the door closed.

"I understand why you find him irritating."

Sael said, "You find everyone irritating."

"Not everyone."

She looked at Ilyan.

"Only the educational ones."

This time he did laugh.

---

They took the evidence back to Reedglass because Vaun wanted Mara and Selwyn's earlier statements compared against CR-88 before she sought the supplemental order.

Damaris was not there.

Rhyl was.

He had been sent by Meris with a basket of food and had apparently decided that delivering it required eating some of it himself.

"Where is she?" Thessa asked.

"River Ledger. Maeryn found three ways to make yesterday's luncheon continue after it ended."

"That sounds ominous."

"One of them is a committee invitation."

"Terrifying."

Rhyl handed her a pastry.

Thessa accepted it without shame.

Ilyan put the certified CR-88 index copy on the table.

Rhyl stopped chewing.

"You found it."

"We found where the packet was sent."

He explained.

Rhyl listened with the particular stillness he used in the woods when he had heard something beyond ordinary hearing.

When Ilyan reached RSV-17-S, Rhyl sat down.

"So the old counterfeit comparison material went into Revision Seventeen support custody."

"A packet labeled as CR-88 defect-inheritance comparisons went into the protected review continuation that became the Revision Seventeen support docket."

Rhyl looked at him.

"Sael has infected you too."

"Apparently."

"Good."

Ilyan frowned.

Rhyl took another bite.

"Annoying, but good."

Thessa sat on the edge of the table.

"We still don't know whether the packet was the missing three-impression sheet."

"But CR-88 was Mara and Selwyn's case," Rhyl said.

"Yes."

"And the packet was moved into Revision Seventeen support."

"Yes."

"Before or after Revision Seventeen was written?"

Nobody answered.

Vaun looked up from Mara's statement.

That was the first question of the day that changed the room.

Ilyan turned toward her.

"The dissolution transfer was years later."

"Obviously," Rhyl said. "I mean when the Joint Office first put CR-88 material with the protected review. The Annex transfer only tells us they were together by dissolution."

Thessa lowered the pastry.

"So the packet could have joined 16-2 years after Revision Seventeen."

"Or before," Ilyan said.

Vaun nodded.

"Now we have the right question."

Not who used the old counterfeit research to create Revision Seventeen.

Not whether the missing comparison sheet had caused Silver Night.

When did CR-88 material enter the protected-review chain that eventually became RSV-17-S?

The answer could destroy the apparent connection.

Or make it much worse.

Vaun amended the supplemental request.

She asked for the packet's first receipt into protected-review custody, every internal transfer attached to that packet identifier, and its final disposition.

Nothing broader.

Nothing theatrical.

Ilyan watched her sand the page.

"How long?"

"If Serrat moves quickly? Tomorrow."

"And if he doesn't?"

"Tomorrow afternoon, because I will go stand in his office."

Thessa smiled.

"I like you."

Vaun did not look up.

"A regrettable lapse in judgment."

Rhyl offered the judge a pastry.

She took one.

That somehow made the whole thing more alarming.

---

Damaris returned after sunset carrying a committee packet thick enough to injure someone.

Rhyl met her in the entrance hall.

Ilyan saw them from the library doorway.

Damaris said something he could not hear.

Rhyl took the packet from her.

She caught the front of his coat and kissed him before he had finished setting it on the side table.

Not a ceremonial kiss.

Not one designed for witnesses.

The sort that made Rhyl forget, briefly, that he was holding several pounds of River Ledger bureaucracy against his hip.

When she let him go, he looked pleasantly disoriented.

Damaris noticed Ilyan.

"How long have you been standing there?"

"Long enough to learn that committee service is apparently exciting."

"It isn't. That's why this was necessary."

Rhyl looked at the packet.

"Should I be jealous of municipal finance?"

"Constantly."

She took his hand and came into the library.

Thessa had gone back to the Warden house. Vaun had taken the supplemental request to the Hall. Meris was upstairs with Aereth.

For once the room belonged mostly to friends.

Ilyan told Damaris what they had found.

Her expression changed at CR-88.

Then again at RSV-17-S.

She sat beside Rhyl.

"Does Mara know?"

"Not yet. Vaun wants to tell her after the supplemental order is filed."

"Good."

Ilyan looked at her committee packet.

"Maeryn?"

Damaris groaned.

"Maeryn has decided that if I insist on being publicly visible, I may as well be useful in public. River transport reconciliation committee. Observer seat."

Rhyl said, "She made it sound like an honor."

"It is an honor. That's the problem."

Ilyan smiled.

Damaris pointed at him.

"You have your own institutional nightmare. Do not enjoy mine."

"Serrat cooperated."

That surprised her.

"Entirely?"

"Within the order. He refused unrestricted docket access and explained why."

"Do you believe him?"

Ilyan considered.

A week ago he might have answered the wrong question.

"I believe the restriction exists. I believe he applied it consistently with what I've seen from him before. I don't know what he knows about the underlying records."

Damaris nodded.

"Better."

"Everyone keeps saying that when I become more annoying."

Rhyl leaned back.

"It's adulthood."

"I was told there would be freedom."

"Who told you that?"

"No one reliable."

Damaris laughed.

For several minutes they did nothing useful.

Rhyl opened the committee packet and made horrified noises at a table of barge-loss adjustments. Damaris tried to take it back. He held it over his head. She climbed half across him to reach it, which defeated whatever practical advantage his longer arms had provided and created several new problems neither seemed eager to solve.

Ilyan looked away before friendship required commentary.

The rain had stopped.

Beyond the window, Reedglass's orchard shone black and silver beneath the cloud-broken moon.

Somewhere in the Hall of Veils, a supplemental order was moving toward Examiner Serrat.

Somewhere behind inherited restrictions, a receipt might still say when a packet from an old counterfeit investigation entered the protected review that became Revision Seventeen's support file.

Tomorrow they might learn that the connection was late, administrative and meaningless.

Ilyan wanted that possibility to remain real.

He was no longer sure he expected it.
