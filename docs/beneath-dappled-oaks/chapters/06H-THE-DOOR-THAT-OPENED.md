# Chapter Six H — The Door That Opened

The issuance register had survived because nobody had thought it important enough to destroy.

That was Examiner Sael's opinion, delivered while Ilyan stood beside him in a basement room under the old Credential Integrity offices and tried not to resent the existence of dust.

"You don't know that," Ilyan said.

"Correct."

"Then why say it?"

Sael looked at the shelf label again. "Because you were looking at the room as though survival were evidence of innocence."

Ilyan opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Thessa, behind him, made a thoughtful noise.

"I hate when he does that."

"You met him yesterday."

"I learn quickly."

Sael ignored both of them.

The room had once been a working archive and had since been demoted to the place where working archives sent things they were not yet permitted to burn. Narrow shelves ran from stone floor to a ceiling low enough that Thessa had already struck her knuckles against a hanging lamp while removing her coat. The air smelled of paste, old leather and the faint mineral damp of a building that had spent two centuries pretending groundwater was someone else's administrative problem.

Judge Vaun stood at the central table with the transfer authorization open before her. Serrat had remained at the Hall of Veils to supervise the restricted-copy chain. Caelen was on Royal Martial duty. Mireth had Green Covenant examinations until midday. Lyrion had been required to attend what he described in his note as a diplomatic breakfast conducted by people who considered toast a negotiating position. Damaris was at the River Ledger.

The Seven had not become an investigative unit merely because they were friends.

They still had jobs.

Ilyan found that reassuring.

Most days.

The archive clerk arrived carrying a grey ledger against her chest.

"Emergency credentials," she said. "Years two-sixty through two-sixty-six."

Vaun checked the seal before touching it.

"Original?"

"Original issuance book."

"Any known removals?"

"One page copied under tribunal order in two-sixty-six. No leaves removed according to the binding inspection."

"Who ordered the copy?"

The clerk glanced at the transfer sheet.

"Dmitrigical post-incident review."

Thessa looked at Ilyan.

He looked at the ledger.

Neither said anything.

Vaun signed the receipt.

The clerk laid the book down.

It was ugly.

Ilyan had expected that too by now.

Important records were almost never beautiful. They were too busy being used.

The leather corners had been repaired twice. The spine bore three different numbering systems. Somebody had spilled wax across the lower edge years ago and scraped most of it away with a knife.

Sael opened to Bloomtide, 264 S.M.

The entries were arranged by issuance batch rather than recipient.

Ilyan read slowly.

Ordinary temporary substitutions first: a bridge inspector whose parent authorization had been damaged in floodwater; two tribunal messengers replacing stolen credentials; a Crown surveyor working beyond the western road network; a witness transferred under emergency seal after a household fire destroyed her papers.

Legitimate uses.

That mattered.

Revision Seventeen had not created a machine whose only possible purpose was murder. It had created a dangerous administrative shortcut inside a system that sometimes genuinely needed one.

Then Sael's finger stopped.

**Batch ES-17-W.**

Six credentials.

Issued four days before Silver Night.

Purpose: **West Tower restricted-access review.**

Requesting authority: **Dmitrigical Tribunal Contingency Secretariat.**

Technical validation: **Credential Integrity / Veyr.**

Custody release: **Joint Order Liaison / T. Valeric.**

Ilyan felt the back of his neck go cold.

Thessa leaned closer.

"Six."

"Yes."

The recipient column did not contain names.

It contained temporary designators.

W-1 through W-6.

Vaun said, "Keep going."

Ilyan did.

Each credential had a destination authorization.

The first four read **Concord Academy — west tower service and receiving levels**.

The fifth read **Concord Academy — west tower third-floor restricted receiving**.

WT-3.

The sixth read **Concord Academy — west tower upper access / witness review rooms**.

Nobody needed to ask which west tower anymore.

Thessa sat down.

Ilyan did not.

He read the issue times.

All six had been released in a span of thirty-seven minutes.

The return column was worse.

W-1 returned.

W-2 returned.

W-3 returned.

W-4 returned.

W-6 returned.

W-5 had no return mark.

Instead, someone had written in a narrow later hand:

**Outstanding at incident. Presumed lost with receiving staff.**

Mara Venn had worked receiving.

Ilyan's eyes remained on the line.

"Don't," Thessa said quietly.

He looked at her.

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face did."

It was becoming an irritatingly common observation.

Vaun drew the ledger toward herself.

"W-5 is not Eren Vale's credential."

Ilyan blinked.

"I know."

"Say why."

He forced himself to think instead of merely react.

"Different issuance. Different date. Eren's bonded-runner credential is the current case. This is eighteen years old."

"Good."

"But the method—"

"May be related."

"Yes."

Sael turned the page.

There was an attached disposition note.

Five returned credentials had been examined after Silver Night. All five were marked **physically valid / lineage consistency confirmed**.

Then a later notation:

**Variation insufficient. Refer CI comparison.**

Pel Ardis's concern.

They had behaved too well.

Ilyan read the six temporary designators again.

"Who were they?"

The archive clerk shook her head. "Designator key was sealed separately."

"Where?"

She checked the old cross-reference.

Her expression changed.

"Tribunal Contingency personnel file."

Vaun said, "Current location?"

The clerk followed the notation to the end.

"Transferred with post-incident materials. Sealed comparative vault."

Tal's die.

Not the same object.

The same vault.

For the first time that morning, Vaun looked tired.

"Of course it was."

Thessa folded her arms. "That's convenient."

"No," Vaun said. "It's centralized. There's a difference."

She closed the issuance register.

"We go to the vault."

---

The Dmitrigical Tribunal occupied a building that had been designed by someone who distrusted sunlight and believed stairs improved moral character.

By the fourth flight Thessa had developed opinions about both positions.

"If forbidden magic doesn't kill them," she said, "their architecture will."

Ilyan was reading the copied issuance extract while walking.

"You could stop climbing like you're attacking the building."

"It started it."

Vaun, three steps above them, said, "Save your strength."

Thessa looked delighted.

"For the architecture?"

"For not speaking."

The sealed comparative vault was below the tribunal rather than above it, which meant the stairs had only been a preliminary insult. They descended by a narrow iron lift operated by a clerk who had no interest in why a judge, a Veil examiner, a Warden and an increasingly irritated young Valeric required access to an eighteen-year-old storage book.

The receiving register waited in a small antechamber behind two locks.

Master Enna Ro met them there.

She was a compact woman with iron-grey hair braided close to her skull and the expression of someone who had spent forty years watching clever people discover that vault procedures applied to them too.

"Judge."

"Master Ro."

"Your order permits register inspection. Not compartment access."

"Correct."

Ilyan liked her immediately.

That was probably unwise.

Ro opened the receiving book herself.

"You want the two-sixty-six transfer."

"Demonstrator 08-D," Vaun said.

Ro found it without using the index.

The entry was precise.

**Legacy demonstrator 08-D. Brass comparison die. Source lot 44-C.**

Tal.

Transferred from Credential Integrity under post-incident review order.

Receiving compartment: **SV-12.**

Associated materials: **ES-17-W return set, five credentials; designator key, sealed; CI variation report; one unreturned credential file, W-5.**

Ilyan stopped breathing for a second.

Not because the die had become connected to the credentials by magic.

Because somebody had placed the physical comparison object, the five returned credentials, their identity key and the missing sixth credential's file into one post-incident review compartment.

A review had happened.

The question was what it concluded.

"Who ordered SV-12 sealed?" Vaun asked.

Ro moved her finger down the page.

"Initial review order: Examiner Pel Ardis. Continuation order after his withdrawal: Contingency Secretariat."

"Personal countersignature?"

"Not in the receiving book."

"Current contents status?"

"Sealed. No withdrawals recorded after two-sixty-seven."

Thessa said, "So they're still there."

Ro looked at her.

"The register says no withdrawals."

Thessa nodded once.

"Right."

She did not repeat the claim.

Vaun read the entry again.

"Why did Ardis withdraw?"

Ro turned one page.

There was a note attached to the continuation.

Not a finding.

A recusal.

**Examiner Ardis requests removal from review on grounds of prior technical participation in underlying substitution procedure and resulting appearance of compromised independence. Recommend independent examiner.**

Ilyan stared at it.

Pel Ardis had not been removed.

He had removed himself.

"Was an independent examiner appointed?" he asked.

Ro checked the next line.

"Yes."

The name was written clearly.

**Examiner Teren Valeric.**

Thessa swore.

Quietly.

Vaun did not reprimand her.

Ilyan felt something inside him go very still.

Teren had not merely renewed a restriction years later.

He had taken over the post-Silver-Night review after Ardis recused himself.

That did not tell them what Teren concluded.

It told them he had been in a position to conclude something.

Ilyan put both hands on the edge of the table.

His uncle's service chest.

His uncle's affection.

The childhood practice token kept among old papers.

Teren telling him he had only handled custody.

All of those facts remained facts.

So did this one.

"Did he file a report?" Ilyan asked.

Ro checked the register.

"Review disposition sealed inside SV-12."

"Of course it is."

Sael would have told him sarcasm was not an evidentiary category.

Sael was not there, which improved the moment considerably.

Vaun straightened.

"I need a compartment-opening order."

Ro closed the book.

"Yes."

"How long?"

"If Serrat agrees the Hall restriction is satisfied and the tribunal presiding examiner signs? Today. If either contests jurisdiction, tomorrow at best."

Ilyan pushed away from the table.

"Then do that."

Vaun looked at him.

He heard himself.

"Please."

"Better."

Thessa put a hand between his shoulder blades.

Not comforting him exactly.

Holding him in place.

He let her.

---

Damaris found out about Teren while Rhyl was trying to convince her that committee arithmetic could not be defeated through personal hostility.

"It can," she said.

"No."

"You lack imagination."

"You have three barges, two dredging contracts and one duplicated billing hour. None of them care whether you hate them."

"The contractors care."

"The contractors aren't arithmetic."

Damaris dipped her pen.

"That sounds like something a man says immediately before arithmetic ruins his afternoon."

They were sharing the narrow observer's table in the River transport committee room. Rhyl had no official business there and therefore had been given the only chair designed for visitors, which seemed to have been built as punishment for visiting.

He had endured it for forty minutes.

Damaris respected this more than she intended to admit.

A runner entered.

Not one of hers.

Judicial grey.

Damaris set down the pen.

The note was from Vaun.

She read it once.

Then again.

Rhyl watched her.

"Ilyan?"

"Teren."

He waited.

She handed him the note.

Rhyl read the three relevant lines and gave it back.

"He'll want to go straight to him."

"Maybe."

"Would you?"

Damaris looked at the duplicated barge hour as though it might offer moral instruction.

It did not.

"Yes."

"Would that make it wise?"

"No."

Rhyl leaned back in the terrible chair.

Damaris looked at him.

"Stop being sensible."

"You like me sensible."

"In selected circumstances."

"Which ones?"

She put her foot against his ankle beneath the table.

"I'll make a list."

He smiled.

Then his expression softened.

"Are you going?"

"After the committee adjourns."

"You could leave."

"I could."

She looked toward the councillors arguing over whether a dredging delay had been caused by weather, contractor incompetence or a definition of the word *available* that had apparently survived three generations of litigation.

"I'm not going to turn every bad thing that happens to one of us into permission to abandon everything else."

Rhyl's gaze stayed on her face.

Damaris felt heat rise under her collar for reasons that had very little to do with the room.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Liar."

"You said I wasn't allowed to become your courier. You didn't say anything about admiring you."

She stared at him.

Then looked deliberately back at the committee papers.

"That was appalling."

"You liked it."

"Unfortunately."

His ankle remained against hers.

The committee continued.

So did the rest of their lives.

---

The Seven gathered that evening in a private room above the same canal eating house.

Not because they had agreed to make it headquarters.

Because the owner had discovered that Lyrion tipped well and that Caelen could be relied upon to prevent Thessa from leaning her chair onto two legs after the second bottle.

Mireth arrived smelling faintly of crushed mint and greenhouse soil. Caelen followed twenty minutes later in Royal Martial blue, tired enough that he sat before removing his sword belt and had to stand again when the hilt caught the chair.

Lyrion came from his diplomatic breakfast with three new rumors and the good judgment not to repeat any of them until somebody asked.

Damaris and Rhyl arrived together.

Ilyan was already there.

Thessa sat beside him.

Nobody commented on that either.

Vaun had not come.

This was not an evidence conference.

It was dinner.

Ilyan told them anyway.

The issuance register.

Six credentials.

Five returned.

W-5 outstanding at Silver Night.

The Concord Academy west tower.

Tal's die and the five returned credentials later stored together in SV-12.

Ardis's recusal.

Teren's appointment as independent examiner.

When he finished, Mireth's hand had gone still around her cup.

"Did Teren tell you he'd reviewed them?"

"No."

"Did you ask?"

Ilyan looked at her.

"Not that question."

She nodded.

No accusation.

Just the boundary.

Caelen said, "And W-5?"

"Designator key is in the vault."

"So tomorrow."

"If the order clears."

Lyrion poured wine.

"What happens if the name under W-5 is someone we know?"

Thessa said, "We don't know that there's a name under it."

"There is a designator key."

"That doesn't mean the person is important to us."

"I know."

Lyrion looked at Ilyan.

"I'm asking what happens if they are."

Ilyan thought of his father.

His uncle.

Mara Venn.

Eren Vale, eighteen years later, carrying a credential that behaved like an old method nobody had successfully buried.

"Then they're important to the evidence," he said.

Damaris watched him.

"And to you?"

He met her eyes.

"Probably."

That was enough.

Rhyl broke a piece of bread and handed half to Damaris without looking at her.

She took it the same way.

Mireth's knee rested against Caelen's beneath the table. Thessa had stolen two slices of pear from Lyrion's plate and he had not noticed, or was pretending not to. Outside, Bloomtide rain began tapping softly against the shutters.

They were adults now.

That had become less theoretical very quickly.

Not because adults knew what to do.

Because nobody was going to remove the consequences while they decided.

Ilyan drank his wine.

Tomorrow, if the order cleared, a vault door would open.

Behind it waited five returned credentials, a missing credential's file, Tal's comparison die, the designator key, and whatever Teren Valeric had written after Pel Ardis decided he could no longer judge his own work.

For the first time since the investigation had reached his uncle, Ilyan knew exactly what question he wanted to ask.

He simply intended to read the answer Teren had written eighteen years ago before asking it.
