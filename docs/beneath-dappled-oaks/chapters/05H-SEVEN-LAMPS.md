# Chapter Five H — Seven Lamps

By the time Damaris decided the silver pins were trying to kill her, Meris had already dismissed two arrangements and was considering a third.

"Hold still."

"I am holding still."

"You are threatening the mirror."

"The mirror started it."

Meris's mouth twitched.

The dressing room at Reedglass had been turned inside out. Silk lay across the bed in folds of green and smoke-grey. A maid stood behind Damaris with three pins between her lips and the careful expression of someone employed by a family old enough to consider stabbing an heiress in the scalp a routine professional hazard.

Rain had finally stopped. The windows stood open to wet leaves and the evening bells.

Damaris looked at herself again.

The gown was not the one she would have chosen.

That was why Meris had chosen it.

It was dark green until she moved, when the woven silver beneath the surface caught the light and made the skirt look briefly wet. The neckline left her shoulders bare. A narrow fall of embroidered leaves ran down one side rather than centering itself politely.

It made her look older.

Not older in years.

More expensive.

More deliberate.

Like someone whose mistakes would have consequences for other people.

She disliked how accurate that was becoming.

Meris came behind her and adjusted one of the pins.

"You look lovely."

"That sounds suspiciously maternal."

"I can be maternal."

"You can imitate it."

Meris met her eyes in the mirror.

"Careful."

Damaris smiled.

The maid found sudden fascination in the pin box.

Meris finished the adjustment and stepped back.

On the little table beside her lay the copied return chit from Mara Venn's green box.

Mera Holt had received Tal's damaged die.

That was where the paper trail stopped.

For now.

Damaris had spent the last hour dressing while trying not to think about a dead customs supervisor, a counterfeit credential case closed eighteen years ago, and the possibility that Teren Valeric would be drinking wine beneath seven ceremonial lamps before the night was out.

It was not working.

"Don't ask him tonight," Meris said.

Damaris turned.

"Ask whom what?"

"Ilyan. His uncle."

Damaris looked at her.

Meris shrugged.

"You have all been walking around Reedglass with the particular expressions young adults wear when they possess information they believe their elders cannot possibly detect."

"That is an offensively broad category."

"It has served me well."

"You don't know what we found."

"No."

Meris said it without irritation.

That was worse.

She crossed to the dressing table and picked up one of Damaris's earrings.

"But I know Ilyan has spent the afternoon looking as though he intends to challenge a relative to single combat using administrative law."

Damaris laughed before she could stop herself.

"That is unfair."

"To administrative law?"

"To Ilyan."

"Mm."

Meris handed her the earring.

Damaris put it on.

"Would you tell him not to speak to Teren?"

"I would tell him to decide what he wants before he opens his mouth."

"Answers."

"Everyone wants answers. Does he want his uncle frightened? Angry? Comfortable? Talking? Lying? Does he want to learn something, or does he want the pleasure of letting Teren know that he knows something?"

Damaris's fingers stopped at the second earring.

Meris watched her in the mirror.

"Those are different evenings."

There it was.

The reason Damaris could resent her aunt and still listen when she spoke.

"Did you learn that from intelligence work?"

"Marriage."

Damaris turned around.

Meris's face remained perfectly composed.

"You're joking."

"Frequently."

"That was not an answer."

"Exactly."

The maid coughed.

Meris took pity on her and moved toward the door.

"Rhyl is downstairs."

Damaris's stomach performed a humiliating little movement.

Meris saw that too.

Of course she did.

"Oh, don't look so alarmed. You're an adult. He's attractive. The condition is survivable."

"Aunt."

"Usually."

Damaris threw a glove at her.

Meris caught it and left with the glove.

---

Rhyl had been taught from childhood that staring was rude.

His education failed him completely.

Damaris came down the Reedglass stairs with one hand on the rail and paused when she saw him.

That helped nothing.

The green made her skin look warm. Silver flashed when she moved. Her hair had been pinned up loosely enough that several dark curls had already escaped around her neck.

Rhyl forgot whatever he had intended to say.

Damaris reached the bottom.

"Well?"

He considered lying.

"No."

Her eyebrows rose.

"I haven't asked anything yet."

"I know. I am declining in advance."

"That bad?"

"Quite the opposite."

She smiled.

That was worse too.

Rhyl offered his arm.

She took it and then, because apparently neither of them had any remaining interest in behaving subtly, leaned close enough to kiss him just below the ear.

He closed his eyes.

"That was cruel."

"You survived."

"Barely."

Behind them, Meris said, "The carriage is waiting."

Rhyl opened his eyes.

Meris stood in the doorway wearing dark blue and the expression of a woman who had watched generations of younger people discover desire and had long ago ceased pretending it was mysterious.

Damaris looked entirely unrepentant.

They went out.

---

The Hall of Seven Lamps had been built by people who believed architecture should make visitors reconsider their place in the universe.

Seven bronze lamps hung beneath the dome, each suspended on chains so fine they seemed to float. Their flames were ordinary tonight. No sacred working had been called. That made them almost more beautiful.

Below them, half the city had dressed for war and called it a reception.

House colors moved through the hall in silk, velvet, polished metal and embroidery. Order insignia flashed on collars. Magistrates stood beside bankers. Old soldiers drank with scholars they had spent thirty years insulting in print. The musicians played from a gallery screened by carved branches.

Damaris felt the room notice her.

Not everyone.

Enough.

She had attended receptions before.

She had never attended one after Silver Night.

That changed the arithmetic.

The Seven were no longer merely academy students with inconvenient surnames.

They were survivors.

They were graduates.

They were available.

For employment.

For patronage.

For marriage.

For blame.

Rhyl's hand rested lightly against the back of hers.

"Still breathing?"

"Against my better judgment."

"Good."

Mireth found them first.

She wore pale gold with no jewelry except a narrow chain at her throat. Caelen stood beside her in formal black, looking as though somebody had forced a sword to attend a poetry reading.

Damaris kissed Mireth's cheek.

"You look beautiful."

"So do you."

Caelen looked at Rhyl.

Rhyl looked at Caelen.

Neither man said anything.

Mireth sighed.

"They've been doing that for ten minutes."

"We are communicating," Caelen said.

"About what?"

"Nothing that concerns you."

"It concerns me if you begin dueling."

Rhyl said, "We have agreed not to."

Caelen nodded.

Mireth looked at Damaris.

"See?"

Damaris did.

Thessa and Lyrion arrived together a few minutes later, Thessa in deep red and Lyrion in the dark formal coat of his family. She had one hand tucked through his arm and the expression of a woman prepared to enjoy herself until someone made the mistake of giving her a reason not to.

Ilyan came last.

He looked immaculate.

He also looked furious.

Damaris intercepted him before he could scan the room a second time.

"Don't."

"I haven't done anything."

"Your face has."

Rhyl made a quiet choking sound.

Damaris looked at him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Ilyan's attention moved past her shoulder.

Damaris followed it.

Teren Valeric stood near the north colonnade speaking with a woman from the Hall of Veils.

He was older than Ilyan by enough to make the resemblance unsettling rather than immediate. Same line of the jaw. Same pale eyes. Teren's hair had gone silver at the temples.

He laughed at something the woman said.

Ilyan went very still.

Damaris lowered her voice.

"What do you want?"

He looked at her.

She did not explain.

He knew what she meant.

After a moment he said, "I want to know why."

"Then don't tell him what you have."

"I know."

"You don't look like you know."

"I look like I want to hit him."

"That too."

A server passed with wine.

Ilyan took one.

Did not drink it.

"Tomorrow morning," he said.

Damaris nodded.

The unrestricted renewal record would be waiting.

Tonight he had only the fact of Teren's delegated seal on the restriction renewal.

Enough to hurt.

Not enough to spend carelessly.

Thessa appeared at Ilyan's other shoulder.

"Are we glaring at relatives?"

"Apparently."

"Good. I was worried this would be dull."

Lyrion touched the small of her back.

She leaned into him automatically.

The gesture was so familiar Damaris almost missed it.

Almost.

Across the hall, someone else did not.

A woman in violet watched them for half a second too long before turning away.

Damaris filed that somewhere in the increasingly overcrowded cabinet inside her head.

Then the musicians changed pieces.

The first formal dance began.

Rhyl offered his hand.

"Before somebody recruits you."

"Into what?"

"Marriage, espionage, municipal drainage. This room contains options."

She put her hand in his.

"Drainage sounds stable."

"Excellent pension."

They joined the floor.

---

Damaris had forgotten that dancing with someone she wanted was different.

The steps were the same.

That was almost insulting.

Rhyl's hand rested at her waist. Her palm fit against his. They turned beneath the lamps while the hall moved around them in measured circles.

"Meris knows we're sleeping badly," Rhyl said.

Damaris nearly stepped on him.

"What?"

"Not together."

"Rhyl."

"That was not what I meant."

"You chose an astonishing way to clarify it."

He was laughing now.

She wanted to kiss him.

This was becoming a recurring administrative problem.

"What does she know?"

"That we're tired."

"Everyone is tired."

"She asked whether the investigation was keeping us awake."

"And?"

"I said yes."

"Truthful."

"I am experimenting."

She squeezed his hand.

They turned.

Over his shoulder she saw Teren again.

He had stopped speaking to the Hall woman.

Now he was watching Ilyan.

Not staring.

Watching.

Ilyan had joined Caelen near one of the side tables and was making an admirable effort to look interested in a discussion about river tariffs.

Teren's gaze lingered.

Then moved away.

Damaris felt Rhyl's hand shift slightly at her waist.

He had noticed her attention.

"Teren?"

"Yes."

"He's watching Ilyan."

"I saw."

"Does Ilyan know?"

"Probably."

"Don't tell him."

Damaris looked at Rhyl.

"Why?"

"Because he's currently succeeding."

She glanced back.

Ilyan was still speaking to Caelen.

His shoulders were no longer quite so rigid.

"Fair."

The dance carried them past Meris.

Her aunt was speaking with an elderly magistrate.

Meris's eyes flicked to Damaris, then Rhyl's hand, then back to the magistrate without losing a word.

Damaris felt heat rise into her face.

Rhyl saw that too.

"Your aunt?"

"Unfortunately."

"Should I remove my hand?"

She pulled him closer.

"No."

His expression changed.

Only slightly.

Enough.

The next turn brought them close to the edge of the floor.

A man Damaris did not know bowed to them as they passed.

"Lady Damaris."

She returned the courtesy.

Rhyl's fingers tightened once.

"Who was that?"

"No idea."

"He knew you."

"Half the room knows me."

"I dislike half the room."

"Jealous?"

"No."

She waited.

He sighed.

"Possibly."

Damaris smiled so broadly that a passing dowager noticed and smiled too.

Rhyl looked offended.

"You are enjoying this."

"Immensely."

"Monster."

"Yes."

The music ended.

They bowed.

Neither moved away immediately.

Then the room changed.

Not dramatically.

No doors slammed. No herald shouted.

A court runner entered through the west arch and spoke to Judge Vaun.

Damaris saw Vaun's face from across the hall.

That was enough.

She touched Rhyl's wrist.

He turned.

Vaun read the folded note once.

Then looked directly at them.

---

They found her in a small side gallery beneath a painted panel of summer orchards.

Ilyan came with them.

Mireth remained in the hall with the others. There was no reason to turn seven visible survivors leaving together into an announcement.

Vaun handed the note to Damaris.

North Quay records office.

One paragraph.

The old counterfeit case had been found.

Not the whole case.

Its transfer register.

Damaris read the receiving line twice.

**Joint Office for Order and Credential Integrity.**

Below it, in a different hand:

**Dmitrigical Review liaison acknowledged.**

Ilyan swore softly.

Rhyl read over Damaris's shoulder.

"That still isn't authorship."

"No," Vaun said.

Her tone suggested approval that he had said it before she needed to.

Damaris looked at Ilyan.

He was pale.

"Your uncle?"

"Not named."

"Good."

He gave her a sharp look.

She held it.

"Good because you still have room to learn something."

His anger lasted perhaps three seconds.

Then he nodded.

Vaun pointed to the second line of the note.

Damaris had missed it.

**Senior examiner of record: Selwyn Arven. Current residence located.**

"Alive?" Rhyl asked.

"Apparently."

"Where?"

"Old Orchard district."

Ilyan looked toward the hall.

"Teren is twenty yards away."

Vaun folded the note.

"Selwyn Arven has been eighteen years away."

Ilyan almost laughed.

Almost.

"Point taken."

Damaris handed the note back.

"Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow," Vaun said.

The reception continued beyond the gallery.

Music. Conversation. Glass against glass.

A hundred people who knew nothing about a damaged die, a buried packet, a counterfeit runner or the old office that had swallowed an investigation and left a liaison acknowledgement behind.

Or perhaps some of them knew.

That was the problem with rooms like this.

Damaris looked at Ilyan.

"Can you go back in there?"

He took a breath.

"Yes."

"Can you speak to Teren without asking?"

Another breath.

"Yes."

Rhyl said, "Convincingly?"

Ilyan looked at him.

"No."

That made all four of them laugh.

Quietly.

It helped.

---

Teren approached Ilyan before Ilyan could decide whether avoiding him would look worse.

Damaris was three conversations away.

Close enough to see.

Too far to hear everything.

That was probably safer.

Teren touched Ilyan's shoulder.

Ilyan did not flinch.

Good.

They spoke.

Teren smiled once.

Ilyan did not.

Then Teren's expression changed.

Not fear.

Concern.

He leaned closer.

Ilyan answered.

Damaris watched his hands.

Open.

Still.

No accusation.

No triumph.

Whatever he said, Teren's shoulders eased.

Then his uncle embraced him.

Briefly.

Ilyan stood rigid for half a heartbeat.

Then returned it.

Damaris looked away.

Some things deserved not to become evidence merely because she happened to see them.

Rhyl appeared beside her with two glasses.

He handed her one.

"Well?"

"He didn't explode."

"Promising."

"Low standard."

"We've had a week."

She drank.

The wine was floral and cold.

Across the hall, Thessa had somehow acquired an elderly knight who was telling her a story with enormous gestures. Lyrion watched with affectionate resignation. Mireth and Caelen had disappeared from Damaris's immediate sight, which in this room probably meant they had found a quieter corner rather than been assassinated.

Probably.

Rhyl followed her gaze.

"Do you ever stop counting everyone?"

"No."

"Good."

She looked at him.

"Good?"

"I like knowing someone is counting me."

That struck somewhere softer than she expected.

Damaris set down her wine.

"Come here."

"We are already—"

She kissed him.

Not long.

Long enough.

When she stepped back, Rhyl looked at the seven lamps overhead.

"I have reconsidered my earlier position."

"Which one?"

"This room may be tolerable."

"Because of the architecture?"

"Entirely."

A woman behind them laughed.

Damaris did not care.

For the first time since Silver Night, she realized she was enjoying herself without having to justify it.

The realization hurt.

Then it didn't.

Not completely.

She let that be enough.

---

Near midnight, the Seven found one another beneath the east colonnade.

No formal meeting.

No synchronized briefing.

Mireth had stolen two honey cakes from a tray and was sharing one with Caelen. Thessa had removed her shoes. Lyrion carried them because apparently some forms of devotion were less poetic than others.

Ilyan looked tired.

Better.

Damaris touched his sleeve.

"Tomorrow."

He nodded.

"Tomorrow."

No one asked what that meant.

They all had their own versions of tomorrow waiting.

Mireth looked around the group.

"Are we leaving?"

Thessa said, "I am."

"Because you're tired?"

"Because if one more elderly knight tells me I remind him of a woman he nearly married in 241, I am going to become historically significant."

Caelen said, "Reasonable."

Lyrion handed Thessa her shoes.

She stared at them.

"No."

He sighed and kept carrying them.

They walked out together.

Not in formation.

Not as investigators.

Seven friends leaving a reception too late, overdressed and exhausted.

The city beyond the Hall smelled of wet stone and flowering trees.

Carriages waited under the lamps.

Damaris paused before climbing into hers.

Across the steps, Ilyan looked back once toward the Hall.

Teren remained inside.

Tomorrow they would find Selwyn Arven.

Tomorrow Ilyan would decide what to do with his uncle.

Tomorrow Damaris would ask Talia what, if anything, Mara remembered after sleeping on eighteen-year-old records.

Tomorrow the Academy delivery would still be there.

None of it needed to be solved tonight.

Rhyl offered her his hand.

She took it.
