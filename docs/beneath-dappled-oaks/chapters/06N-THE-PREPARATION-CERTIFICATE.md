# Chapter Six N — The Preparation Certificate

The preparation certificate was exactly where every surviving record said it ought to be.

This did not make it easy to obtain.

By midmorning, Ilyan had acquired a new appreciation for the difference between a document being lost and a document being protected by three offices whose seals had outlived two of the people who invented them.

Judge Vaun stood at the counter in the Hall of Veils while Examiner Sael read the supplemental order for the second time.

"It names C-17/P," Sael said.

"Yes."

"It authorizes inspection of the preparation certificate, attachments incorporated by reference, and any source schedule physically filed with it."

"Yes."

"It does not authorize opening unrelated Revision Seventeen material merely because somebody filed it in the same box."

"I wrote it that way."

Sael looked over the paper at her.

"I know. I am enjoying the novelty."

Vaun's expression did not move.

Ilyan suspected she was enjoying herself too.

Thessa, beside him, leaned close enough that her sleeve brushed his.

"Do they always flirt like this?" she murmured.

Ilyan nearly choked.

Sael's eyes shifted toward them.

Thessa smiled with the perfect innocence of someone who had never possessed it.

Ilyan looked at the floor.

The previous night's kisses had altered nothing obvious about the Hall. The same lamps burned in their niches. The same clerks carried the same tied bundles through the same narrow doors. Sael remained irritating. Vaun remained constitutionally incapable of hurrying merely because everyone else wanted her to.

What had changed was that Ilyan could feel Thessa beside him with absurd precision.

The warmth of her shoulder.

The faint scent of cedar soap in her hair.

The place where their hands almost touched against the edge of the counter.

He had known all of those things before.

Now his mind had become professionally interested in them.

It was extremely inconvenient.

Sael handed the order to a clerk.

"Restricted implementation compartment. C-17/P only, plus incorporated attachments. Two witnesses. No loose copying until the document schedule is checked."

The clerk nodded and disappeared through the inner door.

Thessa waited until it closed.

"You are red."

"I am not."

"You are."

"This building is warm."

"It is made of stone."

"Warm stone."

Vaun turned her head slightly.

"If either of you requires a recess to resolve the thermal properties of the Hall, say so now."

Thessa bit the inside of her cheek.

Ilyan decided that dying would be simpler.

Sael's mouth twitched.

The clerk returned carrying a flat grey folder between both hands.

That ended the matter.

For the moment.

---

C-17/P was three pages.

The first was a source schedule.

The second was the preparation certificate itself.

The third was a correction sheet added two days later.

No conspiracy announced itself in red ink.

No one had written *we are now destroying the evidentiary value of this packet* across the top.

The source schedule was a list of ordinary records available to the reconstruction clerks four days after Silver Night.

Five returned emergency credentials.

Certified sponsor-seal exemplars.

Academy receiving-room entries.

A certified casualty roll dated the third morning after the incident.

Contingency's retained copy of the emergency substitution procedure.

The disputed-substitution memorandum was listed separately.

Its status read:

**Not incorporated — superseded procedural draft.**

Ilyan's finger stopped beside the line.

Thessa saw it too.

"That's one of the things Haleth said was missing."

"Yes."

Vaun leaned closer.

The three failed credential fragments were also listed.

**Not available to preparer — retained in protected continuity custody.**

That was technically true.

It was also the entire problem.

Mara had carried them away in the original continuity packet.

The reconstruction clerks had not possessed them.

Ilyan read the next line.

**Original provisional casualty list — excluded pursuant to casualty/credential separation directive. Certified casualty roll substituted.**

There was a directive number.

**AD-4.**

Thessa exhaled through her nose.

"Avarn Dethril."

"Probably," Ilyan said.

Vaun glanced at him.

He corrected himself.

"The initials are consistent with the name. The directive is not here."

"Better."

He disliked that he had begun hearing her corrections before she made them.

Sael turned the page.

The preparation certificate had been signed by a senior Academy casualty clerk named **Meral Tovin** and countersigned by Neral Voss.

Tovin certified that the reconstructed packet contained all materials made available to the preparation team and that excluded materials were identified on the source schedule.

That was not the language of somebody secretly slipping pages out of a folder.

It was worse in a quieter way.

The omissions had been written down.

The certificate did not pretend the failed fragments were present.

It did not pretend the disputed memorandum had been included.

It openly stated that the casualty material had been replaced under a separation directive.

"Haleth received this certificate?" Thessa asked.

Sael checked the attachment notation.

"No."

Ilyan looked up.

"What?"

"The messenger manifest names the substitute packet and authority slip. Not the internal preparation certificate."

"So the office documented the omissions for itself and sent Haleth a packet without the document that explained them."

Vaun said, "That is what these records presently indicate."

Thessa's face had gone hard.

"That is a remarkable way to preserve honesty."

Nobody answered.

The third page was the correction sheet.

It had been added after Haleth refused the substitute packet.

Meral Tovin's hand was different there—faster, the strokes compressed.

**Correction: independent examiner declined technical closure on grounds that packet did not preserve rejected comparison material or contemporaneous disputed-substitution record. Preparation team had no custody authority over rejected comparison material. Procedural memorandum excluded under AD-4. Matter returned to Contingency.**

Below it, somebody else had written:

**Do not reconstruct again without Secretary review.**

The initials were **N.V.**

Neral.

Ilyan sat back.

"He knew why Haleth refused."

"Afterward," Vaun said.

"Afterward."

That mattered.

Neral had sent an incomplete packet believing, perhaps, that it was sufficient for a narrow technical answer. Haleth had told him it was not. The correction sheet preserved the reason.

And then the matter had gone back to Dethril.

Sael tapped the directive number.

"AD-4 is not incorporated by reference. Your order does not reach it."

Thessa stared at him.

"You cannot be serious."

"I am almost always serious in this room."

"It explains why the original casualty record was removed."

"Which makes it relevant. It does not make it covered by an order that names incorporated attachments."

Thessa opened her mouth.

Ilyan touched two fingers lightly to her wrist.

She looked at him.

He did not need to explain.

They had spent enough time around Vaun now.

Do it properly.

Thessa's annoyance did not disappear. It merely acquired discipline.

"Fine," she said. "Then we get another order."

Vaun's gaze moved between them.

"Yes."

Sael looked almost disappointed that nobody had provided him with an argument.

---

The original casualty correction was easier.

That should have been suspicious.

Instead it was merely depressing.

The Academy memorial office had preserved every version of the Silver Night casualty roll because families had challenged names, spellings, household affiliations, and in three cases whether the person listed had been present at all.

Eiravel's memorial clerks did not erase earlier rolls when a correction was issued.

They tied them together.

The bundle was thick enough to require both hands.

Ilyan, Thessa and Vaun sat at a long table beneath a silver disc of the Sundered Moon while an elderly memorial clerk named Ysra Pell laid the first four versions in order.

"Do not put tea on them," she said.

"We have no tea," Ilyan said.

"Then you are already ahead of the last magistrate."

The first roll had been assembled before dawn after the attack.

Names were grouped by location and source: infirmary, dormitories, west tower, east court, receiving rooms, missing students, missing staff.

Mara Venn appeared under **west-tower receiving staff — status unconfirmed**.

The second roll, six hours later, still listed her as unconfirmed.

The third roll changed the entry.

**Mara Venn — presumed dead, body unrecovered.**

A correction mark sat beside it.

Not Corren's.

Not Neral's.

The clerk had copied the authority source into the margin.

**Contingency casualty liaison — per AD-4 separation.**

Thessa leaned forward.

"There it is again."

Ysra Pell adjusted her spectacles.

"There what is?"

Vaun answered, "A directive we need to read."

The fourth roll changed Mara back.

**Mara Venn — missing; death not established.**

The authority line beneath it was Corren Valeric's office.

Ilyan stared.

The date was the morning after Silver Night.

The same morning Savel remembered Corren ordering that the west-tower bearer chain remain open.

There was a note attached to the correction.

Not in Corren's hand.

A casualty clerk had summarized the instruction:

**Protected bearer designator remains unresolved. Do not infer death from non-return of credential or absence from receiving station.**

Thessa sat back slowly.

"So Savel remembered correctly."

"On this point," Vaun said.

Ilyan looked at the sequence again.

Unconfirmed.

Presumed dead.

Missing.

Three administrative truths in less than a day.

Mara herself had presumably been only one thing throughout.

Alive somewhere, if Teren's later authentication chain was right.

"Who changed her to presumed dead?" Ilyan asked.

Ysra Pell turned the third roll toward herself.

"The authority source is Contingency casualty liaison. The person entering the correction was Academy memorial staff."

"Can we identify the liaison?"

"Not from this copy."

Vaun pointed to AD-4.

"But the correction says the change was pursuant to the separation directive."

"Yes."

Thessa's jaw tightened.

"Dethril separated the casualty question from the witness-continuity question, and somebody used that separation to presume her dead."

Vaun looked at her.

"That is a reasonable working interpretation. We still need the directive."

Thessa gave her a look.

"I know."

Ilyan almost smiled.

Then he noticed another notation.

The third roll had been certified outward to three offices before Corren's correction arrived.

One copy to the Crown casualty desk.

One to the Academy household office.

One to the **Dmitrigical Tribunal Contingency Secretariat**.

The false presumption had therefore existed long enough to travel.

Not long.

But long enough.

"Could that be why the reconstructed packet used the certified roll?" Ilyan asked.

Vaun followed his finger.

"If the reconstruction team received this certified version before the correction propagated, yes."

"And if they received it after?"

"Then we need to know why they used the older one."

Ysra Pell cleared her throat.

"You may not need to speculate."

She lifted the next sheet from the bundle.

It was not another casualty roll.

It was a transmission receipt.

The memorial office had sent Corren's correction to every office that received the presumed-dead version.

The Crown desk acknowledged it before noon.

The Academy household office acknowledged it thirteen minutes later.

Contingency acknowledged it at second bell the following morning.

The recipient stamp was Neral Voss's office.

Ilyan felt the shape of the problem settle.

"The reconstruction happened after that."

"Yes," Vaun said.

The substitute packet was prepared four days after Silver Night.

Neral's office had possessed the correction for nearly three days.

Yet C-17/P listed a certified casualty roll in place of the original provisional list.

Which certified roll?

The source schedule gave a date.

Ilyan checked it.

The third roll.

The one that called Mara dead.

Thessa swore softly.

Vaun did not rebuke her.

"Now," Vaun said, "we have a narrower question."

Ilyan nodded.

Not why the whole conspiracy existed.

Not whether Corren had been secretly trying to save everyone.

Not whether Dethril had masterminded Silver Night.

A smaller question.

Why had a reconstruction team, working from an office that had already acknowledged the corrected casualty status, used the superseded roll that presumed Mara dead?

---

They obtained the AD-4 order shortly after midday.

Vaun's supplemental application took eleven minutes to write and nearly an hour to move through the appropriate desk because the appropriate clerk had gone to lunch.

Thessa found this offensive on constitutional grounds.

Ilyan bought them road pies from a stall outside the Hall while they waited.

He handed hers over without asking which filling she wanted.

Mushroom, sharp cheese and onion.

Her favorite when she expected to spend the afternoon indoors.

She looked at it, then at him.

"You remembered."

"You have eaten the same one after every long Academy examination since second year."

"That is disturbingly observant."

"I am training for an investigative order."

"You were doing it before that."

He took a bite of his own pie to avoid answering.

Thessa smiled.

Her shoulder settled against his while they ate on the Hall steps.

Neither of them mentioned the oak.

They did not need to.

The fact of it sat comfortably between them, new enough to be exciting and old enough in its foundations not to require immediate definition.

When Vaun returned with the amended order, she looked at their shoulders, the two half-eaten pies and Ilyan's expression.

"Finished?"

"Yes," Ilyan said too quickly.

"With lunch," Thessa added.

Vaun continued up the steps.

"I was asking about lunch."

Thessa waited until the judge was several paces ahead before whispering, "I like her."

"That is because she is cruel."

"That is not the only reason."

---

AD-4 was shorter than expected.

Avarn Dethril had issued it at first bell the morning after Silver Night.

Its purpose was administrative separation during mass-casualty reconciliation.

**Casualty determination shall proceed from physical confirmation, medical witness, and ordinary presence records. Credential-continuity disputes shall not delay publication of casualty rolls except where a protected bearer remains affirmatively known alive.**

The next paragraph created the problem.

**Where protected witness-continuity status cannot be disclosed to casualty staff, the casualty office shall proceed without access to such protected status.**

Thessa read it twice.

"So the casualty office was ordered to decide whether Mara was dead while being forbidden from knowing the information most relevant to whether she was alive."

Sael folded his arms.

"That is one consequence."

"It is an idiotic consequence."

"Those are not mutually exclusive statements."

Ilyan kept reading.

The directive had a stated reason.

Dethril feared that protected witness identities would spread through hundreds of temporary casualty workers if continuity files were opened to the memorial process.

The concern was not imaginary.

Silver Night had produced dozens of emergency clerks, family representatives, healers, runners, priests and Academy staff moving through the same rooms.

If Mara's protected role was supposed to keep her alive, putting it on the casualty desk would have defeated the point.

The directive was therefore not obviously absurd.

It was merely capable of producing an absurd result.

And it had.

At the bottom, in Dethril's own hand, was an added instruction:

**If protected continuity later contradicts casualty disposition, correct the roll prospectively. Do not circulate protected basis.**

Ilyan stared at it.

"He anticipated corrections."

"Yes," Vaun said.

"Then why did he want Mara treated as a casualty problem?"

"Because he appears to have believed the two systems should operate separately."

"Even when one could make the other wrong."

Sael said, "Institutions do that every day. Usually with less blood around them."

The line was not funny.

Nobody laughed.

AD-4 did not order Mara declared dead.

It did not order the old casualty roll used in the reconstructed packet.

It did not mention Haleth.

It did, however, create the wall that allowed a casualty office to be wrong without knowing why, and it instructed later corrections not to disclose the protected reason.

Someone had then used the wrong side of that wall after the correction had already reached Contingency.

That was no longer Dethril's directive acting automatically.

That was a choice made later.

"We need Meral Tovin," Ilyan said.

Sael checked the certificate.

"Dead. Six years."

"Of course."

"Her preparation log may survive."

Vaun shook her head.

"Later. First Dethril. He is living, and Savel places him above Neral when the substitute was ordered."

Thessa looked at the directive.

"Do we know where north is yet?"

Sael did.

Retirement had taken Avarn Dethril to **Larkfen**, a small upland town two days north by ordinary coach and considerably less by Crown post if a judge was willing to make several people unhappy.

Vaun was.

There was already a courier waiting when they left the restricted room.

Her request asked Dethril to appear voluntarily before Truth Beyond the Veil or accept examination under local oath in Larkfen.

Ilyan expected the answer the next day.

It arrived before supper.

Dethril's handwriting was broad and shaky with age but perfectly legible.

He would answer questions.

He would not travel south.

And he had one condition.

Vaun read the last line aloud.

"If the inquiry concerns the west-tower continuity failure, bring Teren Valeric. He knows why I refused to sign the second closure order."

Ilyan went very still.

Thessa's fingers found his beneath the edge of the table.

Vaun folded the letter once.

"Tomorrow," she said, "we ask your uncle whether he is willing to travel north."

Ilyan looked at Dethril's signature.

For once, the next question had supplied its own witness.
