# Chapter Six J — Six Before Names

By the time Ilyan and Thessa reached the Hall again, the rain had become serious.

It came down in silver cords beyond the arcade, flattened the flower sellers' awnings, and sent everyone with any claim to dignity crowding beneath stone eaves with everyone who had none. Thessa's hair had escaped whatever arrangement it had begun the day in. Ilyan's coat was wet through at the shoulders. Neither had remembered to eat.

Examiner Sael took one look at them and pushed a plate across his desk.

There were two cold meat pies on it.

Ilyan stared.

"Is this an examination?"

"Yes."

"Of what?"

"Whether either of you possesses the judgment required to eat before becoming useless."

Thessa took a pie.

"I like this order."

Sael looked at her. "You are not joining it."

"I like it from a safe distance."

Ilyan sat.

The pie was cold, peppery, and better than anything had a right to be after the afternoon he had just had.

Judge Vaun returned twenty minutes later with rain on her sleeves and Teren's formal statement in a sealed folder beneath her arm.

She did not summarize the interview. She put the folder into Serrat's custody, signed the transfer line, and asked Sael for the old request register.

Sael had already sent for it.

That was why Ilyan trusted him more than he enjoyed being examined by him.

The register arrived with a junior archivist and a wheeled stand because it was large enough to injure someone if dropped. Its leather had once been green. Time had negotiated that down to an uncertain brown.

The archivist opened it to the week before the Emergency Substitution Review.

Teren had remembered correctly.

The request for six credentials had been entered two days before the Academy's final temporary staffing list named the six people who would receive them.

Ilyan read the line once.

Then he read the line above it and the line below it, because he had learned what happened when a single interesting sentence was allowed to become the whole world.

The entries around it were ordinary emergency work. Replacement archive passes after a flooded records room. Three temporary mine-inspection seals after a survey party lost its document chest in a river crossing. A set of hospital transport authorities after a fever ward had been isolated.

Then:

**Emergency Substitution Review. Six bearer-neutral witness-access credentials. West-tower restricted receiving continuity. Bearer designations to follow upon Academy staffing certification.**

The request had entered through Joint Order Liaison — Dmitrigical Review.

The administrative countersignature was the same one Jalen and the second examiner had already assessed as highly consistent with Corren Valeric.

The technical referral had gone to Credential Integrity.

And in the margin, in a clerk's smaller hand, was a notation that had not appeared on the later summary extract.

**Quantity confirmed before bearer assignment. Do not reduce upon staffing substitution.**

Thessa leaned closer.

"That sounds less accidental."

Vaun looked at her.

"It sounds intentional."

"Yes."

"Those aren't the same thing."

Thessa nodded.

Ilyan appreciated that nobody in the room felt obliged to explain why.

The archivist turned the page.

A loose slip had been stitched into the binding by one corner. It was not the request itself. It was a routing query from the clerk who had entered it.

**Bearer count not yet certified. Confirm whether six is provisional maximum or required issue quantity.**

The answer had come back the same afternoon.

**Required issue quantity. Bearer names may vary. Quantity does not.**

No personal name appeared beneath the answer.

Only the office mark of the Contingency Secretariat.

Ilyan sat back.

For a moment he could hear only the rain.

"My father knew," he said.

Vaun's gaze moved to him.

"He countersigned the expenditure."

"He told Teren to ask why six had been requested before the names existed."

"Yes."

"So he knew the sequence mattered."

"Yes."

That was enough. She did not rescue him from the next thought and did not put it into his mouth.

Ilyan looked again at the clerk's question.

Six was not the number produced by the final staffing list.

The final staffing list had been made to satisfy a request that already required six.

Why remained unanswered.

Sael rested one hand on the edge of the stand.

"What would make a quantity fixed while bearers remained interchangeable?"

Thessa said, "Six jobs."

"Perhaps."

"Six doors."

"Perhaps."

Ilyan looked at the west-tower notation.

"Six things that needed credentials whether the same six people showed up or not."

Sael nodded once.

"Better. Still broad."

Vaun pointed to the phrase *witness-access credentials*.

"Find the operational attachment."

The archivist grimaced.

"If it survived."

"If it survived."

The search took an hour and involved three cabinets, two obsolete index systems, and one elderly cross-reference book whose compiler had apparently believed alphabetical order was a decadent modern affectation.

Thessa discovered this last fact first.

"I would like permission to fight the dead clerk."

"Denied," Vaun said.

"On jurisdictional grounds?"

"Practical ones."

The archivist made a choking sound that might have been a laugh.

Ilyan found the attachment because someone eighteen years ago had filed a duplicate under **West Tower — Receiving Continuity** instead of **Emergency Substitution — Witness Access**.

It was four pages.

The first two were dull enough to make him briefly hopeful.

They described a contingency staffing problem created by graduation-week security. Regular Academy receiving personnel had been pulled into ceremony logistics, baggage inspection, guest routing and restricted dormitory closure. The west tower still needed to receive sealed witness materials and admit authorized temporary staff.

The third page listed six functional positions.

Not people.

Positions.

**One restricted receiving clerk.**

**One records porter.**

**One lock specialist.**

**One ward inspector.**

**One witness examiner.**

**One mobile continuity bearer.**

Thessa tapped the last line.

"What's that?"

The archivist did not know.

Sael did.

"A person authorized to carry continuity authority between stations when a normal chain is interrupted. Rare. Usually disaster work."

"What could they carry?"

"Authority. Sometimes sealed material. Sometimes a witness instruction. Depends on the instrument."

Ilyan looked back at the designator list from SV-12 in his memory.

W-1 records porter.

W-2 locksmith.

W-3 receiving clerk.

W-4 ward inspector.

W-6 witness examiner.

W-5 Mara Venn.

Mara had not merely filled a vacant receiving shift.

The temporary duty sheet had put her at third-floor receiving, yes.

But the original functional plan contained one position not accounted for by the ordinary job labels.

Mobile continuity bearer.

"Which designator was that?" he asked.

Vaun had already reached the same question.

"The attachment doesn't assign designators."

"The staffing certification might."

It did not.

The final Academy staffing list named six people and their temporary work assignments, but the credential office had translated those assignments into W-1 through W-6 separately. The surviving certification copy contained no functional-position column.

Ilyan felt irritation rising and welcomed it. Irritation was easier to use than grief.

"So we still don't know whether W-5 was the mobile bearer."

"Correct," Vaun said.

Thessa folded her arms.

"But one of the six was."

"Yes."

The fourth page was an approval sheet.

Most of it duplicated what they already knew.

Then Ilyan saw the distribution line.

The completed credential set was to be released in two groups.

Five credentials to Academy staffing control.

One credential to **Contingency witness continuity custody** for hand delivery to its bearer.

Nobody spoke.

The rain struck the windows hard enough to rattle them.

Ilyan read it again.

Five together.

One separately.

"That one should have its own receipt," Vaun said.

The archivist was already writing down the routing number.

---

The separate receipt survived.

It had been filed in a narrow book of Contingency hand-carries because bureaucracies sometimes preserved terrible things simply by being too stubborn to throw away paper.

The credential was W-5.

The recipient signature was Mara Venn's.

Ilyan did not let himself feel relief at that. They already knew signatures could be copied, inherited defects reproduced, credentials made to look legitimate.

Vaun sent for the old signature exemplars from Mara's payroll and receiving records.

They waited.

Thessa found tea.

The archivist found bread and a small crock of soft cheese.

Somewhere between the second cup and the arrival of the exemplars, Ilyan realized the day had become evening.

Bloomtide light lingered late, but the Hall lamps had been lit.

The hand-carry receipt was examined by two document clerks separately.

Both found Mara's signature consistent with the ordinary exemplars available to them. Neither would certify authorship beyond that.

Vaun accepted the limit.

More useful was the witness line beneath the signature.

**Authentication phrase received and matched.**

The phrase itself was not written there.

A reference number was.

Ilyan stopped breathing for half a second.

"Can we follow that?"

The archivist checked the reference.

"Protected witness authentication ledger. Different custody."

Serrat, from the doorway, said, "Mine."

Nobody had heard him arrive.

Thessa looked at him.

"Do you practice that?"

"No."

"Pity. It would be impressive if deliberate."

Serrat ignored her and read Vaun's inspection authority.

The existing order covered the authentication reference but not any location or reassignment destination attached to it.

That mattered.

Vaun requested only the challenge phrase and its date of registration.

Serrat approved that much.

The protected ledger did not come into the room. An examiner brought a certified extract instead.

The challenge phrase registered to Mara Venn was:

**Ask for the blue pears before frost.**

Ilyan closed his eyes.

His father had known it.

The phrase had been registered three days before Silver Night, at the same time Mara entered protected witness handling for the emergency assignment. Access to the phrase was limited to the bearer, the receiving examiner, and Contingency witness-continuity authority.

Teren's original post-incident review file had not contained it.

That corroborated one important part of his story.

Corren had given Teren information Teren could not have obtained from his own review compartment.

It did not explain how Corren obtained it.

The countersignature already placed him inside the administrative chain. The authentication phrase placed his knowledge closer to witness handling.

Closer was not the same as complete.

Ilyan had become very tired of sentences like that.

He had also become grateful for them.

They were the difference between an investigation and a family legend.

"Who was the receiving examiner?" he asked.

Serrat checked the extract.

"Pel Ardis."

Thessa exhaled.

"Of course."

"That isn't evidence of anything except that Ardis did his assigned work," Vaun said.

"I know. I'm allowed to be annoyed by the shape of it."

Vaun's mouth almost moved.

Almost.

The more interesting line came beneath Ardis's name.

**Continuity authority acknowledgment: C.V.**

Not a signature.

Initials.

No surname.

Ilyan stared at them.

He did not say his father's name.

He did not need to.

Vaun requested the authority roster for that date.

Serrat did not have it in the same room.

"Tomorrow," he said.

Ilyan looked at him.

"Can it be preserved tonight?"

"Already is."

That was the answer he wanted.

---

Damaris was still at the River Ledger when the Hall finally released them.

Not investigating Silver Night.

Working.

Her committee had spent the afternoon discovering that the duplicated dredging-barge invoice was not, in fact, a sophisticated fraud. Two district offices had each paid a copy because one clerk had changed the work-order number after a flood inspection and another had not.

The money was recoverable.

The embarrassment was not.

Rhyl had arrived with supper and no documents.

He had apparently taken his refusal to become an unofficial courier as a personal discipline.

Damaris rewarded this by stealing half his roasted onions.

When Ilyan and Thessa entered the committee anteroom, Rhyl was leaning against the window with his sleeves rolled up and Damaris was sitting sideways in a chair, one shoe off beneath the table, reading a corrected account sheet.

She looked up.

Whatever she saw in Ilyan's face made her put the sheet down.

"Bad?"

"Complicated."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the one I have."

She studied him for another second and accepted it.

Rhyl handed him a wrapped piece of flatbread.

Ilyan took it.

"Does everyone think I don't eat?"

"Today?" Rhyl asked.

"Apparently."

Thessa sat on the edge of Damaris's desk.

"He failed an examination on the subject."

"I passed."

"Under supervision."

Damaris's mouth curved.

It was the first thing all evening that made Ilyan feel twenty-three instead of ancient.

He told them only what Vaun had cleared for the Seven.

Six had been fixed before six names existed.

The original plan described six functions, including one mobile continuity bearer.

Five credentials went through Academy staffing control.

W-5 went separately to Mara.

Mara authenticated receipt with the blue-pears phrase.

The phrase Teren said Corren later used was real and had not been in Teren's review file.

He did not tell them the protected-ledger access structure beyond what Vaun had authorized.

Damaris listened with her fingers resting against Rhyl's wrist.

"So your father knew something he shouldn't have known from Teren's papers."

"Yes."

"But might have known through his own office."

"Yes."

Rhyl looked at Ilyan.

"And Mara actually received W-5?"

"Her signature is consistent. The authentication was accepted."

"Which still doesn't prove she used it at the tower."

"No."

Nobody sighed at the answer.

That, more than anything, told Ilyan how much the last week had changed them.

They had learned to live with a fact ending before they wanted it to.

Damaris put her shoe back on.

"What happens tomorrow?"

"The authority roster. Then the request-entry chronology. Vaun wants to know who had the power to fix six before the staffing list existed."

"And Mara?"

Ilyan looked toward the rain-black windows.

"We verify the reassignment chain without exposing where it ends."

Damaris nodded.

She did not ask whether he wanted to find Mara.

He was grateful for that too.

Rhyl unwrapped another piece of flatbread and handed it to Thessa.

"Lyrion says we're all becoming impossible to invite anywhere."

"Lyrion says that as though it will stop him," Thessa said.

"It will not."

Damaris leaned back against Rhyl's arm.

"Mireth and Caelen?"

"Dinner with her family," Rhyl said. "Apparently Caelen has been informed that touching Mireth under the table is not subtle simply because the tablecloth is long."

Thessa brightened.

"By whom?"

"Her mother."

Ilyan laughed before he could stop himself.

The sound hurt less this time.

For a few minutes they ate and complained about work.

Damaris described the two district clerks who had each been absolutely certain the other office was responsible for the duplicate payment. Rhyl described a Red Hart quartermaster who had requisitioned six waterproof saddle covers and received six waterproof covers for chairs. Thessa described a Warden lieutenant who believed maps became more accurate if he stabbed them with a finger hard enough.

Nobody mentioned Silver Night.

Then Ilyan looked at his friends and remembered the line in the old attachment.

Six functions.

Bearers to follow.

He had spent the afternoon thinking about six as a number chosen before six people.

Now, absurdly, he was sitting with three of the six people who remained from a graduating class that had almost ceased to exist.

He did not say that either.

Some thoughts did not improve by being given to a room.

---

Vaun's runner found him before he left the Ledger.

The note was short.

The original request-entry clerk's routing packet had been located in an off-site judicial store and placed under preservation seal for morning review.

One surviving cover sheet carried a handwritten instruction not present in the central register.

Vaun did not reproduce the whole line in an unsecured note.

She reproduced four words.

**Selection follows credential capacity.**

Ilyan read them twice.

Thessa read them over his shoulder because she had never respected the fictional privacy of a note held openly at chest height.

"That's worse," she said.

"Maybe."

"You hate that word now."

"Deeply."

He folded the note.

The rain had stopped.

Across the canal, lamps trembled in the black water. Bloomtide flowers sagged under the weight of the storm and would probably stand again by morning.

Tomorrow they would read the routing packet.

Tomorrow they might learn whether *selection follows credential capacity* meant nothing more sinister than an administrator filling six predetermined jobs.

Or whether someone had built six usable identities first and chosen the people afterward because the people were the disposable part.

For tonight, the difference remained exactly where it belonged.

Unproven.
