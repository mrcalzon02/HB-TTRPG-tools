# Chapter Twenty-Eight — The Acknowledging Hand

The closing procedure was four pages long.

Ilyan had expected something larger.

After weeks of paired authorities, sealed notices, protected classes, competing duties and records that seemed to reproduce whenever he learned a new noun, four pages felt almost insulting.

Judge Vaun set them beside his breakfast.

He looked at the pages, then at the eggs.

"You've begun timing these."

"Timing what?"

"Giving me unpleasant documents while I'm eating."

"You are less inclined to dramatics with bread in your mouth."

"I have never been dramatic in this room."

Vaun buttered her toast. Ilyan looked toward Neral. The clerk became fascinated by the kettle.

"Traitor."

"Not an evidentiary category," Vaun said.

Ilyan took the pages.

The procedure governed closure of controlled non-person custody under the civic half of JEC-R/6. It said almost nothing about what could be held. Records, sealed testimony, instruments, property and other protected materials all entered the same procedural shell once the receiving authority accepted them.

Closure began with the receiver.

The receiving officer determined that the controlled obligation could end under the terms governing that custody. The officer prepared a sealed closure notice. If no initiating-side reservation arrived within the prescribed interval, the receiving record closed and the appropriate civic commissioning office acknowledged that the receiver had acted within its authority.

Ilyan read the paragraph twice.

"That's not what I thought acknowledgment meant."

Vaun lifted her cup. "What did you think it meant?"

"That the civic office had taken custody at the end. Or approved a transfer."

"Why?"

He considered the question.

"Because the account showed an outside acknowledgment after the controlled custody ended."

"That tells you what?"

"That I filled in the verb."

"Yes."

He read on.

The acknowledgment did not certify the identity of whatever had been held. It did not certify destination, disposition, ownership or later possession. It certified that the receiving commission's closure had been reviewed where review was required and that the closure could stand.

There were three ordinary paths: expiration under the original terms, completion of the protected purpose, or a receiving-side determination that continued custody was no longer lawful or necessary under the governing protection.

A fourth path existed when the initiating side objected.

The objection was called a scope reservation.

It did not automatically prevent closure. It required the receiving officer to state, under seal, the authority relied upon for ending custody. The civic commissioning office then reviewed only that authority. If the dispute implicated the receiver's independent-protection reservation, the civic reviewer could uphold the receiver without disclosing protected particulars to the initiating side.

If the dispute could not be resolved there, it went to sealed judicial review.

A handwritten notation at the bottom cross-referenced a register class.

Not a case number. Not a bearer number. A class for closure disputes.

Ilyan slid the paper toward Vaun.

"This?"

"That is a question."

"Can I ask it?"

"You just did."

"Can I ask someone useful?"

Neral coughed into his hand. Vaun smiled into her tea.

The civic register office occupied the back half of a building that had once been a merchant hall and still seemed faintly surprised by government. Its front chamber retained carved vines around the windows, a ceiling painted with ships and silver fish, and a broad stair designed for people who wanted to be seen ascending it. The bureaucracy had answered by putting shelves everywhere.

Ilyan sat beneath a painted dolphin and waited while a registrar checked Vaun's authority. He had learned not to resent waiting. Waiting meant someone was checking.

The registrar returned with a woman whose grey hair was pinned through with two black sticks.

"Master Valeric."

"Ilyan is fine."

"It is also shorter."

She sat opposite him.

"Registrar Halen. Judge Vaun's order permits a bounded answer concerning the closure-dispute class associated with commission 264-B/17."

"I understand."

"Did that post-Silver-Night closure enter the dispute class?"

"Yes."

The answer landed with less force than he expected. The mechanism had been built for disagreement. Finding disagreement no longer felt like discovering sabotage. It felt like finding a door had once been used.

"Which branch?"

"Initiating-side scope reservation."

"Was the closure stopped?"

"No."

"Was it sent to sealed judicial review?"

"No."

"Then the civic reviewer upheld the receiving officer?"

"Yes."

Ilyan looked down at his notes. Somewhere behind the partition, paper slid against paper.

"Did the reviewer record a defect in the receiver's authority?"

"No."

"Did the reviewer state a basis?"

"Partially. The review cites the commission's ordinary closure authority and the changed-interest contingency in the formation instrument."

Ilyan did not write immediately.

The previous day's papers had shown that the initiating office anticipated a future in which an authority that had properly invoked protection might later become conflicted, pressured, displaced or otherwise interested in a way that made obedience dangerous.

Now that clause had appeared in an actual closure dispute after Silver Night.

He wrote the phrase carefully.
