# Chapter Eleven — The Second Signature

The selector receipt had survived because somebody had been afraid of keys.

Not the black key in particular. Keys generally.

The old Joint Emergency Coordination rules required a separate custody receipt for any object capable of opening a protected compartment without also identifying what lay inside it. The rule had been written for cabinets, witness rooms, sealed dispatch boxes and the little iron archive cages used by Crown couriers. It had also covered Teren's black routing selector because the selector could move protected material through a continuity chain even after the ordinary receiving route failed.

Ilyan learned this from a regulation book thick enough to stop a door.

He had been reading it for forty minutes when Thessa came into the Hall carrying breakfast.

She put a paper parcel on top of the page.

"No."

Ilyan looked at the parcel.

"Good morning."

"You have been here since before sunrise."

"How do you know?"

"Because I came by your rooms before sunrise."

"Why?"

"To see whether you were there."

"That is circular."

"Eat the pie."

He opened the parcel.

Mushroom, onion and sharp white cheese.

"You bought my cooperation."

"Cheaply."

She sat on the edge of the table while he ate. Her Warden coat was still damp at the shoulders from a fine Bloomtide rain, and there was mud drying in a pale line above one boot. She had brought the formal records request her captain wanted. Judge Vaun had signed it the previous evening. The request gave Thessa a Hall file number, a bounded purpose and permission to retrieve historical movement records when Vaun specifically asked for them.

Nothing secret about it.

Nothing heroic either.

It meant that if the Western Wardens were later asked why one of their officers had spent work time helping an eighteen-year-old Hall inquiry, there was an answer better than because her lover asked nicely.

Thessa watched him reach for the second half of the pie.

"I like the official answer better."

"I had not said anything."

"Your face did."

"Mireth says I have a face."

"You have several. That one was guilty bureaucracy."

He smiled around the pastry.

She touched two fingers to the corner of his mouth, removed a crumb, and ate it herself.

Ilyan forgot the regulation book for a moment.

Thessa noticed.

Of course she did.

Her mouth curved.

"Work."

"Cruel."

"Tonight."

That was worse.

She kissed his temple and went to deliver the request to Sael.

Ilyan returned to the page with considerably less concentration than before.

---

The receipt came up from Crown storage at midmorning.

It arrived in a narrow grey case with two clerks, one Crown archivist and a custody officer who treated the case as though it contained a live wasp.

Teren had arrived early enough to be wearing matching boots.

Ilyan considered this progress.

Damaris came with Rhyl because the River Ledger had a noon committee sitting two streets away and because, as she put it, she had become professionally offended by the number of different accounting systems Silver Night had managed to involve.

Rhyl had no institutional excuse at all.

"I was invited," he said.

"By whom?" Ilyan asked.

"Damaris."

Damaris did not look up from the Crown custody cover.

"He carries things."

Rhyl leaned against her shoulder.

"Romance."

"You knew what this was."

"I did."

Judge Vaun broke the case seals.

The receipt inside was one sheet folded around a thinner strip of verification paper.

Ilyan had expected Corren's name.

It was not there.

The first signature was Teren's.

Not the small custody countermark from Dethril's retained papers. His full hand.

**Teren Valeric, outgoing Joint Order liaison chair. Temporary selector authority surrendered upon termination of appointment.**

Beneath it was a time.

Second bell after noon.

Two days after Silver Night.

Teren sat very still.

"I signed that."

Vaun looked at him.

"Do you remember signing it?"

"No."

Nobody filled the silence for him.

The second line named the receiving office rather than a person.

**Received for continuity custody: Crown Emergency Secretariat, protected-routing desk.**

Then a clerk's authentication mark.

Then a second signature.

**Neral Voss, deputy receiving secretary.**

Ilyan read it twice.

Damaris said, "Not Corren."

"No."

Teren leaned over the page.

"Neral received it."

"According to this," Vaun said.

The verification strip explained the handoff.

The black selector had been surrendered at the Hall, sealed in a numbered leather sleeve and sent by bonded runner to the Crown Emergency Secretariat. The runner had carried no witness name and no protected destination. Teren's authority ended when he signed the surrender line. Neral's office became responsible for the physical selector.

Possession had moved cleanly.

For approximately four hours.

The bottom third of the receipt contained another entry.

**Released onward under continuity necessity.**

The authorization line was signed by Neral.

The recipient line carried two marks.

One was a compact set of initials Ilyan knew immediately.

**C.V.**

The other was not a name.

It was a second custody authentication from the protected-routing desk.

Teren breathed out through his nose.

"There he is."

Ilyan did not answer.

His father had received the selector lawfully.

At least the paper said he had.

That distinction mattered now.

It had taken him several weeks to learn how much could live inside the distance between those sentences.

Vaun read the verification strip before she allowed anyone to touch the conclusion.

"The recipient was authenticated how?"

The Crown archivist pointed to a tiny notation beside C.V.

"Paired custody phrase and personal seal impression."

"Does the impression survive?"

"On the sleeve record. Not here."

"And the phrase?"

"Destroyed at closure under the old rule. Only successful match is recorded."

Damaris folded her arms.

"So somebody presented Corren's seal and knew his custody phrase."

The archivist nodded.

"Yes."

"That is not the same as proving Corren personally stood at the desk."

Ilyan looked at her.

She met his eyes without apology.

"You know I'm right."

"I do."

Rhyl glanced between them.

"Could the seal be delegated?"

"Not that one," the archivist said. "Personal emergency seals were not supposed to leave the officer's possession."

"Supposed to?"

The archivist gave him a tired look.

"I archive government records. I have learned to fear that word."

Teren almost smiled.

Vaun tapped the second mark.

"What did the protected-routing desk authenticate?"

"That the recipient was entitled to take physical custody under the release authority. It did not authenticate why the release was necessary."

"Where is the necessity instrument?"

The archivist turned the receipt over.

A reference had been written along the lower edge.

**PRD-264-B4-19.**

Another file.

Of course.

Ilyan felt the old impulse to be angry at paper and let it pass.

The reference was useful.

That was enough.

---

The necessity instrument was not in the same building.

It was held in the Crown protected-routing archive beneath the east administrative wing, which meant Judge Vaun could request a certified extract but could not simply send Sael downstairs.

The request went out before lunch.

Then everyone had jobs.

That fact continued to irritate investigations.

Damaris went to her committee sitting. Rhyl walked with her as far as the river offices before continuing to a Red Hart provisioning review. Thessa had already gone west. Mireth was in the Green Covenant court. Caelen had Crown duty. Lyrion was attending a diplomatic luncheon whose menu, according to his note, contained seven courses and no edible quantities of food.

Ilyan had three oath disputes and a petition involving a cooper who had sworn never to sell barrels to his brother and had subsequently discovered his brother had purchased half the local brewery.

Teren stayed at the Hall.

Not because anyone required him to.

Because he did not want to go home.

Ilyan found him after midday sitting in the small interior courtyard beneath a pear tree just beginning to set fruit.

Teren had taken off his coat. His sleeves were rolled to the elbows. He held a cup of coffee he had apparently forgotten to drink.

Ilyan sat beside him.

For a while neither spoke.

The courtyard fountain clicked softly where water fell over a chipped stone lip.

Teren finally said, "I remember the desk."

Ilyan turned.

"Which desk?"

"Not Neral's. The Hall receiving desk."

"When you surrendered the selector?"

"Maybe."

Ilyan waited.

Teren stared into the coffee.

"Black stone top. Brass edge. Someone had spilled wax in the left corner and scraped most of it away. There was a clerk with a bandage around two fingers."

"Do you remember signing?"

"No."

"Do you remember the selector?"

"I remember putting something into a leather sleeve."

Ilyan's pulse quickened.

"Black?"

"I don't know."

"Small?"

"I don't know."

Teren looked at him sharply.

"Don't help me."

Ilyan stopped.

"Right."

"I know you mean well."

"That has never prevented me being annoying."

"True."

Teren rubbed his thumb over the cup handle.

"I remember being angry."

"At whom?"

"I don't know. I remember the feeling. I remember thinking I had done what I was required to do and that somebody else could now explain themselves."

Ilyan let the sentence sit where it landed.

It might have belonged to the selector.

It might have belonged to something else that day.

Teren knew that too.

"Tell Enara," Ilyan said.

"I will."

"Exactly that much."

Teren gave him a sideways look.

"You have become tiresome."

"Good teachers."

"Sael?"

"Mostly you."

That silenced him.

Teren looked away toward the pear tree.

Ilyan could see the effort it took not to make a joke.

He loved him for trying.

That did not make the receipt disappear.

It did not make Corren innocent.

It did not make Teren's missing years less frightening.

Love had stopped being an answer to any of those things.

It remained a reason to stay on the bench.

---

Damaris returned before the Crown archive answered.

She had changed from her committee coat into a lighter river-blue jacket and looked annoyed enough that Rhyl, who came in behind her, was carrying two pastries and maintaining a prudent distance.

"Bad meeting?" Ilyan asked.

"Three districts discovered the same dredging appropriation and each believes the money belongs exclusively to them."

"Does it?"

"No."

"That seems promising."

"It is not."

Rhyl offered her a pastry.

She took it.

He moved closer.

The sequence appeared practiced.

Damaris looked at the selector receipt again while she ate.

"You are going to dislike this."

Ilyan sat opposite her.

"Probably."

"The receipt helps your father."

Teren, at the next table, looked up.

Ilyan said, "In one narrow respect."

"Yes. In one narrow respect. We previously knew he had the selector and later returned it. That looked like unexplained possession. Now we know a Crown office released it to someone authenticated as him under a continuity-necessity authority."

"We don't know the necessity."

"Correct."

"We don't know what he did with it."

"Correct."

"We know he returned it two days later."

"Also correct."

Ilyan leaned back.

"You are enjoying this."

"A little."

"Why?"

"Because you are getting better at being disagreed with."

Rhyl bit into his own pastry to hide a smile.

Ilyan pointed at him.

"Coward."

"Completely."

Damaris brushed sugar from her fingers.

"I am not saying Corren was protecting anyone. I am saying lawful transfer changes the question. If he stole the selector, possession itself is suspicious. If it was released to him under valid authority, then possession is expected and use becomes the important question."

Ilyan knew she was right.

He also knew something in him had wanted the black key to be simpler.

A secret object in his father's hand was emotionally satisfying evidence.

A properly receipted object was not.

"Fine," he said.

Damaris smiled.

"That hurt."

"Deeply."

Teren came over and took the second pastry from Rhyl's hand.

Rhyl stared at him.

"I brought that for myself."

"You are young. You will recover."

"This family is criminal."

Ilyan looked at Teren.

"Careful."

For half a second the joke threatened to become something else.

Then Teren broke the pastry in half and gave part back.

"Allegedly criminal."

Rhyl accepted the correction.

The room moved on.

---

The Crown response arrived at fourth bell.

The protected-routing archive did not refuse Vaun's request.

It narrowed it.

The necessity instrument could be disclosed in part because the emergency office no longer existed and because the selector itself had long since been returned. Protected witness identity, destination and route geometry would remain sealed.

The authority basis could be released.

So could the names of officers who had exercised custody.

There was one complication.

The instrument had been created as a personal emergency custody delegation.

Under the archival rule still governing that class of record, a living person whose personal seal authenticated the delegation had to be notified before a court opened the retained explanatory statement, unless the judge found a present danger in giving notice.

Vaun read that paragraph twice.

"Is there a present danger?" Ilyan asked.

"Not established."

He knew the answer before she gave it.

"So you notify him."

"Yes."

Teren had gone very quiet.

Damaris asked, "Corren is still alive?"

Ilyan nodded.

The room seemed to alter around that fact, though nothing in it moved.

His father had not been a dead signature for Ilyan.

He had been a living absence.

There were easier things to investigate than a man who could answer back.

Vaun continued reading.

The unsealed portion of the necessity instrument established why the selector left Neral's desk.

Pel Ardis was dead.

Teren's temporary chair appointment had ended.

The ordinary west-tower receiving chain remained compromised and the credential discrepancy was still open.

The black selector therefore required temporary custody by an officer whose authority crossed Contingency continuity and Joint Emergency Coordination liaison functions until either the protected route closed or a successor receiving authority was appointed.

At the relevant hour, the instrument identified one eligible officer.

**Corren Valeric.**

Ilyan read the line without speaking.

There was no claim that Corren had created the emergency.

No claim that he had used the selector.

No claim that he had acted benevolently.

Only that the temporary system, after Ardis's death and Teren's departure, had routed the object to him because he held the surviving authority package.

Damaris was right.

Possession was no longer the mystery.

What happened during the two days before Corren returned it was.

Vaun turned to the notification form.

"I will send this through ordinary judicial service."

Ilyan looked at her.

"Today?"

"Yes."

"Can I see the notice?"

She slid it across the table.

It was offensively calm.

It informed Corren Valeric that the Hall of Veils, under judicial review authority, intended to open the explanatory portion of an archived emergency custody instrument bearing his authenticated seal. It gave him the accession number. It gave him two days to object, appear, submit a statement, waive notice or do nothing.

It did not accuse him of Silver Night.

It did not mention Ilyan.

It did not mention Mara or any other protected person.

Ilyan read it again.

"He will know why."

Teren said, "Probably."

"He may know I'm involved."

"Probably."

"He may come here."

Vaun answered that one.

"Yes."

Ilyan felt his stomach tighten.

He had spent weeks following dead paperwork because dead paperwork could not look at him and say son.

Damaris's hand settled briefly over his wrist.

Not romantic.

Not secret.

A friend's hand.

Rhyl stood behind her with one palm against the back of her chair.

Teren watched Ilyan from across the table.

"You do not have to be present if he comes."

Ilyan looked at him.

"Would you be?"

Teren considered the question rather than answering as his uncle.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I want to know what I did. Because I want to know what he did. Because I am tired of discovering my own life in accession numbers."

Ilyan nodded.

"Then I'll be here."

Vaun did not praise him for courage.

She signed the notice.

---

Thessa came to his rooms after dark.

The rain had strengthened. She arrived with wet hair, cold hands and the expression of a woman who had spent the last hour arguing with a road engineer about drainage.

Ilyan opened the door and she walked straight into his arms.

He held her.

For several breaths that was all either of them did.

Then she pulled back enough to look at him.

"What happened?"

"How do you know something happened?"

"I have met you."

He let her in.

She kicked off her wet boots by the door and stole his dry socks without asking. He told her about the receipt while she sat on the edge of his bed pulling them on.

Teren had surrendered the selector.

Neral had received it.

The Crown desk had released it onward.

Corren had been the only eligible surviving officer under the continuity authority.

And now Vaun had sent him notice.

Thessa finished with the socks and looked up.

"So your father may come to the Hall."

"Yes."

"How do you feel?"

"Badly."

"Specific."

"I am learning from judges."

She stood.

"Do you want me there?"

He almost said yes immediately.

Then he thought about her captain, her file number, the Warden records, the way private support became institutional presence the moment uniforms entered rooms.

"As Thessa?"

She understood.

"As Thessa."

"Yes."

"Then I will come as Thessa."

No Warden coat unless work required it.

No official request.

No invented reason to turn his family confrontation into Western Warden business.

Ilyan had not realized until that moment how much he needed the distinction.

He touched her face.

Her cheek was still cool from the rain.

"Thank you."

"You can thank me properly."

"Extortion."

"Private-sector extortion. No file number."

He kissed her.

She laughed against his mouth, then stopped laughing when he drew her closer.

The day did not disappear.

Corren's notice did not disappear.

Teren's missing memory did not disappear.

They remained where they were, waiting for morning and whatever followed it.

Thessa's hands slid beneath his coat and settled warm against his back.

For a little while Ilyan allowed himself to be a man in his own rooms with a woman he wanted, rather than a son preparing to meet the father whose signatures had begun to surface inside the worst week of his life.

Later, with the rain tapping against the shutters and Thessa's head resting beneath his chin, he found himself thinking about the receipt again.

Not C.V.

The first signature.

Teren's.

His uncle had surrendered the selector exactly when his authority ended.

Corren had received it because the emergency machinery had nowhere else lawful to put it.

Then, two days later, he had returned it.

The next record would not tell Ilyan whether his father had been a monster.

He already knew enough to reject anything that simple.

It might tell him what Corren had done while the key was in his hand.

And for the first time since the investigation began, Corren would know they were looking.
