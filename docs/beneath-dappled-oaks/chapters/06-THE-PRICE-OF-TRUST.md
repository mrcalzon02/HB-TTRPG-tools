# Chapter Six — The Price of Trust

The Seven met after dark in a room meant for twelve.

That was the first thing Damaris noticed when she came in with the certified River Ledger copies under her arm. The private dining room at Reedglass had been laid with twelve places before someone—probably Talia, because Talia noticed such things—had quietly removed five settings and pushed the remaining chairs closer together.

The result looked less formal than the house intended and more formal than any of them wanted.

Rain whispered against the canal windows. A fire burned low behind the green tile stove. Someone had sent up bread, cold roast fowl, pickled pears, cheese, a bowl of early greens and two bottles of wine, then wisely disappeared.

Thessa was already eating.

"You are late," she said.

"I was obtaining certified evidence."

"I was obtaining chicken."

"One of those things is more important."

Thessa tore a leg from the bird.

"I agree."

Lyrion, beside her, moved the mustard nearer without being asked.

Damaris put the papers on the table.

Rhyl had taken the chair nearest the door. Mireth sat beside Caelen with one knee turned toward him beneath the table, close enough that their sleeves touched whenever either reached for anything. Ilyan stood at the window rather than sitting.

He had been doing that more often.

Standing where he could see the room and the way out.

Damaris hated that she had noticed.

She hated more that she understood why.

"Where is your cousin?" Caelen asked.

"Upstairs. Voluntarily confined, which is a phrase I hope never to hear again."

"Is he all right?"

"No."

Nobody tried to make the answer kinder.

Damaris took the empty chair between Rhyl and Ilyan's abandoned place.

Rhyl looked at her.

His hair was still damp from the walk back. He had changed out of his field coat, but there was mud along one cuff he had missed.

She wanted to brush it away.

Instead she said, "You smell like Lower Reed."

"You smell like archive dust."

"Cloves, actually."

His expression changed.

Only a little.

Enough that she wished she had chosen another joke.

Mireth reached for the certified packet.

"May I?"

Damaris handed it across.

Ilyan left the window.

The movement was immediate.

Not hurried.

Worse.

He came to stand behind Mireth's chair while she opened the folder.

"Start at the beginning," he said.

Damaris looked at Judge Vaun, who was not there.

She had remained downstairs with Meris, the tribunal runner and three separate preservation orders.

No one at this table had her authority.

That mattered.

"The beginning is a dead rosemary bed," Damaris said. "Not Silver Night."

Ilyan's jaw tightened.

"I know."

"I'm saying it because we need to keep knowing it."

He looked at her.

Then nodded.

So she told them.

Not every question. Not every interruption. Not the exact shape of Aereth's fear when he realized he remembered a breakfast that had never happened. That belonged to him unless it became necessary.

She told them about the buried silk bundle, the black waxed thread, the transit token, the household material inside it and the grey powder.

Mireth took over for the laboratory result.

"Refined mnemonic salt. Stabilized for damp storage."

Caelen stopped with his wine halfway to his mouth.

"The same as the Academy shipment?"

"We don't know."

"Same material."

"Same broad material," Mireth said. "Not a matched batch. Not a matched source."

Caelen set the cup down.

"Right."

Damaris continued.

Pellin.

His sister.

The courier.

The ring.

The cloves.

The false credential.

When she reached the token series, Ilyan sat.

At last.

When she reached the Academy delivery, nobody ate.

Damaris placed the certified receipt in front of him.

He did not touch it.

"Four days," he said.

"Yes."

"Before Silver Night."

"Yes."

"West Tower."

"The routing mark says WT-3. Talia says that meant the third-floor internal receiving cage. We still need the Academy routing record."

Ilyan read the signature.

"Mara Venn."

"Talia's mother."

His eyes lifted.

"The woman who died?"

Damaris nodded.

"Receiving and stores. Twenty-three years."

Ilyan looked back at the paper.

His hands remained in his lap.

Damaris understood the restraint. He had spent days learning that wanting to seize a document was not the same as having permission to touch it.

"You can hold the copy," she said.

"Certified?"

"Yes."

"Logged?"

"Yes."

"To you?"

"To the inquiry."

Only then did he pick it up.

Rhyl watched him do it.

So did Caelen.

Their separate institutions were teaching them the same lesson in different uniforms: desire did not create authority.

Ilyan read the receipt once.

Then again.

"What was in the parcel?"

"Unknown."

"Who collected it from the cage?"

"Unknown."

"Was her signature genuine?"

"Unknown."

He breathed through his nose.

"You sound like Vaun."

"I have had a long day."

That got the smallest smile from him.

Then it disappeared.

"My father knew about the west tower."

No one moved.

Damaris had heard the confession beneath the oak. They all had.

But this was different.

Ilyan was not breaking apart in the dark as a student anymore.

He was looking at a certified route record and choosing what to do with memory.

"He knew the tower was going to fail," Ilyan said. "Or be made to fail. I still don't know which words are honest. He knew enough to keep Valeric students away from the west rooms that evening."

Thessa put the chicken down.

"You didn't say that part."

"I didn't know it mattered."

"It matters now," Lyrion said.

Ilyan looked at him.

"Yes."

Damaris felt Meris's instruction like a hand at the back of her neck.

Stay close to him.

Learn enough to understand whether the Valerics were frightened, divided, or preparing to move.

This was exactly the kind of thing her aunt wanted.

Not because Meris had ordered betrayal.

That would have been simpler.

Because Meris had asked for understanding, and understanding was made of moments like this.

Ilyan continued.

"My uncle said the casualties were worse than intended."

Mireth's face changed.

"Intended."

"That was his word."

"You remember exactly?"

"Yes."

"Who else heard it?"

"No one."

He looked at the receipt again.

"He said they had expected the west section to be contained."

Caelen leaned forward.

"Contained how?"

"He didn't say. I didn't ask. I was busy discovering that my family had helped kill our classmates."

Caelen flinched.

Ilyan saw it.

"Sorry."

"Don't be."

"I am anyway."

For a while there was only rain.

Damaris knew she should remember every word.

That realization made her feel filthy.

She looked down at her hands.

Rhyl's hand rested on the table near hers.

Not touching.

He had learned her too quickly.

Or perhaps she had become easier to read.

"Damaris," Ilyan said.

She looked up.

He was watching her.

"What?"

"Your aunt will see the query."

"She already has."

"And this?"

He tapped the receipt.

"The River Ledger knows the copy was made. Meris knows I used the cipher. She does not automatically know what you just told me."

Ilyan held her gaze.

"Will you tell her?"

There it was.

Not accusation.

Not even distrust.

A question asked by a friend who had already been told that her family wanted her near him.

Damaris could have answered quickly.

Quick answers were useful when one wanted people to believe certainty existed.

She did not have any.

"I don't know."

Thessa looked at her.

Caelen did too.

Mireth stayed very still.

Rhyl's hand did not move.

Ilyan's expression closed by a fraction.

Damaris felt it happen.

"That's not the answer I wanted," he said.

"I know."

"Is it the true one?"

"Yes."

He looked away first.

That hurt more than anger would have.

Damaris forced herself not to fill the silence.

At last Ilyan said, "Then decide before she asks."

"I intend to."

"Good."

The word was flat.

Rhyl picked up his cup.

"What does Meris actually want?"

Damaris looked at him.

He had asked it without looking at her.

A gift.

He was making the question belong to the table instead of turning it into a private confrontation.

"To know whether the Valerics are going to fracture," Damaris said. "Whether Ilyan's family is divided. Whether somebody inside the house is going to move against somebody else. Whether their position around Silver Night is going to become a political fire before everyone else has chosen where to stand."

Ilyan's mouth twisted.

"Efficient."

"Yes."

"And you?"

"I want to know who killed our friends."

"That isn't all."

Damaris looked at him.

"No."

Her voice came quieter.

"I want my family not to be involved."

Nobody had expected that.

She had not expected to say it.

Once spoken, it sat between the plates with all the other ugly things.

"I keep looking at every ledger entry and every house connection and thinking please not us," she said. "Not because I think we're too good. Because I know we aren't."

Meris's face rose in her mind.

Affection.

Calculation.

Worry.

"And every time my aunt helps me, I wonder whether she's helping because she wants the truth or because she wants to know where the truth is going before it arrives."

Rhyl turned his cup slowly between his hands.

"Both can be true."

"I know."

"Does that make it worse?"

"Much."

Ilyan looked at her again.

Something in his face had reopened.

Not trust restored. Trust had not vanished.

That was the point.

Trust could be injured without becoming dead.

"I don't want you to report what I said about my uncle," he said.

Damaris nodded immediately.

"All right."

"Even if Meris asks."

"All right."

"Even if refusing tells her there is something."

Damaris hesitated.

There was the price.

"Yes."

Ilyan studied her.

"Thank you."

She wished the gratitude did not feel like a debt.

Below them, somewhere in the house, a door closed.

A few moments later came footsteps in the corridor.

Talia entered carrying a folded note on a tray.

She looked as though she had considered throwing the tray into the canal instead.

"For Lady Damaris. From Lady Meris."

Of course.

Damaris took it.

The paper was unsealed.

One line.

**When you are finished upstairs, come to my study. Bring no papers.**

Thessa read Damaris's face.

"Bad?"

"Efficient."

Ilyan almost smiled again.

Damaris folded the note.

Rhyl's fingers touched hers under the edge of the table.

Not enough to stop her.

Enough to remind her that he was there.

She squeezed once.

Then stood.

"Do not wait for me."

"We will," Mireth said.

Damaris looked at her.

Mireth shrugged.

"You can object downstairs."

Caelen reached for the bread.

"We have food."

Thessa lifted the remaining chicken.

"Some of us do."

For the first time since the receipt came out, Ilyan laughed.

It was brief.

It was real.

Damaris carried the sound with her into the corridor.

She left the certified records on the table.

That was deliberate.

So was going downstairs empty-handed.

Lady Meris was waiting behind the closed door of her study.

The River Ledger audit lay open on the desk between them.

Meris looked at Damaris's empty hands.

Then at her face.

"Well?"

Damaris closed the door.

"Aereth is staying home tonight. Judge Vaun has the laboratory result. The courier credential is fraudulent. The other delivery sites are being preserved or checked quietly."

Meris waited.

Damaris waited too.

Her aunt's eyes narrowed slightly.

"And Ilyan?"

There it was.

Damaris thought of him at the table.

My uncle said the casualties were worse than intended.

She thought of the promise she had made less than ten minutes ago.

Then she thought of the woman in front of her, who had fed her as a child, taught her to read contracts, corrected her arithmetic, given her the cipher and never pretended that family love was free of politics.

Damaris loved her.

That did not make the choice easier.

"Ilyan has seen the certified Academy receipt," she said.

Meris's gaze remained steady.

"And?"

"And nothing I am going to report tonight."

The silence was immediate.

Not shocked.

Measured.

Meris leaned back in her chair.

"You are refusing me."

"Yes."

"Because he asked you to?"

Damaris felt the trap and disliked that it was not really a trap at all.

It was simply a good question.

"Because I agreed not to repeat something he told me in confidence."

Meris's expression did not change.

"Was it relevant to Valeric stability?"

Damaris said nothing.

Her aunt closed her eyes for one heartbeat.

When they opened again, there was no anger in them.

That was worse.

"I see."

"Do you?"

"More than you intended to tell me."

Damaris's stomach dropped.

Meris tapped one finger against the audit sheet.

"This is the difficulty with principled silence, darling. People can still hear its shape."

Damaris sat opposite her.

"Then perhaps you should stop asking questions you know I cannot answer."

"Cannot?"

"Will not."

Meris's mouth softened.

Not quite a smile.

"Better."

Damaris stared at her.

"Are you angry?"

"Yes."

The answer came without drama.

"Are you going to close the cipher?"

Meris looked down at the audit.

"No."

That frightened Damaris more than anger had.

"Why?"

"Because you used it well."

"I just refused to tell you what you wanted."

"Those are separate questions."

Damaris hated how familiar the sentence felt.

Meris folded the audit shut.

"If I wanted a niece who repeated every confidence placed in her, I would have raised a gossip."

"Then what do you want?"

Her aunt looked at her for a long moment.

"I want you to understand that protecting a confidence is also an action. It may protect Ilyan. It may protect a Valeric faction you do not know exists. It may deny this family warning we need. It may preserve the only friendship through which anyone outside House Valeric can learn the truth."

Damaris felt her shoulders tighten.

"That sounds suspiciously like approval."

"It is not."

"Disapproval?"

"Not entirely."

"Aunt."

Meris finally smiled.

"Welcome to the part of adulthood where people you love make choices you would not make for them."

Damaris thought of Aereth upstairs.

Of Talia accepting an apology without offering forgiveness.

Of Rhyl giving her space instead of demanding confession.

Of Ilyan asking a question and accepting an answer he hated because it was true.

"You knew this would happen when you gave me the cipher."

"I knew something would happen."

"That's evasive."

"It is accurate."

Damaris stood.

Meris did not stop her.

At the door, Damaris turned.

"If you learn what I refused to tell you from someone else, do not punish Ilyan for trusting me."

Meris's face lost the smile.

"I do not punish people for being correctly cautious around my family."

"That is not a promise."

"No."

Damaris waited.

Meris sighed.

"I will not retaliate against Ilyan for your refusal."

"Thank you."

"Do not make me regret teaching you to negotiate."

Damaris opened the door.

"You started too early."

Behind her, Meris laughed once.

Damaris went back upstairs.

The Seven were still there.

Of course they were.
