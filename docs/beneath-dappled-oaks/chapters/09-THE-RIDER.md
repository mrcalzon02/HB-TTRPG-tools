# Chapter Nine — The Rider

The Crown rider arrived while Ilyan was losing an argument with a goat.

Not the goat itself. That would have improved matters considerably. The animal had been dead eleven years and had therefore achieved the enviable legal position of no longer caring who owned its descendants.

The living parties cared enough for everyone.

Ilyan had three generations of breeding records open across his desk, a partnership agreement beneath his left hand and Examiner Sael standing at the window with the expression of a man who had already decided how the case ought to end and intended to make Ilyan suffer until he found the same answer honestly.

"The phrase is not ambiguous," Ilyan said.

"Then why are you angry?"

"Because the phrase is stupid."

"Those are different defects."

Ilyan looked up.

Sael did not smile.

The outer bell rang twice.

A clerk crossed the corridor at speed. Boots followed, hard on the stone, then stopped outside the examination room.

Ilyan's irritation vanished before the knock came.

Sael noticed that too.

"You are becoming easy to read."

"I am waiting for one particular packet."

"That is what makes you easy to read."

The clerk entered carrying a narrow leather dispatch case wet from the morning rain. Behind him stood a Crown post rider in dark blue, mud to both knees and a silver transit badge pinned high at her throat.

"For Judge Vaun," the clerk said. "Restricted supplemental custody. Personal receipt."

Sael glanced at Ilyan.

"Not yours."

"I know."

"Your face disagrees."

The rider's mouth twitched.

Ilyan returned to the goat with as much dignity as remained available.

Judge Vaun arrived seven minutes later.

He knew because he counted.

She signed the rider's book, checked the seals against the notice already on file and carried the case into the smaller review room without opening it in the corridor. Sael followed. Ilyan stayed at his desk until Vaun looked back.

"Are you coming?"

He stood so quickly his chair struck the wall.

The rider waited outside with the patience of someone accustomed to delivering objects that caused other people to forget manners.

Inside, Vaun shut the door.

The case contained three things.

A certified extract of Teren's acceptance instrument.

A segregated extract from the protected-routing rider.

And the dual-override custody sheet.

The Crown had redacted names and destinations belonging to protected witnesses who had nothing to do with their inquiry. It had not redacted offices, authority limits, signatures relevant to the west-tower cabinet, or the language Teren had sworn.

Ilyan looked first at the oath.

Vaun covered it with one hand.

"In order."

He exhaled.

Sael sat down.

"She does that because it works."

"I know why she does it."

"Good."

Vaun removed her hand only after Ilyan took the chair opposite her.

The acceptance instrument was dated the morning after Ardis's recusal. It appointed Teren Valeric as temporary Joint Order liaison chair for emergency west-tower review, with authority over inter-order custody disputes, credential review continuity and protected-routing decisions requiring Joint Order concurrence.

The appointment was narrow.

It was also real.

Teren's signature sat beneath it.

Ilyan had seen the same hand on leaf seventy-three. It hurt less the second time.

Not because he trusted it more.

Because the shock had acquired furniture.

Sael read the authority schedule twice.

"He could not originate a protected witness movement."

Vaun nodded.

"No."

"Could not identify a protected destination unless receiving disclosed it to him."

"Correct."

"Could refuse Joint Order concurrence."

"Yes."

Ilyan looked at the line beneath that.

"And he could require preservation."

Vaun turned the page toward him.

The clause was short.

If ordinary receiving failed, the liaison chair could place affected material under temporary dual-control preservation until a lawful receiving authority resumed custody.

That explained the cabinet.

It did not explain the black selector in Corren's hands.

The oath came next.

Most of it was familiar: custody, truthful record, refusal of improper instruction, protection of restricted persons, no disclosure beyond lawful need.

Then the language changed.

**Where emergency continuity requires protected identity, route, compartment, or method to remain undisclosed, I accept bounded secrecy upon those categories and consent to such lawful mnemonic compartment as is specifically countersigned, witnessed, and entered in the protected rider. No compartment shall conceal unlawful conduct from later authorized review.**

Ilyan stopped breathing for a moment.

Sael read it again.

Vaun did not speak.

"He consented," Ilyan said.

"To the possibility," Vaun said.

He nodded before she could correct him further.

"Not proof it happened."

"Good."

The next sentence mattered more.

Mnemonic compartment required a separate countersignature by the appointee after the protected category had been defined. The general acceptance oath alone was not enough.

Sael tapped the rider extract.

"There."

The segregated rider contained four numbered authorities.

The first allowed Teren to know the existence of protected witness continuity without receiving witness identities automatically.

The second issued the black routing selector as the Joint Order half of the two-person cabinet control.

The third established emergency override procedure if the receiving key became unavailable.

The fourth concerned mnemonic compartment.

Beside it, in the space where a countersignature should have appeared, was a notation.

**Not activated at appointment.**

Ilyan read it until he was certain the words would not change.

"So he did not consent to compartment then."

"Not at appointment," Sael said.

"Could it have been activated later?"

Vaun pointed to the rider's continuation reference.

A second leaf existed.

The Crown had not supplied it.

Not because it remained protected.

Because it was not in the appointment packet.

**Continuation transferred to medical-security custody, day +2.**

The room became very quiet.

Ilyan thought of Enara's list.

Treatment records.

Sleep preparations.

Mnemonic preparations.

The week after Silver Night.

"Day plus two," he said.

Sael nodded.

Two days after the attack.

Three days before Corren returned the selector.

Vaun moved to the dual-override sheet.

"Do not make those dates into causality yet."

"I wasn't."

She looked at him.

"I was about to."

"Better."

The override sheet had two signature blocks.

The first was Joint Order.

**T. Valeric.**

Ilyan's stomach tightened.

The second was Contingency witness receiving supervisor.

**Pel Ardis.**

He stared.

"Ardis was dead."

Sael leaned closer.

The signature was not dated Silver Night.

It had been placed on the rider before the attack.

Beneath it, a clause authorized the receiving supervisor's standing concurrence for emergency sealing if the receiving key became unavailable and the bearer could not safely be contacted.

Ardis had pre-authorized one half of the override.

Teren supplied the other three days after Silver Night.

No dead man had signed from beyond the grave. No forged signature was required to explain the paper.

The mechanism was less dramatic than Ilyan had feared.

That made it more useful.

"What did the override seal?" he asked.

The custody sheet answered only by class.

**Protected witness-continuity material: bearer authentication, independent receiving authority, transfer instructions, associated credential discrepancy.**

Mara's name did not appear.

It did not need to.

The shape was unmistakable.

Sael sat back.

"They preserved the discrepancy."

Ilyan looked at him.

"Not closed it."

"No."

Corren had later returned the selector belonging to the office that performed the preservation.

Ilyan felt the temptation immediately: Corren had helped save the evidence. Corren had taken the key from Teren. Corren had protected Mara. Corren had—

He stopped.

Vaun saw him stop.

She said nothing.

That was worse than praise.

"We still don't know how he got it," Ilyan said.

"Correct."

"Or whether Teren gave it to him."

"Correct."

"Or whether the compartment was later activated."

"Correct."

Sael turned back to the continuation reference.

"But we know where the next record went."

Medical-security custody.

A treatment event or security event existed two days after Silver Night.

Something specific enough to take custody of the rider continuation.

For the first time since Enara had described the edges of Teren's missing week, the hole had an administrative boundary.

---

Teren came to the Hall willingly and hated every step of it.

Ilyan met him in the courtyard.

His uncle had brought a basket.

"What is that?"

"Preserves."

"Why?"

"I relabeled them."

Ilyan stared.

Teren lifted the basket.

"You laughed. I assumed that constituted interest."

Inside were six small jars of plum preserve with neat paper labels.

Ilyan took one.

"You brought evidence of being uninteresting."

"I am considering making it my principal defense."

They went inside.

Enara had returned for the reading. Vaun gave Teren the documents and then, with deliberate cruelty, allowed him to read them at his own speed.

He reached the mnemonic clause.

His face changed.

Not recognition.

Fear.

"I agreed to this?"

"You agreed that a later bounded compartment could be offered under those conditions," Enara said. "The appointment rider says it was not activated then."

Teren kept reading.

He reached his override signature.

His fingers stopped on the page.

"Nothing," he said.

Nobody asked what he meant.

"I know that signature. I know how I make the T when I am tired. I know I was tired because it slopes. I can tell you I probably signed sitting down because the lower stroke drags."

He looked at Ilyan.

"I remember none of it."

Enara said, "Then leave it there."

Teren closed his eyes.

"I am beginning to dislike you."

"That is not medically concerning."

He laughed once, without humor.

Then he reached the transfer line.

Medical-security custody, day +2.

The color left his face.

Enara noticed before Ilyan did.

"What happened?"

Teren did not answer immediately.

"A smell."

Enara waited.

"Bitter orange."

"Anything else?"

"Wet wool."

His right hand went to the inside of his left elbow.

"Pain there."

"Direct memory?"

"Sensation. No scene."

"Do not build one."

Teren opened his eyes.

"I know."

This time he sounded angry.

Not at her.

At the absence.

Vaun asked, "Does bitter orange mean anything institutionally?"

Sael shook his head.

Enara did not.

"It means many things. Cleaning oil. Digestive cordial. Certain sedatives. Two mnemonic preparations I know of. Perfume. Candied peel."

Teren rubbed his arm.

"Helpful."

"Reality often is not."

Ilyan thought of Thessa's wall.

Concrete. Mud. Men with pry bars. A problem that did not care how elegant anyone's theory became.

He wanted one like that.

Instead he had bitter orange.

Vaun requested the medical-security continuation by accession reference before Teren left the room.

This time Enara added a medical necessity statement: not to expose protected witness material, but to establish whether Teren underwent lawful treatment, consented mnemonic compartment, emergency sedation, injury care, or some other intervention relevant to interpreting his current memory.

The request was narrow enough to fit through the institutional keyhole.

They sent it.

Then Teren handed Enara a jar of plum preserve.

She looked at it.

"Why?"

"I have six."

"That is not a reason."

"It is the reason you are getting one."

She accepted it.

Ilyan loved him so abruptly it hurt.

The feeling did not answer a single question.

He was learning to allow that.

---

Damaris's answer came before the medical record did.

She found Ilyan at midday in the River Ledger annex, where he had gone because she refused to carry restricted commercial material into the Hall merely for his convenience.

Rhyl was not with her.

That was unusual enough now that Ilyan noticed.

Damaris noticed him noticing.

"Boundary survey."

"I didn't ask."

"Your face did."

He sighed.

"This is spreading."

She led him into a consultation room overlooking the canal.

On the table lay no protected names, no route ledger and no historical manifest. Only a certified statistical extract.

"Emergency bonded traffic," she said. "Capital district, three days before through six days after Silver Night."

Ilyan read the columns.

Ordinary bonded transfers had continued through the crisis, though at reduced volume. Protected emergency traffic spiked the morning after the attack.

One line mattered.

**Joint Crown–Contingency medical conveyance: one sealed passenger movement, capital inner district to Eiravel Memorial Infirmary annex. Day +2.**

Ilyan looked up.

Damaris folded her arms.

"It is not a name."

"I know."

"It is not proof Teren was the passenger."

"I know."

"It is not Mara."

"I know."

She watched him for another moment.

"Good."

"You and Thessa are becoming unbearable."

"We compare notes."

He could not tell whether she was joking.

"Why medical conveyance?"

"Because the carrier billed medical-security tariff rather than witness tariff. The destination annex is the only location the certified extract permits me to disclose. It was a neutral memorial infirmary facility used by Crown offices during the emergency."

"Who paid?"

"Joint Emergency Coordination."

That old temporary room again.

Half the people who mattered and twice as many who thought they did.

"Can you see the passenger identity?"

"No. And I did not ask."

Ilyan nodded.

Damaris's expression softened.

"You look disappointed."

"I am relieved."

"Those are not mutually exclusive."

"Apparently nothing is anymore."

She pushed the extract toward him.

"Take the certified copy. The request is logged to me. If someone asks why I pulled it, I will answer."

"Meris?"

"Especially Meris."

There was more confidence in that than there would have been a week ago.

Not defiance for its own sake. A decision about what she could defend.

Ilyan tucked the copy into his case.

"Rhyl knows?"

"That I was checking aggregate traffic. Not the result. He is working."

"Healthy."

"Do not sound surprised."

"I have met us."

She laughed and opened the door.

Before he left she said, "Ilyan."

He turned.

"If the passenger was Teren, that still doesn't tell you whether he went willingly."

"I know."

"Or unwillingly."

"I know."

"Good."

This time he smiled.

---

Caelen's discovery was less dramatic and more damaging to several of Ilyan's favorite theories.

Modern Crown custody still used dual-control cabinets.

Overrides were boring.

They happened when one key holder was injured, unreachable, replaced, compromised, dead, or physically separated from the secured material. Proper override required two independent authorities, a reason code and later audit.

"So the override itself proves almost nothing," Caelen said over a late meal in the Royal Martial refectory.

"It proves Teren acted."

"Yes. It does not prove the situation was improper."

Caelen tore bread and dipped it into stew.

He had come directly from drill, hair damp at the temples, uniform coat hanging open. Mireth sat beside him in Green Covenant robes, one sleeve stained with pollen from whatever hearing had occupied her morning.

She stole his bread.

He looked at his empty fingers.

"You have your own."

"Yours was ready."

He gave her the expression of a man confronting an injustice too intimate for formal remedy.

Ilyan waited.

Caelen surrendered and took another piece.

"There is one unusual part," he said. "Standing concurrence. We do not use it now for witness cabinets. The second authority has to be alive and present or separately authenticated at the time of override."

Mireth swallowed.

"But that may be a reform caused by Silver Night."

"Exactly. The instructor didn't know when the rule changed."

Ilyan wrote that down.

Mireth had brought doctrine of her own.

Green Covenant oath jurists recognized category-bounded secrecy workings. They could make a person unable to consciously organize protected material under a forbidden category while leaving unrelated episodic memory largely intact.

But lawful use required consent and a release condition.

"Release could be time, office, named authority, death of a protected person, judicial review," Mireth said. "It depends on the oath."

"Could the person forget consenting?"

"Silae says yes, if the consent itself identifies the protected category. But the external record must survive. Otherwise the oath becomes impossible to audit."

Ilyan thought of the rider continuation transferred to medical-security custody.

External record.

Somewhere.

"And hostile alteration?"

Mireth gave him a look.

"She said exactly what Enara said. Similar edges can come from different causes."

Caelen smiled.

"You are being professionally corrected across several institutions."

"I have noticed."

Mireth reached across the table and squeezed Ilyan's wrist.

"We are very proud."

"I regret telling any of you anything."

"No, you don't."

He didn't.

---

Thessa arrived at his apartment after sunset with mud on her trousers and a folded Warden memorandum in her pocket.

The retaining wall had survived the day.

Barely.

"We removed the upper load," she said while washing her hands at his basin. "Reset two drains. Tomorrow we rebuild the eastern footing."

Ilyan leaned against the doorframe.

"Did you come here to tell me about masonry?"

She glanced over her shoulder.

"You asked about my day."

"I did."

"This was my day."

He smiled.

She dried her hands and kissed him.

He had become accustomed enough to her mouth that anticipation now preceded contact. That did not make it weaker. It made it specific. He knew the slight upward pull at one corner when she was tired, the way she pressed closer when she had spent all day around other people, the brief touch of her thumb beneath his jaw before she stepped away.

Tonight she did not step away immediately.

"You ate?" she asked against his cheek.

"Yes."

"Truthfully?"

"Caelen fed me."

"That was not the question."

"Yes."

"Good."

She handed him the memorandum.

It contained aggregate Warden movement counts for protected road transits after Silver Night.

No names.

No destinations beyond control sectors.

No protected identity chain.

One movement left the capital west sector on day +2 under joint medical-security authority.

One passenger.

Two escorts.

The route did not continue onto the western trunk road.

It terminated inside the capital's outer memorial district.

Ilyan looked at her.

"Eiravel infirmary."

"Could be."

"Damaris found a medical conveyance there the same day."

Thessa's eyes sharpened.

"Then you have independent systems describing a compatible movement."

"Not identity."

"No."

"Not purpose."

"No."

"But compatible."

"Yes."

He put the memorandum down.

Thessa studied him.

"You are doing better."

"Stop grading me."

"No."

He caught her by the waist and pulled her in.

She laughed, then kissed him hard enough to end the argument for a while.

When they parted, her hands remained under his coat.

"There is another thing," she said.

"That is a cruel place to put another thing."

"You will survive."

"Unclear."

Her smile faded only slightly.

"The Warden request was noticed."

Ilyan's hands stilled.

"By whom?"

"My captain asked why a new officer needed eighteen-year-old aggregate protected-movement counts."

"What did you tell her?"

"That I was assisting a lawful Hall inquiry and did not require names."

"And?"

"She said fine. Then she asked whether House Eris should expect to hear about it elsewhere."

There it was.

Not spies in the walls.

Not omniscient enemies.

A supervisor seeing an unusual request and thinking about the institution that employed both of them.

"What did you say?"

"That I had no House instruction and was acting as a Warden."

Ilyan looked at her.

"Did she believe you?"

"Yes."

"Will she tell anyone?"

"Probably her superior. That is what reporting chains are for."

He let out a breath.

Chapter Eight's private coordination was becoming visible exactly as ordinary work made it visible.

Thessa touched his face.

"Don't borrow tomorrow's disaster."

"That sounds like something an elder says."

"My grandmother says it."

"I knew it."

"She is usually right. It is extremely irritating."

He kissed her palm.

"Stay?"

"Obviously."

---

The medical-security answer came after dark.

Not by Crown rider this time.

By an Eiravel infirmary archivist carrying a grey case and an expression suggesting that anyone who mishandled it would be personally fed to the dead.

Vaun sent for Ilyan, Teren and Enara.

Thessa went with Ilyan as far as the Hall steps, kissed him once beneath the lamps and left for his apartment with his spare key.

She had Warden duty at dawn.

Her life did not stop because his had become strange.

He loved that too.

The infirmary extract was narrower than any of them wanted.

It was enough.

On day +2 after Silver Night, a patient identified in the surviving restricted index only as **Joint Order emergency officer, temporary chair authority** had been admitted under medical-security custody.

Teren closed his eyes.

Nobody needed to say his name.

The treatment line recorded exhaustion, smoke exposure, minor lacerations, prolonged wakefulness and acute stress.

Then one additional procedure.

**Protective mnemonic compartment performed under patient consent pursuant to emergency rider. Category: protected witness-routing identity and continuity access. Release condition: authorized post-emergency review.**

Ilyan read the words slowly.

Teren had consented.

At least according to the medical record.

The compartment had been lawful on its face.

It had also been supposed to release.

Enara leaned over the extract.

"There."

A second notation sat below the procedure.

**Release review deferred: patient transferred before scheduled examination. Rider continuation returned under sealed custody.**

Vaun looked up.

"Transferred where?"

The archivist shook her head.

"Not in the medical extract. That is security custody."

Teren's voice was quiet.

"Do I remember the infirmary?"

Enara answered carefully.

"You remember bitter orange, wet wool and pain in your arm. Those are compatible with being there. They are not a recovered scene."

He nodded.

"Right."

Ilyan stared at the release condition.

Authorized post-emergency review.

Eighteen years.

No release.

No remembered office.

No remembered compartment.

Not necessarily because somebody had attacked Teren's mind.

Possibly because a lawful emergency protection had never been properly unwound.

Institutional failure could injure a person just as thoroughly as malice if everyone involved believed someone else owned the final step.

Sael, reading over Vaun's shoulder, said, "Who held responsibility for release review?"

The archivist pointed to the procedure code.

Joint responsibility.

Medical-security physician.

Joint Order chair or successor authority.

Protected receiving supervisor if the witness category remained active.

Three offices.

One temporary emergency structure.

One dead receiving supervisor.

One chair who had been transferred and no longer remembered being chair.

And a protected witness chain that had remained sealed.

Ilyan felt the terrible ordinary logic of it.

Not proof of sabotage.

A machine built for three days had lost the people who knew how to turn it off.

Teren sat down.

"Eighteen years."

Enara pulled a chair beside him.

"Yes."

"Can you release it?"

"Not tonight."

His head came up.

She held his gaze.

"The category touches a protected witness chain that may still be active. We first establish whether the release condition can be satisfied without exposing someone who is still entitled to protection."

Teren looked furious.

Then tired.

Then, after a long moment, he nodded.

"Of course."

Ilyan moved closer but did not touch him until Teren reached out.

His uncle caught his wrist.

"Do not make her pay for my memory."

Ilyan knew who he meant.

Mara.

"I won't."

Teren's grip tightened.

"Even if I ask tomorrow."

"I won't."

That promise mattered more than anything they had learned about the key.

Vaun closed the case.

"Then tomorrow we identify the transfer action and the current lawful authority capable of reviewing release without opening the witness destination."

Teren gave a tired laugh.

"Tomorrow."

Ilyan looked at him.

"Serrat was right. It isn't aware of us."

Teren stared.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Outside, the Hall clock struck the late hour.

Somewhere across the city Thessa was probably asleep diagonally across his bed and stealing most of the blanket. Damaris would eventually receive questions about her expensive Ledger queries. Caelen's training instructor would remember the young officer asking about obsolete override procedure. Mireth's cousin knew she was studying compartment oaths. A Warden captain had already passed Thessa's unusual records request upward.

The Seven had not been discovered by a hidden enemy.

They had become visible by doing work in institutions built to remember who asked for things.

Ilyan understood, suddenly, that this was adulthood too.

Not merely being permitted to act.

Leaving a trail when you did.

He looked at the medical record one last time.

Teren's missing week had not opened.

But its edges had moved.

There was now a rider, a lawful compartment, a failed release, and a transfer that had carried both Teren and the unfinished obligation somewhere beyond the infirmary.

The next question was no longer what had happened to his memory.

It was who had been responsible for giving it back.
