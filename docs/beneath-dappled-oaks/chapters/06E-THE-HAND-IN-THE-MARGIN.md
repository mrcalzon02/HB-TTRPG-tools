# Chapter Six E — The Hand in the Margin

The handwriting room had six windows, no curtains and a table scarred by people who had been told not to press too hard.

Ilyan noticed the table first.

Thessa noticed him noticing it.

"You are becoming very exciting."

"I noticed furniture."

"Exactly. Another month in the Hall and you'll have opinions about storage cord."

"I already have opinions about storage cord."

She stopped beside him.

"That's worse."

Examiner Serrat, three paces ahead, did not turn around.

"Blue cord sheds fibers into wax impressions."

Thessa stared at his back.

"There are two of you now."

Judge Vaun entered behind them carrying the certified copy of the annotation under a stiff protective leaf.

"Sit down."

They did.

The Hall's script examiner was an elderly woman named Pellis Nareth whose left hand trembled until she put it on paper. Then it became perfectly still.

She did not ask why the annotation mattered.

Serrat gave her the question instead.

"Can the hand be associated with an archived office writer without attributing the substance of the note to anyone not demonstrated by the writing?"

Pellis looked over her spectacles.

"Yes."

"Can you do it today?"

"Possibly."

Thessa leaned toward Ilyan.

"I like her."

Pellis heard her.

"Most people do until I tell them their grandmother did not write the romantic letter they have built their personality around."

Thessa sat back.

"I like her more."

Pellis ignored this and examined the copied annotation.

The initials sat at the lower edge of the note.

**T.V.**

Ilyan had spent the previous night refusing to make names from them.

That had not prevented names from arriving anyway.

Teren Valeric had been the obvious one. So obvious that Ilyan distrusted it immediately. Teren's surviving service hand was square, heavily separated, the product of a man trained to make warehouse instructions legible to people carrying crates in rain. The annotation was narrower and ran forward at the top, impatient without becoming untidy.

Still, initials were cheap things. Families reused them. Offices accumulated them. Half the Realm seemed to contain a T or a V when one badly needed it not to.

Pellis set three transparent measuring strips over the copy.

"This isn't enough by itself."

Vaun nodded.

"What is?"

"Comparison samples. Preferably routine work. Not signatures. People perform signatures. I want notes they wrote while annoyed."

Serrat had anticipated that.

A clerk brought in two archive boxes.

The first held surviving technical-review sheets from Emergency Substitution Review. The second held Joint Office liaison notes from the same month.

Pellis worked through them without ceremony.

Ilyan watched hands emerge from the dead.

Some leaned backward. Some crowded the margins. One writer made every lower loop so broad that it threatened neighboring words. Another crossed every final stroke twice. The people were gone or retired or somewhere eating breakfast without knowing that four young adults and a judge were studying the way they once wrote *insufficient sample*.

Thessa slid a cup of water toward Ilyan.

He drank it.

She did not ask whether he was all right.

That helped.

After forty minutes Pellis stopped.

"This one."

She placed a routine defect-review note beside the annotation.

The note had been written twenty-eight days before Silver Night.

One day after CR-88 entered the Emergency Substitution Review working file.

The author line read:

**Tavian Veyr — Technical Examiner, Credential Integrity Liaison.**

Ilyan looked at the initials again.

T.V.

Thessa exhaled.

"Not Teren."

"No," Pellis said. "Unless Teren Valeric spent several years disguising his hand in routine technical work before anyone had reason to care, which is a theory you may entertain somewhere else."

Vaun's mouth moved slightly.

Not quite a smile.

"Strength?"

Pellis pointed with the blunt end of her pencil.

"Same formation of the capital A. Same open lower loop on the g. Same habit of beginning a second line beneath the final third of the first rather than returning to the margin. Same pressure break before a terminal t. There are twelve useful correspondences and no meaningful contradiction in the available samples."

"Conclusion?"

"The annotation is strongly consistent with Tavian Veyr's ordinary technical hand."

Not *was written by*.

Strongly consistent.

Ilyan had learned to love irritating language.

It survived contact with tomorrow.

"Is Veyr alive?" he asked.

Serrat opened a personnel extract.

"No. Died nine years ago."

"Family?"

"Not relevant yet."

Ilyan looked at him.

Serrat held his gaze.

"Employment is relevant. Authority is relevant. Who instructed him is relevant. His widow's breakfast is not."

Ilyan sat back.

"Fair."

Pellis returned to the samples.

"You also have a better question than who wrote the note."

Vaun said, "Whether he had authority to make it."

"Yes."

Serrat turned another page.

Tavian Veyr had been seconded from Credential Integrity to the Emergency Substitution Review for eleven days.

His technical remit was narrow: determine whether substituted witness credentials could preserve lineage verification when the parent authorization object could not be physically presented.

Ilyan read the sentence twice.

That was almost exactly what the annotation addressed.

No secret office.

No midnight appointment.

A technical examiner had been assigned a technical problem and had written a technical answer.

The danger lay elsewhere.

"Who gave him the problem?" Thessa asked.

Serrat turned the personnel extract over.

"The secondment request survives. The approving office is Joint Order Liaison. The requesting desk is Dmitrigical Review. The individual requester is coded to the sealed review roster."

Ilyan felt anger arrive and settle.

Not flare.

Settle.

"Can Vaun's order reach the roster?"

"If she demonstrates why the identity is necessary."

Vaun said, "We are getting there."

Pellis pushed the handwriting samples back toward Serrat.

"You have your hand. Don't make it carry the arm."

Thessa looked at Ilyan.

"I definitely like her."

---

The Eren Vale credential had spent the morning in a locked evidence case at the River Ledger while two departments argued politely about who was permitted to carry it across a courtyard.

Damaris arrived with it just before midday.

She was wearing committee grey over a dark green dress, the compromise produced when Maeryn informed her that an observer was still visible and Damaris informed Maeryn that she intended to spend part of the day in an archive.

Rhyl came with her as far as the Hall gate and stopped there.

"I am not carrying that."

Damaris looked at the case in her own hands.

"You have made this point four times."

"I am preserving my professional development."

"Your professional development involves deer."

"And not becoming an unofficial River Ledger courier."

She smiled.

He bent and kissed her.

Not furtively.

Not extravagantly.

Long enough that the gate clerk found a reason to examine a rain gutter.

When Rhyl drew back, Damaris still had one hand fisted lightly in the front of his coat.

"Dinner?" he asked.

"If the committee doesn't murder me."

"If it does, I'll object."

"Professionally?"

"Furiously."

She kissed him once more, shorter, and went inside.

Ilyan had watched from the inner steps.

Damaris saw him.

"Not a word."

"I wasn't going to say anything."

"Your face was."

"My face is innocent."

Thessa appeared behind him.

"It has never been innocent."

Damaris handed the evidence case to the receiving clerk and signed the transfer line.

Only then did she look properly at Ilyan.

"You found the hand."

He told her.

Tavian Veyr. Technical examiner. Dead nine years. Assigned to the exact problem described in the annotation. Individual requester still sealed.

Damaris absorbed it without reaching for the easiest conclusion.

"So whoever asked the question may matter more than the man who answered it."

"Yes."

"And Veyr may have done nothing wrong."

"Yes."

"I hate competent bureaucracy."

"You work for the River Ledger."

"I hate *other people's* competent bureaucracy."

That was more like her.

The examination took place in a room with better light than the handwriting chamber and less personality.

The River Ledger sent Master Assayer Jalen Caladren-Voss because the credential had first entered evidence through a commercial audit. Serrat supplied a Hall materials examiner. Vaun supplied herself. Sael arrived late with another terrible drink.

Nobody touched the credential directly.

It lay beneath glass with the dead freight assessor's sponsor impression visible along the lower edge.

Jalen looked at Ilyan.

"You understand that resemblance is not identity?"

Ilyan nodded.

Jalen looked at Damaris.

"And you?"

"I have been related to you for twenty-two years. I understand that you can make any sentence irritating."

"Excellent."

He began.

The CR-88 method had three observable consequences if used competently.

First, the inherited defect would be too stable across impressions made under different pressure because the flaw had been copied deliberately rather than generated naturally by the parent die.

Second, the secondary-stage cutting process tended to broaden the shallow side of the defect by a minute but measurable amount.

Third, the mineral suspension used to exaggerate the source impression could leave trace particulate in recessed cuts if the replica die was not thoroughly cleaned.

The first could be examined from existing images.

The second could be measured optically.

The third required light and magnification, not sampling.

No scraping.

No solvent.

No destruction.

Damaris stood beside Ilyan while Jalen and the Hall examiner worked.

Thessa stood on his other side.

He was beginning to suspect his friends had developed a formation.

The first result came quickly.

The crescent defect on Eren Vale's sponsor impression was too consistent with the surviving secondary reference impression.

Not identical.

Consistent beyond what ordinary pressure variation predicted.

The second took longer.

Jalen measured twice.

Then a third time.

"Broadening on the shallow edge," he said.

The Hall examiner checked his scale.

"Within CR-88 trial range."

Nobody spoke.

The third result required a lamp angled almost flat across the wax.

At first Ilyan saw nothing.

Then the examiner changed lenses.

Tiny pale grains caught the light in the recessed crescent.

Jalen's expression changed.

Damaris knew him well enough to notice.

"What?"

"Possibly nothing."

"Jalen."

"The particles are where CR-88 predicts residue could survive. That does not tell us what they are."

"Can you tell?"

"Not without sampling."

Vaun answered before anyone else could.

"Then we don't."

Jalen nodded.

The credential remained whole.

Ilyan looked through the glass.

Eren Vale's false name sat above a sponsor lineage that had survived inspection because it looked old enough to be trusted.

The dead assessor had never sponsored him.

The original die had not been required.

The credential carried two observable features predicted by CR-88 and a third possible feature they would not damage the evidence to confirm.

"How strong?" Vaun asked.

Jalen folded his arms.

"Strongly consistent with the CR-88 replication method or a materially similar derivative. Not unique attribution. Another competent examiner could have independently developed comparable technique."

Serrat said, "But this method was in the Emergency Substitution Review before Revision Seventeen."

"Yes."

"And the credential was issued under the substituted-witness lineage procedure?"

Damaris answered.

"Yes."

The room went quiet.

There were moments when silence was not dramatic.

It was simply what happened when everyone had reached the same uncomfortable place and nobody needed it explained.

Vaun broke it.

"Now I can ask for the roster."

Ilyan looked at her.

She was already writing.

---

They ate late in the Hall courtyard because Damaris had to return to her committee and refused to do so hungry.

Someone had bought road pies from the Selvaran stall outside the west gate. Rhyl reappeared carrying two paper packets and, with exaggerated care, handed neither of them to a River Ledger clerk.

Damaris took one.

"Brave."

"I crossed a street."

"Songs will be written."

"Bad ones, probably."

She leaned into him while she ate.

It had become ordinary quickly.

Not invisible.

Ordinary.

People looked. Some smiled. One elderly Hall clerk looked at Rhyl's hand resting at Damaris's waist and then at Damaris's expression and returned to her lunch with the serene indifference of someone who had survived several generations of young adults discovering each other.

Ilyan sat on the low wall opposite them.

Thessa stole half his pie.

"You had your own."

"Past tense."

"That isn't an argument."

"It worked."

He let her keep it.

Serrat remained inside with Vaun, preparing the necessity showing for the sealed review roster.

The handwriting result mattered.

The credential result mattered more.

But neither had produced the thing Ilyan had feared and, somewhere shamefully, expected: his uncle's name appearing neatly beneath the annotation.

He did not know whether he was relieved.

He did not know whether relief would survive the roster.

Thessa finished the stolen half of his lunch.

"You're doing it again."

"What?"

"Trying to have tomorrow's disaster today."

Ilyan looked at her.

"That's annoyingly good."

"I know."

"Did Avera teach you that?"

"No. She says if you're going to panic, panic after you've checked whether the bridge is actually falling."

"That's less elegant."

"Wardens."

Across the courtyard, Damaris had her forehead against Rhyl's shoulder while he said something too quiet to hear. She laughed into his coat.

For a moment Ilyan envied the simplicity of it.

Then he remembered that Damaris's aunt had put her on a public committee partly because visibility could be useful, that Rhyl's House could turn a wilderness route into political leverage without changing a single tree, and that half the court now knew exactly where Damaris preferred to put her hands when she thought nobody important was looking.

Nothing was simple.

Some things were still good.

That would have to be enough.

Vaun came out of the Hall with Serrat behind her.

She held one sheet.

"The roster request is filed."

Ilyan stood.

"How long?"

"Serrat can authorize a preliminary identity match today. Full protected-role disclosure requires the senior countersignature."

Serrat looked at him.

"There is another matter."

Ilyan waited.

"The Tal comparison die."

Damaris straightened.

The separate chain.

Serrat continued.

"Saren's receiving inventory does not identify individual dies inside the twenty-one comparative-property bundles. But the bundle-level retention ledger survives. One bundle from North Quay was transferred out of Crate Seventeen before dissolution."

"Where?" Vaun asked.

"Back to Credential Integrity."

Ilyan felt disappointment before he understood why.

Then Serrat added, "Under the same implementation audit that retained the CR-88 working copy."

Nobody moved.

Vaun's expression hardened.

"Same audit is not same object."

"No."

"Do we have the bundle number?"

"Yes."

"Do we know Tal's die was in it?"

"No."

"Then that's tomorrow."

Serrat nodded.

A loaded handoff.

Not an answer.

Ilyan found he preferred those now.

Damaris looked at the committee packet under her arm, then at Rhyl.

"I have to go discover whether two bridge authorities are thieves or idiots."

Rhyl kissed her hair.

"Aim high. They could be both."

She smiled, kissed him once, and left.

Ilyan watched her cross the courtyard toward the River Ledger offices.

Thessa bumped his shoulder.

"Come on."

"Where?"

"You owe me a pie."

"You stole mine."

"Exactly. Now you don't have one either."

He stared at her.

She was already walking.

Ilyan followed.

Behind them, the Hall kept its sealed rosters, its old hands, its inherited mistakes and the names of people who had once been given technical questions without being told what those answers might later permit.

Tomorrow they would ask who had posed the question.

Today, for once, they had learned what the answer had been used to make possible.
