# Chapter Six D — The First Receipt

Examiner Serrat sent for Ilyan before breakfast.

The message arrived at Reedglass folded into a narrow black envelope with no seal beyond the Hall's ordinary receiving stamp. Ilyan found it beside his tea while Rhyl was attempting to convince Damaris that the River transport committee could not possibly require a packet thick enough to stun livestock.

"It has bridge schedules," Damaris said.

"It has ambitions."

"It has appendices."

"That is what I said."

Damaris kicked him lightly beneath the table.

Rhyl smiled into his cup.

Ilyan opened the note.

Thessa watched his face from across the table.

"Good?"

"Serrat has the supplemental order."

"That isn't what I asked."

Ilyan read the last line again.

**The requested packet history survives. Attend Restricted Testimony at second bell if you wish to witness inspection.**

"I don't know yet."

Thessa reached for another piece of toast.

"Better."

Damaris had already stopped pretending to read her committee agenda.

"The packet itself?"

"He says the history survives. Not the packet."

She nodded once.

No one made the sentence carry more than it did.

That had become a habit among them. An irritating one. A necessary one.

Rhyl leaned back in his chair.

"I'm taking her to the Ledger."

Damaris looked at him.

"You are walking in the same direction as me."

"With exceptional professionalism."

"You kissed me against the canal wall yesterday."

Thessa looked delighted.

Rhyl considered this.

"Professionally."

Damaris's mouth twitched despite herself.

Ilyan looked down before he smiled. There was something indecently comforting in the fact that the world still contained ridiculous people before second bell.

Then he folded Serrat's note and put it inside his coat.

---

Restricted Testimony occupied the oldest eastern range of the Hall of Veils, where the windows were too narrow for fashionable architecture and the doors had been built when iron was cheaper than trust.

Thessa came with him.

She had not asked whether she should.

Ilyan had not asked her to stop.

Judge Vaun was already in the inspection room when they arrived. Examiner Sael stood beside her with a cup of something that smelled strong enough to remove varnish. Serrat had laid three folders on the table.

No blue book today.

No dramatic crate.

Three folders, two pencils, a sand tray and a clerk who looked as though he had been born disappointed by handwriting.

Serrat indicated the chairs.

"The supplemental order authorizes the packet-specific receipt chain, transfer annotations and disposition record. It does not authorize inspection of unrelated testimony in RSV-17-S."

Vaun sat.

"Understood."

Ilyan sat beside Thessa.

Serrat opened the first folder.

"CR-88 entered Joint Office protected-review custody on Bloomtide twenty-third, two hundred sixty-four Sunder Moon."

Ilyan stared at him.

He knew the date before he finished converting it.

"Before Revision Seventeen."

"Yes."

Sael set down his cup.

"How long before?"

The clerk slid a certified chronology across the table.

Revision Seventeen had been filed seventeen days before Silver Night in the current year.

CR-88 had entered protected review eighteen years earlier.

The apparent answer should have been disappointing.

Instead Ilyan felt his pulse slow.

Eighteen years meant the packet had not been slipped into the review chain immediately before Silver Night to manufacture a convenient credential. Whatever had happened was older.

Older did not mean harmless.

It meant the question had changed.

Vaun read the first receipt.

"Reason for transfer?"

Serrat turned the page.

**Comparative methods retained for recurrent credential-defect reference. Joint review requested after sponsor-die defect reproduced across nonmatching impressions.**

Thessa leaned forward.

"Recurrent reference."

"Yes," Vaun said.

Ilyan read the line again.

The old case had not vanished into a conspiracy cupboard.

Someone had considered it useful enough to keep.

"Who requested joint review?"

Serrat looked to the clerk.

The clerk produced another slip.

**Office of Credential Integrity, technical referral. Reviewing liaison: Joint Order Liaison Desk.**

No personal name.

No Dmitrigical Review.

No Valeric.

Ilyan was surprised by the relief and disliked himself for it.

Thessa noticed that too. She said nothing.

Serrat opened the second folder.

"The packet remained under comparative-method reference for sixteen years. It was consulted nine times. Eight consultations concern credential forgery, sponsor-seal replication or inherited die damage. The ninth is the transfer relevant to your order."

There it was.

The clerk placed a single certified slip in front of Vaun.

The date was twenty-nine days before Silver Night.

Twelve days before Revision Seventeen was filed.

Ilyan's mouth went dry.

**Temporary transfer: CR-88 defect-inheritance comparison packet. From Credential Integrity reference custody to Emergency Substitution Review working file. Purpose: assess reproducibility of inherited sponsor-seal defects under substituted witness-credential procedure. Return required at review closure.**

Below it:

**Requested through Joint Order Liaison — Dmitrigical Review.**

Nobody spoke for several breaths.

Thessa's hand closed around the edge of her chair.

Ilyan looked at Vaun.

She was reading the line a second time.

"Twelve days," he said.

Sael answered him.

"Before Revision Seventeen was filed."

"Yes."

The room seemed suddenly too small.

The old counterfeit case had not merely been associated with Revision Seventeen years later when the Joint Office dissolved.

Its comparison packet had been deliberately borrowed into the emergency substitution review before Revision Seventeen existed.

That still did not tell them why.

It did not tell them what the three sheets contained.

It did not prove the procedure had been designed to facilitate Silver Night.

But the chronology no longer allowed the comfortable explanation that the two files had merely been shelved together afterward.

Vaun looked at Serrat.

"Return?"

Serrat opened the third folder.

"Not at review closure."

Ilyan felt Thessa turn slightly beside him.

The disposition record was shorter.

**Review closed: Revision Seventeen adopted. Supporting comparative packet retained under RSV-17-S pending credential-integrity implementation audit.**

A second notation followed three months later.

**Original CR-88 comparison packet returned to Credential Integrity reference custody. Certified working copy retained in RSV-17-S.**

Ilyan stopped.

"Copy."

"Yes," Serrat said.

"So the packet in Crate Seventeen at dissolution—"

"Was the original returned to Credential Integrity custody."

Thessa finished the thought.

"And Annex C put it back into sixteen-two because the exception clerk saw it belonged to the protected review that had retained a copy."

Serrat nodded.

"That is what the surviving transfer annotations indicate."

Vaun tapped the disposition line.

"Does the certified working copy survive in RSV-17-S?"

"Yes."

Ilyan looked at him.

Serrat did not make him ask twice.

"The present order establishes relevance to the packet chain. I will permit inspection of that copy's three sheets under the same restriction: no unrelated testimony, no protected identities beyond what appears on the sheets themselves."

Vaun's expression did not change.

"Today?"

"The copy is being brought from lower custody."

Thessa exhaled through her nose.

"You could have led with that."

Serrat regarded her.

"Then you would have listened less carefully to the chronology."

Thessa looked at Ilyan.

"I continue to understand why you dislike him."

Sael drank from his cup.

"Educational people."

"Exactly."

Ilyan almost laughed, but his eyes had returned to the date.

Twenty-nine days before Silver Night.

The old counterfeit research entered the emergency substitution review.

Seventeen days before Silver Night, Revision Seventeen was filed.

Twelve days before Silver Night, it took effect.

Then the west-tower witness-material chain began.

He could feel his mind trying to draw the whole shape at once.

His father.

His uncle.

Dmitrigical Review.

The altered oath.

The witness screens.

The Academy dead.

Vaun's pencil touched the table once.

Ilyan looked up.

"Don't," she said.

One word.

Enough.

He sat back.

"I know."

"Do you?"

He looked again at the transfer slip.

"I know the packet was requested into the review before Revision Seventeen. I know the request passed through the Dmitrigical liaison. I don't know who made the request. I don't know whether the method was used to build a fraudulent credential. I don't know whether this had anything to do with the Academy delivery."

Vaun nodded.

"Better."

Thessa's knee touched his beneath the table.

He let it stay there.

---

The working copy arrived under two seals.

Serrat inspected both before the clerk opened the outer case.

Three sheets lay beneath old review cord, each protected by a translucent archival leaf.

Ilyan had expected diagrams.

There were some.

Mostly there were impressions.

Sponsor seals pressed into black wax. Enlarged tracings of tiny defects along the crescent edge. Measurements. Notes in two hands.

The first sheet was Mara Venn's.

The second was Selwyn Arven's.

The third bore no examiner's name on its face, only a comparison grid and a notation in the lower corner.

**Replication trial: inherited flaw reproduced from secondary impression without access to parent die.**

Thessa went very still.

Ilyan read it again.

Without access to parent die.

Vaun bent closer.

"Method?"

Serrat turned the archival leaf just enough for the lower section to be visible.

The notes described a process, but not a simple one: layered impression transfer, compensating pressure, a mineral suspension used to exaggerate shallow defects, then a second-stage cut made from the transferred pattern rather than the original die.

Ilyan understood perhaps half of it.

Enough to understand the danger.

A forged sponsor seal did not need the sponsor's original die if the forger possessed a sufficiently good impression and knew how inherited defects propagated.

The dead freight assessor's duplicated seal on Eren Vale's credential returned to him with nauseating clarity.

Thessa whispered, "Oh."

Vaun did not correct her.

The third sheet carried a later annotation in different ink.

**Applicable to emergency substituted witness credentials where parent authorization cannot be physically presented. Defect continuity may satisfy visual lineage checks if secondary reference is accepted.**

Ilyan's hands went cold.

That was not a confession.

It was worse in a quieter way.

It was a technical answer to a bureaucratic problem.

How to make a substitute credential appear descended from a legitimate authorization when the original die was unavailable.

Serrat's face had changed.

Only slightly.

The attentive neutrality remained, but the distance was gone from his eyes.

He had not known what was on the sheets either.

Ilyan realized that before Serrat said anything.

"This annotation is not original to CR-88."

Vaun looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"Different ink. Different hand. And it references emergency substituted witness credentials, which did not exist under that terminology when CR-88 was opened."

"Can you date it?"

"Not from sight."

Sael leaned over the table without touching it.

"Initials?"

There were two at the edge of the annotation.

**T.V.**

Ilyan stared.

Not Teren.

Not any name he knew.

Vaun wrote them down.

"We identify the hand before we identify the person."

Ilyan nodded.

He was grateful she had said it before his family name could manufacture another suspect for him.

The second sheet held Selwyn's three comparison impressions.

There was no question this time.

The missing comparison sheet had survived.

Not in the old case file.

Here.

Copied into the support docket of Revision Seventeen.

Selwyn's handwritten note ran along the bottom margin:

**All three retain identical crescent break despite nonmatching pressure artifacts. Replication is procedural, not coincidental.**

Thessa looked at Ilyan.

"So this is it."

"This is Selwyn's sheet," Vaun said. "Yes."

A conclusion at last.

Small enough to survive.

Serrat looked toward the clerk.

"Seal the room log. No further access to RSV-17-S without my countersignature or Judge Vaun's order until provenance on the later annotation is established."

The clerk nodded and left.

Ilyan watched Serrat.

"You didn't know."

Serrat met his eyes.

"No."

Ilyan believed him.

That was not the same as trusting him.

It was one fact.

He could live with one fact.

For the moment.

---

Damaris found them outside the Hall at midday.

She had escaped the transport committee carrying fewer papers than she had entered with, which Rhyl declared a political triumph.

He was beside her, close enough that their sleeves brushed whenever they walked.

Damaris saw Ilyan's face and stopped teasing him before she had begun.

"What happened?"

They crossed to the quieter side of the court.

Ilyan told them.

Not every technical line.

Enough.

CR-88 had entered protected review eighteen years ago. Its packet had been borrowed into the emergency substitution review twenty-nine days before Silver Night. The request had passed through the Dmitrigical liaison. Revision Seventeen followed twelve days later. A copy remained in the support docket. Selwyn's missing comparison sheet was there. A later hand had annotated the method for emergency substituted witness credentials.

Damaris's face lost color.

Rhyl asked the useful question.

"Does it tell us how Eren Vale's credential was made?"

"No."

"Could it?"

"Yes."

"Different question."

"Yes."

Damaris looked toward the Hall doors.

"And the initials?"

"T.V. We don't know whose."

Rhyl's mouth tightened.

"That will produce an unpleasant number of guesses."

"Which is why we're not guessing."

Damaris looked at Ilyan for a long moment.

"How are you?"

He almost said fine.

She would not have believed him.

Thessa would have kicked him.

"Angry."

"At whom?"

"I haven't earned that yet."

Damaris nodded.

Then she stepped forward and hugged him.

It was not elegant. Her committee packet caught between them and dug into his ribs.

Ilyan laughed once despite himself.

"Ow."

"Sorry."

She moved the packet and hugged him again.

Rhyl looked away toward the rain-dark court, giving them privacy without going anywhere.

Thessa stayed at Ilyan's other shoulder.

The Seven were becoming very good at this strange new adult art: standing beside one another while refusing to pretend they could solve one another's lives.

When Damaris let him go, she said, "I have something less terrible."

"Please."

"The committee discovered that two bridge authorities have been billing the same dredging barge for three months."

Rhyl brightened.

"Crime?"

"Incompetence, probably."

"Tragic."

"Maeryn says I get to find out."

Ilyan smiled.

There it was again.

The world refusing to become only one thing.

Vaun emerged from the Hall behind them.

"Valeric."

Ilyan turned.

She held a fresh request slip.

"The annotation hand is next. Then we compare the CR-88 method against the fraudulent credential without consuming or altering it. Separately, we trace Tal's die."

Separately.

Still.

Ilyan nodded.

"And the Academy parcel?"

"Waits until the credential evidence earns it."

He had expected frustration.

Instead he felt relief.

One chain at a time.

Not because the chains were unconnected.

Because if they were connected, he wanted the connection to survive being hated.

Damaris slipped her hand into Rhyl's.

Thessa bumped Ilyan's shoulder with hers.

Rain began again over the Hall court, light enough that nobody moved for shelter.
