# Chapter Eighteen — The Reserve Hand

The Commonwealth Seal Office occupied a building that looked as though it had been designed by someone who distrusted ornament and then inherited a niece who carved flowers.

The lower two floors were severe grey stone. The windows were narrow, the steps broad, the brass plates beside the doors polished without affection. Above them, however, ran a band of climbing roses cut into the lintels so delicately that the petals seemed to lift when the morning light crossed them.

Ilyan noticed the roses because Thessa stopped on the pavement and looked up.

"Your civic republic has flowers."

"I didn't know I owned it."

"You complain about it as if you do."

"That is citizenship."

She smiled and caught the front of his coat before he could climb the steps.

For a moment he thought she had found something wrong with his collar.

Then she kissed him.

Not long. Not discreetly enough to be mistaken for anything else, either.

A clerk coming down the stairs looked away with the practiced generosity of someone who had seen young adults make poor use of public architecture before.

When Thessa released him, Ilyan glanced toward the doors.

"Was that for courage?"

"No."

"Good luck?"

"No."

"An attempt to compromise a Commonwealth officer before we enter?"

"You are not a Commonwealth officer."

"Then I am out of theories."

She straightened the collar she had wrinkled.

"Eleven days."

The words landed more heavily than the kiss.

Yesterday there had been twelve.

He knew perfectly well how counting worked. He disliked the practical demonstration.

"I noticed."

"You looked at the calendar before breakfast."

"I look at calendars."

"You stared at that one like it had insulted your mother."

"My mother is harder to insult."

Thessa's thumb brushed once over his lapel.

"I have fittings this afternoon. Warden field coats."

"Tragic."

"You like me in uniform."

"That is unrelated to the administrative burden of tailoring."

"Of course."

She stepped away before he could kiss her again.

"Come on. Find your ugly red wax."

They climbed the steps together.

The Seal Office did not, in fact, keep wax in the public reading room.

It kept indexes.

Thousands of them.

The clerk who received Judge Vaun's authorization was a compact elderly woman named Nera Pell whose spectacles were attached to a silver chain and whose expression suggested that she had spent sixty years watching important people discover that records did not become more cooperative when shouted at.

She read the authorization twice.

"Commission number?"

Ilyan gave it to her.

"Date?"

"Bloomtide, 264. Three days before Silver Night."

"You want the commission instrument?"

"The non-identifying face, appointing authority, authority class, activation condition, duration, accountability archive, and any lawful cross-reference to JEC-R/6. We do not want bearer identity, protected destination, route geometry, or protected testimony."

Nera looked over her spectacles at Judge Vaun.

"He rehearsed that."

"Several times," Vaun said.

"I did not."

Thessa coughed into her hand.

Nera turned back to the authorization.

"The commission is old enough for ordinary civic release unless the protective schedule survived closure. If it survived, this paper gets you the outer instrument only."

"That's enough," Ilyan said.

"People who say that usually come back."

"Then you can enjoy being right twice."

Nera's mouth twitched.

She disappeared through a door behind the desk.

Thessa leaned against the reading table.

"I like her."

"You like anyone who is rude to me."

"Not anyone."

"A select professional class."

"Exactly."

Vaun was reading the room notice that prohibited ink near original seals. She did not look at them.

"If you two become more distracting, I will separate you like children."

"We are adults," Thessa said.

"Then the embarrassment will be worse."

That ended the matter for nearly a minute.

Nera returned carrying a narrow grey archival box.

She placed it on a felt pad, opened it, and removed three folded sheets and a card bearing a seal impression under translucent protective paper.

The wax on the card was red.

Not Valeric red.

Bright chalk-red, with a faint pale grain through it.

Ilyan did not feel triumph.

He felt the immediate urge to find Teren and ask whether this was what he remembered, followed by the equally immediate recognition that doing so would prove very little.

Thessa saw him looking.

"Administrative corroboration," she murmured.

"Not identity."

"I know."

Nera glanced between them.

"Good. Saves me a speech."

The commission's title occupied only one line.

**Compact Emergency Civic Commission 264-B/17.**

Below it, in smaller script:

**Temporary Commission for Protected Continuity of Civic Witness and Student Safeguard.**

Ilyan read the second line again.

Student safeguard.

No one in the room spoke for several seconds.

Vaun moved first. She bent closer without touching the paper.

"Appointing authority?"

Nera indicated the lower block.

The commission had not been created by a Great House.

It had not been created by the Crown.

It had been constituted jointly under a dormant clause of the Sunder Compact by the Commonwealth High Magistracy and the chartered Academy Student Safeguard Board.

Ilyan felt Thessa straighten beside him.

"Academy-independent," she said.

"Yes," Vaun replied.

The Safeguard Board was not the Academy administration. It existed precisely because Academy masters, noble patrons, parents, and House officers could all become interested parties in disputes involving protected students. Its members were appointed from civic magistracy, temple advocates, licensed guardians, and retired educators barred from current Academy employment.

The commission had been given a narrow purpose.

If an emergency created a credible risk that ordinary institutions could not safely hold a protected witness, protected student, or protected testimony without exposing that person to an interested authority, the commission could accept receiving custody from another lawful emergency instrument.

It could not investigate.

It could not arrest.

It could not compel testimony.

It could not authorize force.

It could receive, hold, attest, and later transfer.

Thessa read the powers twice.

"A hand waiting on the other side."

Vaun nodded.

"That is what it was built to be."

Ilyan looked at the activation clause.

The commission would become active only upon paired certification from two officers drawn from different appointing bodies. Neither could be serving the Academy administration, a Great House household, or the Dmitrigical Tribunal at the time of certification.

"So the Tribunal couldn't simply activate its own receiver," he said.

"Correct," Vaun said.

"And neither could the Academy."

"Correct."

"Who certified it?"

Nera tapped the authorization.

"That is inside the surviving protective schedule."

Ilyan looked at the blanked block.

He had expected frustration. Instead he felt something almost like relief.

The mechanism had boundaries.

Someone had thought about capture.

Someone had known enough, three days before Silver Night, to create a receiver that neither the Tribunal nor a House nor the Academy administration could control alone.

That did not tell him whether those people were wise, frightened, compromised, manipulated, or all four.

It did tell him they had believed there was something worth protecting.

"Duration?" Vaun asked.

Nera turned the second sheet.

Thirty days unless renewed. Automatic dissolution upon certified transfer of all protected holdings and completion of the accountability abstract. If dissolved while protective risk remained, the accountability archive survived under Commonwealth seal until a magistrate authorized release.

The commission had dissolved after nineteen days.

Its accountability archive had not.

Ilyan's pulse quickened.

"Where?"

Nera pointed to the final line.

**Commonwealth High Magistracy — Sealed Civic Accountability Repository, Witness Continuity Series.**

There it was.

Not a witness.

Not a route.

Not an accusation.

A shelf.

The other half of the record had a lawful home.

---

They did not go there immediately.

Vaun refused.

Ilyan argued for perhaps thirty seconds before realizing she was not refusing access. She was refusing haste.

"The repository request needs to name the record class we can lawfully receive," she said as they left the Seal Office. "If I ask for the entire commission file, I invite a protective challenge we deserve to lose."

"We know the receiving abstract exists."

"We know Pell Aris saw a transfer authorization for it. We know the commission could lawfully receive it. We know the accountability archive survived. Those are not the same as knowing which surviving packet contains the paired abstract."

"So we ask for the index."

"Yes."

Ilyan stopped on the pavement.

Vaun stopped too.

"That was going to be my next sentence."

"I wanted the pleasure of saying it first."

Thessa laughed.

Vaun gave them both a look that had probably made defendants reconsider entire careers.

"The non-identifying accession index. Commission 264-B/17. Record classes only. Dates, custody status, transfer state, and authority necessary to open each class. Nothing that identifies a protected bearer."

Ilyan nodded.

"And if the receiving abstract is listed?"

"Then I ask for that record and no other."

"Today?"

"You have become very attached to today."

"It has the advantage of being here."

"Tomorrow will manage the same trick."

Thessa took his arm.

"I have fittings."

He looked at her.

"I remember."

"Come with me."

"To Warden tailoring?"

"You said you liked the uniform."

Vaun walked away before either of them could involve her further.

Ilyan watched her go.

"I think she abandoned the investigation."

"I think she has clerks."

That, irritatingly, was true.

The Seven had spent the first weeks after graduation behaving as if every useful development required one of them physically standing beside it. Adult institutions kept demonstrating otherwise. Requests could travel without Ilyan. Judges could read without him. Clerks could find things while he slept, ate, kissed Thessa, or performed the radical investigative act of having another obligation.

He let her pull him toward the western quarter.

"If they put you in one of those heavy green coats—"

"They will."

"I may object to the posting on aesthetic grounds."

"You have eleven days to file."

---

The Warden tailor was a broad-shouldered man named Oren who treated Thessa's body as a logistical problem and Ilyan as furniture.

"Arms up."

Thessa raised them.

Oren pulled the half-finished field coat over her shoulders, tugged it flat, pinned the side seam, and frowned at the back.

"Ride much?"

"Yes."

"In armor?"

"Sometimes."

"Then this needs another finger across the shoulder. Turn."

She turned.

Ilyan, seated beside three bolts of dark green wool, discovered that watching one's lover fitted for practical field clothing could be indecent in ways no one had warned him about.

The coat was not revealing.

Quite the opposite. It was heavy, high-collared, reinforced at the shoulders, cut long enough to cover the thighs in rain, and presently bristling with pins.

Thessa looked competent in it.

That was the problem.

She caught him looking in the mirror.

Her mouth curved.

Oren followed her gaze.

"If he distracts you on the road, leave him here."

"He is staying here."

The smile left her face a little.

Oren, who had clearly fitted enough departing officers to know when he had stepped on something tender, returned to the hem.

"Good. He'd complain about the mud."

"Constantly," Thessa said.

Ilyan considered defending himself and decided dignity had already left the room.

The tailor marked two alterations and went to fetch a heavier lining.

Thessa remained on the fitting platform.

"Come here."

Ilyan stood.

"Pins."

"I know."

He stopped in front of her.

The raised platform put her nearly level with him. She touched his jaw.

"You don't have to look like that every time someone says I'm leaving."

"What do I look like?"

"Like you're trying to solve it."

He almost denied it.

Instead he rested his hands carefully at her waist, avoiding the pinned seams.

"I don't think it's a problem."

"I know."

"I dislike it anyway."

"I know that too."

"This is inconvenient."

"Terribly."

She kissed him, slower than on the Seal Office steps.

He felt the rough unfinished edge of the coat beneath his fingers and the warmth of her through the linen underneath.

When they parted, Thessa stayed close enough that her breath touched his cheek.

"Three months," she said.

"I can count."

"Poorly, apparently. Yesterday you thought I had twelve days."

"Yesterday you did."

"And tomorrow?"

"I refuse to participate in this exercise."

She laughed against his mouth.

Oren returned carrying the lining.

"Good," he said. "You found something to do. Arms up again."

Thessa obeyed.

Ilyan went back to being furniture.

---

The accession index arrived before dinner.

Not because Ilyan had been present.

Judge Vaun sent a messenger to the Warden quarter with a copy certified for discussion outside the repository. The messenger found Ilyan and Thessa at a small wine shop with Mireth, Caelen, Damaris, Rhyl, and Lyrion, where the Seven had attempted to arrange a meal before Thessa's departure preparations consumed another evening.

The attempt had already failed in the ordinary way.

Caelen was late because a Royal Fellowship drill had overrun. Mireth had brought three living-memory petitions she refused to read at the table but also refused to leave at her rooms. Damaris had a grain-price memorandum folded inside her sleeve. Rhyl smelled faintly of horse. Lyrion had been summoned twice by a Silver Bough secretary and ignored both summonses with increasing theatricality.

Thessa's new field-coat fitting receipt sat beside the bread.

The messenger handed Ilyan the envelope.

Everyone looked at it.

No one asked him to open it.

That pleased him more than he expected.

He checked Vaun's covering note first.

**Non-identifying accession index. May be discussed with the Seven. Protected contents remain sealed. Do not infer bearer count from accession count.**

He passed the note around.

Damaris read it and pushed the wine bottle toward him.

"You are going to need that."

"Promising."

"Not necessarily."

"You have become unbearable."

"Professional development."

The index contained eleven surviving accession classes.

Administrative constitution.

Appointment certifications.

Seal and supply ledger.

Safeguard legal opinions.

Protected intake abstracts.

Custody transfer abstracts.

Expense and support accounts.

Closure certifications.

Post-closure accountability notes.

A sealed challenge file.

And, ninth on the list:

**JEC paired-receiving abstract — JEC-R/6.**

Status: retained.

Transfer date: the morning after Silver Night.

Opening authority: Commonwealth High Magistrate or delegated magical-crimes judge upon a showing that the paired initiating record is under lawful review.

Ilyan stopped reading.

Rhyl leaned forward.

"That's you."

"That's Vaun."

"You know what I meant."

"I am learning to resist being included in other people's authority."

Damaris raised her cup.

"At last."

Mireth took the page when he offered it.

"Retained means it still exists?"

"The index says it does."

"Not destroyed?"

"Not according to this."

Caelen looked at the dates.

"The morning after."

"Yes."

"Same day JEC-R/6 activated."

"Yes."

"Which still doesn't mean it was exercised."

Ilyan looked at him.

Caelen shrugged.

"You have all trained us very thoroughly."

Lyrion reached across Thessa for the page.

"What are the expense accounts?"

"Probably expenses."

"Thank you, Ilyan. The Veil has sharpened you."

He read the entry.

"Support accounts survive unsealed at category level after twenty years, protected at line-item level while bearer protection survives."

Damaris held out her hand.

Lyrion gave it to her.

She scanned the column.

"Category level could tell us whether this commission actually spent money."

"It existed nineteen days," Rhyl said. "It probably bought lunch."

"Lunch matters. Horses matter. Rooms matter. Couriers matter. If it never did anything except exist on paper, the spending should look different from a commission that actually received and supported someone."

Mireth frowned.

"But not prove who."

"Exactly."

Ilyan looked around the table.

There had been a time, not long ago, when every new scrap of information would have become a group argument about what it meant. Now the distinctions came from different mouths without prompting.

The Seven were still young.

They were still, in several important ways, spectacularly inexperienced.

But they were no longer the students who had sat beneath the oldest oak believing that keeping a secret among seven friends was a sufficient security architecture.

Thessa touched his knee beneath the table.

"Vaun can open the abstract."

"Yes."

"Tomorrow."

He looked at her.

She smiled sweetly.

"It will manage the same trick."

He laughed.

The tension around the table broke.

For a while they ate.

They talked about Thessa's route west, about whether Low March had repaired the inn roof, about Caelen's bruised shoulder, about a petition Mireth could not discuss, about a horse Rhyl wanted and could not afford, about Damaris's conviction that the wine had been watered, and about Lyrion's increasingly doomed effort to pretend he had not received two summonses.

The investigation sat among them without consuming the table.

Later, when the plates had been cleared, Ilyan read Vaun's final line again.

**Application for the JEC-R/6 receiving abstract filed. Hearing unnecessary unless challenged. Earliest lawful release: tomorrow after second bell.**

Below it, in her smaller handwriting:

**Do not come before second bell.**

Thessa read over his shoulder.

"She knows you."

"Unfortunately."

"Eleven days."

"You are abusing arithmetic."

"Ten tomorrow."

He folded the note.

Outside, Bloomtide evening had turned the street gold beneath the lamps. Somewhere beyond the roofs stood the Commonwealth repository, holding a sheet of paper that had waited eighteen years for the other half of its question to arrive.

Ilyan could wait until morning.

He was getting better at it.
