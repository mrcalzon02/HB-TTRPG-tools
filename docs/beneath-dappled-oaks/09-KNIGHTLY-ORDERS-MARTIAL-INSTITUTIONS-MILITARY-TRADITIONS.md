# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Knightly Orders and Martial Institutions

This document is active setting canon.

Elaranth is not defended only by noble House guards, Crown soldiers, magical orders, temple clergy, and local militias.

It also possesses a dense network of **knightly orders, martial fellowships, chartered wardens, military confraternities, border brotherhoods and sisterhoods, mounted companies, riverine orders, engineer corps, oath-bound guards, and religious warrior traditions**.

Some are ancient.

Some were founded during or after the Delphimi Wars.

Some are attached to the Crown.

Some are tied to a god.

Some are regionally rooted.

Some are formally independent.

Some are effectively dominated by one House while insisting, often truthfully, that their charter forbids direct House control.

They matter because military legitimacy is distributed.

A Great House may command retainers.

The Crown may command armies.

A magistrate may call militia.

But a famous knightly order can possess something harder to manufacture:

**reputation.**

A respected order may be able to:

- shame a noble into honoring a road obligation;
- refuse an unlawful command;
- certify an officer;
- provide trusted escorts;
- train heirs;
- arbitrate duels;
- defend a shrine;
- hold a bridge;
- rescue a mining town;
- hunt a monster;
- investigate battlefield cowardice;
- provide neutral bodyguards;
- lend prestige to a marriage;
- become the one institution everyone wants on their side when the banners finally come down.

---

# I. What Knighthood Means in Elaranth

"Knight" is not a single legal status.

The word covers several overlapping traditions.

A knight may be:

- a mounted noble warrior;
- a professionally trained common-born officer;
- an oath-bound member of a chartered martial order;
- a religious warrior;
- a border warden granted knightly privileges;
- a military engineer invested into an order;
- a bodyguard entrusted with royal or civic authority.

Not every knight owns land.

Not every knight is noble.

Not every noble is a knight.

This distinction matters.

A common-born veteran may possess more military prestige than a young lord who has never heard arrows in flight.

## Typical rank structure

Orders differ, but common ranks include:

- Aspirant;
- Squire or Probationer;
- Companion;
- Knight;
- Senior Knight;
- Banner-Knight;
- Chapter Captain;
- Marshal;
- Grand Marshal or Grandmaster.

Religious orders may substitute:

- Brother;
- Sister;
- Blade;
- Warden;
- Prior;
- Preceptor.

Frontier orders tend to use practical ranks.

Courtly orders tend to use impressive ones.

The difference becomes less important once somebody is trying to hold a gate.

# II. The Royal Martial Fellowship

Existing canon.

Primary association:
Moon-Crown / royal service.

Known recurring face:
**Captain Arven Tal**.

Known member:
**Caelen Avariel-Caith**.

The Royal Martial Fellowship is one of the Realm's most prestigious routes into commissioned military service.

It is not merely a social club for noble heirs.

Its training covers:

- mounted combat;
- formation command;
- field logistics;
- dueling law;
- bodyguard duty;
- campaign administration;
- siege fundamentals;
- ceremonial command;
- rules of military oath.

The Fellowship deliberately mixes young nobles with selected professional soldiers.

This is intended to prevent aristocratic officers from believing rank and competence are the same thing.

The experiment works inconsistently.

## Tradition

New Fellows are expected to spend time in:

- field units;
- garrison duty;
- road patrol;
- supply administration.

Purely ceremonial postings are considered suspicious for new members.

## Political character

House Avariel has enormous influence.

The Fellowship nevertheless guards its reputation as a Crown institution rather than private Avariel cavalry.

Its officers are acutely aware that royal service becomes dangerous when the difference between Crown and family becomes unclear.

# III. The Order of the Sundered Shield

Founding:
immediately after the Delphimi Wars.

Primary loyalty:
the Sunder Compact itself.

Patronal associations:
Aurel and Eiravel.

Seat:
Aurelith, with chapters in Sunderhall and Westgate.

Role:
constitutional defense, emergency command, neutral protection of major civic institutions.

The Order of the Sundered Shield was created specifically to embody the postwar settlement.

Its central oath is not to a person.

It is to the constitutional order binding Crown and Commonwealth together.

This makes the Order politically uncomfortable.

A member may be required to defend:

- the Crown from unlawful seizure;
- the National Council from royal intimidation;
- the royal household from mob violence;
- civic delegates from House coercion.

All four duties have occurred historically.

## Martial tradition

The Shield specializes in:

- heavy infantry;
- shield formations;
- urban defense;
- palace and council protection;
- evacuation;
- riot containment;
- command continuity during crisis.

## Recruitment

Both noble and common-born officers are accepted.

Promotion is examination-heavy.

House sponsorship can get an applicant noticed.

It is not supposed to get them passed.

## Heraldry

A shield split by a silver vertical fracture, one half crowned, one half ringed with small civic stars.

## Political problem

Every generation accuses the Order of secretly favoring whichever constitutional half the accuser currently dislikes.

The Order considers this proof that it is doing its job.

# IV. The Dawn Circlet

Primary association:
Aurel.

Role:
ceremonial guardians, royal oath escorts, battlefield command tradition, defense against unlawful usurpation.

Seat:
Aurelith.

The Dawn Circlet is older in spirit than the Sunder Compact but was heavily reformed after the Delphimi Wars.

It once served royal absolutism.

That history nearly destroyed it.

The modern Order insists that legitimate authority is not identical with obedience to whoever possesses the palace.

## Martial tradition

Known for:

- disciplined cavalry;
- royal escort;
- standard defense;
- duel certification;
- officer ethics.

## Famous rite

At investiture, the knight kneels before an empty circlet rather than a monarch.

The symbolism is deliberate:

authority is an office before it is a person.

## House relation

House Avariel has deep ties.

The Order resists becoming an Avariel family regiment.

This produces frequent tension with ambitious royal cousins.

---

# V. The Broken Spear

Founding:
late Delphimi Wars.

Primary loyalty:
veterans, battlefield dead, civilians caught in war.

Patronal associations:
Eiravel and Aurel.

Seat:
Sunderhall.

The Order of the Broken Spear began as a battlefield fraternity formed by officers who refused to continue pointless retaliatory killing after the political outcome of a campaign had already been decided.

Its members became famous for evacuating wounded soldiers from both sides.

## Martial tradition

- disciplined infantry;
- battlefield medicine;
- rear-guard actions;
- prisoner protection;
- surrender negotiations;
- casualty recovery.

## Cultural position

The Broken Spear is respected by soldiers and occasionally despised by politicians who want war to remain rhetorically simple.

## Recruitment

Veterans dominate.

Noble pedigree counts for little.

Aspirants are expected to have actually served.

---

# VI. The Western Wardens

Existing canon.

Known recurring face:
**Warden-Commander Veyra Dorn**.

Known member:
**Thessa Eris-Vey**.

Region:
Western Marches.

Role:
fortress defense, road security, border patrol, military intelligence, reserve mobilization.

The Western Wardens are more regional than courtly.

They combine elements of:

- knightly order;
- military district command;
- frontier police;
- scouting service.

## Martial tradition

- cavalry patrol;
- fortification;
- cross-country pursuit;
- convoy defense;
- small-unit tactics;
- winter campaigning.

House Eris is deeply influential, but the Wardens predate several current cadet branches and do not consider themselves anybody's household troops.

---

# VII. The Iron Hart

Region:
Western Marches and Ironspine approaches.

Primary role:
heavy cavalry, armored convoy relief, breaking sieges.

Seat:
Daeron Bastion.

House influence:
Eris-Daeron strong, but not exclusive.

The Iron Hart is famous for riding into situations sensible people are trying to leave.

Its doctrine emphasizes:

- decisive mounted action;
- relief of isolated garrisons;
- breaking encirclements;
- rapid reinforcement.

Members favor practical armor over ornamental excess.

## Reputation

Brave.

Expensive.

Hard on horses.

Harder on bridges.

---

# VIII. The Stone Banner

Patron:
Kharun.

Region:
Ironspine Mining Districts.

Primary role:
military engineering, fortification, mine rescue, siegecraft, collapse response.

Seat:
Hammerdeep.

The Stone Banner is a knightly engineer order.

Its members are expected to understand:

- masonry;
- timbering;
- excavation;
- bridges;
- fortifications;
- siege engines;
- mine collapse;
- controlled demolition.

Many are smiths, engineers, miners, or masons before investiture.

## Martial tradition

A Stone Banner knight may carry a sword.

They are more feared when carrying a measuring rod.

## Political importance

Regional Councils compete for their attention during:

- floods;
- bridge failures;
- mine disasters;
- fortification emergencies.

House Eris courts the Order heavily.

Kharun temples regard it with pride.

Contractors regard it with dread.

---

# IX. The Ember Anvil

Patron:
Kharun.

Role:
armored infantry, smith-knights, field repair, weapon certification.

Seat:
Embercross.

The Ember Anvil trains warriors who are also expected to understand the tools they carry.

Senior members commonly know:

- armor repair;
- weapon inspection;
- field forging;
- supply discipline.

The Order is strongly represented among military quartermasters and arsenal officers.

## Rivalry

The Stone Banner regards the Ember Anvil as excessively interested in weapons.

The Ember Anvil regards the Stone Banner as people who would rather marry a bridge.

Both cooperate extremely well during war.

---

# X. The Red Hart Wilderness Vigil

Existing canon.

Known recurring face:
**Huntwarden Peren Ash**.

Known member:
**Rhyl Orthell-Faen**.

Region:
Red Hart Uplands and forest frontier.

The Red Hart wilderness vigil is not always called a knightly order by urban heralds.

The Red Hart does not care.

Its experienced members perform every practical function that matters:

- scouting;
- monster hunting;
- border patrol;
- trail protection;
- rescue;
- wilderness intelligence;
- pursuit.

## Martial tradition

- bow;
- spear;
- light blade;
- animal handling;
- tracking;
- ambush;
- survival;
- terrain use.

House Orthell is strongly associated with the Vigil without fully controlling it.

---

# XI. The White Stag Riders

Region:
Northern Lake and Snow Country.

Patron:
Old White Stag in folk practice; Faelor officially in some chapters.

Role:
winter cavalry, snow-road rescue, pursuit, message carrying.

Seat:
Whitebarrow.

The White Stag Riders specialize in conditions that make conventional cavalry useless.

They maintain:

- winter mounts;
- snow-trained pack animals;
- sled teams;
- cold-weather scouts.

## Reputation

A White Stag Rider arriving at your door in Longnight usually means:

- someone is missing;
- someone has been found;
- the road is about to close;
- you are about to be asked to do something unpleasant in the snow.

---

# XII. The Order of the Green Lance

Patronal association:
Maereth and Green Covenant traditions.

Region:
Vaelwood, Aesryn Green, Eastern Grain Plain.

Role:
defense of agricultural districts, granaries, seed vaults, waterworks, rural evacuation.

The Green Lance emerged from repeated wars and famines in which armies discovered that destroying food is easier than conquering people.

The Order's doctrine treats:

- seed vaults;
- irrigation;
- granaries;
- orchards;
- mills;

as strategic infrastructure.

## Martial tradition

- mounted patrol;
- rural skirmishing;
- defense of farms and mills;
- anti-raider operations;
- evacuation.

## House relation

House Vael is strongly connected.

The Green Covenant has major influence.

The Order's charter forbids using food protection duties to settle private House feuds.

Violations have occurred.

---

# XIII. The Thornwall Companions

Region:
Greenwood and forest-edge districts.

Role:
defense against magical beasts, grove incursions, hostile enchanted flora, relic contamination.

The Thornwall is a specialist martial order trained to fight threats that ordinary soldiers may not understand.

Its members cooperate with:

- Green Covenant botanists;
- Nymrael alchemists;
- Red Hart scouts.

## Equipment

Commonly:

- hooked polearms;
- heavy gloves;
- pruning blades;
- burn-resistant cloaks;
- anti-spore masks.

Court poets find this less romantic than lances.

People whose villages are being eaten by a hedge disagree.

---

# XIV. The Silver Heron

Region:
River Cantons.

Role:
river marines, bridge seizure, anti-piracy, flood rescue, convoy escort.

Seat:
Caladris and Sereth Quays.

The Silver Heron is one of the Realm's best-known riverine martial orders.

Its knights train to fight:

- on decks;
- on narrow bridges;
- in shallow water;
- around docks.

## House relation

Caladren sponsorship is enormous.

The Order nevertheless resists becoming a private commercial navy.

Its charter requires escorting certain public relief shipments regardless of House preference.

## Famous saying

"A Heron does not need a horse to be a knight."

The Iron Hart finds this unnecessarily pointed.

---

# XV. The Bridgewardens of Nera

Patron:
Nera Bridgewife.

Role:
bridge defense, ferry protection, flood rescue, traveler safety.

Not all members are formally knights.

Senior Bridgewardens may receive knightly investiture.

They are widespread and locally beloved.

## Duties

- inspect strategic bridges;
- defend crossings;
- organize flood evacuations;
- protect repair crews;
- arbitrate emergency crossing priority.

Their strongest loyalty is often to the bridge and the communities on both sides.

This occasionally annoys everyone with banners.

---

# XVI. The Seven Roads

Patron:
Selvara.

Role:
caravan escort, messenger protection, anti-bandit warfare, road reconnaissance.

Seat:
Luneth Crossing, with chapterhouses along major routes.

The Seven Roads is a mobile order.

Members spend more time traveling than at chapterhouses.

## Martial tradition

- mounted escort;
- ambush response;
- route scouting;
- wagon defense;
- pursuit.

## Social character

The Order includes many common-born knights.

Caravan masters often trust them more than House troops because their charter prohibits favoring one merchant after accepting protection duty for another.

---

# XVII. The Argent Thorn

Primary association:
Silver Bough diplomacy.

Role:
diplomatic bodyguard, embassy protection, formal duel response, extraction.

Seat:
Silvercourt.

The Argent Thorn is the martial counterpart to elegant diplomacy.

Members are trained to:

- guard diplomats without dominating a room;
- identify threats;
- manage formal challenges;
- evacuate delegations;
- protect treaty documents.

## House relation

House Sylvaren has enormous influence.

The Order maintains professional pride around not being decorative swords at a banquet.

## Cultural reputation

Beautiful uniforms.

Extremely dangerous people.

Court painters consistently remember the first part.

---

# XVIII. The Quiet Blade

Primary association:
Truth Beyond the Veil.

Role:
protect investigators, secure dangerous evidence, arrest magically trained suspects, defend witnesses.

Seat:
Veilcross.

The Quiet Blade is controversial.

Its members combine:

- martial training;
- anti-magic restraint;
- evidence security;
- witness protection.

They are not investigators themselves in theory.

In practice, proximity to investigations makes the distinction complicated.

## Reputation

If a Quiet Blade is guarding a door, whatever is behind it is probably going to become someone's problem.

---

# XIX. The Starward Aegis

Primary association:
House Nymrael, magical courts, Academy-trained battle magic.

Role:
anti-magic defense, warded combat, protection from hostile enchantment.

Seat:
Starfall.

The Starward Aegis is a knightly order for warriors trained to fight around magic without necessarily being full mages.

Members learn:

- ward discipline;
- anti-charm drills;
- fighting through illusion;
- mage protection;
- magical hazard recognition.

Some members are minor spellcasters.

Others are not.

## Political importance

Nymrael scholars influence doctrine heavily.

The Crown uses Aegis knights for high-risk magical arrests.

---

# XX. The Moonward

Primary association:
Saelith, Eiravel, funerary and memorial traditions.

Role:
cemetery defense, protection of memorial sites, recovery of remains, defense against restless dead.

Seat:
Saint Veyren's Chair.

The Moonward began as guardians of war graves.

Over centuries it developed expertise dealing with:

- disturbed tombs;
- battlefield remains;
- spirit unrest;
- grave robbery;
- funerary processions during dangerous travel.

## Martial tradition

Somber.

Disciplined.

Uninterested in theatrics.

The Moonward has a disproportionately high number of widows, widowers, veterans, and people who joined after losing someone.

---

# XXI. The Lantern Hospitallers

Patrons:
Maereth, Eiravel, and regional healing saints.

Role:
battlefield medicine, plague cordons, evacuation, protected hospitals.

Seat:
Vireth Wells.

The Lantern Hospitallers combine martial protection with medicine.

They maintain protected field hospitals.

## Combat role

They fight primarily to:

- defend patients;
- recover wounded;
- hold evacuation corridors.

Some chapters carry weapons openly.

Others employ sworn guards.

## Neutrality

The Hospitallers' strongest custom is treating wounded enemies once they are under the Lantern.

Violating a Lantern hospital is considered one of the fastest ways to make unrelated military orders hate you.

---

# XXII. The Black Oak

Role:
royal and civic bodyguards, anti-assassination, defensive fighting.

Seat:
Aurelith.

The Black Oak specializes in one task:

keeping one specific person alive.

Its members train in:

- close protection;
- room defense;
- crowd extraction;
- carriage ambush response;
- poison awareness;
- route planning.

They are commonly hired or seconded to:

- magistrates;
- witnesses;
- foreign envoys;
- threatened heirs.

## Reputation

A Black Oak knight is supposed to be boring.

If they become interesting, something has gone wrong.

---

# XXIII. The Crimson Pennon

Role:
tournament culture, formal cavalry competition, martial display, recruiting.

Seat:
Moonmere.

The Crimson Pennon began as a tournament fellowship.

It remains partly that.

It has also become an important pipeline for identifying:

- talented riders;
- lancers;
- duelists;
- officers.

## Cultural role

The Order is glamorous.

Young nobles adore it.

Veterans roll their eyes.

The same veterans still attend the tournaments.

## Political danger

Tournament fame can become political capital.

A charismatic Pennon champion may acquire followers faster than a minor House can acquire allies.

---

# XXIV. The Grey Mantle

Role:
military judges, discipline, investigation of battlefield misconduct, officer courts.

Seat:
Westgate.

The Grey Mantle is half knightly order and half professional military judiciary.

Its members investigate:

- desertion;
- looting;
- unlawful killing;
- false surrender;
- officer misconduct;
- theft from supply trains.

## Reputation

Nobody is delighted to see them.

This is regarded as healthy.

---

# XXV. The Order of the Open Hand

Patronal association:
Lysara and Aurel.

Role:
truce escort, hostage exchange, surrender negotiation, protection of envoys under flag.

Seat:
Silvercourt and Sunderhall.

Members travel unhelmed during formal parley whenever conditions permit.

The exposed face symbolizes:

"I have come to speak, and I expect the same courage from you."

## Martial doctrine

The Order is fully combat-capable.

Its prestige depends on knowing exactly when not to draw a sword.

---

# XXVI. The Ash Riders

Region:
areas devastated during the Delphimi Wars.

Role:
rapid response to fires, raids, magical disasters, civilian evacuation.

Seat:
multiple small chapters rather than one central seat.

The Ash Riders were founded after towns repeatedly burned faster than conventional garrisons could react.

They train for:

- fast mobilization;
- evacuation;
- firebreak protection;
- relief convoy escort.

They have little patience for court ceremony.

# XXVII. House Guards Versus Knightly Orders

Every Great House maintains retainers, guards, escorts, and military clients.

These should not be confused with independent orders.

## House troops

Loyalty:
primarily to House and estate.

Funding:
House money.

Promotion:
House command.

## Knightly orders

Loyalty:
charter, oath, god, Crown, region, profession, or institutional mission.

Funding:
mixed.

Promotion:
internal structure.

House influence can be immense without becoming formal command.

This distinction creates politics.

A House heir may belong to an order that refuses the House's demand.

A House may fund a chapterhouse and then discover that the chapter votes against it.

A knight may face conflicting obligations between blood and oath.

That conflict should matter.

---

# XXVIII. Clerical and Magical Orders

Martial orders coexist with non-martial institutions.

Major examples already established include:

- Green Covenant;
- Truth Beyond the Veil;
- Silver Bough;
- River Ledger;
- Dmitrigical Arts tribunals;
- Academy magical schools;
- Moon-Crown household institutions;
- temples of the Concordant Gods;
- Little Court cults.

Some knightly orders have clerical or magical associations.

They should not collapse into the same thing.

A priest of Kharun is not automatically Stone Banner.

A Nymrael mage is not automatically Starward Aegis.

A Red Hart hunter is not automatically invested as a knight.

Institutions overlap.

They do not erase one another.

---

# XXIX. Recruitment and Social Mobility

Knightly orders provide one of the major routes by which talented commoners enter elite society.

Possible routes include:

- battlefield promotion;
- squire service;
- guild sponsorship;
- temple recommendation;
- military academy;
- House patronage;
- order examinations;
- exceptional civic service.

A common-born knight may gain:

- social prestige;
- pension;
- chapter housing;
- legal privileges;
- marriage prospects;
- access to noble circles.

This creates class tension.

Some aristocrats dislike common knights.

Some Houses deliberately cultivate them.

Some common knights become more respected than the nobles who sponsored them.

# XXX. Romance and Knightly Orders

Orders create romantic complications constantly.

A knight may be:

- forbidden to marry during a probationary term;
- expected to obtain chapter permission;
- stationed far from home;
- sworn to protect someone they desire;
- courted because their order has political influence;
- pressured to marry into a House;
- ordered to escort a former lover;
- assigned to guard a rival suitor.

Some orders encourage marriage.

Others prefer members without immediate household obligations.

Some chapters have long traditions of spouses living near chapterhouses.

The setting should not assume one universal rule.

## Uniform attraction

Military orders also create their own romantic aesthetics:

- polished boots;
- fitted riding coats;
- ceremonial armor;
- scars;
- gloves;
- sword belts;
- unit colors;
- riding posture;
- formal salutes.

Young adults notice.

Institutions pretend this is irrelevant.

It is not.

# XXXI. Rivalries

Institutional rivalry is common and usually not murderous.

Examples:

- Iron Hart versus Silver Heron over whether "real knights" require horses.
- Stone Banner versus Ember Anvil over whether wars are won by fortifications or weapons.
- Crimson Pennon versus actual campaign veterans.
- Dawn Circlet versus Sundered Shield over royal versus constitutional ceremony.
- Quiet Blade versus Grey Mantle over jurisdiction in mixed magical-military crimes.
- Red Hart versus Seven Roads over who knows wilderness routes better.
- White Stag Riders versus everyone who believes winter maps are reliable.

These rivalries create:

- jokes;
- tournaments;
- professional competition;
- recruitment battles;
- occasional fistfights;
- surprisingly effective cooperation under pressure.

# XXXIII. Story-Runner Rules

When a military or security institution appears, determine:

1. Is this House military, Crown military, local militia, or knightly order?
2. What oath governs it?
3. Who funds it?
4. Who appoints its leaders?
5. What House influences it?
6. What region does it know best?
7. What military specialty justifies its existence?
8. What rival order dislikes it?
9. What civilian institution relies on it?
10. What happens when House loyalty conflicts with order oath?

Do not create generic "knights" with no institutional identity when an established order fits.

Do not make every order cavalry.

Do not make every order aristocratic.

Do not make every order religious.

Do not make every order politically neutral.

Do not make every order corrupt.

Military institutions should have:

- doctrine;
- history;
- uniforms;
- rivalries;
- pensions;
- chapterhouses;
- widows;
- veterans;
- scandals;
- heroes;
- incompetent officers;
- excellent sergeants;
- political patrons;
- institutional memory.

They are part of civilization, not decorative armor.
