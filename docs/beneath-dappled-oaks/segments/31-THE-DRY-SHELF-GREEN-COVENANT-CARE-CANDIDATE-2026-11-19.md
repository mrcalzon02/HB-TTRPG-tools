# The Dry Shelf

The rain came into the Covenant infirmary on people's clothes. It dripped from two cloaks onto the tiles, collected beneath a chair, and darkened the edge of Mireth's working skirt when she knelt to move a traveller's bag.

A man held his hand inside a wet scarf. The woman beside him could put weight on one foot, but only briefly. They had come in from the western road. Neither knew whether the next carrier would run before nightfall.

The senior healer took them behind the screen. Mireth went to fetch clean linen.

The caretaker was standing before the cupboard with its doors open. On the upper shelf lay twelve folded lengths, dry and wrapped against the damp. She touched each bundle as she counted.

“Two for arrivals,” she said. “Ten set aside for tomorrow's dressings.”

“May I take the two?”

“Of course. But if there's a third arrival, you must come and ask me again.”

Mireth carried the linen to the healer, then returned for water. The kitchen kettle was hot; the second stood empty. She filled it and watched the caretaker feed another piece of wood beneath the fire.

“We could use tomorrow's cloth,” Mireth said.

“We may have to. Tomorrow's people will still be here.”

The caretaker's sleeves were wet above the elbows. The laundry room had no sun, and rain was working its way beneath the outer door. A row of washed cloth hung from the ceiling poles without stirring.

“The afternoon teaching hour,” Mireth said. “Could we give it to the wash?”

“Whose hour?”

“Mine, at least. The healer will have to decide about the rest.”

The caretaker looked toward the screen, where the woman was answering questions in a tired voice. “Your pupils came for instruction.”

“I know.”

“And they'll come tomorrow, having lost an afternoon they can't get back.”

Mireth felt a small, absurd flare of annoyance. She had spent the morning arranging a lesson she wanted to give. The waiting pupils had done nothing to deserve its cancellation.

“Then I should tell them myself,” she said.

The senior healer emerged to request another basin of clean hot water. Mireth explained the cloth and the proposed change. After a glance at the cupboard, the healer agreed to postpone the demonstration, not the existing dressing rounds. She would assess the new arrivals; no one else was to promise them a particular outcome.

Mireth went out beneath the covered walk. Three waiting pupils looked up from their notes. She told them what had happened, without showing them the patients or offering them practice at anyone's expense.

One folded her notebook sharply. Another asked whether the hour would be made up.

“I don't know yet,” Mireth said. “I'll tell you when I do.”

Back inside, the caretaker had started a fresh washing basin. The clean bundles on the upper shelf remained counted, not replenished. Mireth tied up her sleeves and reached for the basket.
