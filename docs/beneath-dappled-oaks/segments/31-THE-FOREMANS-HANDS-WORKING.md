# The Foreman's Hands

The cooper's nephew had been examined by a physician, bandaged, and forbidden to lift anything heavier than a water jug for a fortnight. His employer had also stopped his wages until someone decided who was responsible for the falling timber.

He sat beside the Hall window with his injured arm in a blue sling. His uncle broke pieces from a loaf neither of them seemed hungry enough to eat. The foreman stood behind them, holding a narrow leather book against his coat.

Ilyan recognized the cooper from the morning he had been sent away to find a physician. The man recognized Ilyan too, and looked at him without pleasure.

"You found the foreman," Ilyan said.

"He came when the master told him."

"I came when the new brace was finished," the foreman replied. "The shop has to stand while we argue about what happened."

Neral brought them into the smaller workroom, where a pot of mint drooped over the sill. He gave the nephew a chair and asked whether he wanted his uncle present. The young man said yes.

"Then he stays."

The nephew had been carrying seasoned staves from the drying rack when the upper bundle slipped. He tried to catch it. The weight drove him into the lower rail. He had not seen the binding fail, if it had failed at all.

"I heard the fall," the foreman said. "I didn't see it."

"Then why bring your book?" Ilyan asked.

"Because I know which rack he was using."

The entries were cramped but regular. Two days before the accident, three workers had loaded the upper shelf while a rush order occupied the lower space. A different hand had noted that the lower rail needed tightening.

"Whose hand?" Neral asked.

"The master's."

The nephew sat forward.

"He knew it was broken?"

"He knew it needed work. I can't tell you what gave way. I was at the river job that morning."

"Did you tell the master the boy was careless?" Ilyan asked.

"I told him the boy tried to catch a falling bundle. I would have done the same stupid thing at his age."

The nephew looked at him for the first time.

"You said it was my fault."

"I said I couldn't tell whose fault it was."

"That's not what he told me."

Outside, a cartwheel struck a loose paving stone. The driver shouted at the street as if it could apologize.

Neral requested the original loading book, the repair entry, and the physician's certificate. The master's account would be taken separately. Nothing in the foreman's testimony decided the wage claim. The nephew would at least speak at his own hearing rather than be discussed by people who had not asked him what happened.

After they left, Ilyan helped Neral return the chairs.

"I thought the foreman would lie."

"Why?"

"Because he didn't come when we first sent for him."

"He was repairing a rack."

"I know that now."

Neral gathered the papers. "You can be annoyed that someone is late without deciding why before he arrives."

---

The depot's answer came six working days after Vaun's inquiry had left the outgoing tray.

It was folded around a certificate no larger than a dinner invitation. Neral checked the seal and enclosure count before carrying it to the judge.

The custodian had examined the surviving requisition schedule for the transferred returns-series volume. **No requisition by the Tribunal Contingency Secretariat was recorded during Edrin Salver's two-week assignment to its returns desk.**

The certificate said nothing about copies, informal consultation, other offices' requests, or the contents of the volume. It answered the question Vaun had signed.

Ilyan read it twice.

"Useful," Neral said.

"Yes."

"You sound disappointed."

"I wanted him to have asked for it."

Vaun waited while he looked at the ink. He had imagined Salver retrieving the old register and recognizing the earlier matter. The image had acquired furniture, lamplight, even a particular tired expression on the man's face. None of it had come from a witness.

"The later desk isn't our route," he said.

"Not by an official requisition in that interval," Vaun replied.

"I could ask whether he saw a copy."

"On what basis?"

He could think of several suspicions and no evidence that would justify the question. He set the certificate down.

"The earlier office," he said. "We know he logged the lawful timing material and later handled the interference memorandum. We don't know who ordered the memorandum circulated or who received it."

"Then ask where that office kept its circulation records."

He reached for a sheet and began writing too much. He crossed out the demand for every communication during Salver's employment. He removed Salver's name from the heading. What remained was a request for the existence and custodian of the early memorandum's administrative circulation register. Whether any entries could later be disclosed was a separate question.

Vaun read the draft.

"Leave it with me until the afternoon docket is finished."

The nephew's physician's certificate lay beside her hand. His hearing had a date now, and his shoulder would still be injured when it arrived.

"All right," Ilyan said.

---

The foreman was collecting his book when Ilyan came downstairs. His sleeves were rolled up; a pale scar crossed his thumb where an old cut had healed badly.

"Will they pay the boy?" he asked.

"I don't know."

"I don't want him turned out."

"Then say so when the master asks what you think."

The foreman studied him, apparently uncertain whether a Valeric was entitled to offer advice about wages. Then he tucked the book under his arm and went back toward the workshop.

Neral found Ilyan on the steps and handed him a bowl of barley with spring onions and a little smoked fish. The kitchen had been clearing the last of supper.

Students passed the gate in pairs, their bright gowns moving through the Bloomtide evening. Ilyan watched them until the old ache became bearable again.

"Thessa would have called that man stubborn," he said.

"Was he?"

"Very."

"Then she would have been right."

"She would also have told me to eat before making another enemy of a clerk."

Neral took a spoonful of barley.

"Perhaps you should write to her."

"I already have."

"Then let the post do its work."

Ilyan ate while the food was warm. Upstairs, the depot certificate had been filed with the question it answered. The new circulation request lay unsigned on Vaun's desk. The injured young man's hearing had been scheduled. None of the three matters was finished.

For once he did not go back upstairs to hurry them.
