# The Wages Already Earned

The cooper's nephew arrived before the Hall opened. His injured arm hung in a blue sling. His uncle tried to straighten his collar, and he turned away.

“I can do that.”

“You've been doing it badly.”

“Then I'll do it badly.”

Neral moved a chair out of the doorway. Beyond the arcade, Bloomtide rain had left the courtyard shining. The master cooper waited with a wage ledger and a leather purse. He had old resin in the seams of his nails and the tired look of someone who had worked late to finish an order, only to discover that finishing it was the beginning of another problem.

The foreman came in smelling of wet timber. He glanced at the sling.

“Are you fit to stand?”

“I can sit.”

“That wasn't what I meant.”

“I know.”

A guild assessor had come to examine the apprenticeship pay account. Judge Vaun would preserve the testimony; the injury claim belonged to a later proceeding. The assessor opened the ledger to the last completed pay period. Five days were marked in the master's careful hand.

“Those are his,” the master said.

“And the payment?”

“I held it until the loss was counted.”

“The damaged staves?”

“And two men repairing the rack instead of making barrels.”

The apprentice shifted in his chair. “I didn't drop them.”

“I haven't said you did.”

“You told my uncle I cost you a day's work.”

“You did. That isn't the same as saying you caused the fall.”

Beneath the five days was a pencilled deduction for damaged stock, entered after the accident. The apprentice had not acknowledged it.

“Did he agree to the charge?” the assessor asked.

“He hasn't agreed to anything since it happened.”

“Then we can settle the earned days and leave the damage claim.”

The master took his hand from the page. “If I pay now and the guild later says the damage was his, what do I recover it from?”

“You can bring that claim.”

“By then he may have left the shop.”

The boy looked up sharply. “I haven't said I'm leaving.”

“You haven't said you're coming back.”

“The physician told me not to lift. I can still sharpen and mark staves.”

“Some of that takes two hands.”

“Some doesn't.”

His uncle leaned forward. “Don't quarrel about your place before they've paid you.”

“It is my place.”

Neral set a jug of water within reach and returned to his notes. Ilyan recognized the silence as a decision. The young man had come to speak for himself, and he was doing it.
