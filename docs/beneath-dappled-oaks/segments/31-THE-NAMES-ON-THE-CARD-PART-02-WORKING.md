# The Names on the Card — The Missing Six

The memorial paragraph was short. It honored the thirty-six students lost at the Academy. Below it the proof promised a musical interlude and an exchange of gifts. There was no mention of the servants.

Lyrion held the page by its corners.

"Six more," he said.

Alenor stopped sorting ribbons.

"I know."

"Then why aren't they here?"

"The reception office copied the Academy's student roll. The servants' names are kept in a separate household register. The office has requested the public memorial wording, but the reply has not arrived."

He had been ready to accuse someone of choosing the more fashionable dead. The omission was still there, whatever its cause. He disliked how relieved he was to discover that somebody had already noticed it.

"Can the printer wait?"

"Until the second bell. After that, we pay for new plates."

"How much?"

Alenor gave him the figure. It was more than he had expected and less than the cost of the flowers in the entrance hall.

"Could we print the count now and the names later?"

"We could. The guest copies need the programme. The memorial sheet can be an insert."

"Would that be acceptable?"

"To the dead?"

He looked up.

"To the people responsible for the reception," she said. "I cannot speak for the others."

She slid him a blank page.

"Write what you actually want them to do."

He wrote quickly, angrily, and too long. By the end of the first paragraph he had accused the reception office of making his friends into ornaments and the Academy of forgetting the people who had kept its lamps lit.

Alenor read it without interrupting.

"Do you want a reply," she asked, "or an apology?"

"Both."

"Choose which one you need before second bell."

He took back the page. The dog in the courtyard had finally surrendered the glove. Its owner was now trying to persuade the boy to return it.

Lyrion began again.

He removed the accusation and left the practical requests. No seats would be assigned to people who had not accepted invitations. The programme would record thirty-six students and six servants, pending the Academy's approved public wording. The memorial names could follow on an insert if the custodian supplied them in time. The reception office would be told what the change cost before anyone agreed to spend it.

He reread the page. It was shorter than the first version and made him feel considerably less heroic.

Alenor took it.

"Better. Now bring it to Ilvaren yourself."

"Will he be angry?"

"Perhaps. He has been an ambassador longer than you have been offended by printing."

She reached for the next bundle of cards. Lyrion went to find his coat.
