# Two Unsettled Accounts

By the fourth morning, the cooper's nephew had stopped counting the rent coins. There were enough for a portion, not enough for the whole demand. Counting them again would not change that.

His uncle stood in the doorway with the letter from the night before.

“Shall I take the money as well?”

“No. First ask whether he'll record a part-payment as part-payment. If you give him the coins before he answers, we won't know what he's agreed to.”

“He gave you four days.”

“And this is the fourth.”

The cooper put the envelope inside his coat. He almost added the relief-counter receipt. His nephew saw his hand move.

“Leave that here. It proves they took my application, not that anyone owes me an award.”

“I know.”

“Then carry what I wrote.”

Behind the baker's shop, the landlord read the offer while his ovens warmed the wall. The cooper watched him look toward the rent book, and waited for him to turn the page.

“I'll have to check how a short payment is entered,” the landlord said. “I haven't accepted one yet.”

“Will you give him that answer in writing?”

“I can acknowledge the question today.”

He dated a slip to show the letter had arrived before the extension expired. He did not amend the amount due or extend the date. The cooper carried the slip home before the market closed, with no coins missing from his nephew's piles.

The young man read it in silence.

“He hasn't said yes,” his uncle offered.

“He hasn't said no. Tomorrow I'll need to know which.”

He set the acknowledgment beside the other papers. He had spent the morning obtaining a record of a question, not another day's rent.

At the western Warden station, Thessa found that the draft report had done a kindness she had not authorized. Someone had summarized the bridge payment as *reconciled*.

She laid the intermediary's clearing-book copy beside it. The advance and the later balance fitted together. The carpenter's final receipt had not been independently confirmed. Nor had anyone produced the quarry dispatch or the treasury entry that might explain the road-stone money.

Captain Sorn read the correction over her shoulder.

“You can say what the books support.”

“Then I must also say what they don't.”

“The commander wants the road report before supper.”

Thessa drew a line through *reconciled* and substituted *provisionally accounted for, subject to final receipt*. The dangerous verge belonged in the patrol report regardless of what the payment records eventually showed. She would not make the workmen guilty to explain a missing delivery, or make a missing delivery harmless because their wages appeared accounted for.

Sorn took the report. The hour spent making it accurate was an hour they could not spend inspecting the next section of road.

“Tomorrow, then,” Sorn said.

“Tomorrow,” Thessa answered, leaving the accountant's unanswered questions on their own page.
