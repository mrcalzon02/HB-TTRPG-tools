# The Answer We Give

The invitation came on paper too good for a question about supper.

Mireth laid it beside a corrected infirmary roster. Her mother had written only that Caelen would be welcome at the family table, should his duties permit. There was no mention of an understanding, no promise of an announcement.

Caelen arrived with two warm rolls in a cloth and an apology for being late. The weapons store had required another count.

“Did you lose a sword?”

“We found one. The difficulty was persuading the book to admit it.”

He saw the letter. His expression changed.

“Mine has asked whether I intend to go.”

“Does she intend to come?”

“No. She intends to know what the rest of us are doing.”

Mireth broke a roll in half and offered him the larger piece. They ate on the covered step outside the Covenant workroom.

“I want you to meet them properly,” she said. “I don't want supper reported as a joining of two households.”

“I want to go,” Caelen said. “Not to demonstrate that Crown officers are welcome in your mother's drawing room.”

“You might be welcome there anyway.”

“I hope so. I'd rather be welcome because I can speak without being instructed.”

She smiled, but the thought stayed between them. The invitation was kind. Kindness would not prevent a cousin from repeating who had eaten at whose table, or a patron from deciding that Mireth could now procure an introduction through Caelen.

“My mother may ask about the Covenant's intake,” Mireth said. “I can tell her what the public notice says. Not promise a place we cannot teach.”

“And if someone asks me about appointments or the palace?”

“You can tell them whether the bread is good.”

“I am qualified to judge bread.”

“On most days.”

Caelen brushed a crumb from his cuff. “What if they mistake our going for consent to discuss us?”

“Then we correct them. Together, if we're both there. Separately if one of us needs to leave.”

“Would you leave?”

“If they stop speaking to me and begin speaking about me, yes.” She watched him. “Would you?”

“Yes. Though I should like the chance to enjoy the soup first.”

She laughed. It was a relief that he had not offered to brave the entire evening on her behalf, or asked her to sacrifice her own work to prove she wanted him.

They agreed to answer their mothers themselves. He would check his watch before accepting a date; she would make no promise of a Covenant introduction. Neither would ask the other to pretend they were strangers.

Mireth folded the invitation without creasing the written lines. Caelen held out the remaining bread.

“For the record,” he said, “I'd come even if the soup were dreadful.”

“Then you'd better not tell my mother that.”

The letters remained unsent beside the roster. Their decision did not.
