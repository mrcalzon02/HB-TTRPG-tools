# The Names on the Card — The Final Proof

Near sunset the public household reply arrived. It confirmed that a separate memorial list existed for the six servants and named the custodian from whom an approved public extract could be requested. It did not include the names themselves. The printer would use the agreed count in the programme and wait for the insert.

Lyrion took the corrected proof to the window. The courtyard had emptied. The dog lay asleep beneath the water trough, its owner apparently reconciled to the loss of one glove.

He thought of the Academy dining hall, of the noise made by students trying to talk at once, and of the people who had cleared the dishes while those students argued about examinations and love. He had not known every servant. He had not known every student. That was part of the shame of losing so many people at once: afterward, one could remember them clearly and still discover how much of their living days one had never bothered to see.

Alenor appeared with a small plate of honey cakes.

"Eat," she said. "You have been making diplomacy look like starvation."

He took one. The honey had soaked through the crumb.

He had expected his first useful diplomatic refusal to feel grander. Instead there were seven chairs to move, an insert to pay for, and a programme to correct.

Alenor carried the remaining cakes back to her desk. Lyrion followed, because the printer still needed the final proof.
