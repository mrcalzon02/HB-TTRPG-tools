# What the Rain Left

The western-road bridge was open to people on foot. Rain had made that distinction important.

Mireth noticed the wheel rut first. Its outer edge ended in exposed earth, where runoff had carried the packed approach downhill.

The woman from the nearest house had asked Mireth to look at her husband's swollen wrist. Caelen had come along on his free afternoon, in an ordinary coat rather than Crown colors. Beside them stood a cart loaded with meal sacks.

“My brother thinks he can keep the wheels against the inside,” the woman said. “He crossed here before the rain.”

Caelen crouched without stepping onto the broken edge.

“Before the rain is not today.”

“Can you tell us if it will hold?”

“No. I can tell you what I can see.” He pointed to the rut and the dark pocket beyond it. “I cannot see what is left underneath.”

The woman's brother came from the house carrying two boards, cut to replace the rotten lower half of the stable door.

“We could lay these over it.”

“And put a loaded wheel on them?” Mireth asked.

“Only to get the meal across.”

“The boards would need something firm beneath them,” Caelen said. “I can't tell you that they have it.”

The brother looked toward the stable, where a horse pushed its nose through the broken door. If he sacrificed the boards to the road, the horse would remain badly sheltered and the approach might still fail.

“There's a paved way around,” he said. “It costs a toll, and the mill won't wait forever.”

Mireth had finished binding the husband's wrist. She could not make that toll vanish. She could help them make a warning that said only what they knew.

They brought a spare stake, a scrap of board, and cord. Caelen held the stake while the brother drove it into firm ground away from the damaged verge. Mireth wrote in large letters: **LOW APPROACH WASHED OUT AFTER RAIN. HEAVY CARTS NOT ASSESSED. ASK THE ROAD KEEPER.**

“Will people think that's an order?” the woman asked.

“Then we must add who put it here,” Mireth said.

She wrote: **NOTICE FROM THE NEARBY HOUSEHOLD — NOT A ROAD CLOSURE.**

The brother decided to leave the meal sacks dry until he could pay the paved-road toll. He would lose a morning's delivery, and his sister would walk the message to the road keeper before tending the household fire. No one called the crossing repaired.

As they left, Caelen touched Mireth's hand.

“I wanted to say we'd make it safe.”

“I know.”

“I couldn't.”

“You helped them say what was unsafe without pretending to know the rest.”

Behind them, the warning leaned in the wet wind. The bridge remained; the household still had its meal to deliver, its stable door to mend, and a costly choice to make tomorrow.
