# The Clearing Book at River Market

The intermediary kept its clearing books above a grain warehouse. By the time Thessa climbed the narrow stairs, the river had risen over the lowest landing steps and the smell of damp flour had worked its way into her coat.

The district auditor had arrived before her. Captain Sorn waited at the window, where she could see the horses and the road that would take them back to the Warden station. They had finished the delayed northern inspection that morning. The broken verge was marked and reported, but the repair crew had not yet come. Thessa could not stay all afternoon.

The intermediary's clerk wore linen sleeves over his cuffs. He had opened the account to the road-stone warrant and laid the bridge carpenter's copied payment note beside it.

"Here," the auditor said.

The warrant had cleared into the intermediary's account. Two days later, most of the money had gone out as an urgent advance on the bridge repair. The timber yard and the crew foreman had signed for their shares. A smaller amount remained against handling and cartage.

"And the bridge appropriation?" Thessa asked.

The auditor turned another page. The district had paid the balance of the certified bridge claim, not the entire claim again.

Thessa read the figures twice.

"So the men weren't paid twice."

"Not on these records. I am still confirming the carpenter's final receipt. But we have a supported account of the advance and the later balance."

The clerk's shoulders eased. He had probably been expecting her to accuse him of stealing from the men who had kept the bridge open.

"Why was bridge money taken from a stone purchase?" she asked.

"We were told to pay them," he said. "They couldn't wait."

He produced a folded instruction bearing the works-office stamp. It authorized payment from available emergency funds pending settlement of the bridge claim. The signature was an initial, not a full name.

"Who signed it?"

"The office sent it. I didn't see the person."

The auditor held out her hand for the instruction. "I will request the authority book."

Thessa looked again at the road-stone entry. It had been authorized for replacement material, yet no quarry delivery acknowledgment accompanied the warrant. The intermediary's file contained two dated reminders asking for the missing voucher. The second had been marked received by the works office.

"Was the advance restored?" she asked.

"Not here," said the auditor. "It may appear in the treasury transfer account. That is another book, and I have asked for it."

Outside, a cart rattled over the cobbles. Thessa thought of the road surface that had given way under a loaded wheel. The missing stone and the failed verge belonged to the same district. She wanted them to belong to the same explanation.

"Does the purchase order name the north road?"

The clerk found the copy. It named the district stores account. No destination was specified.

Thessa wrote down the wording without adding a milepost.

The auditor closed the clearing book. "I can narrow the bridge question. I cannot close the stone question. I will seek the authority entry, treasury transfer and quarry dispatch records. There is no finding against this clerk."

"Will the works office stop our payments?" he asked.

"An audit is not an order to withhold wages already earned. I won't promise how the office will respond."

He looked at Thessa. She had the badge and the family name. She could have made an easy promise, and it might have comforted him until the next unpaid invoice arrived.

"I'll put my name to the request for the copies," she said. "Not to an answer we haven't found."

On the stairs Sorn waited for her.

"Did you find your thief?"

"No."

"Did you find the missing stone?"

"No."

"Then what did you find?"

"The bridge workers were paid once, as far as these books show. Someone advanced the money from the stone account. We don't know whether it was restored."

Sorn considered that while they emerged into the market. A girl carrying river eels was arguing with a wagoner who had blocked her stall. The world had continued conducting business without waiting for an inquiry to conclude.

"Will the auditor finish it?"

"She'll take it as far as her authority goes."

"And you?"

Thessa looked toward the horses. She had an inspection report to deliver and a road that would be dangerous again after rain.

"I have work of my own."

Sorn handed her the reins. Thessa folded the copies into her coat and mounted.
