# The Unmarked Parcels

The caretaker knew the weight of a full linen basket and the sound of a cupboard latch that needed oil. She did not know what was inside the four waxed packets at the bottom of the drying-room tray.

The packets had been tied together with green cord. A narrow paper label hung from it by one torn corner; whatever had been written there was gone. The transfer book named the tray and the morning shift, but its contents column had been left empty.

Mireth came in carrying a clean bowl. “The healer asked whether the small stores were ready.”

“Three trays are accounted for. Not this one.”

Mireth set down the bowl. Outside, the late rain turned the courtyard trellis into a row of dripping strings.

“Could the book from yesterday tell us?”

“It might tell us what someone expected to send. That isn't the same as what is here.”

The caretaker drew the tray away from the shelves used for issue. Mireth reached toward the cord, then stopped.

“I wasn't going to open one,” she said.

“I know. But we've both been wondering whether a look would spare us trouble.”

Mireth looked at the torn label. “Or a smell.”

“Particularly a smell. You cannot put one in the book and ask the next shift to verify it.”

She disliked how sharp she sounded. Mireth had spent the afternoon doing the work of three people without claiming she could do all three jobs. The caretaker had no wish to reward that effort with a rebuke.

“I know the hand that made the entry,” she said more gently. “I'll ask who moved the tray. Until then, it stays apart.”

“Can you decide that?”

“I can decide what leaves my shelves before I have a reliable count. The healer decides what may be used on a patient.”

The senior healer arrived before they could seek her. Mireth explained what the book said and, more importantly, what it did not. The caretaker showed her the torn tag without offering a theory about why it had torn.

“Anything issued from these?” the healer asked.

“Not on this shift,” the caretaker said. “I haven't checked the earlier pages.”

“Then leave that answer open. Hold the four apart and bring me the records when you've found them. We will use only supplies we can identify.”

She returned to her work without pronouncing the packets dangerous, harmless, spoiled, or fit for anything.

Mireth helped lift the tray onto an empty bench beyond the issue cupboard. The caretaker placed a fresh slip beside it: **Four sealed packets. Contents unverified. Do not issue pending review.** She added the time and her own mark. Mireth witnessed the count, not an identification.

The empty place on the shelf would inconvenience the next shift. That was the cost they could see. What the packets contained, and who had let the label disappear, remained questions worth leaving unanswered until someone could answer them properly.
