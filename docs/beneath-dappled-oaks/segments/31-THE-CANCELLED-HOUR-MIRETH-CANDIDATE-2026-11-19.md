# The Cancelled Hour

The three notebooks were still closed when Mireth returned to the covered walk. Rain had started dripping through a seam above the far bench. One pupil had moved her satchel twice to keep it dry.

“I ought to have told you sooner,” Mireth said.

The tallest of them stood. “Will they mark us absent?”

“No. I’ll record that the lesson was cancelled here.”

“Who records that?” asked the woman with the satchel.

“I do, in the teaching book. I can ask the senior healer to confirm it.”

The third pupil, who had hardly spoken all afternoon, opened her notebook. A list of measurements filled the first page. There was a little drawing of a hand beside each entry.

“I traded a shift at the orchard to come,” she said. “My sister has tomorrow, and the day after that is mine again. If this becomes tomorrow’s lesson, I can’t come.”

Mireth had been thinking of an hour lost. The woman was counting days other people had rearranged.

“I won’t promise tomorrow,” she said.

“Then when?”

“I don’t know. You deserve a better answer. I haven’t got one yet.”

The tall pupil glanced toward the infirmary door. “Could we at least watch you work?”

“Not on these travellers. They came for treatment, not to become our lesson.”

“I didn’t mean to pry.”

“I know. It’s an easy suggestion when you’ve spent a wet afternoon waiting.”

The woman with the satchel brought out a folded sheet bearing the practice questions Mireth had sent them. “We could do these without the demonstration.”

“You could,” Mireth said, “but I won’t call that the same instruction. A question on a page won’t tell you whether your hands are clean or your linen has been prepared properly.”

“Then there’s nothing for us?”

Mireth almost offered to correct their answers before the light went. It would have made her feel less wasteful. It would also have kept three people sitting beneath a leaking roof while their journeys home grew harder.

“There is an honest record of what happened,” she said. “And there are your questions. Keep them. I’ll bring them to the healer when she can review the next teaching day. She decides what practice we can safely offer.”

The orchard worker shut her notebook. “Please put down that I cannot simply return at an hour’s notice.”

“I will.”

“And that we came?”

“Yes.”

Only after they had gone did Mireth fetch the teaching book. The senior healer, still attending the new arrivals, had time to listen but not to examine another schedule. She authorized Mireth to enter the cancellation as the infirmary’s decision, with the three pupils present; no replacement date was approved.

Mireth wrote the entry carefully. The page had a column for attendance and one for instruction delivered. For the first time she understood how easily a blank in the second could be mistaken for a failure in the first.

She left both columns legible, and the promised hour unsettled.
