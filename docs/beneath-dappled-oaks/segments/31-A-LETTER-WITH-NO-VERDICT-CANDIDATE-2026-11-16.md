# A Letter With No Verdict

The letter had traveled farther than the question inside it.

Neral brought it to Ilyan at the end of the morning petitions. There was a smear of road dust along the outer fold and a small nick in the wax. The name was written in Thessa's hand, narrow where she had been hurried and unexpectedly generous around the capital letters.

“From the west?” Ilyan asked.

“Unless someone has begun sending you western dust as a courtesy.”

Ilyan turned the cover over. The seal was intact where it mattered. He could have opened it at once. Instead he looked for the date, calculating how many days of bad roads lay between her pen and his hands.

“You may read it,” Neral said. “The post has already done its part.”

The first half was numbers.

Two payment references, copied from the district works office; dates that were close enough to invite an easy conclusion and not close enough to justify one; a request not to circulate the figures beyond Vaun's inquiry until the intermediary's clearing entries had been checked. Thessa had underlined *not yet* once.

He put the sheet beside his breakfast plate.

What he wanted to do was fetch a map, mark the bridge and the road, and draw a line between every uncertainty until the line resembled an answer. What he actually did was read the dates again and leave the map closed.

“The bridge?” Neral asked.

“An account Thessa is checking. The figures are hers to verify, not mine to improve.”

“Shall I file them?”

“Not as evidence received by the Hall. This was sent to me, not to an office. If Vaun needs the references, I'll ask Thessa what she can authorize us to use.”

Neral nodded and went back to the petitioners.

Below the figures Thessa had written about soaked boots, a carpenter who considered tea an insult to boiled water, and a question she had clearly added after deciding the letter was finished.

*Tell me what you've been eating. Properly. I know what you call supper when nobody is there to stop you.*

Ilyan glanced at his breakfast: one pear, still whole, and a heel of bread that had become an excellent place to rest a paperweight.

He laughed.

It was an inconvenient sound in the records room. Across the table, a junior copyist looked up and then politely pretended not to have heard it.

Ilyan moved the letter away from the ink and went downstairs. He returned with a bowl of lentils and a hot roll from the kitchen. Neither would persuade Thessa that he had become a model of domestic sense, but both represented honest improvement.

He began a reply while the roll cooled.

He told her what he had eaten. He asked whether Sorn was making her sleep between inspections. He did not ask for a thief's name or tell her that the stone account explained the road. He thanked her for leaving the uncertainties visible.

At the end he wrote, *You needn't answer quickly. I know how long a road can be.*

He left the reply unsealed. His earlier packet might still be going west; one received letter did not tell him where the other had reached.

For the first time that day, he finished a meal before beginning another question.
