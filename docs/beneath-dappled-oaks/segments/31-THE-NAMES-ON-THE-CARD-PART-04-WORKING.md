# The Names on the Card — The Correction

The secretary turned the plan around. "Our patrons wished to meet them. Some lost kin at the Academy. They thought a public remembrance might offer comfort."

Lyrion could not dismiss that. Grief was not the private property of the Seven. Families had come to the Academy gates carrying coats and sweets for people who would never come out.

"Would your patrons wish to be seated beneath a heading naming them as bereaved relatives?" he asked.

"No."

"Some might. Others would hate it."

She studied the paper for a while. "Yes."

Ilvaren placed the revised card beside the original. "The reception will remain. The invitation must be optional. What should the memorial say?"

Lyrion had prepared a new paragraph. It named the thirty-six students and six servants. He had not invented the servants' names or borrowed an unofficial list. The Academy's public household register was still being checked, and Alenor had proposed a separate insert once the names could be certified.

The secretary read it.

"Why specify servants?"

"Because they died there."

"I did not ask why they deserve remembrance. I asked why you changed the wording."

He felt the heat in his face. He had heard an objection she had not made.

"The original came from a student roll," he said. "It was incomplete."

"Then I agree with the correction."

"The donors may be disappointed that the Seven will not attend," she added.

"They may attend if they wish," Ilvaren said. "They have not been asked."

"Would you ask them?"

The reception was three days away. A courier might reach one or two of the Seven by then. Thessa's western station was beyond any ordinary promise of delivery.

"I can write invitations," Lyrion said. "I cannot honestly offer you seven answers by the reception."
