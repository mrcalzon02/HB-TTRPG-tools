# Chapter Five Continuation — Pellin Doss and the East Room

Rhyl found Pellin Doss three streets inland from the canal, in a building that leaned toward its neighbor as though the two had agreed to share the burden of remaining upright.

The rain had softened to mist by then. Water ran from slate roofs in patient threads and gathered in the seams of the street. Lower Reed smelled of wet stone, coal smoke, boiled onions and the river, which had a way of entering every neighborhood near it whether invited or not.

The address Talia had given him belonged to a narrow upper room above a cooper's shop.

Rhyl stood beneath the awning and listened before he knocked.

A cough sounded upstairs.

Then another.

Not the theatrical cough of someone who had heard a stranger on the stair.

Deep. Wet. Exhausting.

He knocked.

A chair scraped overhead.

The door opened after a while.

Pellin was younger than Rhyl had expected, perhaps thirty-five, with laundry-red hands and a shirt that had been slept in. He looked at Rhyl's Red Hart clasp, then at Rhyl himself.

"House send you?"

"No."

"Court?"

"No."

Pellin waited.

Rhyl said, "Judge Vaun asked me to find you and ask a few questions. You don't have to come anywhere with me."

The tension in Pellin's shoulders changed without disappearing.

"About Lord Aereth."

"About what you handled while he was staying at Reedglass."

A woman coughed upstairs again.

Pellin glanced back.

"My sister's sick."

"Talia said."

"She thinks I'm hiding?"

"She said your sister was sick."

Pellin looked at him for another moment, then stepped aside.

"Keep your boots off the rug."

The rug was mostly patches.

Rhyl stepped around it anyway.

Lessa Doss lay under two blankets beside the small iron stove. She had the flushed, hollow look of someone several days into a chest fever and deeply offended by the inconvenience. A kettle steamed near her bed. Two medicine bottles stood on a crate beside it.

She opened one eye.

"If you're arresting him, wait until the broth is done."

"I'm not arresting anyone."

"Then you can stir it."

Pellin closed his eyes.

"Lessa."

"He's standing near the pot."

Rhyl stirred the broth.

It seemed easier.

Pellin sat on the edge of the other chair.

"Ask."

Rhyl did not take out a notebook. He had never understood why investigators liked making people watch themselves become paperwork.

"You carried the east-floor basket when Lord Aereth arrived?"

"Yes."

"Anything unusual in it?"

Pellin rubbed his palms together.

"First day? No. Travel clothes. Shirts. Two coats. Linen. His valet sent things down after unpacking."

"Second day?"

"Shaving cloth. Had blood on it."

Rhyl looked at him.

"Much?"

"No. Cut himself. Talia said so."

That matched.

"What happened to the cloth?"

"Wash soak."

"You put it there?"

"Yes."

"Did you see it afterward?"

Pellin thought.

"No."

"Anything else from his room?"

"Blue coat went to mending. Two buttons loose. One nearly off."

"Who mended it?"

"Sera, probably. I dropped it on her table."

"Probably?"

"I don't stand over people while they sew."

Fair.

"Black waxed thread?"

Pellin looked at him.

"That's what we use."

"Could you take some?"

"Yes."

"Could anyone?"

"Anyone who works there. Anyone who asks someone who works there. It's thread."

Rhyl nodded.

Lessa coughed into a cloth.

Pellin stood at once, poured water, waited until she drank, then sat again.

Nothing about him looked hurried except when she coughed.

"Why did you leave yesterday?"

"Because she sent a boy to say she couldn't get downstairs."

Lessa raised two fingers from beneath the blanket.

"I remain tragically unable to descend stairs."

"You nearly fell down them."

"Technical distinction."

Rhyl looked at the medicine bottles.

One bore a neighborhood physician's seal dated the previous afternoon.

He let the question go.

"Did anyone speak to you about Lord Aereth's laundry?"

Pellin's answer took longer.

"A courier."

Rhyl kept still.

"When?"

"Day Lord Aereth arrived. Maybe an hour after the first basket."

"River Ledger?"

"Grey coat. Ledger badge."

"What did he want?"

"Directions."

"To?"

"Receiving. He had a packet for Aereth."

"You walked him there?"

"No. Told him through the service court, left at the pump, second door."

"Did he ask about the guest room?"

"No."

"Laundry?"

"No."

Rhyl waited.

Pellin frowned at the floorboards.

"He asked if Lord Aereth had arrived yet."

"What did you say?"

"Yes."

"Anything else?"

"That his things had. I don't remember."

"Would he have seen the basket?"

Pellin looked up.

"It was beside me."

There it was.

Not a confession.

Not a secret passage.

A man asking directions beside a basket.

"What did he look like?"

"Young."

"Our young or your sister's young?"

Lessa spoke from the bed.

"Careful."

Pellin almost smiled.

"Thirty, maybe. Dark hair. Clean face. Better boots than a runner should waste on Lower Reed."

"Anything you'd recognize?"

"Ring."

"What kind?"

Pellin touched his right hand.

"Silver. Green stone. Big enough I noticed."

"Crest?"

"Didn't see one."

"Anything else?"

Pellin shook his head.

Then stopped.

"Cloves."

Rhyl waited.

"He smelled like cloves. Strong."

"Oil?"

"Maybe."

"Would you know him again?"

"I think so."

Rhyl nodded.

"Don't try to. Not yet."

Pellin frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"It means if someone brings you a portrait and asks whether that's him, don't decide because they want an answer. Tell them what you actually remember."

Pellin's expression changed.

A little less defensive.

"All right."

Rhyl rose.

"One more thing."

Pellin looked tired already.

"The packet. Did you see Aereth receive it?"

"Not receive it. Saw it later."

"Where?"

"East room. Talia sent me up for the coat."

"Opened?"

Pellin's brow furrowed.

"No."

"You're sure?"

"Wax was still across it."

He hesitated.

"But he said he'd already read it."

Rhyl sat down again.

Lessa stopped pretending not to listen.

"Tell me that part."

Pellin rubbed the back of his neck.

"He was looking for something on the desk. Asked where his council packet was. I pointed at it. He got angry. Said that wasn't the packet, he'd already opened the packet."

"Then?"

"Picked it up. Looked at the seal. Went quiet."

"Did he open it?"

"Yes."

"In front of you?"

"Broke the wax. Read the first page."

"And later?"

Pellin looked uncomfortable.

"Later that evening he asked Talia why the council packet hadn't arrived."

Rhyl felt the room become very still.

He did not improve the story.

He did not tell Pellin what it meant.

He did not know.

"Who else heard that?"

"Talia. Maybe his valet."

"Good."

Lessa coughed again.

Rhyl stood.

Pellin looked at him.

"Am I in trouble?"

"I don't know."

Pellin laughed without humor.

"Useful answer."

"It's the one I have."

At the door, Lessa called after him.

"Hart."

Rhyl looked back.

"If you people drag him away, somebody else has to bring coal."

"I'll put it in the paperwork."

"You said you weren't court."

"I'm learning."

Her smile was brief and exhausted.

Rhyl left them with the broth.

---

Aereth came downstairs under his own power and in a foul temper.

Damaris considered both encouraging.

He stopped at the bottom of the stair when he saw the winter room occupied by a judge, a Covenant trainee, his cousin, his hostess, a court clerk and three sealed evidence bags.

"No."

Meris folded her arms.

"Good morning."

"It was until I reached the stairs."

Damaris watched his face.

He looked tired. Not theatrically tired. His eyes were bruised beneath, and he had shaved badly along the left jaw, leaving a narrow missed strip near his ear.

He looked at Talia.

She stood near the sideboard.

Aereth's mouth tightened.

"You are still here."

Talia's shoulders went rigid.

Damaris felt her own anger return.

Meris spoke first.

"She works here."

Aereth looked at her.

"I know that."

"Yesterday you briefly forgot."

He flinched.

Small.

Real.

Damaris saw it.

So did Talia.

Vaun stepped away from the evidence table.

"Lord Aereth, I need to ask you about your room and several objects that may have come from it."

"May have?"

"Yes."

"What objects?"

Damaris looked at Vaun.

Vaun nodded once.

So Damaris told him.

Not everything they feared.

What they had.

"A shaving cloth. Hair. Nail clippings. A button. Black repair thread. Grey powder. They were wrapped in household linen and buried beneath the rosemary under your window."

Aereth stared at her.

"My window."

"The east guest room."

"And you think it's mine."

"I think some of it may have come from your room."

"That isn't what I asked."

"It's the answer I have."

His gaze moved to the sealed bags.

"The powder?"

Mireth answered.

"Being tested."

"For what?"

"Several things."

He looked at her, disliked the answer, and let it pass.

Then his eyes returned to Talia.

"You had access."

Talia went pale.

Damaris said, "So did half the people required to keep a guest room functioning."

"I wasn't speaking to you."

"You were speaking about someone you accused yesterday without being right."

"Damaris."

Meris's warning was quiet.

Damaris ignored it.

Aereth looked at her.

"My signet was moved."

"Yes."

"Someone moved it."

"Probably."

"And she was in the room."

Talia's face changed.

Not fear now.

Anger.

"So were you, my lord."

Aereth turned toward her.

The room held its breath.

Talia swallowed but continued.

"You were in your own washroom. You could have put it there."

Aereth's face went white.

Damaris understood then that the possibility had occurred to him.

He hated it.

That did not make it false.

Vaun said, "Lord Aereth, may I inspect the room?"

He looked at her.

"You need my permission?"

"At the moment, yes."

"And if I say no?"

"I decide whether I have grounds to seek authority without it."

No threat in her voice.

No comfort either.

Aereth looked toward the stairs.

"What are you looking for?"

"Whether the objects in the garden have ordinary counterparts in the room. Whether anything appears disturbed. Your medicine, if you consent to testing it. The council packet you came here to review."

His attention snapped back.

"Why the packet?"

Damaris saw it before anyone answered.

Fear.

"Aereth?"

He rubbed one hand over his mouth.

"I read it."

Nobody spoke.

"When?" Vaun asked.

"Yesterday morning."

"Where is it?"

"Desk."

"Do you remember opening it?"

His irritation returned.

"Of course I remember opening it."

Damaris watched him.

"How?"

Aereth looked at her as if the question were absurd.

"How does anyone open a packet?"

"Knife? Finger? Broke the wax?"

"I broke the wax."

"What color?"

He opened his mouth.

Stopped.

His eyes moved away.

Damaris wished immediately that she had not asked.

Not because it was unfair.

Because she had watched certainty leave him in real time.

Aereth looked toward Talia.

"You brought it."

She shook her head.

"Receiving sent it up."

"You handed it to me."

"I put it on the desk."

"And I opened it."

Talia did not answer.

Aereth's voice sharpened.

"I opened it."

Damaris stepped closer.

"All right."

He looked at her.

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"Speak to me like I'm ill."

"You haven't slept. You have headaches. You misplaced your signet. You remember something about the packet that may not fit what happened. That is what we know."

"And what do you think?"

There it was.

The invitation to choose a story because uncertainty hurt.

Damaris could have given him one.

Curse.

Poison.

Exhaustion.

A servant.

An enemy.

Anything was kinder than the open space.

"I think I want the room checked," she said.

Aereth stared at her.

Then laughed once, bitterly.

"Coward."

"Probably."

The answer surprised him.

Damaris moved close enough that only he needed to hear the next part.

"I don't know what's happening to you. I'm not going to make something up because you're frightened."

His jaw worked.

For a moment he looked much older than she remembered.

Then he turned to Vaun.

"Inspect it."

Vaun nodded.

"And the tincture?"

Aereth closed his eyes.

"Take it."

Talia looked down at her hands.

Aereth saw.

The anger left him slowly.

He stood there for several seconds before speaking.

"Talia."

She looked up.

"My lord."

"Yesterday."

He stopped.

Damaris had never seen Aereth struggle for an apology. She realized she had never seen him need one badly enough to matter.

"I accused you of taking my signet. I dismissed you. I was wrong."

Talia's face remained guarded.

"Yes, my lord."

"I'm sorry."

She looked at him for a long moment.

"Thank you."

Nothing else.

No absolution.

No assurance that it was forgotten.

Aereth nodded once.

It seemed to cost him more than anger had.

Vaun sent her clerk upstairs first to photograph the room as it stood.

Mireth prepared clean envelopes.

Meris went to speak with the steward about preserving the receiving log.

Damaris stayed with Aereth.

He sat in the winter room while the others moved around him, hands clasped between his knees.

After a while he said, "I remember opening it."

Damaris sat beside him.

"I know."

"That isn't the same as saying I did."

"No."

He looked at her.

"You believe me?"

"I believe you remember it."

Aereth stared at the rain on the glass.

This time he did not get angry.

"That's worse."

Damaris did not lie.

"Yes."

They sat together until the clerk came back down.

---

Rhyl returned just before noon with rain on his shoulders and no prisoner.

Damaris noticed the second part before she noticed the first.

She crossed the receiving hall before he had finished giving his cloak to the porter.

"Pellin?"

"At his sister's. She's genuinely sick."

Damaris's shoulders loosened.

Rhyl saw.

"You expected me to drag him back?"

"No."

"You looked relieved."

"I can be relieved that you did the thing I expected."

"That sounds suspiciously like praise."

"Don't become difficult."

"Too late."

She wanted to kiss him again.

The receiving hall was full of servants.

This was not, Damaris was beginning to understand, much of a deterrent.

She settled for taking his hand and pulling him toward the winter room.

"What did he say?"

Rhyl told Vaun first.

That mattered too.

Pellin's sister.

The shaving cloth.

The coat buttons.

The mending thread.

The courier.

The green-stone ring.

Cloves.

Then the packet.

Aereth sat very still through the last part.

"He saw me open it?"

Rhyl looked at him.

"He says he saw you say you'd already opened it while the wax was still across the fold. Then he saw you break the seal."

Aereth's face emptied.

"And later?"

"He says you asked Talia why it hadn't arrived."

Aereth looked at Talia.

She nodded once.

"You did."

He covered his face with both hands.

No one spoke.

The silence lasted long enough to become uncomfortable.

Damaris let it.

Aereth finally lowered his hands.

"The packet is upstairs."

Vaun said, "We'll preserve it."

"Do that."

He sounded tired rather than angry now.

Rhyl looked toward the evidence table.

"Anything from the room?"

Mireth answered.

"The shaving cloth is missing from the linen set. One old service button was found under the dressing table. That proves very little. The tincture is sealed for testing."

"Packet?"

"Still there."

Damaris looked at Rhyl.

"Receiving log is being brought up."

He nodded.

Two separate routes had reached the same piece of paper.

She could feel the temptation in the room to make that mean more than it did.

Vaun simply held out her hand for the log when the steward arrived.

The entry was ordinary.

River Ledger courier.

Council packet.

Delivered first afternoon.

Signed at receiving.

The runner's name was written in a hurried hand.

**Eren Vale.**

Damaris read it twice.

"Do you know him?" Rhyl asked.

"No."

Meris looked over her shoulder.

"Neither do I."

The steward shook his head.

Vaun tapped the line.

"Then we begin there."

Damaris looked at the copper token sealed on the evidence table.

A River Ledger token.

A River Ledger courier.

Her aunt's cipher waited in the pocket of her coat upstairs.

The route was obvious enough that everyone in the room could see it.

So was the cost.

Rhyl's fingers brushed hers.

Not asking.

Not stopping her.

Just there.

Damaris turned her hand and caught two of his fingers.

"I can check the runner registry," she said.

Meris watched her.

Vaun said, "Can you do it without altering the record?"

"Yes."

"Can you preserve what you find?"

"Yes."

Rhyl looked at her.

Damaris answered the question he had not asked.

"It will be audited."

"I know."

"My family will know what I searched."

"I know."

"And you're not going to tell me whether to do it."

"No."

She hated how much she liked him.

"Infuriating."

His thumb moved once across her knuckle.

"After breakfast."

Damaris almost smiled.

Then she looked at Aereth, at Talia, at the packet waiting upstairs, and at the name in the receiving log.

The smile went away.

"Bring me the cipher."
