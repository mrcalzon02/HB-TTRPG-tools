## Full-series trajectory and completion authority

This section supplies the destination beyond the rolling five-chapter window. It does **not** create a second installment tracker: the N+1 through N+5 cards above remain the only near-term scheduler. Unwritten architecture is directional; committed prose and newer explicit canon win.

**Series end condition:** the series is complete when the Silver Night generation has moved from being the surviving object of older houses' plans into adults capable of choosing, defending, and accepting the costs of their own political, professional, familial, and romantic commitments; the Silver Night responsibility chains have produced durable institutional consequence without violating protected-person privacy for reader satisfaction; the principal institutions have reformed, broken, or openly chosen what they are; and the central relationships have reached durable chosen states rather than indefinite suspense.

### Book One — The Surviving Generation

**Hard ceiling:** 40 numbered chapters.

**End condition:** the Seven have crossed into adult institutional life; the investigation has established the first defensible causal bridge between legitimate pre-Silver-Night concentration management and unlawful intervention, or proved that the bridge must be sought elsewhere; Corren's admitted enabling role has received consequence rather than absolution; 264-B/17 and JEC-R/6 have yielded every fact that can lawfully be learned without reverse-engineering the protected bearer; and the Seven understand that surviving the massacre did not exempt them from the political future it tried to prevent.

**Macro movement:** Chapters 1–10 establish survival, graduation, grief, houses, orders, desire, family pressure and Ilyan's intolerable private knowledge. Chapters 11–20 turn suspicion into disciplined evidence while distributing the Seven into real work. Chapters 21–30 test demographic concentration, Pellisar's tasking, the receiving authority, initiating-office expectations and lawful remedies without collapsing competent institutional work into conspiracy. Chapters 31–36 permit only evidence chains that earn convergence and force houses and institutions to choose among procedure, kin, reputation and truth. Chapters 37–40 deliver the bounded Silver Night reckoning and leave the Seven moving forward into chosen adult positions rather than pretending politics has been cleaned.

Before Book One closes, the story owes graduation/adult induction; at least one relationship choice that cannot simply reset to flirtation; an independent professional choice for each of the Seven; a supported disposition of Corren's culpability; resolution of the safe evidentiary value of 264-B/17 and JEC-R/6; a supported answer to why Pellisar crossed from declared concentration into predictive kinship; and a concrete institutional response to the murder of protected students.

### Book Two — Houses in the Open

**Working budget:** approximately 30–36 chapters, adjustable to causal need rather than symmetry.

**End condition:** Book One's findings have propagated through houses, orders, commerce, appointments, marriage strategy and regional government. The Seven can no longer rely on shared victimhood as sufficient reason to agree, and each has acquired something worth losing.

The book opens with settlement shock: reform creates winners as well as losers. The Seven's professions give them incompatible legitimate duties. Love becomes leverage through concrete consequences rather than slogans. Thessa's western work, Damaris's commercial sphere, Mireth's Green Covenant obligations, Caelen's royal/martial service, Lyrion's diplomacy, Rhyl's wilderness responsibilities and Ilyan's Valeric/Truth Beyond the Veil problem each generate independent pressures that may intersect without becoming one conspiracy. Counter-coalitions should contain opportunists, frightened institutionalists, genuine ideologues, family protectors and criminals whose interests overlap temporarily. The book ends when a major house/order settlement—an alliance, office, succession arrangement, prosecution, schism, marriage, refusal or disclosure—permanently changes the political board.

### Book Three — What They Choose to Keep

**Working budget:** approximately 26–32 chapters, expandable only for unresolved causal obligations.

**Series completion boundary:** the Seven and their generation establish the adult order they are willing to live inside. The final conflict is not another deeper conspiracy. It is whether institutions and families changed by Silver Night will reproduce the same logic under a more respectable name.

The Seven are now people others petition, fear, employ, love, oppose or imitate. A real political or security problem makes a Silver-Night-adjacent shortcut attractive to otherwise reasonable people, forcing the survivors to decide what limits they defend when restraint is inconvenient. Central relationships and household structures reach durable states; durable does not require universal marriage, reconciliation or happiness, only a clear answer about what each central character chooses and what it costs. Each major institution touched by the Seven receives a visible final state: reformed practice, defended tradition, schism, replacement, loss of authority or consciously unresolved tension. The Seven act from distinct adult positions rather than reforming the academy friend group as a single adventuring unit.

The coda follows long enough to make consequences observable: offices occupied or refused, relationships settled as publicly or privately as their circumstances require, the dead remembered without being used as permanent narrative fuel, and the next generation able to enter adulthood under rules materially different from those that enabled Silver Night.

### Seed/payoff obligations and continuity controls

The series owes payoff, bounded closure, or an explicit reason for continued uncertainty for the Silver Night responsibility chain; Corren's enabling role; the established Valeric father/uncle responsibility; Pellisar's predictive-kinship provenance; demographic concentration and lawful remedies; 264-B/17 and JEC-R/6; the protected-student boundary; the receiver's independent-protection reservation; the civic acknowledgment; the Seven's distributed professional lives; the political use of sincere attachment; and the consequences of deliberately thinning a generation before graduation.

Do not manufacture payoff by violating a protection rule. A sealed identity, protected route, unresolved surname or deliberately bounded fact may remain unknown when the dramatic payoff is that lawful people accept the limit.

Track knowledge temporally: who knew what, when, with what confidence, and what later evidence changed it. Preserve professional travel and communication delay. Preserve independent institutional incentives. Do not convert representation into authorship, access into use, acknowledgment into conspiracy, or family relation into shared culpability.

Reader-facing chapters contain story only. Planning labels, chapter-state declarations, staging markers, production summaries, draft references and automation commentary belong in governance or commit history, never chapter prose.

Book One closes by Chapter 40 at the latest. Book Two ends when consequences have forced irreversible adult alignments and the Seven possess independent stakes worth losing. Do not add a fourth book solely because the world still contains politics, romance, unsolved crimes or interesting secondary characters.
