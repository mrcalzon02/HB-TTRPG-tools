# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Five-Chapter Lookahead System

This file prevents chapter-by-chapter tunnel vision.

The story runner must not look only backward at established canon and sideways at the immediate scene. It must also look **forward up to five chapters**, understand where the current arc is going, and deliberately plant the character choices, clues, relationships, obligations, locations, and emotional pressure needed for those future chapters to work.

This is a **planning document**, not a license to declare unwritten events already canon.

## 1. Planning horizon

Maintain a rolling window of:

- **NEXT — Chapter N+1:** committed intent. This is the chapter the story is actively moving toward.
- **NEAR — Chapter N+2:** strongly planned. Its major function and required setup should be stable.
- **MID — Chapter N+3:** planned. Characters should already be making choices that naturally lead here.
- **FAR — Chapter N+4:** directional. Major transitions, revelations, or relationship movements may be known, but details may remain flexible.
- **HORIZON — Chapter N+5:** directional destination. It answers "where is this movement going?" without pretending every scene is fixed.

Never expand the active detailed horizon beyond five unwritten chapters. Longer-range Book and Act material belongs in the master setting/archive or an arc outline, not in the operational chapter window.

## 2. Forward-plan authority

The lookahead has three certainty levels.

### LOCKED INTENT

A planned consequence, destination, or setup already required by committed canon or explicit user instruction.

Do not casually discard it.

### PLANNED

A deliberate forward choice made to give the story structure.

It may change when newly written character behavior, explicit user instruction, or stronger continuity makes a different route better, but a change must be conscious rather than accidental.

### EXPLORATORY

A useful possibility, foreshadowing target, candidate POV, possible reveal, or optional complication.

It should guide seeding without becoming binding merely because it appears here.

**Unwritten planning is not historical fact.** Once a planned element appears in finished committed prose, its actual written form becomes canon and this file must reconcile to what happened.

## 3. The chapter card

Every one of the five forward slots should track the following.

### Chapter identity

- working chapter number and title;
- certainty level;
- likely POV character or POV rotation;
- primary location(s);
- approximate time elapsed from the previous chapter.

### Dramatic purpose

- what this chapter must accomplish that no earlier chapter has accomplished;
- what changes by the end;
- why the chapter exists beyond atmosphere.

### Character intentions

For every major character appearing:

- what they **plan to do** at the beginning;
- what they believe will happen;
- what private or family instruction they are following;
- what they are hiding;
- what emotional weakness may distort the plan;
- what they should carry forward into later chapters.

Characters should possess plans that survive beyond individual scenes. They may fail, revise, conceal, or betray those plans, but they should not behave as if they wake each chapter with no memory of what they intended yesterday.

### Relationship movement

Track:

- relationship entering state;
- attraction or aversion being reinforced;
- trust gained or spent;
- jealousy, tenderness, resentment, obligation, or desire introduced;
- family or political leverage attached to the relationship;
- relationship exiting state.

Use the Dramatis Personae attraction-continuity rules. A future romantic turn should normally receive some earlier sensory, emotional, social, or behavioral groundwork.

### Political / institutional movement

Track:

- house objective;
- order or institution objective;
- alliance movement;
- succession or marriage pressure;
- intelligence operation;
- legal, commercial, military, magical, or household consequence.

### Mystery / evidence movement

For Silver Night, cursecraft, corruption, or other investigations:

- clue introduced;
- clue reinterpreted;
- false explanation or incomplete assumption;
- evidence chain preserved;
- who learns what;
- who still does not know;
- what future chapter will pay it off.

### Seed debt

This is the core lookahead mechanism.

For every important element in a future chapter, ask:

**What must exist earlier for this to feel earned?**

Record those requirements as seed debt.

Examples:

- A betrayal in N+3 may require a promise in N+1.
- A new lover in N+4 may need an introduction, reputation, glance, letter, rumor, or shared obligation in N+1 or N+2.
- A curse discovery in N+2 may require a rash, failed consummation, disturbed garden bed, servant access, missing household object, or suspicious repair in N+1.
- A political appointment in N+3 may require lobbying and family instructions two chapters earlier.
- A character choosing family over a lover in N+4 may require several chapters showing why that family bond actually matters.
- A clue proving falsified testimony later must be physically or administratively present before someone suddenly "discovers" it.

Each chapter-writing run must check the future four cards and deliberately pay some of their seed debt where natural.

### Payoff debt

Also ask:

**What has already been seeded and now requires payoff?**

Do not indefinitely accumulate flirtations, promises, threats, clues, letters, injuries, invitations, family instructions, debts, curses, rivalries, or secrets with no consequence.

### Exit state / handoff

Define:

- where each major character physically ends;
- what they now intend;
- what they now know;
- what they incorrectly believe;
- which relationships changed;
- what unresolved action is already in motion;
- the concrete reason the next chapter happens.

A chapter should hand the next chapter a loaded weapon, not an empty room.

## 4. Pre-writing lookahead pass

Before writing the next chapter:

1. Read the automation master archive.
2. Read the Dramatis Personae / kinship index.
3. Read this five-chapter lookahead.
4. Reconcile against any newer committed chapter prose.
5. Identify the actual current chapter endpoint.
6. Review N+1 through N+5.
7. Inspect **seed debt** from N+2 through N+5.
8. Choose which future setup can be naturally planted in the chapter being written.
9. Check whether the current POV character has an existing long-term plan and attraction grammar.
10. Write the chapter to satisfy its own purpose while advancing future chapters.

Do not dump obvious foreshadowing into a scene merely because it appears in a future card. Seed through ordinary life: invitations, meals, clothing preparation, family instructions, gossip, letters, servant activity, training, appointments, travel arrangements, physical symptoms, ledger entries, casual introductions, repeated attraction, uncomfortable silences, and things characters think are unimportant.

## 5. Post-writing roll-forward pass

After a chapter is completed and committed:

1. Compare what actually happened against the planned N+1 card.
2. Record deviations caused by character decisions or newly established canon.
3. Promote the actual exit state into the historical continuity record.
4. Remove the completed chapter from the forward window.
5. Shift N+2 → N+1, N+3 → N+2, N+4 → N+3, N+5 → N+4.
6. Create a new N+5 directional card.
7. Recalculate seed debt.
8. Recalculate payoff debt.
9. Carry unresolved character plans forward rather than silently resetting them.
10. Preserve promises, invitations, assignments, injuries, suspicions, attraction patterns, lies, and deadlines that remain active.

The horizon therefore moves with the story.

## 6. Character plan persistence

Every recurring major character should have at least one active intention unless they are genuinely directionless for a story reason.

Track intentions such as:

- whom they intend to speak to;
- what they intend to investigate;
- which family order they are trying to obey or evade;
- who they intend to seduce, court, protect, expose, recruit, or avoid;
- which ceremony they are preparing for;
- which letter they must answer;
- what evidence they are trying to obtain;
- which appointment, license, marriage, posting, or inheritance they are pursuing;
- what lie they are maintaining;
- what private promise they intend to keep.

A character's plan may last three chapters before execution.

That is desirable.

Long-running intentions create the feeling that characters have lives and agency outside whichever POV currently occupies the page.

## 7. Transition tracking

Future cards should identify transitions before they occur.

Possible transitions include:

- academy student → inducted order member;
- friend → operative assigned against a friend;
- courtship target → genuine lover;
- lover → political liability;
- family loyalist → reluctant dissident;
- witness → conspirator;
- investigator → blackmailer;
- servant → trusted confidant;
- cousin → succession rival;
- neutral house → coalition participant;
- suspicion → evidence;
- private grievance → public scandal.

A transition should usually receive preparation before the chapter in which it becomes official.

## 8. Anti-railroad rule

Looking ahead is not the same as forcing characters to obey an outline.

The purpose of the horizon is to make consequences coherent, not to make characters puppets.

If finished prose establishes that a character would not plausibly execute the planned action, revise the future plan. Do not violate characterization merely to hit a card.

However, do not abandon a planned arc because writing the setup requires effort. First ask whether the existing character plan, family pressure, attraction, fear, debt, or institutional obligation can credibly carry them there.

## N+1 — Chapter Four: "Necessary Truth"

**Certainty:** LOCKED INTENT / PLANNED

**Purpose:** Convert the linked Hall of Veils transfer into the first independently testable contradiction of the official Silver Night narrative without requiring an implausible theft or instant confession.

**Likely focus:** Ilyan, Caelen, and Damaris, with Mireth or another member providing a second evidentiary method rather than all Seven sharing equal page time.

**Character intentions entering:**
- Ilyan intends to identify when the altered oath language entered official use and who authorized or handled the relevant forms.
- Caelen intends to perform his escort duty correctly while still helping his friends notice lawful or incidental information surrounding the transfer.
- Damaris intends to use the River Ledger cipher without revealing the full extent of her family order or the fact that her aunt deliberately put the tool in her hands.
- Mireth intends to determine whether any testimony or institutional statement can later be preserved through living-memory procedure.
- Rhyl intends to inspect the west-tower service-road retaining wall on his assigned route and remains mildly suspicious that Damaris withheld something under the oak.

**Mystery movement:**
- Establish a dated record, registry discrepancy, transfer notation, transport abstract, oath-form sequence, witness-screen requisition, or other document that proves the official account is incomplete.
- Preserve the evidence chain: who sees it, who is permitted to copy it, who merely remembers it, and who notices the request.
- Do not yet reveal the entire conspiracy.
- The altered phrase must have a traceable administrative history rather than existing as free-floating sinister wording.

**Relationship movement:**
- Caelen/Mireth sincerity should remain distinct from family strategy; helping with the investigation should not magically resolve that ambiguity.
- Damaris/Ilyan friendship becomes practically valuable because she has access he may need.
- Rhyl/Damaris strain begins as observation rather than confrontation.
- Thessa/Lyrion retain the aftereffect of the arcade question: intimacy is real, but both now know they may need to ask whether a gesture was personally or institutionally motivated.

**Seed debt for later chapters:**
- Establish an enforcement or forensic practitioner who can later identify sympathetic cursecraft.
- Revisit the household-access fact from Chapter Three naturally, preferably through a location or person rather than exposition.
- Establish the household/estate that will host the Chapter Five curse case.
- Keep the Hall of Seven Lamps reception three nights away and make at least one character prepare for it.

**Exit handoff:** the Seven possess their first independently testable contradiction of the official Silver Night narrative, and someone outside the Seven becomes aware that records are being compared.

## N+2 — Chapter Five: "Something Buried Near the House"

**Certainty:** PLANNED

**Purpose:** Introduce the first substantial sympathetic-curse investigation as a household mystery and pay the Chapter Three access seed: active cursecraft requires nearby physical access.

**Case shape:** a socially devastating but initially nonlethal curse affecting an adult noble or politically relevant household member.

**Required function:**
- Tie the victim, suspected planter, investigator, or evidence directly to one of the Seven's houses, orders, or newly established adult duties.
- Search bedrooms, dressing rooms, household shrines, planters, walls, foundations, clothing, carriage compartments, or private gardens where servants and trusted staff routinely have legitimate access.
- Make the gardener/chamber-attendant/shrine-access logic matter.
- The curse should create political and emotional consequences, not merely a puzzle.

**Character movement:**
- At least one member of the Seven learns that household servants and tradespeople can possess more actionable knowledge than noble relatives.
- At least one character's attraction grammar becomes more specific under stress without being treated as a permanent universal type.
- Someone must decide whether to report forbidden magic immediately or preserve proof for leverage.
- A professional investigator, judge, alchemist, examiner, or order specialist must demonstrate knowledge of prohibited magic without automatically implying illegal practical casting.

**Payoff debt:**
- Pay Chapter Three's mundane household-access seed.
- Pay Chapter Four's enforcement-knowledge seed.
- Carry the Silver Night contradiction forward even if the curse case occupies the foreground.

**Exit handoff:** the Seven now know forbidden sympathetic magic is an active political tool, and at least one household's trust structure has been compromised.

## N+3 — Chapter Six: "The Price of Trust"

**Certainty:** PLANNED

**Purpose:** Make Damaris's covert family order regarding Ilyan impossible to keep morally abstract.

**Character intentions entering:**
- Damaris intends to protect Ilyan enough to remain his friend while still preserving her standing within Caladren-Ilyra.
- Ilyan intends to follow the new documentary lead without becoming dependent on a single house's interpretation.
- Rhyl intends to learn what Damaris is withholding rather than accuse her without evidence.

**Character movement:**
- Ilyan gives Damaris access, confidence, evidence, or a vulnerable disclosure because of real friendship.
- Damaris must decide what to report, distort, omit, delay, or protect.
- Rhyl recognizes that her concealment is institutional rather than merely romantic.
- The conflict must not resolve through one clean confession.

**Relationship movement:**
- Damaris/Rhyl attraction acquires a concrete trust problem.
- Damaris/Ilyan friendship gains genuine stakes beyond her assignment.
- Damaris's behavior should preserve separate states for what she knows, what she believes, what she tells her family, and what she chooses to do.

**Political movement:**
- Caladren-Ilyra notices either the information Damaris sends or the information she withholds.
- Someone begins testing whether Ilyan can be separated from the other six.
- Evidence from the curse case may become leverage that a house wants to trade rather than report.

**Exit handoff:** Damaris makes a private choice that creates consequences for both her family and the Seven, and Rhyl can no longer treat his unease as imagination.

## N+4 — Chapter Seven: "A Table Set for Seven"

**Certainty:** PLANNED / DIRECTIONAL

**Purpose:** Pay the Flowered Sunder reception setup by bringing the Seven into one deliberately observable social space after several days of separate adult service.

**Primary frame:** the Hall of Seven Lamps reception established in Chapter Three, with the possibility of a private supper or side room becoming the literal "table set for seven."

**Desired payoff:**
- Each arrives carrying an institutional instruction, active plan, or secret.
- Thessa/Lyrion must navigate real intimacy in a space where their orders are watching.
- Caelen/Mireth courtship pressure becomes visible to outsiders rather than remaining mostly family-private.
- Damaris/Rhyl strain should be legible to friends before either fully explains it.
- Ilyan's access to Truth Beyond the Veil makes him socially valuable and personally vulnerable.
- Silver Night evidence, the curse investigation, and house instructions begin touching the same social network.

**Political function:**
- An older power explicitly recognizes that the Seven may be functioning as an informal cross-institutional bloc.
- The event should show the difference between friendship as the Seven experience it and friendship as institutions model it.

**Exit handoff:** at least one house or order begins planning how to recruit, divide, isolate, marry, promote, compromise, or otherwise manage the Seven as a network rather than as individuals.

## N+5 — Chapter Eight: "Separate Invitations"

**Certainty:** HORIZON / EXPLORATORY DIRECTION

**Purpose:** Show the adult world responding to the realization that the Seven still choose one another.

**Directional movement:**
- Several members receive attractive opportunities that would physically or politically separate them from the group: postings, travel, private patronage, a courtship invitation, order advancement, commercial access, or confidential work.
- At least one offer should be genuinely beneficial rather than obviously sinister.
- At least one character must consider accepting for a reason unrelated to romance.
- A family or institution tests whether individual ambition can fracture the Seven more effectively than overt threats.
- The curse evidence and Silver Night evidence should begin revealing overlapping intermediaries, administrative habits, or access networks without collapsing into one omnipotent conspiracy.

**Relationship function:**
- A relationship under pressure should confront the fact that love and friendship cannot require permanent physical proximity without becoming another form of control.
- A character may knowingly choose a dangerous person, post, or obligation despite credible warnings; the warning must remain remembered even if ignored.

**Seed debt beyond the window:**
- Prepare a future choice over whether explosive evidence is more valuable as justice, blackmail, protection, or bargaining leverage.
- Seed the first serious consequence of an institution attempting to separate one of the Seven from the others.

**Exit handoff:** the Seven's unity becomes a choice that must be repeatedly remade, not a default inherited from school.

# 10. Rolling-window quality check

Before any chapter is written, the runner should be able to answer:

- What is the next chapter for?
- What are at least three characters already planning beyond the current scene?
- Which element in N+2 needs setup now?
- Which element in N+3 needs setup now?
- Is there any subtle seed worth planting for N+4 or N+5?
- What previously seeded element now demands payoff?
- Which relationship is changing over more than one chapter?
- Which investigation has an evidence chain rather than a sudden revelation?
- Which family or institutional obligation is already in motion?
- What concrete event, promise, invitation, assignment, discovery, or failure causes the following chapter to happen?

If those questions cannot be answered, the story is not looking far enough forward.
