# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Active Five-Chapter Lookahead

This is the operational N+1 through N+5 plan for the first-principles rewrite. Unwritten plans are directional rather than historical fact; committed prose wins.

## Current committed rewrite state

Canonical numbered prose now runs through **Chapter Ten — What the Oath Keeps**.

Chapter Ten establishes the failed-release mechanism without converting lawful consent into hostile memory alteration. Teren's day +2 movement from Eiravel Memorial Infirmary was ordinary emergency bed redistribution: fourteen recovering patients moved, with Teren going only three streets to a Hall of Veils convalescent ward. The crucial split was administrative. Teren moved to the Hall while the protected rider continuation returned under seal to Joint Emergency Coordination.

When that temporary emergency body dissolved, its functions were distributed by subject. Teren's rider combined protected witness continuity, credential discrepancy, medical-security treatment, temporary Joint Order authority and an extinct emergency office. Contingency, the Joint Office for Order and Credential Integrity, and the Hall each correctly refused to exercise authority they did not possess. Contingency ultimately held the rider pending successor receiving authority or judicial substitution, but the old law imposed no automatic review date or recurring notice. The post-Silver-Night reform later added mandatory review intervals and individual transfer of open release obligations.

Doctor Aven Sorel's surviving note confirms Teren objected to transfer before his release review and requested written assurance that medical transfer would not terminate the obligation. The assurance survived. Teren therefore acted to protect his own future review even though he no longer remembers doing so. The attached rider-return slip also directs the black routing selector to be surrendered separately when Teren's temporary chair authority ended and provides a selector-custody accession reference. This is the dependency-valid bridge into Corren's later possession and return of the selector.

Thessa's Warden supervisor has now required future historical-record assistance to be formalized under a Hall file number. This is ordinary institutional accountability, not evidence of conspiracy surveillance.

## Governing debts

**Teren memory debt:** preserve the distinction between lawful compartment, failed release, later review and any still-unproven manipulation. Teren's forgotten self objected and demanded a written safeguard; do not flatten him into a passive victim.

**Mara/protected-person privacy debt:** Ilyan promised Teren not to expose the protected person to repair Teren's memory. Preserve that boundary even if later access becomes technically possible.

**Corren complexity debt:** Corren's established responsibility for Silver Night remains. His possession and later return of the black selector now has a concrete custody handoff to trace. Complexity is not innocence.

**Institutional failure debt:** Chapter Ten establishes a real structured failure: split jurisdiction, extinct temporary authority, a dead receiving supervisor, correct narrow refusals and no automatic review trigger. Later evidence may add deliberate intervention, but do not erase this demonstrated mechanism merely to make the mystery more dramatic.

**Relationship/adult-life debt:** Ilyan and Thessa are physically familiar and increasingly domestic while retaining independent jobs. Other relationships and professional lives continue to consume time and create discoverable trails.

**Demographic motive debt:** student-shield violation and unresolved heir-demographic pressure remain active architecture. Do not prematurely fix the expanding family, rival faction or exact numbers.
