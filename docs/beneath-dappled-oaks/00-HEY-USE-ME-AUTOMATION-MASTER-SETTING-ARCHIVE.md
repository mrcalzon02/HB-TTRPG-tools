## 2. Series identity

**Beneath Dappled Oaks** is an adult elven romantic political drama centered on a rotating ensemble of seven surviving academy friends entering adult society after a catastrophe killed almost their entire graduating class.

The core dramatic engine is the collision between:
- youthful friendship and inherited obligation,
- genuine attraction and strategic seduction,
- private grief and public ceremony,
- love and house duty,
- sacred orders and institutional corruption,
- noble marriage and reproductive politics,
- friendship and espionage,
- rebellion against family control and dependence on family status,
- official magical law and the dangerous people trained to enforce it.

The series should feel lush, intimate, socially observant, and politically dangerous. Characters are not detached pieces on a strategy board. They eat, dress, ache, grieve, desire, resent, flirt, sweat, sleep badly, notice each other's hands, remember dead classmates, and then sit through ceremonies where their families calmly discuss how those feelings may be used.

## 3. Prose and scene conventions

The desired prose is **lush and highly descriptive**, with long-form scenes that move naturally among:
- character detail,
- architecture,
- rooms,
- furniture,
- meals,
- uniforms,
- fabrics,
- gardens and weather,
- social ritual,
- magical ceremony,
- charged romantic observation,
- family pressure,
- political maneuvering.

Do not write the setting as a dry political synopsis with occasional romance pasted onto it. Likewise, do not let romance float free of the political structure.

A ballroom scene should tell us who is watching.
A kiss should create or end possibilities.
A dress, uniform, cloak, scar, insignia, seating arrangement, shared meal, private room, or ceremonial oath can all carry political meaning.

Dry wit may appear where appropriate, particularly around aristocratic hypocrisy and institutions that insist their contradictions are perfectly reasonable.

## 4. Book One: fixed premise

Book One begins with the final surviving members of an academy class preparing to graduate into the adult orders, houses, and political structures that have been waiting for them.

There were once many more.

A recent academy disaster killed **36 students and 6 servants**.

Only the central seven remain from the graduating cohort.

Publicly, the catastrophe is treated as a terrible accident associated with the west tower.

Privately, Ilyan Valeric reveals a far more dangerous truth.

### The Silver Night Disaster / west-tower failure

The west-tower catastrophe involved wards failing in sequence. The destruction affected the laboratories, residence wing, alchemical court, and Dmitrigical annex.

Ilyan reveals that his father and uncle deliberately arranged the failure. The motive was political: too many of the students approaching graduation belonged to factions whose survival and advancement threatened Valeric influence over the **Dmitrigical Arts**, including chairs, licenses, and tribunals.

Ilyan's uncle later congratulated him for "understanding" the necessity of what had been done.

This revelation is one of the moral fractures at the center of Book One.

Do not soften it into an unfortunate misunderstanding.
Do not casually resurrect the dead students.
Do not change the casualty count without an explicit canon correction.

The seven survivors are bound together not merely because they were classmates, but because they are the remnants of a generation that someone decided was politically inconvenient.

## 5. The Last Seven: core cast index

All seven are **adults**.

### Mireth Vael-Aesryn

**Order / path:** Green Covenant.

Mireth is part of the central surviving friendship circle and is entering adult induction under strong family pressure. Her house expects her personal relationships to have political utility.

**Relationship pressure:** Caelen Avariel-Caith is strongly attracted to her, and his family has already moved toward treating that attraction as potentially useful courtship strategy.

Do not reduce Mireth to "Caelen's love interest." Her choices, loyalties, house obligations, grief, and relationship with the wider seven must retain independent weight.

### Caelen Avariel-Caith

**Age:** 23.
**Order / path:** Moon-Crown royal service / Royal Martial Fellowship.

Caelen is physically marked by adult martial life, including a scar and stubble already used as sensual observational details.

He is attracted to Mireth. The attraction is genuine, but his family is willing to make it strategic and has placed courtship expectations around it.

His story should preserve the friction between sincere desire and the degrading realization that powerful relatives may consider even sincere desire an asset to be deployed.

### Thessa Eris-Vey

**Order / path:** Western Wardens.

Thessa is one of the seven survivors and faces recruitment and institutional pressure as she moves from academy life into the adult structures waiting for her.

**Relationship:** Lyrion and Thessa have established intimate/romantic tension and closeness. Preserve the emotional history between them rather than treating each appearance as a first encounter.

### Lyrion

**Order / path:** Silver Bough diplomacy.

Lyrion belongs to the central seven and is tied to the diplomatic sphere.

**Relationship:** Lyrion and Thessa have established intimacy/tension.

The full surname/house rendering is not yet sufficiently locked in this archive. Do not invent a definitive full surname merely to fill the blank. Use the established first name until a committed source resolves it.

### Damaris Caladren-Ilyra

**Order / path:** River Ledger / commercial councils.

Damaris is being drawn into the economic and political machinery of the houses.

Her family has ordered her to gain Ilyan Valeric's trust. This makes friendship itself an intelligence task.

**Relationship:** Damaris and Rhyl carry established romantic tension.

Her approach to Ilyan must retain ambiguity and moral cost: she is his friend, she is under orders, and those facts cannot be made comfortably compatible.

### Rhyl Orthell-Faen

**Order / path:** Red Hart wilderness vigil.

Rhyl is one of the surviving seven and is associated with the Red Hart's wilderness tradition.

**Relationship:** Rhyl and Damaris carry established romantic tension.

Do not isolate Rhyl from the larger political story merely because the Red Hart path is more wilderness-facing. The point of the seven separating into adult institutions is that their friendship becomes a bridge among systems that would otherwise manipulate them separately.

### Ilyan Valeric

**Order / path:** Truth Beyond the Veil.

Ilyan is the survivor most directly broken against his family's moral order.

He discovers that his father and uncle deliberately caused the west-tower catastrophe for Valeric political advantage. His uncle's reaction makes clear that the family regards such sacrifice as comprehensible, defensible, and potentially necessary.

Ilyan turns against that logic and seeks help among the friends who survived it.

The Truth Beyond the Veil has also introduced or accepted altered oath language around **"necessary truth"**, sharpening the conflict between the order's professed purpose and the manipulation of truth for power.

Ilyan's arc is not simply "good son discovers evil family." He has been raised inside the language, assumptions, education, rituals, and advantages of the system he now rejects. His rebellion should therefore be difficult, costly, and incomplete rather than morally effortless.

## 6. Relationship continuity matrix

Current fixed relationship threads:

- **Caelen ↔ Mireth:** genuine attraction complicated by family strategy and courtship pressure.
- **Damaris ↔ Rhyl:** established romantic tension.
- **Lyrion ↔ Thessa:** established intimacy/romantic tension.
- **Damaris → Ilyan:** ordered by her family to gain his trust; this is an intelligence/political demand layered over real friendship.
- **Ilyan ↔ the other six:** his disclosure about the Silver Night Disaster forces the friendship circle to decide whether loyalty to one another is stronger than loyalty to their houses and orders.

Do not assume these pairings are mechanically exclusive or guaranteed endgames. The world encourages flirtation, strategic seduction, jealousy, coercive courtship, political engagement, and divided loyalties. Preserve emotional continuity while allowing pressure to complicate it.

## 8. Noble houses and family power

The noble houses are active institutions, not surnames attached to characters.

Families attempt to control:
- marriages,
- lovers,
- introductions,
- ceremonial appearances,
- order placement,
- access to royal bloodlines,
- commercial arrangements,
- magical licenses,
- political sponsorship,
- inheritance,
- reputation,
- scandal,
- intelligence.

Young nobles may be instructed to seduce friends, secure allegiance, obtain secrets, create obligations, prevent rival marriages, or make themselves emotionally indispensable to politically useful people.

Some comply.
Some rationalize compliance.
Some refuse.
Some discover too late that what felt spontaneous was being encouraged by several older relatives at once.
Some weaponize the expectation back against their families.

The important dramatic rule is that **attraction can be real even when someone is trying to use it**.

That ambiguity should recur constantly.

## 9. Dark sympathetic magic: fetishes, Totems of Sin, and fertility curses

This is active setting canon.

A forbidden branch of shamanistic/sympathetic cursecraft uses small **fetishes, puppets, bound objects, sigils, personal remnants, and Totems of Sin** to affect a specific victim.

### Core limitation: proximity

These curses have **limited range**.

If a victim is suffering a persistent intimate or bodily curse, the working object is probably hidden near the victim's ordinary life.

For a noble this means:
- bedroom,
- dressing room,
- private garden,
- household shrine,
- carriage,
- office,
- cellar,
- walls,
- foundations,
- nearby grounds,
- frequently worn or carried possessions.

This rule matters enormously because effective placement usually requires **access**.

A distant enemy may have ordered the curse, but a servant, relative, lover, guest, craftsman, guard, gardener, valet, maid, or household officer may have physically planted the fetish.

Thus curse investigation becomes household treason investigation.

### Valid curse effects

Such magic may target ordinary-looking bodily or social vulnerabilities rather than spectacular injury.

Established examples include:
- impotence,
- infertility or impaired conception,
- disruption of marital consummation,
- diminished arousal,
- beauty curses,
- conspicuous moles or blemishes,
- rashes,
- itching,
- hair or complexion problems,
- other humiliating or strategically disruptive bodily effects.

These effects are especially dangerous in a noble culture where marriage, heirs, seduction, reputation, and bloodline politics have material consequences.

An impotence or infertility fetish can prevent a political marriage from being consummated.
A beauty curse can damage courtship or public confidence.
A rash before a major ceremony can become social sabotage.
A curse does not need to kill someone to alter succession politics.

### Totems of Sin

Totems of Sin are sympathetic objects constructed to represent, bind, distort, shame, inhibit, or weaponize a trait, desire, bodily function, temptation, or perceived failing of the target.

They are usually meant to be hidden, not displayed.

### Legal status

Malicious dark sympathetic magic is illegal and heavily punished.

Use against important nobles or royal bloodlines can become a matter of treason or succession security.

However, prosecution only matters if the crime is officially discovered and actually brought forward.

### The enforcement paradox

Those who police the Dark Arts must understand them well enough to identify and dismantle them.

Therefore investigators, magical orders, scholars, and enforcement specialists are taught the signs, sigils, materials, theory, and operation of prohibited magic "for educational and protective purposes."

This creates an institutional contradiction:

**the organizations responsible for suppressing forbidden magic necessarily produce people who know how to perform it.**

That knowledge can escape through:
- corrupt investigators,
- stolen instructional books,
- copied notes,
- retired examiners,
- compromised teachers,
- ambitious students,
- internal betrayal.

The orders policing Dark Arts are therefore also a recurring source of practitioners and leaked technique.

### Evidence as political leverage

Discovering a hostile fertility fetish does not automatically mean reporting it.

If a noble house can prove that a prominent member of another family used forbidden Dark Arts against them, the evidence may be more valuable as blackmail than as a criminal complaint.

A preserved fetish, testimony, materials trace, or investigator's proof can support:
- coerced alliances,
- trade concessions,
- marriage agreements,
- appointments,
- silence,
- votes,
- protection,
- long-term family leverage.

If the guilty practitioner is prominent and valuable to their house, the stain may threaten the wider family for generations.

Therefore characters discovering a curse must confront two separate questions:

1. Who did this?
2. Is justice actually the most valuable use of knowing?

### Investigation rule

When a curse is suspected, the story should respect the proximity rule.

Do not send characters across the kingdom looking for a remote caster while ignoring the household.

**If the curse is still working, search nearby.**

That rule should generate locked-room political mysteries, servant betrayals, family suspicion, planted evidence, compromised investigators, and uncomfortable searches of intimate spaces.

## 10. Romantic and reproductive politics

Fertility is political because bloodlines, inheritance, and marriage are political.

The setting may explore:
- pressure to produce heirs,
- anxiety around consummation,
- strategic marriage,
- annulment threats,
- accusations of deliberate infertility,
- fear that one spouse cursed another,
- a third party preventing an alliance,
- a house attempting to control succession through illicit magic,
- seduction schemes disrupted by a hidden curse,
- lovers discovering that a supposedly personal problem was political sabotage.

Treat these consequences as character drama, not merely plot machinery. Humiliation, suspicion, tenderness, anger, guilt, and misplaced blame can persist even after a fetish is removed.


## 10A. POV desire continuity and courtship norms

Recurring viewpoint characters must develop a stable, evolving **attraction grammar**.

Over repeated chapters, internal monologue should establish what each POV character notices first, which adult body types and presentations draw their eye, what voices or speech patterns affect them, which gestures or mannerisms repeatedly catch them, what emotional or power dynamics they gravitate toward, what kind of person reliably damages their judgment, and what exceptions surprise them.

Preferences should persist across chapters and be allowed to grow. They may broaden, become more specific, contradict earlier self-understanding, or change through experience, but they must not reset simply because the scene changed.

Use **docs/beneath-dappled-oaks/01-DRAMATIS-PERSONAE-INDEX.md** to record established attraction patterns once prose has demonstrated them.

Adult attraction is not restricted to one sex. Same-sex relationships are socially recognized and not inherently scandalous. Court scandal attaches primarily to house, marriage, succession, secrecy, betrayal, coercion, divided loyalty, and political consequence.

Great Houses nevertheless care strongly about succession and bloodline continuity. Same-sex dynastic couples may therefore require a negotiated heir compact, cadet-line succession, designation of a blood relative as heir, a prior child, or another explicitly agreed reproductive arrangement. That practical complication should create additional relationships and obligations rather than invalidate the couple.

The useful story question is: **who becomes part of the heir arrangement, what are they promised, and what new loyalty does that create?**


## 11. Book One dramatic trajectory

The first book is about the seven leaving the relative enclosure of academy life and discovering what adulthood inside their houses actually means.

Their families begin inducting them into the "truth beyond the veil": the cold reality of what they will be asked to do.

They may be told to:
- seduce friends,
- secure alliances,
- obtain secrets,
- bind houses through romance or marriage,
- betray confidences,
- accept atrocities committed for family power,
- treat dead classmates as necessary losses.

Ilyan's revelation about the Silver Night Disaster makes the conflict immediate.

The question is not only whether the seven believe him.

The deeper question is what they are willing to lose once believing him requires them to oppose the institutions that raised them.

### Governing relationship rule

**The bonds of love and the bonds of family are lethal political weapons in this setting.**

No burden is heavier than loyalty to family. No vulnerability is more exploitable than genuine love.

Family trees are therefore operational maps rather than decorative genealogy. Siblings, cousins, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, lovers, servants, household officers, guards, physicians, teachers, creditors, merchants, and institutional patrons can all become channels of pressure, rescue, betrayal, intelligence, blackmail, divided loyalty, or political consequence.

When expanding a major character, build outward toward parents or guardians, siblings, aunts and uncles, cousins, nieces and nephews where chronologically plausible, former and potential romantic attachments, closest friends, household staff, sworn guards, commercial contacts, institutional patrons, and at least one relationship the character would protect at real political cost.

Do not make these networks mechanically evil. Their dramatic force comes from the fact that affection may be sincere, loyalty may be honorable, and either can still be exploited.

## 13. Continuation priorities for the hourly story runner

Prefer the following kinds of advancement:

- Complete or deepen the seven separate graduation/induction tracks.
- Show house elders converting personal relationships into instructions and obligations.
- Let characters compare what their families demanded of them.
- Advance the investigation of the Silver Night Disaster.
- Develop who inside the Academy, orders, or houses knew what happened.
- Place friendships under pressure from incompatible instructions.
- Use courtship, flirtation, jealousy, genuine affection, strategic seduction, and interrupted intimacy as political action.
- Introduce sympathetic-curse mysteries where proximity and household access matter.
- Develop the contradiction inside the Dark Arts enforcement institutions.
- Preserve the grief of the dead classmates as an ongoing social fact.
- Let revelations create consequences that remain in later chapters.

Do not fill an hourly run with housekeeping prose about what should be written later.

Write the next useful material.

## 14. Anti-drift rules

**DO NOT:**
- turn any romantic member of the cast into a minor;
- replace the Last Seven with a new ensemble;
- forget the 36 students and 6 servants killed;
- retcon the west-tower disaster into a purely accidental event;
- absolve Ilyan's father and uncle without an explicit new canon development;
- make every family equally evil or every young noble equally rebellious;
- make all attraction fraudulent;
- make all attraction innocent of political consequences;
- treat seduction as consequence-free;
- turn sensuality into explicit pornographic description;
- convert the story into generic adventure fantasy that forgets houses, orders, rooms, meals, ceremonies, fabric, architecture, and social ritual;
- make Dark Arts fetishes work effortlessly across unlimited distances;
- forget that a working curse implies nearby physical access;
- make Dark Arts enforcement magically immune to corruption;
- introduce unrelated crossover lore from other elven projects unless explicitly authorized;
- import Nowhere King, Alfheim Reclaimed, Elder Wood restoration, or other unrelated setting material merely because it also involves elves.
