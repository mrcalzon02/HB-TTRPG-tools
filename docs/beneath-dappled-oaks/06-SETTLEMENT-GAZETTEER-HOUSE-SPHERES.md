# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Settlement Gazetteer and House Spheres

This document is active setting canon and supplements the World Almanac.

The Realm contains hundreds of named towns and thousands of villages, hamlets, mining camps, ferry settlements, temple communities, road stations, orchard clusters, charcoal camps, and seasonal markets.

Only a representative network is fixed here.

The governing principle is simple:

> **Once a permanent settlement grows much beyond roughly one thousand people, somebody important usually cares enough to dominate it.**

That does not mean every town is legally owned by a noble House.

The Concord of Crown and Commonwealth still gives settlements magistrates, charters, councils, guild rights, temple rights, local franchises, and regional representation.

"Control" usually means **dominant influence** through one or more of:

- surrounding land ownership;
- access to water;
- ownership of mines or mills;
- road and bridge finance;
- river or harbor access;
- guild patronage;
- banking and credit;
- major employers;
- military garrison;
- temple or order presence;
- control of market charters;
- public works;
- family alliances;
- influence over magistrate blocs.

A city may loudly insist that it is free while voting in almost every practical matter the way its dominant House prefers.

Both statements can be true.

# I. Settlement Scale and Political Attention

## Hamlets — fewer than 250

Usually too small to justify permanent outside administration.

Typical authority:
- village elder;
- reeve;
- temple keeper;
- estate steward;
- mine foreman;
- ferry family.

A Great House may technically own the surrounding land without caring about the hamlet's daily affairs.

## Villages — roughly 250–1,000

Often locally self-directed.

Outside influence exists, but it may be distant or informal.

These are the Realm's most common genuinely fringe communities.

A village under one thousand may remain effectively neutral if it has:
- poor roads;
- little taxable surplus;
- no strategic bridge;
- no valuable mine;
- no major temple;
- no unusual magical resource;
- no useful pass;
- no politically important family.

The moment one of those changes, neutrality becomes less durable.

## Small towns — roughly 1,000–5,000

Usually within somebody's recognized sphere.

At this size a permanent market, road junction, mine, mill complex, river landing, garrison, academy, or processing industry normally makes control worthwhile.

## Towns and lesser cities — roughly 5,000–20,000

Always politically valuable.

Such places usually contain several competing factions but have one dominant House, organization, Crown institution, guild bloc, or religious order.

## Major cities — over 20,000

No major city is politically simple.

Even when one House is dominant, rivals maintain:
- cadet families;
- guild allies;
- creditors;
- temples;
- legal offices;
- marriage networks;
- informants;
- warehouses;
- salons.

"Controlled by House Caladren" does not mean "everyone is Caladren."

It means Caladren interests are the default political gravity that everyone else must account for.

# II. House Avariel — The Crown

House Avariel dominates the Crownheart, royal administration, military roads, ceremonial districts, and places whose prosperity depends directly on the royal household.

## Aurelith — Royal Capital

Population: approximately **64,000**.

Sphere: House Avariel / Crown institutions.

Region: Crownheart.

Primary functions:
- royal palace;
- National Council;
- high courts;
- national ministries;
- major academies;
- luxury trades;
- diplomatic quarter;
- national archives.

Aurelith is formally governed through Crown and civic institutions rather than as a private Avariel estate.

In practice the royal House shapes:
- senior appointments;
- military presence;
- public works;
- ceremonial contracts;
- court economy;
- access to national decision-makers.

No other House can dominate Aurelith outright.

Every Great House nevertheless maintains substantial property, staff, salons, agents, and commercial interests inside it.

Political character:
dense, procedural, fashionable, expensive, suspicious of provincials, and dependent on food arriving from regions whose inhabitants resent being called provincial.

## Moonmere

Population: approximately **11,400**.

Sphere: Avariel-Caith and Royal Martial Fellowship.

Primary export/value:
horses, cavalry training, leather, military tack, officer schooling.

Moonmere sits on broad meadow country surrounding an old royal cavalry estate.

The Avariel-Caith branch dominates through:
- horse breeding;
- barracks;
- contracts;
- local land;
- officer patronage.

Caelen's branch is immediately recognizable here.

## Luneth Crossing

Population: approximately **7,200**.

Sphere: Avariel-Luneth.

Primary value:
royal petition road, bridge crossing, legal clerks, inns, document traffic.

The city grew around the last major crossing before petitioners enter the capital district.

Its scribes, advocates, copyists, notaries, messengers, and inns make bureaucracy into an industry.

## Merovan Downs

Population: approximately **3,800**.

Sphere: Avariel-Merovan.

Primary value:
ceremonial horses, country estates, court retreats, hunting.

The town appears politically sleepy.

That is partly because important agreements are often made in country houses outside town rather than in the magistrates' hall.

# III. House Vael — The Greenwood

House Vael dominates orchard country, managed ancient groves, living-wood industries, Green Covenant institutions, botanical magic, and several highly productive agricultural districts.

## Vaelwood

Population: approximately **23,500**.

Sphere: senior House Vael.

Region: Greenwood / southern Crownheart edge.

Primary value:
living wood, orchard produce, botanical magic, Green Covenant law, high-value timber.

Vaelwood is built around groves older than the Sunder Compact.

The city government is unusually constrained by grove law.

A magistrate can approve a road.

That does not mean the trees agree to move.

## Aesryn Green

Population: approximately **6,600**.

Sphere: Vael-Aesryn.

Primary value:
ritual botany, seed banks, medicinal cultivation, Green Covenant jurisprudence.

Mireth's cadet branch is culturally prominent here.

The settlement is known for:
- botanical courts;
- garden schools;
- herbal markets;
- legal disputes involving living-memory testimony.

## Thirren Orchards

Population: approximately **4,900**.

Sphere: Vael-Thirren.

Primary value:
fruit, cider, breeding stock, orchard estates, preserved luxuries.

This is one of the most productive orchard towns in the Realm.

Grandmother Apple-Bough observances are unusually strong here.

## Corath Reach

Population: approximately **2,300**.

Sphere: Vael-Corath, with strong adventurer and antiquarian presence.

Primary value:
relic traffic, forest-edge exploration, expedition supply.

Corath Reach is useful precisely because respectable society ends only gradually around it.

# IX. House Valeric

# XII. Neutral and Fringe Settlements

True neutrality is easiest below one thousand residents.

These places are often:
- remote;
- poor;
- difficult to reach;
- politically inconvenient;
- seasonal;
- culturally stubborn.

Their independence is not necessarily ideological.

Often nobody has yet found them profitable enough to conquer administratively.

## Cedra Delby

Population: approximately **740** in the village proper, with scattered farms bringing the surrounding district to roughly **1,100**.

Status:
locally governed farming community; nominally within a wider agricultural jurisdiction but not firmly dominated by a Great House.

Primary value:
grain, bread wheat, small orchards, household agriculture.

Why it remains locally distinctive:
its surplus is useful but not exceptional, its roads are secondary, and neighboring powers have generally found cooperation cheaper than control.

Canon custom:
the **Finger of Dough** harvest threshold offering.

Cedra Delby sits precisely on the edge of the thousand-person rule: the village itself remains small enough to preserve local autonomy, while the surrounding farm district occasionally attracts outside attention.

## Kallioused

Population: approximately **430** around the lake and road hamlets.

Status:
fringe lake community.

Primary value:
fishing, reeds, seasonal visitors, lovers behaving badly.

The lake is culturally famous but economically modest.

No Great House has found kissing sufficiently taxable to justify taking full control.

# XV. Political Rule for Settlement Control

A settlement's controller should never be treated as omnipotent.

Every controlled city still contains:
- magistrates;
- rival Houses;
- guilds;
- temples;
- creditors;
- neighborhood blocs;
- cadet families;
- labor organizations;
- smugglers;
- professional associations;
- personal grudges.

Control means the dominant faction can usually:

- set the agenda;
- place measures before magistrates;
- recruit sponsors;
- influence appointments;
- shape public works;
- provide or deny credit;
- determine which disputes receive attention;
- mobilize local allies.

It does **not** mean it automatically wins every vote.

A House that abuses a city can lose it.

A House that ignores a small town can discover a rival has quietly built influence there.

A neutral town that becomes prosperous will attract attention.

A controlled city whose main industry collapses may become politically available.

# XVI. Settlement Economy and the Seasonal Year

Every settlement should interact with the calendar.

Examples:

- Thawcall determines whether bridges and roads reopen.
- Seedwake determines agricultural credit.
- Leafrise determines first market advantage.
- Haycrest determines whether mine towns receive enough fresh food to expand crews.
- Deepdelve raises mineral output.
- Goldgrain creates wagon competition.
- Amberfall local political selection can change House influence.
- Casktide reveals whether a town has adequate winter stores.
- Frostgate isolates fringe settlements.
- Longnight concentrates politics indoors.
- Hearthwane sets the next year's local agenda.

A city cannot be understood apart from:
- what it imports;
- what it exports;
- what road or river reaches it;
- who finances that route;
- what happens when winter closes it.

# XVII. Automation and Story Use

Before placing a chapter in a settlement, the story runner should determine:

1. settlement name;
2. approximate population;
3. region;
4. dominant House or institution, if any;
5. rival influences;
6. primary import;
7. primary export;
8. reason the place exists;
9. seasonal vulnerability;
10. local Little Year custom;
11. which magistrates, guilds, temples, or families matter locally.

For settlements above roughly one thousand permanent residents, do not default to political neutrality.

If a settlement is larger than one thousand and still independent, establish **why**.

Possible reasons include:
- charter protection;
- competing powers balance one another;
- strong guild coalition;
- sacred status;
- academy or order autonomy;
- Crown protection;
- unusual legal history.

For settlements under one thousand, neutrality is plausible but not mandatory.

A tiny village with a valuable mine, bridge, shrine, pass, magical spring, rare crop, or strategic location may be more contested than a city ten times its size.

The question is never merely:

**How many people live here?**

The real question is:

**What is here that someone powerful cannot afford to ignore?**
