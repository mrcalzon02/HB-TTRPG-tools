# Beneath Dappled Oaks — Ecology Index of Elaranth

This document is active setting canon.

Elaranth is an ecologically living world.

Its forests, lakes, rivers, fields, marshes, mountains, coastlands, farms, pastures, roads, orchards, mines, cities, and magical regions are inhabited by ordinary animals, selectively bred domestic stock, magical variants, dangerous predators, migratory beasts, spirit-touched creatures, and a small number of legendary or national-threat entities.

The ecology should behave like a system.

Animals require:

- food;
- water;
- territory;
- nesting or breeding grounds;
- migration corridors;
- seasonal shelter;
- prey;
- pasture;
- disease resistance;
- reproductive success.

Magical creatures do not invalidate ecological reasoning.

They complicate it.

A magical predator still needs something to hunt.

A spirit-touched herd still requires winter forage.

A dragon-sized animal cannot simply appear in a forest without affecting prey, scavengers, streams, roads, and local settlement patterns.

This matters because **changes in animal behavior are information**.

A village may not know what moved into the northern woods.

It may know the boars are leaving.

That can be enough to mobilize an order.

---

# I. Ecological Intelligence

The Realm has centuries of practical knowledge about wildlife behavior.

Hunters, shepherds, foresters, wardens, farmers, miners, ferrymen, road keepers, cavalry officers, herbalists, and rural magistrates all watch for ecological anomalies.

Common warning signs include:

- prey animals abandoning good forage;
- predators entering villages in daylight;
- unusual migration direction;
- animals crossing roads outside normal season;
- birds abandoning nesting sites;
- fish dying or leaving a stretch of river;
- livestock refusing water;
- insect swarms appearing early;
- wolves leaving known territories;
- boars moving in large groups;
- deer descending from high ground too early;
- magical animals losing characteristic coloration;
- scavengers gathering where no carcass has been found;
- unusually silent forest areas.

The correct question is often not:

**What is this animal doing?**

It is:

**What has changed strongly enough to make the animal do it?**

## Displacement logic

A sudden animal migration may indicate:

- fire;
- flood;
- drought;
- famine;
- disease;
- poisoned water;
- logging;
- mine collapse;
- magical contamination;
- a new predator;
- territorial expansion by a legendary beast;
- a disturbed ancient site;
- large military movement;
- curse activity affecting land;
- climatic anomaly.

The animal itself may be dangerous.

The cause may be worse.

# II. Wild Boar

Earth analogue:
wild boar.

Wild boars are common in wooded and agricultural regions.

They are:

- strong;
- territorial;
- omnivorous;
- intelligent;
- destructive to fields;
- dangerous when cornered.

Ordinary local populations are manageable through:

- hunting;
- fences;
- dogs;
- crop timing;
- culling.

## Why boar migration matters

Boars do not normally abandon rich territory in large numbers without pressure.

A broad outward movement from established forest ranges can indicate:

- predation;
- shortage of mast;
- disease;
- fire;
- magical disturbance;
- territorial displacement.

This is a recognized warning sign.

## Felridge Forests incident

In 282 S.M., the **Northern Orders** report unusually active wild boar movement along the northern **Felridge Forests**.

The concern is not merely that more boars are roaming near farms.

The pattern resembles migration rather than ordinary expansion.

The boars appear to be moving away from deeper forest territory.

That raises the possibility that something larger has:

- entered their range;
- driven them from forage;
- begun hunting them;
- disrupted water;
- altered the magical ecology.

The **Midtrash Seventh Legion** is ordered north to investigate alongside local orders and wardens.

This is treated as an ecological-security matter rather than an ordinary hunt.

## In-universe news phrasing

A typical public report might say:

> Grim news from the northern reaches. The Orders report unusually heavy boar movement along the Felridge Forests. That alone would not ordinarily merit alarm, but the animals are not merely ranging farther than usual. They appear to be migrating. Wild boars are stubborn creatures and do not abandon good territory without cause. Something may be pushing them south and east. The Midtrash Seventh has been ordered north to determine what.

The information is unsettling precisely because nobody yet knows what the cause is.

# XIII. Monster Ecology

"Monster" is a cultural category, not a scientific one.

In practice it often means:

- too dangerous to tolerate near settlement;
- magically unstable;
- predatory toward people;
- territorially incompatible with agriculture;
- associated with curses or ancient ruins.

Some monsters are natural species.

Some are magical mutations.

Some are relic populations.

Some may be unique.

The Red Hart, Thornwall Companions, Western Wardens, Starward Aegis, and regional hunt orders maintain records distinguishing:

- breeding populations;
- solitary specimens;
- migratory threats;
- magically spawned anomalies.

# XVII. National-Level Ecological Threats

A national threat is not merely "a very large monster."

It is an ecological event capable of affecting:

- multiple regions;
- major roads;
- harvests;
- migration;
- water;
- military deployment;
- trade;
- settlement survival.

Examples include:

## Great predator displacement

A legendary predator expands territory and forces ordinary wildlife outward.

This is the logic behind concern over the Felridge boars.

## Magical bloom

Rapid spread of magical plants or fungi.

Can alter:
- soil;
- water;
- pollinators;
- livestock behavior.

## Watershed corruption

A major river becomes magically contaminated.

Effects can spread hundreds of miles downstream.

## Migratory collapse

A major herd or fish run fails.

This can cause:
- famine;
- predator movement;
- trade disruption.

## Ancient beast awakening

A long-dormant creature resumes feeding or territorial behavior.

The first evidence may be indirect.

## Ecological cascade

One species disappears or explodes in number and destabilizes several others.

These events may require:

- Crown military action;
- Regional Council coordination;
- knightly orders;
- magical specialists;
- Green Covenant intervention;
- Nymrael scholars;
- emergency food policy.

# XVIII. Threat Assessment

Field institutions commonly classify ecological incidents informally.

## Green

Normal seasonal activity.

## Amber

Unusual but locally manageable.

Example:
boar activity increasing near farms.

## Red

Regional threat requiring organized response.

Example:
large predator displacement, magical disease, hydra nesting.

## Black

Multi-region or national ecological emergency.

Example:
watershed corruption, ancient beast migration, major magical bloom.

These colors are not universal legal categories.

They are a widely understood operational shorthand.

# XX. The Midtrash Seventh Legion

The **Midtrash Seventh** is an established Crown field formation.

Common short forms:

- Midtrash Seventh;
- the Seventh;
- Seventh Legion.

Role:

- northern reinforcement;
- field investigation;
- large-animal response;
- road security;
- support for local orders.

It is not a monster-hunting order by itself.

It provides the manpower, logistics, camp security, scouts, engineers, and command structure needed when a local anomaly may become a regional problem.

In 282 S.M., the Seventh is ordered toward the Felridge Forests after reports of abnormal boar migration.

## Social consequence

Orders like this disrupt private life.

A soldier, officer, knight, engineer, healer, or scout may receive notice with only hours to prepare.

A romantic evening can end because the army has been ordered north.

That is normal military reality.

## In-universe scene tone

An adult officer might tell a lover:

> "I'm sorry, my dear. I cannot stay. The Seventh is being sent north. The Orders have reported a disturbance beyond Felridge and the King's command reached us before supper. I would much rather remain here, but we ride before midnight."

The lover may be disappointed.

The officer still goes.

Duty does not disappear because the scene was romantic.

# XXVIII. Story-Runner Ecology Rules

Before using an animal or ecological event, consider:

1. region;
2. season;
3. food source;
4. water;
5. breeding cycle;
6. normal range;
7. predators;
8. human pressure;
9. magical pressure;
10. whether current behavior is normal.

If animal behavior is abnormal, ask what caused it.

Do not make wildlife move randomly merely to create danger.

Do not make magical predators exist without prey.

Do not make giant creatures ecologically invisible.

Do not make farmers ignorant of livestock.

Do not make hunters ignorant of tracks.

Do not make every unusual animal automatically supernatural.

# XXIX. Canon Anchors

The following are established:

- Elaranth has a complete ecology of ordinary, domestic, working, magical, dangerous, and legendary animals.
- Animal behavior is a legitimate intelligence source.
- Wild boar migration can indicate displacement by a larger ecological threat.
- The northern Felridge Forests are currently experiencing unusual wild-boar movement in 282 S.M.
- The Northern Orders consider the movement significant.
- The Midtrash Seventh Legion has been ordered north to investigate.
- Military duty can abruptly interrupt romance, domestic life, trade, and politics.
- Magical ecology should remain internally coherent rather than operating as random monster placement.
