The groom was weighing the evening oats when a messenger appeared beneath the stable arch. Rain had darkened the man's cuffs, but not the square of paper he carried.

"Residence carriage to stand ready," the messenger said.

The groom read the instruction twice. It named the carriage and the hour. The line for destination was empty. Beneath it, the space for authority to depart was empty too.

"Who is travelling?"

"No passenger named."

"Who signed it?"

"I was told to deliver it."

That was an answer about a journey through the corridor, not a journey beyond the gates. The groom had learned to recognise the difference without enjoying it.

Two horses had already worked the lower road that day. Their ordinary night ration lay measured in a wooden bin, enough for recovery if no one invented a second shift. One oil flask remained beside the tack-room lamp. Its chalk mark showed how far the last trimming had brought the level down. No additional issue had been entered.

He could hitch the pair now and let them stand on the stones until someone remembered where they were meant to go. He could also spend the remaining light preparing a vehicle that might never move, while the horses waited hungry between the shafts.

The messenger tapped the paper. "They said ready."

"Then they can have the carriage ready to be harnessed."

He rolled the wheels a hand's breadth to test the brake, checked the linchpins, and laid the clean traces across the rail. The groom kept the horses loose in their stalls. He divided the measured oats between their pans, leaving the feed bin empty and the quantity recorded in the daily issue book. The single lamp he placed at the stable door, where it could illuminate both harness hooks without lighting an empty carriage.

"Will your officer call that disobedience?" the messenger asked.

"He may call it anything. Have you an order to take the team out?"

"No."

"Then I have not refused one."

In the margin, the groom wrote: *Carriage inspected; harness prepared; team not hitched. Night feed issued from existing allowance. Lantern retained at stable. Destination and departure authority not supplied.* He showed the entry to the messenger, who would sign only for delivery of the slip, not for any transport he had not witnessed.

The messenger went back through the rain. The horses lowered their heads to their pans. The empty destination line remained on the desk, and the wheels stayed behind the arch.
