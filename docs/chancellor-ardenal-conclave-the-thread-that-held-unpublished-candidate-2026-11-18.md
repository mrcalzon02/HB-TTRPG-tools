The boiler stair smelled of wet wool and iron. The junior laundress had dragged the repair basket down it because the washroom's front door was barred, and nobody seemed able to say whether the old route counted as an entrance anymore.

On the bench lay two grey service tunics. The first had split beneath the arm where a porter reached across his chest for the gate chain. The second had lost half its collar. Both bore ordinary laundry tags, not the numbered seals the magistrates had fixed to the Chancellor's private effects.

She laid out what she owned: a needle, a palm-sized offcut from the workroom's existing scrap tray, and thread wound around three fingers. There was enough for one sound repair, perhaps. Not two.

She turned the first tunic inside out. The tear ran beyond the old stitching into sound cloth; she could set the offcut behind it and spread the strain. Each prick of the needle made a small, unwilling sound. She tested the mend by pulling the sleeve across her own shoulder, then twice more, harder. It held. She did not trim the last thread until she was certain.

A porter came for his tunic while she was shaking the loose ash from it. He glanced toward the other one.

“Is that ready too?”

“No. The collar needs a strip wider than what I have.”

“Could you take it from the lining?”

“And leave the back rubbing your neck raw next week?”

He smiled, then stopped. “They say the wages are being arranged.”

“They say many things.” She handed him the first tunic. “Try your arm.”

He raised it as if hauling the gate chain. The patch stayed flat.

She found his tag in the ordinary laundry book and marked the article returned after repair, with the offcut and thread used. Beside the second tag she wrote *held for suitable cloth*. She did not write *lost*, because it was on the bench, or *ready*, because it was not.

At the bottom of her shift slip, a box asked whether labour had been paid. The previous shift had left it blank. She left it blank too. The repair had happened; that was a fact she could sign for. Nobody had shown her a wage receipt, a fresh supply delivery, or authority to promise either.

Beyond the boiler stair, boots struck the stones as the repaired tunic went back to work. The other remained folded, its torn collar showing, where the next shift could find it without being told a comforting lie.
