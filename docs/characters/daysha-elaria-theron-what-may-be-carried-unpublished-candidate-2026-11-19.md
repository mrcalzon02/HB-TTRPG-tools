## Scene

The little pots occupied the broadest window in Elaria's sitting room. Between rosemary and thyme stood a shallow dish of dark soil, the remains of an experiment that had not yet decided whether to grow.

Daysha set down a folded scrap of cloth. “I thought it might keep the roots damp on the way.”

“On the way where?” Elaria asked.

“To the new house.” Daysha heard herself and corrected it. “To the house we are thinking about. If it becomes yours.”

Theron, working a loose handle back onto a small garden trowel, looked up. “That is quite a journey for a plant with no destination.”

Elaria pressed her thumb against the nearest pot. The soil yielded, then held its shape. “These cuttings came from our first garden after the border negotiations. I carried them through three removals. I don't intend to lose them to somebody's enthusiasm for a field.”

“Somebody's?” Daysha said.

“Mine,” her mother replied. “Mostly.”

Daysha had pictured the orchard at Applewoud Lane so vividly that her mother's herbs had already found a place beneath an imaginary kitchen window. She had even imagined the smell of warm earth after rain. Nothing on that field belonged to them yet, not the old well, not the hedge, not a square foot where a pot might sit.

“Perhaps I should have brought a basket instead of a plan,” she said.

“I didn't say it was a bad plan.” Elaria turned the pot so a pale shoot faced the light. “I said a root ought not be lifted before there is somewhere sound to put it.”

Theron tested the trowel. Its handle held, although the repaired joint was plainly visible. “You could order us a greenhouse, I suppose. Someone would bring one before the ink dried.”

Daysha's smile faded. “I could ask for one. That doesn't mean I ought to.”

“No,” he said. “And the difference is precisely what concerns your mother.”

Elaria shook her head. “What concerns me is that my daughter has begun speaking of our home as though it were a kindness she must complete before supper.”

“I wanted you to have somewhere peaceful.”

“We want that too. But wanting is not a debt you can settle for us.”

For a while there was only the quiet scrape of Theron's trowel against the edge of the cloth. Daysha touched the handle, remembering how her father had always repaired things that still had useful work in them.

“Then tell me what I may carry,” she said.

Elaria considered the row of pots. She chose the shallow dish of undecided soil and placed it in Daysha's hands.

“This one may go as far as the sunny table,” she said. “We can watch it together. The others stay here until we know more.”

Daysha carried the dish across the room. Its weight was slight, but she used both hands.
