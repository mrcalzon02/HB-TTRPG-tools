## The Crooked Constellation

The first star came out looking like a comma.

Daysha lifted the piping bag and stared at the spot of pale icing on the practice parchment. “It was a star while I was thinking about it.”

“That is the trouble with stars,” Tavren Duskwick said from his copper bowl. “They have no consideration for the people drawing them.”

Selienne Moonmere did not laugh. Her silver-blond hair was tucked beneath her bonnet as she turned Daysha's crooked mark toward the light.

“You pressed after you stopped moving,” she said gently. “The bag kept answering the question your hand had already finished asking.”

Daysha inspected her gloved fingers. “I do that in council meetings.”

“Then perhaps this lesson will be useful in more than one room.”

The next star acquired six points, though no two seemed to belong to the same design. Daysha's long sapphire-blue hair fell over one shoulder as she bent closer, violet eyes narrowed in concentration. Her crown had slipped a little to one side.

Across the room, Mirelle Rosethorn was sorting fruit for her own tarts. “I can show you how I stop the last curl,” she offered.

“You're meant to be working.”

“I am working. These will be ready when the glaze is. Showing you one motion won't make the fruit ripen slower.”

Mirelle demonstrated on the empty edge of the parchment: squeeze, move, release, lift. Her chestnut bun bobbed as she counted the four actions. Daysha repeated them aloud and made a third star. It was smaller and quite unmistakably crooked.

Master Caelion Valebrook came by with his flour-dusted rolling pin. He examined the sheet with the solemnity of a judge.

“This one,” he said, pointing to the comma, “is a respectable beginning.”

“Of a sentence?” Tavren asked.

“Of learning to stop squeezing.”

Daysha laughed so hard she had to put down the bag. For a moment she considered asking Selienne to make the decoration properly and letting the lesson quietly disappear. Every exquisite thing in the palace seemed to arrive already finished. It was easy to forget that someone had once stood beside an imperfect first attempt.

“May I try the six-point one again?” she asked.

Selienne slid a fresh corner of parchment toward her. “You may try it as often as you like while this practice batch lasts. But let your wrist rest between stars.”

The fourth had six points, one absurdly long. Daysha looked at her three failed efforts and placed a tiny dot beside the longest point.

“There,” she said. “It has acquired a moon.”

Mirelle grinned. Tavren declared it an entirely new constellation. Even Caelion admitted he might recognize it if he saw it twice.

Selienne left the parchment on the practice table to dry, nowhere near the trays meant for serving. Daysha took off the piping glove and straightened her crown.

The crooked stars were still crooked. That was rather wonderful. For once, she had made something no one could mistake for the work of a master confectioner, and everyone in the room had helped her learn how.
