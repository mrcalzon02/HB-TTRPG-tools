## The Ground Before the House

At No. 62 Applewoud Lane, the path ended in grass. Daysha could see the old orchard beyond a broken line of hedges, but no house stood between the gate and the trees. The place where one had burned years ago had been cleared long before any of them came to look.

She lifted the hem of her light-blue day dress above the damp stems. Her long sapphire-blue hair kept escaping over her shoulder in the breeze.

“From the road,” Theron Lameris Dalunea said, “you would swear the whole field was level.”

“Then let us mistrust the road,” Elaria replied.

Daysha laughed and led her birth parents farther in. The ground was nearly even, but a slight fall carried yesterday's rain away from the old house site. Near the well the soil remained dark, and Elaria crouched to test it with two fingers.

“We should have someone examine the drainage before choosing where a floor belongs,” she said. “And the well. It is a well, not proof of safe water.”

Theron walked toward the orchard, looking back at the distance to the gate. “I could put a workbench somewhere with morning light.”

“You could,” Daysha said. “Provided you do not build it across Mother's kitchen window.”

“An east-facing window was a wish,” Elaria reminded them. “Not a plan submitted to a mason.”

Daysha turned slowly, imagining that window catching the first sun while the orchard lay beyond it. She could picture her father at a bench, her mother carrying herbs through an open door. The picture came so easily that she had to remind herself it was only a picture.

The grass hid an uneven patch beside the hedge. Theron reached back to offer Elaria his hand before she stepped across. Daysha watched the little movement and frowned at the distance they had already walked.

“If this is meant to be home, the walk from the road cannot become an obstacle every wet morning,” she said. “We need to know where a firm path can go before anyone promises a doorway.”

“And where guards might keep watch without making a prison of the garden,” Theron added.

Daysha looked toward the empty pasture. “Those are our security costs to discuss. Not something to bury in Lillian's sale price.”

Lillian loved this ground, Daysha knew, and could not shoulder its taxes and the expense of building here. Through the realtor she had offered the present valuation, before any royal connection could change what people would ask for it. That generosity deserved clear papers, not a procession of workmen acting as though the land had already changed hands.

“No stakes for foundations,” Daysha decided. “No orders to clear the orchard. First, a survey and a proper account of what the household and its protection would really require. And the purchase must be settled properly.”

Theron nodded. Elaria took her daughter's arm for the walk back.

“May I still hope for a peach tree?” Elaria asked.

“Of course,” Daysha said. “Hoping is the one part we can afford today.”

They left the field as they had found it: open, unbuilt, and still belonging to its own unfinished story.
