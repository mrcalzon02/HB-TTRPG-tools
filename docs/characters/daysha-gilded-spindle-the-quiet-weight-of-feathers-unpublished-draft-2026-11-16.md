## The Quiet Weight of Feathers

At ten, the auburn-haired seamstress had already answered the morning's ordinary questions, emptied her coffee cup, and checked the workroom hooks twice.

The gown hung from them in a red and gold cascade. It had no mannequin beneath it to lend it false shape. Every bright feather panel had to bear its own weight.

She remembered carrying the first version into the Gilded Spindle: *Butterfly Flame*, a startlingly sheer red design she had imagined selling to a noble customer. Queen Daysha had loved the movement of it. She had also looked through one glowing panel toward the public street and said, with considerable kindness, that she did not intend to show quite so much of herself to the court.

That had been the beginning, not the rejection.

Twice this week the queen had returned to the cutting tables. Daysha proposed turning the butterflies' flight into a phoenix rising across the skirt. She wanted the firelight to travel outward from the waist, but she also wanted the bodice properly covered. The seamstress sketched overlapping feathers; Daysha moved their sweeping line higher; the seamstress added a closely woven backing where bright gaps had once been the principal effect.

Now Master Garrick Vellmont stood beside the hooks with his ledger closed. The senior silver-haired seamstress checked a hem nearby, but left the new maker's chalk and pins untouched.

Daysha entered with her long sapphire-blue hair over one shoulder, violet eyes already searching for the changed silhouette. One pointed ear showed where she tucked her hair behind it.

“Oh, you kept the flames.”

“I kept what could move with you, Your Majesty,” the seamstress said. “The feathers are not merely sewn over the open places. They are supported from underneath. Otherwise they would pull the new backing out of shape.”

Daysha held out her arms for the fitting. The dress came off the hooks, and the two women eased its weight into place. In the mirror, gold-edged feathers climbed the red skirt; none of the old transparent panels opened against the light.

Then Daysha turned.

A short plume beneath her left arm lifted the backing with it. The seamstress saw the narrow strip of light before anyone remarked upon it.

“Please stop there,” she said.

Garrick went still. Daysha stopped at once.

“The shoulder anchor is pulling the panel upward. I can hide that with another feather, but it would happen again whenever you reach.”

“What would you do instead?” Daysha asked.

“Shift the strain to the side seam. One finger's width. I need to pin it now and stitch it properly after you take the gown off.”

“Then do that.”

The seamstress placed one pin, released the shoulder's false tension, and asked Daysha to turn again. The backing remained smooth. Daysha smiled at the mirror; the maker watched the seam.

“Better,” said the queen.

“Better for a fitting,” the seamstress replied. “Not yet finished.”

The silver-haired master gave the smallest approving nod. Garrick recorded the alteration, not a sale, while the auburn-haired seamstress marked the pin's position before anyone could mistake a promising turn for a completed dress.
