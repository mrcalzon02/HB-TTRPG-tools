## The Hundredth Bend

The square of cloth stretched across the embroidery hoop was barely wider than Daysha's palm. That made it more interesting than any of the splendid gowns arranged beyond it.

“May I?” the queen asked.

The elven seamstress did not surrender the hoop. “You may watch first, Your Majesty. I haven't proved that the edge will hold.”

Garrick Vellmont, his ledger tucked beneath one arm, looked almost relieved. “An excellent rule. I should like to keep both the cloth and the royal fingers intact.”

Daysha's long sapphire-blue hair brushed her shoulder as she leaned closer. Her crown remained firmly in place; her violet eyes were fixed on the needle.

The seamstress drew a single bright thread through the sample. For a heartbeat a bead of water rested on the stitch, held in a tiny curve of cool air. Then the thread warmed, and the bead lifted as mist. No flame touched the silk.

“Water and warmth?” Daysha asked.

“Water and the *limit* of warmth,” the artisan corrected. “If I bind the elements too tightly, the fabric pulls against itself. If I bind them too loosely, the enchantment wanders into the next seam.”

Daysha considered the unassuming patch. “So it isn't enough for the magic to be beautiful.”

“Beauty is the easy test,” said the seamstress, and immediately looked appalled at having said it to a queen.

Daysha laughed. “Please don't apologize. Half the kingdom would benefit from that lesson.”

From a nearby table, another elven artisan offered a wooden form. The seamstress slipped the sample over its curved edge and flexed it. A faint silver line appeared along the fold.

“There,” she said. “A failure. It endured the demonstration, but not the movement. Were that a finished garment, the wearer would pay for my impatience.”

Daysha's smile softened. She had been admiring the shimmering mist; now she was studying the fault. “Can you mend it?”

“Yes. But first I must find out whether the thread is wrong or my stitching is.”

Garrick opened his ledger. “Then the workshop will account for the examination time. We do not disguise an experiment as a completed commission.”

“Quite right,” Daysha said. “And no one will tell me the piece is ready merely because I have come to see it.”

The seamstress met her gaze at last. “Then, Your Majesty, would you return when I can show you what happens after the hundredth bend?”

“I would rather see the hundredth bend than the first sparkle,” Daysha replied. “Though I confess I intend to enjoy both.”

Garrick closed the ledger without recording an order. The needle returned to the cloth, and the queen stayed a little longer to watch the work.
