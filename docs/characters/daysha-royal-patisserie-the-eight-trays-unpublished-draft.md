## The Eight Trays

Daysha had been certain there was room. The long table was nearly empty, and eight trays seemed a modest request.

Master Caelion Valebrook did not look at the table. He opened the oven door a careful inch, let the heat out against his flour-dusted sleeve, and closed it again.

“Two ovens,” he said. “Both committed. One to the household bread, one to the bread for the people coming off night duty. If I move either batch, someone eats late.”

“The pastry could go in afterward,” Daysha suggested.

“It could go in afterward,” Caelion agreed, “but it couldn't come out cool before your afternoon table.”

Mirelle Rosethorn slid a rack nearer the open window, then stopped when its wheels caught against another. “And these are full. Hot choux collapses when we pack it, Your Majesty. Even an empty oven wouldn't give us another cooling rack.”

Tavren Duskwick lifted his whisk from a bowl of custard. “And I won't fill warm pastry and pretend that's an improvement. It needs time in the cool pantry, which is holding the milk for supper.”

Selienne Moonmere, silver-blond hair tucked neatly under her bonnet, set down a half-finished sugar leaf. “We can finish the ornaments. We cannot make a safe place to put the finished sweets by drawing one on paper.”

Daysha looked again at the table. It was large enough to display anything and equipped to make nothing. Her proposed eight trays had already become a promise to the household steward. She could send a royal instruction and make the bakers choose whose work to spoil.

“Could we use the west corridor?” she asked.

Caelion shook his head. “It's warm beside the chimneys. The glaze would run.”

“An extra shift?”

Mirelle's smile faded. “Our next shift starts before sunrise.”

That was the answer Daysha had been hoping not to hear. She lifted the request slip, blue hair falling over her shoulder, ready to cross out a name, and found she had written only the quantity.

“What can you give them,” she asked, “without moving the bread or keeping anyone here late?”

Caelion considered the shelves. “The tea biscuits baked this morning are already cool and set aside for that table. One tray, not eight. Mirelle can arrange them with the fruit that's already washed. Selienne can add her sugar leaves. Tavren's custard stays for the service it was made for.”

“No fresh cream puffs?” Daysha said.

“Not today.”

She felt the little sting of having promised a spectacle and received, instead, the truth. Then she put the slip flat and struck through the eight.

“One tray,” she said. “No one else's supper delayed. I'll tell the steward I asked for more than this kitchen could make, and that the smaller service is my decision.”

Tavren returned the custard to the cool room. Mirelle made space on one rack rather than forcing two into the same place. Selienne rescued her sugar leaves from the heat. Caelion kept the bread at the center of his schedule.

When the steward came, Daysha gave him the corrected slip herself. He looked disappointed. She let him look disappointed.

Behind her, the first oven opened on time.
