## The Rose Must Have a Root

The first thing Daysha moved was not a jewel, but the drawing.

“Here,” she said, turning the sketch so the two seamstresses could see. “The heart was lovely. But I want the center to bloom.”

On the cutting table lay ivory satin, a deep-blue waist panel, and a length of gauze fine enough to lift whenever the door stirred. The younger auburn-haired elven seamstress placed five sapphire-colored glass counters across the paper, overlapping them like rose petals.

“A sapphire rose,” Daysha said, delighted. Then she pressed one finger beside the arrangement. “But if those were stones, wouldn't they pull the entire front downward?”

The senior silver-haired seamstress nodded. “They would, if we stitched them to the satin alone. A heavy flower needs a hidden foundation.”

She set a narrow strip of stiff woven tape beneath the sketch and drew lines from the proposed rose toward the bodice seams.

“Then the front would carry it?” Daysha asked.

“The fitted inner layer would,” said the younger seamstress. “Not the decorated outer one. And we must leave enough give here for breathing.”

Daysha stood and bent slightly, testing the imagined weight against her own movement. Her iridescent blue hair slipped across one pointed ear. Her violet eyes returned to the counters. “Make the inner petals smaller. The silver embroidery can finish the outer ones. I want a rose, not a stone breastplate.”

The younger seamstress gathered the glass counters inward. The senior maker marked a second, lighter outline. No stone was fastened.

Across the table, a sleeve sample floated from an ivory shoulder piece in two airy arcs, a drape inspired by flowing desert court garments. Daysha drew it over her forearm. The gauze shimmered with scattered silver stitches, but its weight caught at one point and slipped toward her elbow.

“It looks marvelous while I'm still,” she said. “It would spend a dance trying to escape.”

“Or drag your shoulder seam with it,” said the senior maker.

Daysha lifted the loose end higher. “Could the drape begin behind the shoulder and meet the wrist at two places? Let the silk beneath cover the arm where it should, and let this be air.”

The younger seamstress pinned the sample to a separate linen strap, then asked Daysha to raise her arm and turn. The gauze opened like a sail and settled without pulling the strap out of line.

Master Garrick Vellmont, an elven proprietor with his ledger tucked under one arm, watched the two artisans exchange a cautious smile.

“A promising design,” he said.

“An unanswered design,” corrected Daysha, setting the sleeve aside. “We haven't tested a real setting or made the bodice.”

Garrick opened his ledger and wrote *rose support and sleeve trial*, nothing resembling a sale. The younger maker kept the pins in the sample, and the older one left the rose unmounted, so the next review would begin with work still to prove.
