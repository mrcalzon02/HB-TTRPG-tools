## What the Garden Can Give

The palace herb plot looked generous from the path. Up close, Daysha could see the gaps: one bed of thyme with bare earth between the stems, mint crowded against a stone, and a row of seedlings still wearing the pale tags of the head gardener.

“Enough for a banquet?” she asked.

Her mother crouched beside the thyme. Elaria Lameris Dalunea rubbed a leaf between two fingers, then let Daysha smell it. “Enough to make a banquet smell as though there were more.”

“That is a very diplomatic answer.”

“It is an accurate one.” Elaria turned over a tag. “These are young. Cut them hard now and we may have no thyme here next month.”

Daysha tucked a loose strand of iridescent blue hair behind her long elven ear and looked again. She had counted green leaves from the path; Elaria was counting roots.

An attendant had left a small basket by the gate, beside a list from the kitchens. Daysha read the requests aloud: thyme for broth, mint for tea, balm for a settling infusion. None was extravagant on its own. Taken together, they would strip the beds.

“Could we move the seedlings to the warm wall?” she asked.

“Perhaps. But we would need to ask whose seedlings they are, and whether the wall is meant for them.” Elaria pointed to a damp patch under the stones. “Those roots may rot there. The gardener knows which part stays wet after rain. I do not.”

Daysha smiled. “You can admit someone else knows a garden better than you?”

“I would be a poor scholar if I could not.”

At the far end, a few empty pots stood beneath the potting shelf. Daysha picked one up, then stopped herself.

“I was going to send for seed stock,” she confessed. “And then I was going to tell the kitchens the problem was solved.”

“Seed is a possibility,” Elaria said. “Not a sprig for today's pot. Nor is a request a delivery.”

Daysha replaced the pot. “I miss when you could make a whole afternoon out of showing me a leaf.”

“I can still show you a leaf.” Elaria lifted one bruised mint tip. “I cannot promise you an afternoon without petitioners.”

They laughed softly. For a moment neither queen nor noblewoman had an answer ready.

Daysha turned the kitchen list over and wrote a question for the head gardener: what could be cut without harming the beds, and was propagation or new seed stock feasible? She left the requested quantities open.

“Nothing has been approved,” she said, handing the paper to the attendant. “Please ask, don't order.”

Elaria stood, brushing soil from her palms. She offered her daughter the unbruised side of the mint leaf.

“Tea?” Daysha asked.

“A suggestion,” Elaria said. “We have not asked the kitchen yet.”
