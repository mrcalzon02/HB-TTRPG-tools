## Scene

Elaria had drawn the garden twice. On the first sheet, neat rows ran from a little square marked WELL. On the second, every row had been rubbed out.

Daysha turned the papers beneath the sitting-room lamp. Her long sapphire-blue hair fell over one shoulder, catching a pale glimmer as she studied the crossed-out lines.

“Did you change your mind?”

“About the garden? Certainly not.” Elaria tapped the empty square. “About where to put it? I remembered that a well drawn on a property sketch tells me nothing about its water, or who has the right to use it.”

In the corner Daysha recognized the address: #62 Applewoud Lane. For the moment it belonged to Lillian's family, however willing they might be to discuss a sale.

“You have designed half a lifetime for a place we haven't bought,” Daysha said.

“I have imagined half a lifetime. Designing comes after someone agrees that the ground is ours to disturb.”

Daysha put down the map, relieved and unexpectedly disappointed. She had been picturing her mother among herbs instead of far away, and had quietly made a hundred arrangements that no one had requested.

“There could be room near the orchard,” she offered. “A bed against the hedge, perhaps.”

“Perhaps. Unless that is where the cattle go, or where the roots take all the water.” Elaria tilted her head. “What has made you so certain I should grow anything there?”

“Because you always knew which leaves to pick when I was small. Because there were evenings when you could make the whole house smell of mint, and I knew you were home.”

The answer surprised them both.

Elaria folded one corner of the sketch, then smoothed it again. “You want me nearby.”

“Yes. And I am afraid I will ask you to come, then be too busy being queen to visit you.”

“You are my daughter before you are a hostess, Daysha. I can bear an unanswered invitation better than a visit offered as a duty.”

“Rome will want us to find the time.”

“Then the two of you should find it when you have it. I did not teach you to tend living things so you could turn yourselves into them.”

Daysha laughed. “That is a terrible gardening lesson.”

“It is an excellent lesson about not overwatering.”

They sat together while the lamp sank lower. Elaria listed what she would need to learn before suggesting a single planting: which ground held winter wet, where the shade fell, whether the well could sustain another use. She made no claim that her herbal knowledge could answer questions she had not investigated.

Daysha drew a small question mark where the first bed had been.

“May I come with you to look, if the purchase ever goes through?”

“Of course,” Elaria said. “But not as the queen bringing an estate into order. As the daughter who remembers the smell of mint.”

The garden remained only a possibility. The visit, at least, had been honestly asked for.
