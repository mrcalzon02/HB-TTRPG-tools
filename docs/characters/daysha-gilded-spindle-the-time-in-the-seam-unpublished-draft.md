# The Time in the Seam

**Continuity:** An undated *possible* repair visit **after** Daysha has already acquired her midnight/periwinkle-blue gown with long draped sleeves and restrained gold filigree. The blue gown lay inside out across the senior seamstress's knees. Without its draped sleeves spread wide, it looked rather smaller than Daysha remembered.

“Did I ruin it?” Daysha asked.

The elven woman adjusted her spectacles. “You wore it, Your Majesty. There is a difference.”

One sleeve had pulled away from its hidden support seam. The gold filigree was sound, but the weight of the hanging cloth had rubbed a line of tiny holes beneath it. The seamstress showed her where a beautiful outside could conceal a tired inside.

“Could you stitch it closed?” Daysha asked.

“I could make it look closed by luncheon. Then it would tear again the next time you raised your arm.”

Daysha's long blue hair fell forward as she examined the damage. “And if we do it properly?”

“We unpick the strain-bearing edge, put fresh backing beneath it, then set the sleeve again. I can begin the reinforcement now. I cannot finish the fitting today without taking hours from the other work already promised.”

Master Garrick Vellmont stood beside the cutting table with his ledger closed. He made no attempt to argue with her.

Daysha looked at the gown's empty sleeves. “I had hoped to wear it soon. Perhaps I could choose another—”

Garrick's eyebrows rose with the familiar expression of a merchant who knew exactly how that sentence tended to end.

“Another new dress,” Daysha finished, and heard herself.

The seamstress threaded a needle, waiting. At the far table, another elven worker was setting out a customer's alterations in the order they were due. Daysha could see how easily a royal request might push that careful row aside without anybody daring to say what had happened.

“Whose appointment would mine displace?” she asked.

“We needn't discuss the customer,” the seamstress said. “Only that the time was promised.”

Daysha nodded. “Then keep the promise. There are dresses in my wardrobe that have scarcely had a second outing.”

Garrick opened his ledger, this time to mark the repair as unfinished, not urgent.

The seamstress tucked a small strip beneath the worn cloth and made three close stitches. She turned the sleeve so Daysha could feel the weight resting on the new support rather than the decorative thread.

“Those stitches are the beginning,” she said. “Not permission to wear it away.”

“I understand.” Daysha smiled. “I suppose a queen must learn the difference between having a great many gowns and having a great many seamstresses.”

“A useful distinction,” Garrick said gravely.

Daysha lifted the sound sleeve and let its periwinkle folds spill over her arm. Her pointed ear showed through the blue curtain of her hair as she bent to inspect the work again.

“Tell me when it is ready,” she said, “not when you think I want it to be ready.”

The seamstress returned to her needle. For the first time that morning, she was setting the pace of the room.
