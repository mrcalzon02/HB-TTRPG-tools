The buckle had been made for two hands.

Rome managed the first leather strap with his left and held the cloak against his chest with his chin. The second slipped through the loop, fell, and struck the floor. He looked at it for a while before bending to retrieve it.

Daysha waited by the door. Her long blue hair had escaped its tie, and the violet of her eyes was sharper than the dim light. She had been careful not to watch his empty right sleeve. That had turned out to be another way of watching it.

“You can say something,” Rome told her.

“I thought you might prefer that I didn't.”

“I'd prefer the buckle to stop winning.”

She came closer but didn't take it from him. His father's explanation still lingered in the rooms they had left behind: a final test, a journey, a reason for the court to see less of its prince. None of those words accounted for the weight missing from Rome's right side.

“I know what they were trying to hide,” he said. “I keep reaching for the arm as though it's still there.”

Daysha's hand went to the cloth covering the mark above her own right elbow. She stopped before touching it. The ancient sigil had made them both afraid of things neither could explain. Cutting away the place where Rome's mark had shown had not supplied an answer.

“I was angry with you,” she said.

Rome looked up. “For losing an argument with a king?”

“For standing there and telling me you were all right afterward.”

“I wasn't going to make you watch me fall apart.”

“You needn't perform being all right for me.”

He gave a small, exhausted laugh. “Apparently I've been trained for a different audience.”

Daysha knelt to pick up the strap. She laid it beside him instead of fastening it. Rome's left hand trembled when he tried again. He let the buckle rest.

“Ask me,” she said.

“For help?”

“For whatever you want. You get to decide what that is.”

He studied the two ends of the strap. “Hold the cloak steady. Don't tie it yet.”

She braced the fabric against his shoulder while he threaded the leather through with one hand. When he had it in place he nodded, and only then did she pull the strap snug. The small success cost him more effort than it ought to have.

“Will you ask what comes next?” he said.

“I will ask what they know. I won't accept a story dressed up as an answer.”

“And if they don't know?”

“Then we will still have a question.”

Rome lowered his head until his forehead rested briefly against hers. Outside, someone called for the party to make ready. Daysha did not promise that the curse would break or that the arm would return. For this moment he wanted only the cloak to stay closed when he walked.

He stood. It did.
