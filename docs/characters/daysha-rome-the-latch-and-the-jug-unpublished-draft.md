## The Latch and the Jug

Rome had filled the water jug himself. That was not the difficult part.

The difficult part was the door.

He could carry the jug with his left hand if he kept its weight close to his ribs. But the latch lifted with a thumb, and the door opened toward him. The hand that ought to have held the jug while the other worked the iron was gone. His empty right sleeve moved when he shifted his shoulder, as though it had an opinion.

He set the jug on the floor and opened the door. By the time he bent to pick it up, the door had swung almost shut. He tried holding it with his boot. Water sloshed over the rim and darkened the toe.

“Wonderful,” he told the jug. “Now you've defeated my footwear.”

Daysha appeared on the other side. Her blue hair was loose, and she looked first at the puddle, then at him.

“I can take that,” she said.

“I know.”

She waited. There was no offense in her offer, and he did not want to turn it into one.

“I wanted to bring you water,” Rome said. “Not deliver a demonstration of my ability to defeat a door.”

“Then what do you want to do?”

He considered telling her to hold the latch. That would get them through. Instead he looked at the little wooden block resting against the wall, used to keep the door from swinging when the room was aired.

“Could you move that within reach of my foot?”

She set it beside him, then stepped back.

Rome put down the jug, pulled the door wide with his left hand, and nudged the block under its edge with his boot. He tested the hinge. The door stayed where he had put it. Only then did he lift the jug again.

He carried it across the threshold more carefully than he had carried a shield through any doorway. His sleeve brushed the frame. Nothing else struck it.

Daysha moved an empty cup nearer the middle of the table, where it could not tip over. She did not reach for the jug.

“Would you pour?” she asked.

He did. A little water ran down its lip, but most of it reached the cup. He filled another for himself.

“That took an absurd amount of planning,” he said.

“Yes,” Daysha replied, taking her cup. “And now we have water.”

Rome glanced back at the door, still held by the block. He would have to retrieve it eventually. He could manage that with an empty jug.

He drank first, before attempting the next problem.
