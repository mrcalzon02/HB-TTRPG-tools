# The Crown Beneath the Sunder Moon
## Elaranth: Rome, Daysha, Beneath Dappled Oaks and Heroes of Embryon

> In Elaranth, a coronation is a promise to govern a nation that is constitutionally permitted to answer *no*.

### 1. Canon boundary and the true nation

**Owner-established: these are one fictional universe.** *Beneath Dappled Oaks*, the royal life of Queen Daysha and King Rome, and the fantasy-world fiction behind *Jewel Brook: Heroes of Embryon* all take place in **Elaranth**, formally the **Concord of Crown and Commonwealth** or **Sunder Concord**. *Beneath Dappled Oaks* names the romantic-intrigue novel, not a second country. *Jewel Brook* names the game, not a different planet. The game shares heroes, courts, mineral wealth, and physical world with the novel and royal chronicle. Actual implemented gameplay mechanics and campaign maps are not automatically historical occurrences.

**Owner-established opening:** *Beneath Dappled Oaks* starts **halfway through King Rome's FIRST year as reigning king**. Rome and Daysha are already husband and wife; Daysha is queen. **Princess Estelle has not been born.** She is not a newborn, a student, a council visitor, or a hidden observer in that opening. Her birth IS in Rome's first regnal year, AFTER the novel opens. Working birth: 15 Casktide, 282 S.M. (266 days after the proposed wedding/conception). The family scenes with older Estelle, the garden chase, daycare, rafters prank, and cats are **later historical scenes**, at dates not yet established.

**Calendar alignment, working not confirmed:** The established Sunder Moon calendar has 13 months of 28 days (364 days). If Rome's accession is placed on 1 Thawcall 282 S.M., the civil midpoint is the turn between 14 and 15 Deepdelve 282 S.M. For indexing, use **14 Deepdelve, 282 S.M.** as the *working day-level representation* of the owner-established half-year opening; **the exact accession day and exact novel-opening day remain provisional until synchronized with the committed chapter prose**. Year 282 S.M. is the previously established BDO era. Do not claim the provisional day is directly user-selected, or retrofit season-dependent BDO scenes without review.

**Other continuities remain independent unless subsequently joined by the owner.** The *Nowhere King* prose series (Luceran, Castle Edselsto, Embar-Zara) is **not** by default the Rome court or Elaranth; the *Magenheim* boss named Nowhere King is unrelated to that prose series. *Blacklight Continuum* is an independent far-future mirror-universe campaign. *Kaysender* and other original adventure settings have their own dates, rules and geography; shared names, dragons, elven ears and spells are not evidence of one timeline.


### First-year birth correction and sixteen-year family chronology

The owner's later timeline decision supersedes any earlier suggestion that Estelle is born only after Rome's first year. She is **unborn at the novel's midpoint opening** but **born later during Rome's first regnal year**. Under the working assumption of wedding/conception on **1 Thawcall, 282 S.M.** and a 266-day gestation measured from conception, the expected birth date is **15 Casktide, 282 S.M.** The novel's working opening is **14 Deepdelve, 282 S.M.**, 85 elapsed days earlier. The exact days are calculated planning dates, not witnessed medical records.

The 1,000-day game/royal-history milestone is elapsed day 1,000 (**21 Casktide, 284 S.M.**), by which time Estelle is **two complete 364-day Elaranthi years and six days old**. Sixteen complete life-years following her projected birth end on **15 Casktide, 298 S.M.**—within **Rome's seventeenth regnal year**. The 16-row `data/royal-sixteen-year-timeline.csv` deliberately counts birth-to-birthday life-years, not reign-year chapters. The existing royal story retains 17 intersecting reign-year slots. Art without actual dated story evidence stays unassigned in `data/royal-artwork-placement.csv`, and cross-fiction events appear in `data/master-storybook-events.csv`.

### 2. The state the new monarch inherits

The Delphimi Wars overthrew a tyrannical royal order. At **Year 0 S.M.** the Sunder Compact joined the continuing Crown to a politically autonomous Commonwealth. The moon Saelith bears the Sunder wound, a visible fracture and an unresolved astronomical and theological mystery. The Concord has survived 282 years by preventing either ruler or council from eliminating the other.

The monarch exercises Crown diplomacy, defense command, appointments, constitutional assent, and narrow temporary emergency writs. Permanent taxation, broad civil legislation, military obligations, mining law, major public works and sweeping changes to guild rights ordinarily require a political route through local magistrates, regional councils, and the **two equal National Council halves**, the **Crown Bench** and **Civic Bench**. The city's inhabitants are not mere property of the House whose patronage dominates them.

**Rome inherits a throne, not absolute ownership of a nation.** A royal decree can move guards in an emergency but cannot turn a local slate mine into hereditary Crown income, extinguish a merchant debt without consequence, or overturn the Green Covenant's autonomous stewardship. If Rome is a wise king, his strength lies in achieving the coalition that makes obedience lawful and sustainable. If he is imprudent, the same institutions offer his opponents many durable ways to frustrate him.

The **Moon-Crown** traditionally belongs to **House Avariel**. The established *Beneath Dappled Oaks* dramatis personae identifies Princess Maeriel Avariel as primary royal heir of an earlier reigning generation and Prince Caereth Avariel as a royal second son. **Rome's precise pedigree, accession instrument, previous succession order, and relationship to those named royals are unresolved**. A new king and an older royal-heir record are not permission to silently declare Maeriel his daughter or sister. The eventual dynasty chart must establish the transition cleanly; prior characters remain alive or dead only as their original story authority indicates.

**The Queen Mother is Rome's mother**, Daysha's mother-in-law, as established separately in the character canon. Her personal name remains unknown. Her visual appearances belong to the royal shopping chronicle, but any given image is evidence of a fictional *scene*, not proof of its historical month.

### 3. The seven Great Houses and the rule of four

The seven established houses keep their identities and power:

| House | Role and resources | Reason to resist or help Rome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Avariel** | Moon-Crown, Aurelith, royal appointments, household and garrisons | Claim protection, rival succession loyalties, the price of ceremonial legitimacy |
| **Vael** | Vaelwood, living groves, orchards, memory-magic and Green Covenant influence | Protect grove law and old vows; negotiate timber, seeds and agricultural charters |
| **Eris** | Westgate, passes, fortresses, caravans, mines and cavalry | Demand frontier defense budgets and control of ore supply lines |
| **Sylvaren** | Silvercourt, diplomacy, fashion, salons, artists and court reputation | Determine what the court believes about the king, his queen, and their companions |
| **Caladren** | Caladris, waterways, bridges, grain, banks, insurance and credit | Finance or freeze the sovereign's logistics depending on contracts |
| **Nymrael** | Starfall, observatories, medicine, archives and high magic | Verify dangerous magic and enforce evidentiary limits on prophecy and accusation |
| **Orthell** | Hartspire, scouts, border roads, forests and wildlands | Independent swing power for patrols and fragile coalition majorities |

The **rule of four** stands: any credible stable coalition of four Great Houses frightens the other three into counter-coalition. Political dependence is also organized outside aristocratic heraldry. Royal power depends on the Civic Bench, independent magistrates, temple votes, guild supply, and municipal credit. Therefore four Great Houses can dominate noble strategy but still fail to pass a bridge levy.

Opening bloc alignments inherited from the earlier BDO framework are **Avariel + Sylvaren**, **Eris + Caladren**, **Vael + Nymrael**, with **Orthell** the pivot. These are initial tendencies and relationships, not automatically fixed voting instructions for the day of Rome's accession. Princess Maeriel, Prince Caereth, Lady Aelirien Vael and Lord Thalen Eris have their own prior histories and loyalties. No new regnal story should erase their agency.

**House Valeric** is a politically consequential lesser house tied to the Dmitrigical Arts, tribunal offices and the **Silver Night** atrocity. It is **not an eighth Great House**. The established crime remains: 36 students and 6 servants died in the deliberately induced west-tower disaster at the Concord Academy of Aurelith. Ilyan Valeric knows his father and uncle engineered it; their personal names remain unresolved. The Last Seven live with the consequences. Do not fabricate a royal connection to the massacre simply because the crime falls near Rome's accession; involvement requires source evidence and proper causation.

### 4. Towns, routes, taxes and the movement of real goods

**Aurelith** is the roughly 64,000-person capital and center of the National Council, royal palace, universities, diplomatic quarter, luxury trade and the contest for access. **Aurelith** must not be renamed because an Embryon art concept calls a hub *Aurelian Court*. The latter is a visual/game-location proposal until mapped into the established city or given independent location.

- **Vaelwood** is the green legal and botanical heartland; **Aesryn Green** guards seed law and living memory.
- **Westgate**, **Veyhold** and **Daeron Bastion** control the frontier corridor, mining, garrisons and siege preparation.
- **Caladris**, **Vossbank** and **Sereth Quays** control grain, loans, river freight, barge construction and financial risk.
- **Silvercourt**, **Starfall**, and **Hartspire** keep diplomacy, magic, scouts and independent local constituencies politically real.
- **Three Bridges** is a repeated toll dispute in search of a compromise; **Embercross** places ore refiners and alchemists in competition; **Greenwater** confronts agricultural producers and shippers.
- **Aurelith Academy Ward** shelters the autonomous Concord Academy; **Sunderhall** hosts national political machinery; **Valeron** and **Grey Annex** matter to Valeric institutional influence.

Power has moving physical costs: boots, grain, timbers, river depth, horses, safe crossings, mine ventilation, smelters, winter storage and timely pay. Neither the jewel game nor the court novel may conveniently conjure a fully provisioned army without explaining its supply.

### 5. Guilds, artisans and money

Preserve established institutions: **Crown Factors of Ilyr**, **Golden Table Guild**, **Argent Mantle Atelier**, **Guild of Golden Tables**, **Hundred Hands**, **Deepdelve Charter**, **Iron Lantern Consortium**, **Verdant Lantern Adventurers' Guild**, **Laurel & Lantern Company**, **Red Hart Rangers**, **River Ledger**, **Moonstage Opera**, **Order of the Gilded Mirror**, **Roseglass Apothecaries**, **Green Covenant** and independent magical tribunals.

**The Gilded Spindle** in the Nobles' District, with **Master Garrick Vellmont**, is specifically established in the Daysha wardrobe chronicle. It can coexist with the older **Argent Mantle Atelier** (a favored Crown atelier), the **Hundred Hands** artisan compact, **Crown Factors of Ilyr** (purchasing officers), **Deepdelve** mining suppliers and jewelry brokers. They have overlapping interests, **not identical legal identities**.

A sapphire worth dozens of gold pieces to a dressmaker may be collateral for a caravan bank, laboratory feedstock for an enchanter, a political gift to a river councilor or the result of an expedition fought over by three competing charter holders. No gemstone is simultaneously four different purchases. Record the material's **origin**, **chain of custody**, **cut/quality**, **duty**, **commission**, **cash paid**, **trade or credit agreement**, and **final owner** separately. A personal gift by Rome and an invoice to the Crown treasury are legally different. The existing working clothing prices are *models*, not magically verified receipts.

### 6. Heroes of Embryon in the same physical world

**Queen Daysha is the established playable heroine of Jewel Brook: Heroes of Embryon.** She may undertake hazardous crystal expeditions, magical interventions and noble diplomacy in the same world she inhabits as queen. Her approved art identity is governed by the Jewel Brook project; it does not replace her historical personality with a generic game mascot.

The game's crystal mining loop, equipment, and gemstone economy are a **playable abstraction of Elaranth's mines, excavation charters, refining, crystal trading and elemental magic**. A match-three board or a thousand numbered levels is **not** evidence that the Queen personally fought a thousand canonical battles. Currency balances, boosters, free daily allotments, saved-game ranks and procedural rock output are **game-state**, not a literal audited quantity of royal treasure.

The art guide proposes **Aurelian Court**, **Crystal Hollow**, **Lumenwood**, **Shardsong Heights**, **Prismfall Observatory**, and **Emberglass Depths**. These are **provisional game-map/location names**, not automatically accepted as mapped towns on the BDO gazetteer or as dated expeditions. Future reconciliation may place them as districts, sites, caves or wilderness within Elaranth without displacing Aurelith, Veyhold, Starfall, or Embercross.

**Embryon crystal shards** may underpin an important struggle for charters and energy, but precise metaphysical properties, monopoly claims, named expedition achievements and crisis dates remain development proposals until the stories establish them. Player actions are not retroactive canon without narrative promotion.

### 7. Conflict engines with causality

The following are **long-term story engines / proposed future arcs**, not false claims that their scenes have already occurred. Each one is designed to join personal narrative to military and civic consequence.

**The Miners' Charter Crisis.** An Eris mining branch seeks a monopoly on new crystal-bearing seams; Caladren creditors want guaranteed throughput; Nymrael examiners question unsafe enchantment extraction; local Embercross magistrates demand water protection. A royal decision favoring one bidder shifts jobs and credit. Adventure companies can gather evidence or recover samples; the result changes practical voting power.

**The River Winter.** Flood repairs before the freeze cost more than Caladren banks will lend unsecured. A bridge fails, food moves by bad routes, Orthell scouts guard caravans, and the Civic Bench bargains for inspection rights. A king who saves a town at the wrong legal price may trigger a constitutional inquiry.

**The Thorn Choir's Petition.** The forbidden-grove traditionalists seek proof the Moon-Crown broke an ancient oath. The Green Covenant may cooperate on true testimony without endorsing the Choir's extremism. Nymrael cannot certify prophecy on hearsay. A political enemy of Rome may sincerely favor lawful investigation while rejecting violent zealotry.

**The Valeric Evidence War.** The Last Seven confront a cover-up in the Dmitrigical licensing system. The Crown's duty is to preserve institutional integrity, protect witnesses, and avoid a prejudicial royal verdict. A romance, a family debt, or a noble friendship might affect access to records; none proves guilt without evidence.

**The Westgate Muster.** An external interloper tests border defenses while competing nobles argue who is authorized to command the pass. Eris fortress officers and Crown forces can win the battle yet lose the political settlement if tariffs, reparations, or troop rights are mishandled.

**The Queen's Crystal Commission.** A gem recovered from an otherwise routine mine becomes central to both a civic hazard inquiry and a royal clothing commission. Garrick must choose provenance over profit; Daysha cannot simply wear away a disputed title. When the stone changes hands, its purchase and subsequent appearances remain one material chain.

**The Velvet Court.** Sylvaren patronage, the Moonstage Opera, the House of Seven Veils, secret relationships and aristocratic scandal produce distorted public testimony. The romantic novel sees desire first; the historian seeks corroboration. Private tenderness can be genuine even when someone exploiting the rumor is ruthless.

**The Succession Petition.** A claimant requests disclosure of old Avariel registers, and Rome's right to rule must withstand scrutiny under the Compact. This remains a *future proposed storyline* until his real genealogy and the status of Maeriel and Caereth are reconciled. Do not solve it by killing or disinheriting established characters offstage.

### 8. Distinguishing the romantic chronicle from the nation's history

*Beneath Dappled Oaks* is an intentionally sensual, sometimes untrustworthy social window. Its salon correspondents are more likely to report who entered a private gallery than how many cartloads of grain passed the capital gate. Their stories are useful **testimony**, not independent verification of a voting bloc, a marriage contract, or an assassination.

Use three layers for each important claim: **what happened** (reliable sources and event records), **what each character actually knows** (memory and viewpoint), and **what fashionable society believes** (rumor, satire and propaganda). Sex, jealousy and intimacy complicate the political situation, but civic life, wars, poor harvests, inheritance law, artisan labor, and military intelligence also proceed when nobody attractive is in the room.

The Last Seven's Book One/Silver Night chronology, the Queen's boutique chronology, and the Jewel Brook game are parallel views of one country at different levels of detail. A dating collision must be flagged for reconciliation rather than fixed by deleting an inconvenient person or shifting an atrocity.

### 9. Canon records and unresolved questions

**Date ledger:**
- Year 0 S.M.: the Delphimi settlement and foundation of the Sunder Compact — established background.
- 282 S.M.: established broad era of *Beneath Dappled Oaks*.
- First regnal year of Rome: authoritatively the opening year of that story.
- Midpoint of that year: owner-established opening *relative date*.
- 14 Deepdelve, 282 S.M.: **working exact-date normalization**, pending chapter-level verification.
- 1 Thawcall, 282 S.M.: **proposed coronation alignment**, not separately witnessed as in-world fact.
- Silver Night: **before** the Last Seven's opening, exact day still undetermined; 36 students and 6 servants die.
- Estelle's birth: **during Rome's first regnal year**, AFTER the novel opens, with projected birthday **15 Casktide, 282 S.M.**
- Estelle's daycare/jewel chase/council glitter/catroom: **after her first-year birth**, exact childhood years unknown.
- Estelle's 16th birthday: **16 × 364 days after the projected birth, on 15 Casktide, 298 S.M.**, during Rome's SEVENTEENTH regnal year.

**Unresolved locks:**
1. Rome's exact Avariel relation, coronation ritual, and valid legal accession instrument.
2. Reconciliation of existing Maeriel/Caereth descriptions with the Rome era; no invented death.
3. Exact BDO Chapter One day and its existing seasonal cues; do not rewrite thirty committed chapters by assumption.
4. Silver Night's calendar month and its causal relationship, if any, to changes in the Crown.
5. Exact dates and stages of Daysha's first queenly commissions and her gift of Lexi.
6. Final confirmation of the projected 15 Casktide 282 birth date; her birth occurs within the first regnal year, with her sixteenth birthday falling in regnal year seventeen.
7. Aurelian Court and Embryon game's proposed map-site geographies and whether a mission is actual story canon.
8. Names, governments and wartime incidents beyond established borders.
9. Identity of the Queen Mother beyond her known relationship to Rome.
