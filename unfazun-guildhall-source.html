# Unfazun, the Guildhall of a Thousand Roads

## The Central Guildhall of the Seventh Layer

Unfazun was never merely a mining settlement. It was the beating heart of the Seventh Layer, the Great Ice Caverns, and the central point through which nearly every road descending into the oldest dwarven depths once passed.

Above it rose the Asphelt Lightwell, a monumental shaft containing a magical stair that spiraled through miles of stone. Below it lay the ancestral worship halls of the Eighth Layer, the royal sepulchers of the Ninth, and the primordial magmatic forges created in the earliest days of dwarven civilization. Those forges were vast enough to smelt mountains of ore and shape the wealth upon which generations of dwarven power were built.

Unfazun stood at the point where all of these worlds met.

It was a city hall, mining guild headquarters, transportation authority, labor exchange, warehouse district, courthouse, treasury, rail terminus, and gateway into the sacred depths. Thousands of tunnels converged beneath it. Ore from distant excavations arrived here to be weighed, taxed, graded, recorded, divided among the clans, and sent upward. Workers descended through its halls before scattering into mines that might lie days or weeks away.

The guildhall was therefore not constructed as a single building.

It was a capstone laid over an older network of natural caverns, mining cuts, ventilation shafts, rail corridors, cisterns, crypts, and excavated chambers. Generations expanded it without any single unified plan. New halls were built around older ones. Offices swallowed abandoned tunnels. Warehouses became shrines. Shrines became archives. Archives were breached by rail lines. Maintenance passages connected rooms that had no official relationship to one another.

By the end of Unfazun’s life, even its inhabitants could become lost inside it.

During the dwarven civil war, this labyrinth became the decisive battleground. Whoever held the guildhall controlled the roads leading to the royal tombs. If the necromancers reached the kings of old and compelled them to rise, no living army could have resisted the ancestral dead.

The loyalists therefore shattered the Asphelt Lightwell, sealed the lower roads, and destroyed much of the city around the guildhall. Moraden withdrew his providence from the necromantic clergy, leaving their miracles broken and their servants only partially bound. Ancient protective enchantments failed. The supernatural cold of the Great Ice Caverns crept inward.

Unfazun froze while its defenders were still fighting.

The dead remain at their stations.

The rails still lead into the dark.

And somewhere beneath the guildhall, the final order to awaken the kings was written but never delivered.

# Running the Guildhall

## The Supernatural Cold

The cold within Unfazun is not ordinary weather. It is the natural condition of the Seventh Layer, held back in ancient times by vast civic enchantments.

Without protection, exposed breath turns to glittering frost. Metal adheres to bare skin. Sounds become strangely brittle, as though voices themselves might shatter. Prolonged exposure causes memories to become indistinct. A person may remember that they had a family but forget their faces, or recall that they were afraid without remembering why.

Four surviving warming stations can temporarily restore sections of the guildhall:

- The Chapel of the Banked Ember
- The Guild Bathhouse
- The Ventilation Crown
- The Civic Ward Engine

Restoring one warming station makes its surrounding district tolerable. Restoring all four causes the entire guildhall to awaken. Lights ignite. Bells ring. Sealed doors open. Rail mechanisms begin moving. The dead resume their final assigned shifts.

## The Last Shift

At irregular intervals, translucent dwarven figures appear throughout the guildhall. These are not always conscious ghosts. Many are fragments of the final day, repeating their movements without understanding that centuries have passed.

A clerk may continue stamping nonexistent documents.

A miner may ask where to report for duty.

A dying guard may drag himself toward an alarm bell and vanish before reaching it.

The deeper the party travels, the more these fragments begin to notice them.

## The Three Authorities

Three ancient systems still compete for control of the guildhall.

The **Guild Authority** recognizes badges, labor tokens, stamped orders, and the commands of properly appointed officers.

The **Civic Authority** recognizes elected delegates, judges, treasury seals, and emergency government decrees.

The **Ancestral Authority** recognizes bloodlines, funerary titles, royal commandments, and the servants of Moraden.

Much of the labyrinth can be navigated safely by acquiring symbols of these authorities. Other doors open only when two authorities agree. The deepest chambers were sealed through the combined authority of all three.

## The Dead of Unfazun

The undead here are not one unified force.

Some are laborers compelled to continue working.

Some are volunteers who swore to defend the city after death.

Some are condemned criminals whose sentences were extended beyond the grave.

Some are necromancers who refused Moraden’s judgment.

Some are ancient heroes who willingly marched against the dragons and later discovered that they would never be permitted to rest again.

The tragedy of Unfazun is that many of its dead are neither wholly innocent nor wholly monstrous.

# District I: The Broken Crown

## 1. The Fallen Lightwell Landing

**Connections:** The Frozen Processional, the Broken Liftwell, the ruins of greater Unfazun.

The shattered remains of the Asphelt Lightwell hang above this enormous circular chamber. Portions of the magical stair remain suspended in the shaft, ending abruptly in open air. Frozen bodies lie across the landing where citizens were crushed by descending masonry or killed while attempting to flee upward.

At the center stands a bronze directory displaying the original districts of Unfazun. Most names have been violently chiseled away, but the Central Guildhall, Arcane University, Clan Embassies, and Roads of the Deep remain legible.

A damaged magical announcement repeats every few minutes:

> “The Lightwell is closed by emergency decree. Citizens are ordered to remain calm. All able workers must report to their guild stations. The kings remain asleep.”

Behind the directory is a narrow maintenance stair descending directly to Room 7.

---

## 2. The Frozen Processional

**Connections:** Fallen Lightwell Landing, Hall of the Last Shift, Warming Chapel.

This broad ceremonial avenue once allowed thousands of miners to enter the guildhall during a shift change. Statues depict dwarves bearing ore, tools, food, lamps, ledgers, and wounded companions. The procession represents the belief that no labor exists alone.

The avenue is now filled with waist-high drifts of unnatural blue-white snow. Shapes move beneath it. These are frozen guild banners, discarded equipment, and several dead miners who crawl toward any source of warmth.

Inscribed over the entrance is the guildhall’s ancient declaration:

> “What one hand brings from darkness, ten thousand hands carry into light.”

---

## 3. The Hall of the Last Shift

**Connections:** Frozen Processional, Brass-Breath Gatehouse, Petitioners’ Gallery, Watch Barracks.

Thousands of stone plaques cover the walls. Each bears the name of a worker who died while registered for duty. The plaques were originally memorials, but during the civil war necromancers used them as a directory of available bodies.

Many plaques have been turned around or shattered by grieving relatives.

When living creatures enter, spectral miners gradually fill the chamber. They line up before an invisible clerk, receive assignments, and walk through walls toward distant work sites. One ghost eventually notices the intruders and asks:

> “Has the morning bell sounded, or are we still upon the final shift?”

A surviving registry plaque naming a character’s dwarven ancestor, clan, or known historical figure may be placed here.

---

## 4. The Brass-Breath Gatehouse

**Connections:** Hall of the Last Shift, Civic Rotunda, Security Inspection Room.

Two enormous brass doors stand beneath the face of a stern dwarven guildmaster. In warmer days, enchanted vents exhaled heated air over everyone entering the central complex.

The mechanism still functions intermittently. It now alternates between blasts of searing heat and killing cold.

A speaking tube demands each entrant provide a name, guild affiliation, assigned shift, and purpose. Plausible answers cause the doors to open. Contradictory answers activate the gatehouse guardians: heavy bronze mining automata equipped with gripping claws designed to restrain unruly workers without damaging valuable tools.

A Guild Authority badge bypasses the questioning entirely.

---

## 5. The Security Inspection Room

**Connections:** Brass-Breath Gatehouse, Watch Barracks, Confiscated Property Vault.

Long stone tables divide this chamber into narrow lanes. Workers were searched for stolen ore, forbidden weapons, unregistered relics, and unauthorized magical materials.

The final inspectors found necromantic components hidden among shipments from the lower mines. Several crates remain sealed beneath inspection wards. They contain grave salts, embalming resin, copper funerary wire, powdered gemstones, and clay tablets bearing royal names.

One inspector still sits upright behind the central desk, frozen solid with a warning bell clutched in both hands.

---

## 6. The Civic Rotunda

**Connections:** Brass-Breath Gatehouse, Grand Ledger Hall, Council Stair, Labor Registry, Broken Liftwell.

The Civic Rotunda is the primary crossroads of the upper guildhall. Eight monumental arches open from a circular chamber whose domed ceiling depicts the entire dwarven world as a layered mountain.

At the center stands a model of Unfazun during its height. Tiny lights mark rail lines, mines, clan holdings, and warming stations. Most are dark, though several lights far beneath the guildhall continue to pulse.

Every sound spoken here returns as a whisper from a different archway. The whispers sometimes answer questions, but they speak using voices recorded during the fall of the city.

A bronze map plate can rotate the model, revealing a concealed stair to Room 22.

---

## 7. The Broken Liftwell

**Connections:** Fallen Lightwell Landing, Civic Rotunda, Grand Switchyard, Lift Counterweight Galleries.

The guildhall’s primary industrial lift once carried workers and freight between the civic halls and the rail levels below. Its platform lies shattered at the bottom of a shaft several hundred feet deep.

Massive chains still sway despite the absence of wind.

A narrow spiral maintenance stair follows the shaft wall. Several landings have collapsed, requiring travelers to cross hanging chains or enter side passages through the counterweight galleries.

At intervals, something below pulls sharply upon the chains.

---

## 8. The Watch Barracks

**Connections:** Hall of the Last Shift, Security Inspection Room, Alarm Gallery.

The guild watch slept here in narrow stone bunks arranged around a central stove. The stove contains the petrified remains of the barracks’ last fire.

The walls display competing orders from the civil war. One commands the watch to arrest all practitioners of necromancy. Another orders them to defend registered guild members from religious persecution. A third declares the first two orders illegitimate.

The captain’s corpse remains behind a barricade with three different badges pinned to his uniform. Each badge represents a different authority. His journal reveals that he spent the final hours attempting to determine which government still legally existed.

---

## 9. The Chapel of the Banked Ember

**Connections:** Frozen Processional, Petitioners’ Gallery, Heat Conduit Passage.

This small public chapel predates the civil war. It was dedicated to the sacred pause between periods of labor: the meal, the evening fire, the return home, and finally the rest that follows death.

A black iron brazier stands before a relief of Moraden laying down his hammer at the end of creation.

The brazier can be reignited with ordinary flame, but it burns properly only when someone places a tool they have personally used into the fire. The tool is not destroyed. It emerges repaired, polished, and warm.

Restoring the brazier reactivates heat in the Broken Crown district. It also causes several undead laborers to remember that their shift should have ended centuries ago.

Some collapse peacefully.

Others become furious.

---

## 10. The Confiscated Property Vault

**Connections:** Security Inspection Room, Dead Letter Office.

Rows of cages contain seized weapons, unlicensed ore, smuggled luxuries, counterfeit guild seals, and personal effects taken from arrested citizens.

One cage holds dozens of children’s toys confiscated from families attempting to flee through restricted tunnels. Another contains funerary objects taken from necromancers: ancestor masks, bone chisels, royal grave keys, and small clay figures representing the promised halls of eternity.

A hidden receipt book records which clan officials quietly retrieved confiscated necromantic materials before the war.

---

# District II: The Civic Halls

## 11. The Petitioners’ Gallery

**Connections:** Hall of the Last Shift, Civic Rotunda, Warming Chapel, Hall of Arbitration.

This long gallery once held miners, merchants, clan representatives, widows, and injured workers waiting to present grievances before civic officials.

Stone benches are divided by carved labels: wages, injury, inheritance, tunnel rights, ore claims, religious matters, and death-service disputes.

The death-service benches are stained black.

Several translucent petitioners remain seated. They become agitated if ignored but respond calmly to anyone who formally asks for their grievance. Their complaints reveal much about the last days of Unfazun: missing bodies, altered wills, unpaid resurrection contracts, and dead relatives seen working after funerals.

---

## 12. The Grand Ledger Hall

**Connections:** Civic Rotunda, Hall of Measures, Speaker’s Archive, Labor Registry.

The Grand Ledger Hall rises through three stories of shelves, balconies, suspended walkways, and rotating stone cabinets. Every ounce of ore passing through Unfazun was supposed to be recorded here.

The ledgers reveal the city’s slow corruption. Mining output remained stable, but the number of registered workers increased long after population growth had stopped. Deceased miners continued receiving wages. Entire shifts consisted of workers officially listed as dead.

A mechanical clerk moves along ceiling rails and demands overdue reports. It can guide visitors through the archive but considers all living people several centuries late in filing their documentation.

---

## 13. The Hall of Measures

**Connections:** Grand Ledger Hall, Assay Offices, Treasury Antechamber.

A forest of balances, marked rods, ore standards, calibrated vessels, and enchanted scales fills this chamber. The dwarves of Unfazun considered reliable measurement a sacred defense against corruption.

One enormous balance dominates the center. It can determine the exact weight of nearly anything placed upon it, including invisible creatures or spirits.

During the civil war it was altered to measure the weight of a soul’s attachment to its body. The final readings remain scratched into the floor. Some of the supposedly willing dead resisted resurrection with such force that the scale cracked.

---

## 14. The Council Stair

**Connections:** Civic Rotunda, Chamber of Clan Delegates, Council of Hammers, Emergency Council Bunker.

A pair of sweeping staircases circle a central statue of Unfazun’s founder. One stair was reserved for civic delegates; the other for guild officers. The two meet only before the doors of the council chamber.

During the civil war, each faction barricaded its own stair and accused the other of treason.

Hidden inside the founder’s statue is a vertical escape shaft leading to the Emergency Council Bunker. The shaft can be opened only by placing a civic seal and guild seal into separate recesses at the same time.

---

## 15. The Chamber of Clan Delegates

**Connections:** Council Stair, Hall of Arbitration, Banquet Gallery.

Stone desks form a descending semicircle around a speaking platform. Every recognized clan held a seat here, though the richest clans carved their desks larger than the others.

The chamber preserves the final debate over necromancy. Illusory fragments repeat whenever someone stands upon the speaking platform. Some delegates argue that raising the dead saved the dwarven people from extinction. Others insist that survival purchased through eternal slavery is merely a slower form of extinction.

The final speaker warns that the necromancers have already begun preparing the royal dead.

The illusion ends when an axe strikes him from behind.

---

## 16. The Council of Hammers

**Connections:** Council Stair, Guildmaster’s Office, Civic Seal Vault.

This was the executive chamber of Unfazun. Nine stone chairs surround a circular table carved with a map of the lower layers.

The table can display active tunnels, sealed roads, heat systems, and rail movement. Several routes beneath the guildhall remain active, though no living miners have used them for centuries.

One chair contains the body of the last High Guildmaster, killed by a ceremonial hammer driven through his chest. Beneath his hand lies an unsigned decree transferring all transportation authority to the necromantic defense council.

Whether he intended to sign it remains unknown.

---

## 17. The Hall of Arbitration

**Connections:** Petitioners’ Gallery, Chamber of Clan Delegates, Judges’ Retiring Room, Punitive Descent.

This circular courtroom was designed for disputes too complex for ordinary guild administration. Its floor is divided into geometric speaking zones. Enchantments once prevented anyone standing within them from knowingly lying.

The enchantment still functions, but the supernatural cold has distorted it. A speaker who lies becomes frozen to the floor. A speaker who tells a painful truth causes the ice around them to melt.

The final trial concerned a necromancer accused of raising workers who had explicitly refused posthumous service. The accused remains imprisoned beneath the judge’s platform, alive in a state of magical suspension.

---

## 18. The Speaker’s Archive

**Connections:** Grand Ledger Hall, Dead Letter Office, Hidden Censor’s Passage.

This archive contains official speeches, public announcements, emergency declarations, and approved histories of Unfazun.

Many records have been revised repeatedly. Early accounts praise the dead who fought the dragons. Later accounts describe necromancy as a regrettable necessity. The final versions claim that dwarves had never practiced necromancy at all.

Behind one shelf is a censor’s passage filled with the original documents removed from public circulation.

Among them is a letter from the high priests of Moraden warning that divine power would be withdrawn unless the raising of unwilling dead ceased immediately.

The letter arrived eleven years before the civil war.

---

## 19. The Treasury Antechamber

**Connections:** Hall of Measures, Treasury of Common Weight, Wages Vault.

Stone counters divide the room into channels where taxes, clan dues, transit fees, and guild levies were processed.

Hundreds of citizens died here during a final attempt to withdraw their savings. Their bodies were removed, but their shadows remain burned into the walls by a magical discharge.

A clerk’s station contains emergency promissory tablets guaranteeing payment after the crisis ended. The tablets remain legally valid under ancient dwarven law, representing an enormous claim upon any surviving successor state.

---

## 20. The Treasury of Common Weight

**Connections:** Treasury Antechamber, Civic Seal Vault, Sub-Treasury Descent.

This treasury did not store individual clan wealth. It held the shared reserves used to maintain roads, warming enchantments, rescue crews, and food shipments.

Most precious metals were removed during the war. What remains are less portable treasures: ingots of rare industrial alloys, enchanted rail components, civic gemstones, and hundreds of sealed barrels of perfectly preserved lamp oil.

A false wall conceals evidence that funds intended to maintain the warming enchantments were diverted into necromantic research.

The theft began decades before open conflict.

---

## 21. The Dead Letter Office

**Connections:** Confiscated Property Vault, Speaker’s Archive, Treasury Antechamber, Mortuary Dispatch Tunnel.

Messages undelivered during the city’s final days fill thousands of narrow pigeonholes.

Some are mundane: requests for replacement tools, notices of delayed shipments, complaints about food quality.

Others are final farewells written by soldiers, miners, priests, and necromancers. Many were never sent because the guild closed the transport network before the city fell.

One bundle contains letters from dead workers begging relatives to destroy their bodies before the necromancers could reclaim them.

A hidden dispatch tunnel descends to the mortuary levels.

---

## 22. The Emergency Council Bunker

**Connections:** Hidden stair from Civic Rotunda, Council Stair escape shaft, Alarm Gallery, Sealed Command Descent.

The emergency government of Unfazun spent its final hours here. Maps cover the walls, marked with the movements of loyalist forces, necromantic militias, and civilian refugees.

The central table holds a sequence of increasingly desperate plans.

The first proposed arresting the necromantic leadership.

The second proposed sealing the royal tombs.

The third proposed collapsing the entire Seventh Layer.

The final plan ordered the destruction of the Asphelt Lightwell and the permanent isolation of every settlement below it.

A survivor’s message is carved into the underside of the table:

> “We did not seal them beneath us. We sealed ourselves above them.”

---

# District III: The Halls of Labor

## 23. The Labor Registry

**Connections:** Civic Rotunda, Grand Ledger Hall, Hiring Hall, Shift Assignment Gallery.

This immense chamber contains registration desks for hundreds of professions. Miners formed only part of Unfazun’s workforce. Carters, lamp keepers, cooks, healers, surveyors, engineers, scribes, animal handlers, rail crews, and funerary workers all passed through here.

Tall stone cylinders contain employment records that rotate when a name is spoken.

Searching the records of the dead reveals two classifications:

**Released**, meaning the worker had been permitted to enter the afterlife.

**Retained**, meaning their labor obligation continued after death.

Near the end, almost every deceased worker was classified as retained.

---

## 24. The Hiring Hall

**Connections:** Labor Registry, Tool Issue Hall, Guild Canteen.

Colored mosaics on the floor once directed workers toward employers. Clan companies competed beneath banners advertising wages, housing, meals, injury compensation, and posthumous benefits.

Several banners promise:

> “No service beyond death without written consent.”

The companies displaying those banners were among the first businesses seized during the civil war.

A spectral recruiter continues offering contracts. The terms appear generous until the final clause, which grants the guild authority over the worker’s remains during any declared existential emergency.

The emergency was never formally ended.

---

## 25. The Shift Assignment Gallery

**Connections:** Labor Registry, Bellmaster’s Chamber, Miners’ Descent, Dormitory Ring.

Thousands of small bronze plates once displayed each worker’s daily assignment. Many continue sliding along mechanical tracks, rearranging themselves according to commands no one remembers issuing.

Living visitors may find their names appearing on the boards.

Assignments include legitimate work sites, collapsed mines, sealed royal roads, and places that do not appear on any surviving map.

One assignment repeatedly appears in red:

> “Royal Recovery Detail. Ninth Layer. Report without tools.”

---

## 26. The Bellmaster’s Chamber

**Connections:** Shift Assignment Gallery, Alarm Gallery, Ventilation Passages.

A complex mechanism of chains, gears, hammers, and speaking tubes controlled the bells of the guildhall.

Different sequences signaled shift changes, cave-ins, fire, invasion, religious assembly, rail accidents, or evacuation.

The final bell sequence was never completed. The dead throughout Unfazun remain caught between the command to report for emergency duty and the command releasing them from service.

Completing the sequence may free many of them.

Sounding the wrong sequence may summon every retained worker still capable of movement.

---

## 27. The Tool Issue Hall

**Connections:** Hiring Hall, Lamp House, Toolwright Shops, Confiscated Tool Cage.

Endless racks once held picks, hammers, drills, wedges, saws, shovels, ropes, climbing gear, and emergency breathing equipment.

Most common tools are gone. What remains are specialized instruments too heavy, dangerous, or obscure to steal during the evacuation.

A wall inscription reads:

> “A worker shall not be judged by the tool they inherit, but by the care with which they return it.”

Several undead miners wander the hall searching for tools that no longer exist. Returning the correct tool to one of them may restore enough memory for the dead worker to guide the party through the mines.

---

## 28. The Lamp House

**Connections:** Tool Issue Hall, Powder Passage, Miners’ Descent.

Oil lamps, alchemical lights, crystal lanterns, and enchanted helmet stones were issued here.

The room is completely dark when entered. Any light brought inside is slowly diminished as the Lamp House absorbs it.

At the rear stands the First Lamp of Unfazun, an ancient crystal said to have been carried by the city’s founders. It still burns, but its light is trapped behind layers of black necromantic wax.

Removing the wax illuminates hidden writing throughout the guildhall, including marks left by workers attempting to expose the conspiracy.

---

## 29. The Guild Bathhouse

**Connections:** Guild Canteen, Dormitory Ring, Infirmary, Heat Conduit Passage.

Mineral pools and heated washing channels occupy a broad cavern beneath a ceiling painted like a summer sky.

The pools are frozen solid. Bodies can be seen within them, some bathing and others apparently trying to drown themselves before they could be raised.

The central boiler can be restored through the Heat Conduit Passage. Doing so melts the pools and reactivates warmth throughout the labor district.

One pool contains no water. It is filled with hundreds of identity tokens discarded by dead workers who did not wish to be found.

---

## 30. The Guild Canteen

**Connections:** Hiring Hall, Guild Bathhouse, Kitchens, Banquet Gallery.

Long communal tables extend through several connected caverns. Stone serving counters bear carved portions showing how much food each class of worker was entitled to receive.

The guild officers’ section is modestly better than the miners’ section. The necromantic service staff, however, were allocated no food at all.

Meals remain on several tables, frozen at the moment of the attack. One table seats a family who came to retrieve a relative from work. Their bodies are gone, but their untouched bowls remain.

At midnight by the old guild clock, ghostly workers fill the hall for their meal break and whisper about troop movements elsewhere in the city.

---

## 31. The Kitchens of Endless Broth

**Connections:** Guild Canteen, Cold Stores, Servants’ Tunnel.

Enormous cauldrons supplied food to thousands of workers. Dwarven enchantments allowed a carefully maintained broth to be extended indefinitely without spoiling.

One cauldron still bubbles despite the cold.

The broth provides warmth and nourishment, but anyone consuming it dreams of every person who has eaten from the cauldron since its creation. The final memories are of starving citizens fighting over ladles while the guild reserved food for soldiers and essential workers.

A narrow servants’ tunnel connects the kitchens to several distant districts, creating one of the guildhall’s most useful hidden routes.

---

## 32. The Dormitory Ring

**Connections:** Shift Assignment Gallery, Guild Bathhouse, Union Hall, Infirmary.

A circular corridor surrounds dozens of communal sleeping chambers. Personal belongings remain in small wall recesses: carved family figures, letters, spare boots, dice, pipes, religious icons, and unfinished crafts.

The dormitories nearest the civic halls belonged to temporary laborers. Those farther back were assigned to the dead.

The retained workers’ bunks contain no blankets or possessions. Each has only a stone identification plaque and restraints built into the bed frame.

The deepest dormitory connects to a concealed tunnel used by workers attempting to smuggle their dead companions out of the city.

---

## 33. The Hall of Joined Hands

**Connections:** Dormitory Ring, Cave-In Memorial, Disciplinary Cells.

This was the closest thing Unfazun possessed to a miners’ union hall. Clan affiliations were forbidden inside. Workers met here to negotiate collective demands and organize rescue funds.

A vast relief depicts dwarves lifting a fallen stone together. During the civil war, the relief was split down the center.

The hall became a refuge for workers who opposed both the necromancers and the religious purges. Their final declaration remains nailed to the speaker’s stand:

> “The dead are not property. The living are not fuel. No god, king, clan, or guild shall own the hand after the hand has closed.”

The authors were executed by both sides.

---

## 34. The Infirmary of Crushed Stone

**Connections:** Dormitory Ring, Guild Bathhouse, Mortuary Lift, Rescue Station Passage.

Rows of surgical tables fill this long chamber. The healers of Unfazun specialized in mining injuries: crushed limbs, stone-lung, burns, poison gas, blindness, and magical contamination.

The infirmary’s divine instruments no longer function. Their holy symbols cracked at the moment Moraden withdrew his power from the necromantic clergy.

A sealed treatment room contains patients who were technically dead but maintained between life and death while healers debated whether restoring them constituted forbidden necromancy.

Several remain suspended in that condition.

---

## 35. The Disciplinary Cells

**Connections:** Hall of Joined Hands, Punitive Descent, Watch Passage.

Workers accused of theft, sabotage, assault, desertion, or unauthorized tunneling were held here pending arbitration.

The cell doors identify prisoners by offense. Near the end, new categories were added:

**Refusal of Posthumous Duty.**

**Concealment of a Corpse.**

**Interference with Ancestral Mobilization.**

One cell contains a dead mother still wrapped around the bones of her adult son. She hid his body for eleven days after a mining accident to prevent the guild from retaining him.

---

## 36. The Mortuary Lift

**Connections:** Infirmary, Dead Letter Dispatch Tunnel, Embalming Galleries.

This narrow freight lift transported bodies from the infirmary to the funerary district.

The platform remains operational, but the controls recognize only three destinations: release, retention, and special assignment.

Selecting release leads to the abandoned funerary road.

Selecting retention leads to the Embalming Galleries.

Selecting special assignment descends far deeper than the guildhall’s official plans indicate.

---

# District IV: The Great Rail Nexus

## 37. The Grand Switchyard

**Connections:** Broken Liftwell, Weighing Hall, Dispatch Tower, Turntable Pit, Eastern and Western Rail Tunnels.

The Grand Switchyard is a cavern large enough to contain a small town. Dozens of rail lines cross, merge, rise, descend, and vanish into black tunnels.

Frozen ore carts stand where they were abandoned. Others continue moving without crews, driven by ancient motive enchantments. Some carry ore. Some carry weapons. Some carry coffins.

Overhead signs once displayed destinations. They now change without warning, occasionally naming mines that never existed or settlements erased from history.

The switchyard is the guildhall’s central navigational challenge. Almost every lower district can be reached from here, but tracks may shift when the bell system activates.

---

## 38. The Weighing Hall

**Connections:** Grand Switchyard, Sample House, Tax Siding, Ore Elevator.

Each incoming cart was weighed before and after unloading. Massive scales are built directly into the rails.

One scale remains occupied by a sealed iron carriage. Its recorded weight is far greater than the carriage could physically contain.

Inside are the compressed remains of hundreds of undead laborers transported as industrial cargo. They are folded together in perfect silence, each branded with a mine assignment.

Opening the carriage breaks the compression enchantment.

---

## 39. The Sample House

**Connections:** Weighing Hall, Assay Furnace, Surveyor’s Archive.

Small portions of every ore shipment were stored here to resolve future disputes over quality and ownership.

Cabinets contain metals, crystals, fossils, magical stone, dragonbone fragments, and materials from depths no surviving dwarf claims to have reached.

One sample is labeled only:

> “Recovered beneath the Ninth. Do not expose to memory.”

Anyone touching it remembers laboring in a vast mine beneath a black sun, though no such place exists in the known world.

---

## 40. The Assay Furnace

**Connections:** Sample House, Hall of Measures service stair, Ventilation Crown.

The furnace tested the composition and magical properties of ore. Its interior remains hot enough to glow faintly red.

During the war it was used to destroy necromantic artifacts. Many could not be melted. Their remnants fused into a single mass that whispers in the voices of their former owners.

The furnace can destroy or purify many cursed objects, but lighting it fully sends smoke into the ventilation system and alerts creatures throughout the guildhall.

---

## 41. The Dispatch Tower

**Connections:** Grand Switchyard, Railmaster’s Office, Signal Bridges, Bellmaster’s speaking tube.

This narrow tower rises from the center of the switchyard. Windows overlook every major track.

Hundreds of levers control rail switches, signals, gates, and loading platforms. Most are unlabeled because trained dispatchers were expected to memorize them.

The final dispatcher remains at the controls, transformed into an intelligent frost-wight. He refuses to abandon his station until every train has reached its destination.

He can route the party anywhere in the guildhall, but first demands that they locate three missing trains.

One carried refugees.

One carried loyalist soldiers.

One carried the bodies of royal ancestors.

---

## 42. The Railmaster’s Office

**Connections:** Dispatch Tower, Signal Bridges, Smuggler’s Line.

The office contains track charts, maintenance schedules, accident reports, and sealed emergency orders.

A map reveals that the official rail network is only part of the truth. Several private clan lines bypassed customs and civic oversight. One secret line travels directly toward the royal sepulchers.

The railmaster attempted to block that route during the civil war. His body is pinned beneath a cabinet, one hand still clutching the master switch key.

A second body nearby wears the vestments of a necromantic priest but bears the railmaster’s family name.

---

## 43. The Turntable Pit

**Connections:** Grand Switchyard, Cartwright Shops, Powder Magazine, Lower Maintenance Maze.

A great rotating platform redirected locomotives and heavy carts. It has frozen halfway between tracks, leaving several rail vehicles stranded.

The pit beneath it is a maze of gears, chains, drainage channels, and maintenance platforms. Cold-adapted scavengers nest among the machinery.

The turntable can be restored by clearing the ice and restarting its steam engine. Doing so opens several direct rail routes but also releases a train trapped behind a sealed tunnel gate.

The train arrives at the platform with every passenger sitting upright and dead.

---

## 44. The Cartwright Shops

**Connections:** Turntable Pit, Toolwright Shops, Salvage Yard.

Work bays contain dismantled ore carts, rail engines, cranes, and tunnel-boring machinery.

An unfinished armored locomotive occupies the central bay. It was designed during the civil war to breach barricades and transport troops through hostile tunnels.

Its motive core remains intact.

Completing it would provide transportation through the guildhall, but its front ram bears necromantic sigils intended to draw strength from any bodies crushed beneath it.

---

## 45. The Powder Magazine

**Connections:** Turntable Pit, Lamp House passage, Demolition Gallery, Collapsed Freight Road.

Heavy stone doors isolate chambers filled with blasting powder, alchemical charges, shaped runes, and mining explosives.

Most remain stable because of the cold.

The magazine was deliberately sabotaged during the final battle, but the fuse froze before reaching the central stores. It remains embedded in the ice, still faintly burning.

Removing or thawing the wrong section could complete a detonation delayed for centuries.

Properly used, the explosives could reopen collapsed sections of the guildhall or permanently bury them.

---

## 46. The Ventilation Crown

**Connections:** Assay Furnace, Lower Maintenance Maze, Heat Conduit Passage, Ice Intake Shafts.

This vast machine regulated air and heat throughout the guildhall. Concentric brass rings surround a vertical shaft filled with slowly turning fan blades.

Ice has grown through the mechanism like the roots of a tree.

Restoring the Ventilation Crown reactivates warmth throughout the rail district and clears toxic air from several lower mines. It also distributes every active scent, sound, and magical trace through the ventilation network.

After restoration, inhabitants of the guildhall know that living intruders have arrived.

---

## 47. The Eastern Dead Line

**Connections:** Grand Switchyard, Arcane University ruins, Frozen City Tunnels.

This rail tunnel once served the Arcane University and eastern districts of Unfazun.

The track disappears beneath clear ice. Within the ice are students, professors, soldiers, and civilians caught while fleeing toward the guildhall.

Their ghosts continue walking beside the submerged rails.

The line offers an alternate entrance into the dungeon from the ruined university. It also provides evidence that university mages attempted to reinforce the city’s warming enchantments but were prevented from entering the guildhall by armed civic guards.

---

## 48. The Ore Elevator

**Connections:** Weighing Hall, Thousand-Mouth Gallery, Sub-Treasury Descent.

A colossal platform lowered entire ore trains into processing levels below. Its chains vanish into shafts both above and beneath the chamber.

The elevator operates through counterweights filled with stone. Several counterweights have been replaced with sealed sarcophagi.

The necromancers discovered that bound spirits could move the elevator indefinitely without fuel. The spirits still pull whenever the controls are touched, screaming through the chains.

A mechanical release can free them, but the elevator will then fall unless another power source is provided.

---

## 49. The Smuggler’s Line

**Connections:** Railmaster’s Office, Treasury sublevels, Unauthorized Excavation.

This narrow private railway was carved without civic approval. Its carts are small, fast, and designed to conceal cargo within false ore compartments.

Clan merchants originally used it to avoid taxes. Necromancers later used it to move bodies, relics, and ritual materials beneath the guildhall.

A concealed station contains crates addressed to prominent loyalist families, proving that several public opponents of necromancy secretly purchased posthumous services.

The line forms a rapid but dangerous route into the deepest chambers.

---

## 50. The Refugee Train

**Connections:** Moving route between Grand Switchyard, Eastern Dead Line, and Frozen Freight Road.

This train does not remain in one location. It travels the guildhall’s rails without a locomotive, appearing whenever the shift bells sound.

Its cars contain hundreds of civilians who died attempting to escape. From outside, they appear frozen and motionless. From within, they believe the train has only just departed.

Passengers can speak normally and know much about the fall of Unfazun. They refuse to accept that they are dead unless shown undeniable proof.

At the rear of the train is a locked mail car containing the final evacuation orders and the severed head of the official assigned to deliver them.

---

# District V: The Thousand Excavations

## 51. The Thousand-Mouth Gallery

**Connections:** Ore Elevator, Surveyor’s Maze, Rescue Station, multiple abandoned mine tunnels.

This enormous natural cavern marks the point where the guildhall becomes indistinguishable from the mines beneath it.

Hundreds of tunnels open from its walls at every height. Some are broad rail passages. Others are barely large enough for a crawling dwarf. Several open in the ceiling or floor.

Each tunnel mouth bears a number, clan mark, hazard classification, and date of last inspection. Many dates are later than the city’s destruction.

Calling into one tunnel produces an answer from another.

---

## 52. The Surveyor’s Maze

**Connections:** Thousand-Mouth Gallery, Sample House archive stair, Mapmakers’ Chamber, Unauthorized Excavation.

Movable walls allowed surveyors to reproduce new tunnel arrangements and train apprentices in underground navigation.

The walls continue reconfiguring themselves according to survey data received from abandoned magical instruments. The maze therefore models mines that have continued collapsing and changing for centuries.

Chalk marks left during the civil war identify a route used to move wounded necromancers toward the deep chambers.

Another set of marks, made by children, leads to a hidden refuge containing small skeletons and a still-functioning warming stone.

---

## 53. The Mapmakers’ Chamber

**Connections:** Surveyor’s Maze, Echo Drill Hall, Ventilation Crawlways.

Stone tables display three-dimensional maps grown from crystalline mineral deposits. Pouring water over a table causes the current tunnel network to rise from its surface.

Most maps show expected mines.

One shows an enormous empty space directly beneath Unfazun, shaped like a dwarven skull.

Another shows the Great Ice Caverns as the interior of something much larger, with the city built between structures resembling ribs.

The mapmakers sealed that table beneath a warning plate and omitted it from every official survey.

---

## 54. The Echo Drill Hall

**Connections:** Mapmakers’ Chamber, Crystal Vein, Deep Animal Stables.

Miners trained here to interpret the sound of stone before cutting into it. Rows of practice walls contain hidden hollows, metal deposits, water pockets, and deliberately unstable sections.

Any hammer strike produces echoes from throughout the guildhall.

A careful listener can distinguish moving trains, digging creatures, running water, and the march of undead formations.

One wall answers every strike with a second strike from the other side.

No passage appears on any map.

---

## 55. The Heart-Ice Vein

**Connections:** Echo Drill Hall, Ice Intake Shafts, Frozen Shrine.

A translucent blue mineral runs through this cavern. It is not ordinary ice but crystallized supernatural cold.

The vein pulses slowly, drawing heat from nearby creatures. Faces can occasionally be seen deep within it, including dwarves who died far from Unfazun.

The ancient warming system did not merely repel the cold. It displaced the cold into this vein. Centuries of accumulated winter have become compressed beneath the city.

Destroying the vein might warm Unfazun permanently.

It might also release the full cold of centuries in a single instant.

---

## 56. The Sump Chasm

**Connections:** Waterworks, Rescue Station, Lower Drain Tunnels, possible exterior cave entrance.

A narrow bridge crosses a seemingly bottomless chasm into which the city’s groundwater once drained.

The water is now frozen into a vertical river extending downward beyond sight.

Bodies, tools, and mine carts are suspended within it. Some were thrown into the sump to prevent their recovery by necromancers.

Far below, a red light moves behind the frozen river. The light comes from the magmatic levels and provides one of the few surviving routes toward the primordial forges.

---

## 57. The Waterworks

**Connections:** Sump Chasm, Guild Bathhouse conduit, Fungus Cisterns.

Pumps, filters, cisterns, and pressure tanks supplied water throughout the guildhall.

The system failed shortly before the final battle. Evidence suggests it was deliberately shut down to deprive the necromancers of water needed for embalming.

A colony of pale cave creatures has nested inside the main cistern. They have consumed the memories preserved in the water and can repeat private conversations spoken anywhere in Unfazun before its fall.

Restarting the pumps restores water to several districts but floods the mortuary tunnels.

---

## 58. The Frozen Fungus Cisterns

**Connections:** Waterworks, Kitchens, Deep Animal Stables.

Terraced caverns once produced mushrooms, lichens, edible molds, and medicinal fungi under enchanted lamps.

The crops are frozen but not dead. Restoring heat causes them to grow with centuries of stored energy.

Within hours, the caverns become an overgrown jungle of enormous fungi.

Some varieties absorb necromantic energy and glow near undead creatures. Others were deliberately cultivated to preserve corpses.

A hidden farming ledger shows that demand for corpse-preserving fungus exceeded food production during the city’s final decade.

---

## 59. The Deep Animal Stables

**Connections:** Fungus Cisterns, Echo Drill Hall, Freight Tunnels.

Broad stalls housed subterranean pack lizards, giant beetles, blind goats, and heavy cave oxen.

Most stalls are empty. Deep claw marks score several doors from the inside.

The stablemaster’s office contains breeding records showing that necromancers attempted to raise dead animals as tireless freight beasts. The experiments produced creatures that retained their instincts but no longer recognized pain, exhaustion, or fear.

One enormous undead cave ox remains chained in the lowest stall. It has spent centuries trying to return to its herd.

---

## 60. The Rescue Station

**Connections:** Thousand-Mouth Gallery, Sump Chasm, Infirmary passage, Cave-In Memorial.

Emergency crews once departed from this chamber carrying supports, breathing masks, drilling equipment, medical supplies, and portable warming stones.

A magical wall records unresolved rescue calls.

Most are centuries old.

One was received only a few days ago.

It comes from a mine officially sealed before the civil war. The caller identifies himself as a member of the Royal Recovery Detail and claims his crew has found “the door beneath the kings.”

---

## 61. The Cave-In Memorial

**Connections:** Rescue Station, Hall of Joined Hands, Collapsed Clan Mine.

Names are carved over every surface of this low, solemn cavern. Unlike the Hall of the Last Shift, these names commemorate workers whose bodies were never recovered.

Necromancers considered these dead especially valuable because no physical remains existed to prove whether they had been raised.

During the civil war, families gathered here to defend the names from ritual use. The walls bear weapon scars and dried blood.

Speaking a carved name aloud causes a voice beneath the stone to answer:

> “Still here.”

---

## 62. The Unauthorized Excavation

**Connections:** Surveyor’s Maze, Smuggler’s Line, Bone Seam, collapsed route toward the Ninth Layer.

This mine was never registered with the guild. Its walls bear no safety markings, clan insignia, or official measurements.

The excavation follows a vein of black stone that absorbs divine magic. Necromancers used it to create chambers hidden from Moraden’s sight.

Discarded equipment suggests the miners eventually uncovered something they had not expected: a worked stone doorway older than the dwarven settlement.

The doorway was buried again from the Unfazun side.

Something on the other side has been slowly clearing the rubble.

---

## 63. The Bone Seam

**Connections:** Unauthorized Excavation, Mortuary Tunnels, Hall of Severed Providence.

A pale band runs through the stone, resembling a mineral deposit until examined closely.

It consists of compacted bones extending far beyond the excavated chamber. They belong to dwarves, dragons, giants, and creatures with no known anatomy.

Necromancers mined the seam as a source of ancestral material. The deeper they cut, the older and more powerful the recovered spirits became.

At the far end, the bones have assembled themselves into the outline of a door.

It opens only when addressed by someone who has died and returned.

---

# District VI: The War Beneath the Guildhall

## 64. The Alarm Gallery

**Connections:** Watch Barracks, Bellmaster’s Chamber, Emergency Council Bunker, Loyalist Barricades.

Dozens of bells line this chamber, each connected to a different district, mine, or rail line.

Most are cracked.

One continues ringing silently.

Touching it fills the mind with the moment Moraden withdrew his power. Clerics collapsed. Raised ancestors screamed. Protective wards failed. The cold entered every unheated chamber at once.

At the end of the gallery, loyalist soldiers built a barricade from furniture, bell frames, and bodies.

Something broke through from the opposite side.

---

## 65. The Hall of Severed Providence

**Connections:** Bone Seam, Loyalist Barricades, Clerics’ Refuge, Procession Hall.

This temple corridor was once maintained by guild priests. Every holy symbol of Moraden has split cleanly down the middle.

Dozens of clerics died here at the exact moment their divine power vanished. Their bodies remain kneeling, hands raised in disbelief.

One survived long enough to carve a message:

> “He did not abandon us. He refused to obey us.”

Necromantic clerics wandering this hall repeatedly attempt prayers that no longer function. Some beg visitors to intercede with Moraden. Others insist that the god must be overthrown for betraying his people.

---

## 66. The Loyalist Barricades

**Connections:** Alarm Gallery, Hall of Severed Providence, Punitive Descent, Moraden’s Spear Chamber.

Multiple defensive lines fill a wide descending corridor. The first was built by guild guards. The second by temple loyalists. The third by civilian volunteers.

Each barricade faces a different direction.

The defenders gradually ceased knowing which side controlled which parts of the guildhall. Bodies wear mixed armor and insignia. Several combatants appear to have changed sides more than once.

A locked ammunition chest contains blessed weapons intended to permanently sever necromantic bindings.

The blessings have faded, but they can be restored at the Frozen Shrine.

---

## 67. The Clerics’ Refuge

**Connections:** Hall of Severed Providence, Frozen Shrine, Hidden Confessional Shaft.

Surviving priests gathered here after losing their powers.

Some interpreted the loss as divine judgment and joined the loyalists. Others believed Moraden had been deceived or imprisoned. A final group concluded that the dwarves had outgrown their creator.

The chamber contains theological arguments carved directly into the walls, often written over one another.

A hidden confession records that senior priests had knowingly permitted “temporary” violations of funerary law after the draconic wars. Each generation promised the practice would end once the emergency passed.

There was always another emergency.

---

## 68. The Procession of the Unresting

**Connections:** Hall of Severed Providence, Embalming Galleries, Bone Marshalling Yard.

Two lines of undead dwarves march endlessly around this circular corridor.

One line carries tools toward the mines.

The other carries weapons toward the royal road.

At the midpoint, scribes assign each corpse to labor or war. The scribes are skeletal constructs incapable of recognizing that the city has fallen.

Travelers entering the procession may be stamped with an assignment. Those accepting the stamp can pass safely among many undead workers. Those refusing are classified as unregistered bodies requiring immediate processing.

---

## 69. The Embalming Galleries

**Connections:** Mortuary Lift, Procession Hall, Consent Archive, Soul-Chain Foundry.

Stone tables stand beneath channels carrying resin, salt, wax, preservatives, and alchemical fluids.

The earliest galleries are solemn and carefully maintained. Bodies were prepared individually, with names and funerary honors preserved.

Later expansions resemble an industrial slaughterhouse.

The transition from venerated ancestral ritual to mass production can be followed physically from room to room.

At the far end, bodies were stripped of identity entirely and numbered like ore shipments.

---

## 70. The Archive of Consent

**Connections:** Embalming Galleries, Hall of Names, Dead Letter Office shaft.

Millions of clay tablets record whether individual dwarves agreed to service after death.

Early tablets contain careful limitations: one battle, one generation, defense of a named settlement, or service only under a living descendant.

Later tablets are vague.

The final records consist of collective contracts declaring entire clans available for resurrection.

Many bear forged marks.

A group of intelligent dead guards the archive. They do not ask the party to destroy it. They ask the party to preserve it so the truth of who consented and who did not can never again be hidden.

---

## 71. The Soul-Chain Foundry

**Connections:** Embalming Galleries, Bone Marshalling Yard, Necromancer Command Crypt, Ventilation service tunnel.

Here the necromancers manufactured the chains binding spirits to bodies, objects, places, and commands.

The chains are not physical iron. They appear as glowing script stretched between anvils.

Each link is a word: duty, blood, debt, oath, fear, love, shame, home.

The strongest chains were made not from cruelty but from virtues twisted beyond their proper limits.

Destroying the foundry releases many bound spirits. It also breaks the restraints upon several powerful dead deliberately imprisoned beneath Unfazun.

---

## 72. The Bone Marshalling Yard

**Connections:** Procession Hall, Soul-Chain Foundry, Royal Recovery Depot.

This immense cavern served as the military assembly point for the dead.

Bones are arranged by size, profession, clan, and historical period. Armor hangs from numbered racks. Weapons stand in bundles awaiting hands.

Thousands of incomplete skeletons turn their heads toward any living creature entering the chamber.

They do not attack unless ordered.

A command podium still holds the final mobilization tablet. It orders every available ancestor to prepare for the defense of dwarven civilization but contains no definition of when that defense should end.

---

## 73. The Frozen Shrine of Moraden

**Connections:** Clerics’ Refuge, Moraden’s Spear Chamber, Heat Conduit Passage.

A single statue of Moraden stands beneath layers of clear ice. He is depicted not at the forge but seated after his work, his hammer laid beside him.

The inscription reads:

> “Labor without rest is bondage. Creation without completion is hunger. Memory without release is a chain.”

Melting the ice does not restore necromantic divine power. Instead, it grants the dead within the chamber the ability to choose whether they remain.

Some leave immediately.

Some choose to stay and finish what they began.

Some discover that they no longer know what they want.

Restoring the shrine reactivates holy wards in the lower guildhall.

---

## 74. The Civic Ward Engine

**Connections:** Moraden’s Spear Chamber, Heat Conduit Passage, Sealed Command Descent.

This engine powered the enchantments that kept the supernatural cold outside Unfazun. It resembles a miniature city constructed around a glowing central crystal.

The engine failed because its power was diverted toward the royal resurrection chambers.

Restoring it requires choosing where its remaining power flows.

Directing power toward warmth makes the guildhall habitable.

Directing power toward the seals strengthens the barriers protecting the royal tombs.

Directing power toward the dead allows thousands of trapped spirits enough clarity to speak.

There is not enough power to accomplish all three without finding a new source in the magmatic forges.

---

## 75. Moraden’s Spear Chamber

**Connections:** Loyalist Barricades, Frozen Shrine, Civic Ward Engine, Royal Recovery Depot.

The loyalists constructed this chamber around a weapon intended to kill the kings if they rose.

The “spear” is a colossal drilling machine aligned with the sealed royal road. Its tip is made from sanctified metal forged during the earliest days of dwarven civilization.

Activating it would drive the weapon through miles of stone and into the outer chambers of the royal sepulchers.

It would also collapse much of the Eighth and Ninth Layers.

The loyalists never fired it.

Their final commander could not decide whether destroying the ancestral kings to prevent their enslavement was an act of mercy or the ultimate desecration.

---

## 76. The Royal Recovery Depot

**Connections:** Bone Marshalling Yard, Moraden’s Spear Chamber, Command Crypt, Royal Road.

Weapons, armor, funerary equipment, transport sarcophagi, and ritual machinery were assembled here for the expedition to the royal tombs.

A massive iron train waits upon the central track. Its armored cars contain elite undead soldiers from the draconic wars.

Unlike most retained dead, these warriors are fully conscious.

They remember volunteering to fight the dragons.

They remember returning in victory.

They remember being denied rest afterward because their continued existence had become politically useful.

Their commander will not permit the train to depart unless convinced that the dwarven people face a genuine threat worthy of their oath.

---

## 77. The Necromancer Command Crypt

**Connections:** Soul-Chain Foundry, Royal Recovery Depot, Hall of Names, Sealed Command Descent.

The leaders of Unfazun’s necromantic faction directed the war from this chamber.

Their bodies sit around a black stone table, preserved in a moment of ritual concentration. Their spirits are trapped inside the table, where they continue arguing.

They did not agree on their final objective.

Some wanted to raise the kings to defeat the loyalists.

Some wanted the kings to rule the living and dead together.

Some intended to free every bound spirit after victory.

One believed Moraden’s promised afterlife was itself a prison and sought to empty it permanently.

The command crypt contains the authority needed to launch the Royal Recovery Train.

It also contains the countermand that would release every retained dead worker in Unfazun.

Both orders require the same seal.

---

## 78. The Hall of Names

**Connections:** Archive of Consent, Command Crypt, Royal Road Seal.

The true names of dwarven kings, heroes, saints, traitors, and ancient monsters are carved upon rotating stone rings.

Necromancers used these names to locate spirits across death.

Many rings have been smashed. Others were deliberately altered so that no summoning could reach the intended dead.

At the center is an empty ring intended for the name of Moraden.

The necromancers never discovered it.

A hidden inscription suggests that the name of a creator cannot be commanded by the creation, but the creation may still refuse the creator’s command.

---

# District VII: The Sealed Road Below

## 79. The Sealed Command Descent

**Connections:** Emergency Council Bunker, Civic Ward Engine, Necromancer Command Crypt, Three-Seal Gate.

Three parallel stairways descend through a single shaft.

The civic stair is lined with laws.

The guild stair is lined with contracts.

The ancestral stair is lined with names.

Each reaches the same gate but presents different visions to those descending. The civic stair shows the collapse of social order. The guild stair shows work abandoned and civilization starving. The ancestral stair shows forgotten dead fading into nothing.

No single vision is entirely false.

The stairways periodically cross, allowing travelers to change paths, but each crossing demands that something be surrendered: authority, property, memory, or blood.

---

## 80. The Three-Seal Gate

**Connections:** Sealed Command Descent, Royal Road.

This gate was created during the final hours of Unfazun. It requires the agreement of civic, guild, and ancestral authority.

The required symbols may be recovered from the upper guildhall, but the gate also judges the bearers’ intentions.

It will not open for anyone seeking only wealth.

It will open for someone seeking to raise the kings, free the kings, destroy the kings, or ask the kings for judgment.

The dwarves who built it understood that no seal could determine which of those purposes was righteous.

---

## 81. The Road of Kneeling Kings

**Connections:** Three-Seal Gate, Royal Recovery Terminus, descent toward the Eighth Layer.

Colossal statues of ancient kings kneel along this descending road. Each faces away from Unfazun and toward the deeper halls, symbolizing that even kings must eventually turn from the living world.

The necromancers reversed several statues so that they face upward.

Those reversed kings whisper promises of protection, vengeance, restored glory, and eternal unity.

The others remain silent.

The road is covered in tracks from thousands of bare dwarven feet. The prints begin in the lower depths and end at the sealed gate, showing that the dead once marched upward from the royal halls during the draconic wars.

Some of the tracks are fresh.

---

## 82. The Royal Recovery Terminus

**Connections:** Road of Kneeling Kings, Royal Recovery Depot rail line, lower royal roads.

This station was built to receive the ancestral armies returning from the deep.

Golden platforms extend into darkness. Funerary banners hang beside military signals. Every surface combines the imagery of a tomb, a temple, and a railway station.

A departure board lists the names of ancient dwarven kings.

Most are marked:

**Resting.**

Several are marked:

**Unreachable.**

One is marked:

**Awake. Awaiting lawful summons.**

No name appears beside the final entry.

Only a crown split into three pieces.

---

# The Central Revelation

The guildhall’s deepest records reveal that the civil war was not caused merely by necromancy being outlawed.

The practice had already divided dwarven society for generations, but compromise remained possible until the necromancers began preparing to raise the kings.

The kings of old possessed authority exceeding that of any living government. Their words could unite clans, dissolve contracts, command ancestral armies, and overturn the decisions of later rulers.

Both sides therefore feared them.

The necromancers claimed they needed the kings to preserve dwarven civilization.

The loyalists claimed the kings had earned their rest.

The civic government feared the kings would abolish it.

The guild feared the kings would expose generations of corruption.

The priesthood feared the kings would reveal that Moraden had once permitted necromancy during the draconic wars.

The clans feared the kings would remember ancient debts and crimes.

The final order awakening the kings was written, sealed, and placed aboard the Royal Recovery Train.

The countermand releasing the retained dead was written by the same hand.

Neither was delivered.

The author was the last High Guildmaster of Unfazun, whose body still sits in the Council of Hammers with a ceremonial weapon through his heart.

Whether he was murdered before choosing, killed after choosing, or killed because he attempted to issue both orders remains unresolved.

---

# Possible Final States of Unfazun

## The Guildhall Is Warmed

Restoring all four warming systems awakens the civic machinery of Unfazun. Lights burn across the city ruins. Rail lines resume operation. Frozen survivors, suspended patients, and dormant undead begin moving again.

Unfazun becomes habitable, but also visible to every creature dwelling in the Great Ice Caverns.

The dead will demand legal recognition, release, compensation, or the completion of their ancient contracts.

## The Dead Are Released

Issuing the countermand ends the Last Shift. Thousands of spirits leave their bodies and pass beyond mortal reach.

The guildhall becomes quieter and safer, but much of its machinery stops functioning because bound spirits powered its systems.

Some dead refuse release, claiming unfinished duties or fear of judgment.

## The Royal Train Departs

Launching the Royal Recovery Train begins an expedition toward the royal sepulchers.

The party may accompany it, attempt to stop it, or race ahead through another route.

The kings may be willing to rise.

They may demand to know why the living have disturbed them again.

They may remember the draconic wars differently from every surviving history.

## Moraden’s Spear Is Fired

The ancient drill collapses the royal roads and destroys access to large portions of the Eighth and Ninth Layers.

This may prevent the kings from being raised.

It may also breach chambers never meant to be opened, damage the primordial forges, and sever the dwarven people from the oldest physical evidence of their history.

## The Truth Is Carried Upward

The ledgers, consent records, secret orders, and council debates provide evidence that every major institution in Unfazun participated in the corruption of necromancy.

There are no wholly innocent authorities.

Returning this evidence to the modern dwarven world could shatter clans, temples, royal claims, inherited fortunes, and the accepted history of the draconic wars.

The greatest treasure beneath Unfazun may not be gold, enchanted weapons, or the power to command the dead.

It may be the proof that an entire civilization survived by committing an act it later chose to forget.
